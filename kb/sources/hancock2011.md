## What it says
- 20 male-to-female transgender individuals living full-time as women completed the Transgender Self-Evaluation Questionnaire (TSEQ) and provided a speech sample (description of Norman Rockwell's "The Waiting Room"); 25 undergraduate listeners rated voice femininity and likability from audio-only recordings, and speakers self-rated the same dimensions.
- Quality of life (QoL) was moderately correlated with how listeners perceived participants' voices.
- QoL ratings correlated more strongly with speakers' own self-rated perception of their voice than with listeners' ratings — and this self-rating relationship was stronger for likability than for femininity.
- Concludes that both client self-report and listener perception are valuable, complementary outcome measures for evaluating vocal feminization treatment effectiveness.

## Bearing on voice training
- Evidence for including self-perception measures (not only naive-listener ratings) when tracking therapy or surgery outcomes, since QoL tracks more closely with how clients perceive their own voice.
- Small early study that helped motivate later, larger PROM development work (e.g., dacakis2013's TVQ(MtF)).

## Notes
- n=20 speakers, 25 listeners; single U.S. sample, cross-sectional design, no pre/post treatment comparison.
