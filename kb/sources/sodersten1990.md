# Glottal closure and perceived breathiness during phonation in normally speaking subjects

## What it says
- Nine female and nine male speakers aged 20–35 phonated /i/ at three loudness and three pitch levels; clinicians judged glottal closure from video-fiberstroboscopy and rated breathiness both in the fiberscopy recordings and in separate audio recordings (abstract).
- "The degree of incomplete glottal closure and the degree of perceived breathiness increased significantly as an effect of decreased loudness" (abstract).
- "Neither the degree of closure nor the perceived breathiness were significantly affected by changes in pitch or by interaction effects" (abstract).
- "It was concluded that incomplete glottal closure of the posterior parts of glottis should be regarded as normal primarily in women and that loudness should be taken into consideration when studying glottal closure and breathiness" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- A posterior glottal chink — the back of the vocal folds staying slightly open — is a normal finding in women rather than a fault. It is also loudness-dependent, so anyone measuring their own closure or breathiness has to hold loudness constant or the number means nothing.

## Notes
- 18 Swedish speakers, sustained /i/ only; judgements by clinicians, not naive listeners.
