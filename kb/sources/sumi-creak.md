# Creak (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- "Creak, sometimes called fry is a similar sounding rough, a-periodic noise. It happens when we have too much closure for the amount of airflow, and so the folds don't vibrate in a periodic wave, being interrupted occasionally."
- The wiki separates the two terms: "Creak and fry (M0) are distinct in practice (though they are thought to possibly be the same by some)" — "creak can be at any pitch and is caused primarily by airflow dynamics", while "fry is always at the bottom of the vocal range (though it can be coerced higher) and airflow mostly doesn't matter".
- On gender: "Creak is a fairly common part of female speech patterns. It isn't in itself gendered of course. Some accents (see: valley girl) have more creak in them."
- On removing it: "Since it's airflow based, adding a slight amount of air and making airflow more consistent especially at the end of phrases, can help. SOVTEs can help with this to some extent, particularly lip trills."
- The exercise given is the lip trill, because it "require[s] balanced airflow, which means we can use this to get a consistent airflow habit and therefore avoid creak"; the page links a video by Madeline Harvey (breath-support material from the start, trills at 4:38).

## Bearing on voice training
- Treats creak as an airflow-regulation problem rather than a register to be avoided, which is why the remedy is an SOVT (lip trill) rather than "stop creaking" [[voice-quality]] [[voice-therapy-techniques]].
- Puts creak inside feminine speech patterns rather than outside them — the wiki's position is that creak is not itself gendered and is present in many women's speech, so removing it is a stylistic choice [[gender-perception]].
- Separating creak from fry (M0) matters for training: the wiki assigns fry to the bottom of the range and creak to any pitch [[vocal-registers]].
