# Gender identity impacts the perception of vocal congruence

## What it says
- Study of "vocal congruence" — "the alignment between self-voice perception and the sense of identity" — in 44 cisgender and transgender/gender non-conforming (TGNC) participants, across three conditions: Silent Reading, Reading Aloud, and Listening to recorded speech (abstract).
- "TGNC participants reported significantly lower vocal congruence than cisgender participants across all experimental conditions, with the starkest difference in conditions where auditory feedback was present."
- The experience of incongruence "appears to be modulated by interoceptive sensibility and alexithymia", with TGNC participants reporting "lower interoceptive trust and higher levels of alexithymia".
- "Emotional awareness was positively linked to inner-voice congruence in the TGNC group."
- "Aspects related to gender-minority stress predicted lower congruence."

## Bearing on voice training
- Splits the inner voice from the heard voice: incongruence is largest when the person actually hears themselves, which is the condition a training session creates [[voice-impressions]] [[patient-reported-outcomes]].
- Names non-acoustic correlates — interoceptive trust, emotional awareness, minority stress — so the feeling of a voice not fitting is only partly a spectral question [[voice-health]].

## Notes
- n = 44, Sapienza University of Rome and CNR; the abstract reports no acoustic measurement, only self-report across the three conditions. Cross-sectional.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract (Front Cognit 2026 Mar 10;5:1638501).
