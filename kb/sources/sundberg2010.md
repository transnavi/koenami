# What is "Twang"?

## What it says
- "A single female professional vocal artist and pedagogue sang examples of 'twang' and neutral voice quality, which a panel of experts classified, in almost complete agreement with the singer's intentions."
- Subglottal pressure (oral pressure during /pae/ occlusion) "tended to be higher in 'twang,' whereas the sound pressure level (SPL) was invariably higher."
- Voice source and formants by inverse filtering: "In 'twang,' as compared with neutral, the closed quotient was greater, the pulse amplitude and the fundamental were weaker, and the normalized amplitude tended to be lower, whereas formants 1 and 2 were higher and 3 and 5 were lower."
- "The formant differences, which appeared to be the main cause of the SPL differences, were more important than the source differences for the perception of 'twanginess.'"
- "As resonatory effects occur independently of the voice source, the formant frequencies in 'twang' may reflect a vocal strategy that is advantageous from the point of view of vocal hygiene."

## Bearing on voice training
- Twang is mostly a *resonance* manoeuvre (narrowed epilarynx raising F1 and F2), which is why it makes a voice louder and brighter without making it heavier — the reason it is taught for carrying power and, in trans-feminine work, for brightness without pressing.
- Its accompanying source change is a *higher* closed quotient, i.e. more weight; a trainee chasing brightness through twang can pick up weight they did not want.

## Notes
- One singer, one session. Graded `low`. The "vocal hygiene" claim is a suggestion in the discussion, not a tested outcome.
