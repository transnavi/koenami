# Voice Characteristics at Presentation in Nonbinary Individuals Seeking Gender-Affirming Voice Care

## What it says
- Retrospective review of non-binary patients seeking voice treatment at the UCSF Voice & Swallowing Center: 25 people, 13 of them assigned male at birth, mean age 32.3 years (range 21–50), 84% white non-Hispanic (abstract, "Methods", "Results").
- 80% were on gender-affirming hormone replacement therapy, "with 56% receiving feminizing hormones", and most had already had other gender-affirming treatments (surgery, voice therapy, mental health services).
- All presented for gender-related voice concerns; "92% reported dissatisfaction with voice quality, and 52% utilized gendered language when describing their voice goals".
- Eight patients completed the VENI, mean score 45 out of 68. Mean overall dysphonia grade 29.4 on a 0–85 scale; "No organic changes were noted on videostroboscopy."
- "All patients were stimulable for improvement toward patient-expressed goals based on SLP assessment, and voice therapy was recommended to all patients."

## Bearing on voice training
- Baseline data for the group the literature usually omits: non-binary patients arrive with the same rate of voice dissatisfaction as binary patients, no laryngeal pathology, and — per the authors — goals that are stimulable, which is the clinical case for behavioural work rather than surgery [[nonbinary-voice]] [[speech-language-pathology]].
- The 52% who used gendered language about their goals is a caution against assuming the target is "androgynous"; the paper presents both [[voice-impressions]].

## Notes
- n = 25 from one US tertiary laryngology centre, retrospective, skewed sample (84% white, all help-seeking). The authors state this is "the first comprehensive description, at presentation, of NB individuals seeking gender-affirming voice care".
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
