# Vocal Mechanisms (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Definition: "A vocal mechanism or register is a distinct configuration of vocal fold interaction ... vocal mechanisms are distinct ways the folds interact; they describe how the vocal folds vibrate; phonation is always within one of these mechanisms; you can pass between mechanisms without a break with training."
- **M0** ("fry, strohbass, pulse"): "at the bottom of your vocal range ... This is when the folds are slack and the whole fold vibrates" — "below your lowest M1 note".
- **M1** ("normal speech, modal, chest voice"): "your normal speaking register", "where most people speak the majority of the time", "most of the vocal folds vibrates".
- **M2** ("falsetto, mix, head voice"): "a usually hollow, very high pitch register", "sometimes has a gap between folds / lack of closure", "the TA muscles deactivate somewhat", "mostly just mucosa of the folds vibrates".
- **M3** ("whistle, flageolet, flute register"): "mostly complete closure and a lot of tension, such that only a part of the folds vibrates ... usually starts at 1000hz and can go as high as 5000hz or more." Four sub-types are quoted from "Antonio Di Corcia and Franco Fussi's paper": FLW (Female Laryngeal Whistle — "very thin, stiff and tightly stretched vocal folds", "Often, no contact between the folds during phonation was noted"), SCW (Stop Closure Whistle — "the whistle sound depends on a tiny orifice at the anterior third of the folds", with "a total absence of vibration ... caused by the stop_closure phenomenon"), IW (Ingressive Whistle — "high activity of the adductor muscles", "absence of vibrating parts during ingressive phonation"), and Scream in M3 ("very stretched, thin and stiff vocal folds and an high constriction of the false vocal folds").
- **The No Separation Hypothesis**, presented by the wiki as a hypothesis: "the difference between mechanisms is not a vibratory pattern that is distinct and separated, but instead a grouping of features of voice that coincide with that resulting vibratory pattern". The sketch offered: M2 "might have lower IA closure, leading to incomplete vibration of the folds and lower closed quotient (CQ)", and the break "could be explained as being caused by the imbalance of vibration frequency and CQ"; M3 "is simply very low pressure and high closure", and M0 "is when the vibration cannot happen slow enough". The wiki marks its own confidence: "This hypothesis fits the anecdotal and experimental observation of many people, but it is not known if these are the physical factors or if the relationship is coincidental." A further corollary offered for the absence of a break in some (especially cis female) voices: "either a lack of ability to increase CQ to imbalance causing levels, or that the pressure is too low to cause that CQ; in other words the voice is too light to break."

## Bearing on voice training
- Supplies the register framework the community trains against — M1 as everyday speech, M2 as the light high register, M0 as fry — and states that crossing between them without a break is a trainable skill, which is the claim the register exercises rest on [[vocal-registers]] [[community-training]].
- The M2 description ("the TA muscles deactivate somewhat", "mostly just mucosa of the folds vibrates") is the community's version of the cover–body account of register, and its lack-of-closure note is why M2 unhelpfully interacts with breathiness work [[vocal-folds]] [[breathiness]].
- The No Separation Hypothesis is flagged as unproven in the source itself; it is recorded here as the wiki's proposal, not as an established finding [[voice-quality]].
