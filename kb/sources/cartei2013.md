## What it says
- Notes that listeners can usually identify pre-pubertal children's sex from voice alone despite no sex difference in F0 at that age, and tests whether formant frequency spacing (ΔF, related to perceived vocal tract length) drives this.
- Manipulated ΔF of recordings from four 8-year-olds (2 boys, 2 girls) along a continuum spanning the naturally observed range in pre-pubertal voices, then ran two experiments: sex categorization (male/female forced choice) and gender rating (masculine boy ↔ feminine girl scale).
- As ΔF increased, sex-categorization responses graded smoothly from male to female, and masculinity ratings decreased smoothly — i.e., ΔF alone, with F0 held constant, shifts both perceived sex and perceived gender typicality.
- Concludes that ΔF's known role in adult voice-gender perception extends to pre-pubertal children even though there is no anatomical vocal-tract-length dimorphism at that age yet.

## Bearing on voice training
- Strong controlled (resynthesis) evidence that formant spacing/perceived vocal tract length is a gradient, independently manipulable cue to perceived gender — separable from pitch — reinforcing that formant/resonance work in voice training is not merely cosmetic but has a demonstrated, graded perceptual effect.
- Because this effect appears even in children with no anatomical VTL sex difference, it implies ΔF perception partly reflects learned/behavioral cues (e.g., articulatory setting) rather than pure anatomy — relevant to why resonance training can move perceived gender for adults with fixed vocal tract anatomy too.

## Notes
- Very small source-speaker set (4 children); ΔF manipulated by resynthesis/formant-shifting, not natural production.
- Listener population: adults rating children's voices, not self-ratings or child listeners.
