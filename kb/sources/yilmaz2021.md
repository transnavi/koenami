## What it says
- Retrospective, nonrandomized cohort of 28 transgender women who underwent endoscopic laser reduction glottoplasty at a tertiary center; outcomes measured with VHI-30, Transsexual Voice Questionnaire (TVQ), acoustic analysis (F0, jitter, shimmer, noise-to-harmonic ratio for /a/), and speaking F0, before and after surgery.
- Mean total VHI and TVQ scores improved significantly postoperatively (P < .001).
- Mean F0 rose from 132 Hz to 198 Hz (sustained /a/) and speaking F0 from 123 Hz to 194 Hz (both P < .001); postoperative jitter, shimmer and NHR increased significantly (P < .05).
- 9 of 28 patients (32%) were unhappy with the outcome and were offered anterior glottic web formation as a secondary procedure.
- Self-rating: 3 masculine, 19 feminine, 6 neither, giving a patient gratification score of 68%; medical students rated 79% of postoperative voice samples feminine, voice experts rated 75% feminine.
- Concludes the procedure is durable at 5 years but that a secondary operation may be needed for some patients.

## Bearing on voice training
- Gives concrete magnitude and variance data for laser reduction glottoplasty: roughly +66 Hz for sustained vowel F0 and +71 Hz for speaking F0, alongside a real revision-surgery rate (32%) and quantified voice-quality tradeoff (jitter/shimmer increase).
- Useful counterpoint to Wendler glottoplasty and CTA outcome numbers when comparing surgical techniques.

## Notes
- n=28, single-institution, nonrandomized, no untreated or alternative-technique comparison group.
- Population: transgender women in Turkey; 5-year follow-up claimed but exact per-patient follow-up length distribution not detailed in the abstract.
