## What it says
- Synthesized 210 stimuli (vowels /i, æ, ɑ, u/) crossing 10 fundamental-frequency values, 7 implied-vocal-tract-length values (with associated formant frequencies) spanning male-typical to female-typical, and 3 source spectral-tilt values approximating breathy, normal, and pressed voice quality.
- 23 listeners judged each synthesized "speaker" as female or male; a generalized linear mixed model analyzed how F0, implied VTL, and spectral tilt jointly influenced judgments.
- Increasing F0 and decreasing implied VTL both increased the probability of a female judgment; two interactions were found — an F0 increase and a spectral-tilt decrease (more negative/breathier) together produced a larger boost in female-judgment probability specifically when implied VTL was relatively short.
- Concludes there is no single fixed F0 or VTL "threshold" that sharply flips perceived gender; instead, the relative contribution of each cue shifts across the range of values tested, and spectral tilt (breathiness) interacts with F0 and VTL rather than acting independently.

## Bearing on voice training
- Directly shows breathiness/spectral tilt is not a standalone gender cue but interacts with pitch and perceived vocal tract length — breathier voice quality boosts perceived femininity specifically when F0 is also raised and perceived VTL is already short, meaning breathiness training likely has the most payoff as a complement to, not a substitute for, pitch and resonance work.
- The absence of a sharp F0/VTL threshold argues against "hit this exact Hz number" framing in training; gender perception is a continuous, interacting function of multiple cues across their normal ranges.

## Notes
- Fully synthetic, resynthesis-based stimuli (isolated vowels only) — high internal control but lower ecological validity than natural connected speech.
- Small listener panel (n=23); "implied vocal tract length" is a formant-based proxy manipulated directly, not measured from real vocal tract anatomy.
