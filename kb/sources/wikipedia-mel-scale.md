# Mel scale (English Wikipedia)

## What it says
- Summary endpoint, 2026-09-17: "The mel scale is a perceptual scale of pitches judged by listeners to be equal in distance from one another. The reference point between this scale and normal frequency measurement is defined by assigning a perceptual pitch of 1000 mels to a 1000 Hz tone, 40 dB above the listener's threshold. Above about 500 Hz, increasingly large intervals are judged by listeners to produce equal pitch increments."
- Short description: "Conceptual scale."
- The name, per the extracts API, is "after the word melody".

## Bearing on voice training
- The third pitch scale a learner meets, after hertz and semitones: equal *perceived* steps, not equal frequency ratios. It is the scale the original experiment in [[stevens1937]] constructed. Speech-technology "mel filterbanks" inherit the shape of this curve; they are not a voice-training tool.

## Limits
- Community-edited. Constants differ across later formulae; this page's 1000 Hz = 1000 mels at 40 dB is the historical anchor, not a unique modern definition.
