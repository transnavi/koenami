# Testosterone therapy for transgender men

## What it says
- Review of testosterone therapy for transgender men: the goal is usually serum testosterone in the male reference range; desired effects include "increased facial and body hair, increased lean mass and strength, decreased fat mass, deepening of the voice, increased sexual desire, cessation of menstruation, clitoral enlargement, and reductions in gender dysphoria, perceived stress, anxiety, and depression" (abstract).
- "Most of the effects of testosterone start to develop within several months of starting therapy, although facial hair and alopecia continue to develop after 1 year" (abstract).
- Undesired effects listed include acne, alopecia, reduced HDL cholesterol, increased triglycerides and a possible rise in systolic blood pressure; an added benefit (with or without mastectomy) is reduced breast-cancer risk (abstract).
- Names the field's evidence problem: "a paucity of high-quality data due to a shortage of randomised controlled trials (partly because of ethical issues), few prospective and long-term studies, the use of suboptimum control groups, loss to follow-up, and difficulties in recruitment of representative samples" (abstract).
- A 2017 erratum corrected the published review (PubMed record).

## Bearing on voice training
- The endocrinologist's statement that voice deepening is an expected, months-scale effect of testosterone — the framing [[voice-masculinization]] rests on — together with an explicit caveat that the underlying trial evidence is thin.

## Notes
- Single-author narrative review in a Lancet journal; not a systematic review. Voice is one item in a whole-body review; the voice-specific time course comes from [[irwig2017a]] and [[hancock2017a]].
