# High-speed imaging and electroglottography measurements of the open quotient in untrained male voices' register transitions

## What it says
- 18 untrained male subjects sang a glissando from modal to falsetto while vocal-fold oscillation and open quotient were measured with high-speed digital imaging (HSDI) and electroglottography (EGG).
- "The open quotient changed with register in both HSDI and EGG"; in-class correlations within each method were high but "only weak interclass correlations between both methods" were found.
- "Our results confirm previous observations that falsetto register is associated with a higher open quotient compared with modal register."
- In 10 of 18 subjects, irregularities of vocal-fold vibration occurred during the transition; the audio and EGG irregularities heard at a register break are caused by irregular vocal-fold vibration.

## Bearing on voice training
- The measurable correlate of "light" versus "heavy": falsetto spends more of each cycle open. Open (or its complement, closed) quotient is what an EGG-based trainer is showing when it displays "contact quotient" — but the weak agreement between EGG and imaging warns against reading EGG numbers as direct glottal measurements.
- Untrained speakers break audibly at the transition; smoothing it is a trainable skill, not a property of the larynx.

## Notes
- Male participants only, untrained, singing glissandi — not connected speech.
