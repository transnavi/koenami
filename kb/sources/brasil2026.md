# Mapping the Characteristics of Voice and Communication Harmonization Programs for Transgender Women

## What it says
- Joanna Briggs Institute scoping review, registered on the Open Science Framework, searching LILACS, MEDLINE, EMBASE, Web of Science, Scopus, Cochrane CENTRAL plus grey literature (ProQuest, medRxiv, Google Scholar), citation mapping and expert consultation, with no language or date limits (abstract, "Methods").
- "A total of 1908 records were identified, of which 35 studies met the eligibility criteria." Most were from the United States (28.6%), published in 2024 (22.9%) and appeared in the Journal of Voice (37.1%); mean sample size 22.34 participants.
- Study profile: most studies included participants on gender-affirming hormone therapy (31.4%), without previous vocal feminisation surgery (65.7%) and without previous voice harmonisation programmes (60.0%) — shares of studies, as the abstract phrases it.
- Delivery: "predominantly traditional, in-person, and individual, delivered weekly, with a mean of 12.49 sessions, and lacked follow-up in 54.3% of studies".
- Targets: vocal function (94.3%), speech and communication function (88.6%), pedagogy and counselling (80.0%). Ingredients: "resonance-based methods (88.6%), guidance and counseling (80.0%), pitch and loudness modification techniques (77.1%), and articulatory exercises (71.4%)".
- Conclusion: programmes prioritise voice and communication targets, but ingredients "are largely concentrated on vocal adjustments, while components addressing broader communicative aspects remain less explored".

## Bearing on voice training
- The clearest picture of what clinical training programmes actually consist of: about twelve weekly individual sessions, resonance first, then counselling, then pitch/loudness, then articulation [[voice-therapy-techniques]] [[voice-feminization-therapy]].
- Two figures worth remembering: more than half the studies have no follow-up at all, and resonance-based methods appear in nearly nine of ten programmes — the field's central technique is also the one with the least dose–response evidence [[formants]] [[speech-language-pathology]].

## Notes
- Registry-based review with independent double screening; descriptive synthesis only (the authors do not meta-analyse). Most included studies are small (mean n ≈ 22), and the largest shares are from the United States and from the Journal of Voice, so the "typical programme" is largely that picture.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
