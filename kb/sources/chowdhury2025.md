## What it says
- PRISMA systematic review (searches through January 2025 of Ovid MEDLINE, Embase, CENTRAL, PsycINFO, CINAHL, and gray literature) comparing surgical and nonsurgical pitch-elevation techniques in transgender women (≥18 years), using both acoustic outcomes and validated PROMs (VHI, Trans Woman Voice Questionnaire/TWVQ). Risk of bias assessed with the Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool and Newcastle-Ottawa Scale.
- 22 studies (N = 977) included, covering Wendler glottoplasty (WG), cricothyroid approximation (CTA), vocal fold shortening and retrodisplacement of the anterior commissure (VFSRAC), laser-assisted voice adjustment (LAVA), laser-reduction glottoplasty (LRG), voice therapy (VT), and combinations.
- Weighted mean F0 increases: VFSRAC +73.3 Hz, WG +54.1 Hz, VT +39.9 Hz, CTA +30.3 Hz, LAVA +26 Hz; WG + VT combined gave +54.5 Hz with more consistent outcomes.
- TWVQ score improvement was largest for WG + VT (−47.9), then WG (−29.0), then VT (−18.7); VHI improvement was largest for LAVA (−48.0) and WG (−21.7), with more modest PROM gains for VFSRAC and CTA.
- Adverse events (granuloma, vocal fatigue) were more common with surgical combinations; follow-up ranged 2 weeks to 4.4 years, most studies 6–12 months.
- Conclusion: both surgical and nonsurgical interventions elevate pitch and improve voice-related quality of life; surgery yields larger F0 gains, combining with voice therapy improves patient satisfaction; standardized PROMs are needed in future research.

## Bearing on voice training
- The single best quantitative comparison across techniques currently available in this cluster — gives ranked, weighted-mean F0 and PROM effect sizes for every major surgical and nonsurgical pitch-elevation method, directly supporting a "which technique, how much gain" concept page.
- Strongly supports the case for combining voice therapy with surgery (WG + VT) for best TWVQ outcomes, echoing park2024 and vanborsel2008's caveat that surgery alone underperforms combined approaches on patient-reported measures.

## Notes
- Narrative synthesis with weighted means, not a formal meta-analysis with pooled effect sizes and heterogeneity statistics; underlying primary studies vary widely in design quality (per Cochrane RoB/Newcastle-Ottawa ratings).
- One author reports a consulting relationship with PENTAX Medical (declared conflict of interest, unrelated to the pitch-alteration interventions reviewed).
