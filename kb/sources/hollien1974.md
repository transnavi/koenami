# On vocal registers

## What it says
- The bibliographic record was verified but the full text could not be fetched; the two statements below are quoted from secondary sources that were fetched in full and that cite this paper.
- Hollien's definition, as quoted by [[herbst2020]] (his note 35): "A vocal register is a series or range of consecutive voice frequencies which can be produced with nearly identical voice quality." Herbst adds that Hollien "further suggests that registers should be operationally defined on perceptual, acoustic, physiologic, and aerodynamic levels."
- A fuller form of the same definition, as quoted by [[bozeman2022]] (his note 3): "A voice register is a series or range of consecutive phonated frequencies of nearly identical voice quality; they are totally laryngeal events and there is little or no overlap in fundamental frequency between adjacent registers."
- [[herbst2020]]'s Table 1 lists Hollien 1974 as proposing three registers: pulse, modal, loft.

## Bearing on voice training
- The origin of the speech-pathology triad pulse / modal / loft that still underlies most clinical writing about registers, and of the claim — contested by later acoustic work ([[bozeman2022]]) — that registers are *totally laryngeal* events.
