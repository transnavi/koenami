# Differences between masculine and feminine voices (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Framing: "There's also a huge spectrum of voice types and modifications, which makes comparing them generally almost untenable. However, some generalisations can be made". The perceptual model offered: "when people hear a voice they subconsciously ask themselves 'what is the closest gender category that this falls in'. There are no magic numbers or min/maxing, only vague descriptions."
- Stated effects of testosterone on the voice: "slight elongation of the vocal tract"; "expansion of the oropharynx (mouth and top of throat)"; "expansion of the larynx and lengthening [need verification] of vocal folds (lowering in pitch)"; "slight thickening of the vocal fold ligament (vocal fold mass which affects vocal weight)" — the wiki's own inline "[need verification]" is left in place here because it marks the claim as unconfirmed on the page itself.
- Typical features of feminine voices, as listed: "low in vocal weight", "high in resonance", "bright, clear tone", "high in pitch with a high pitch ceiling", "use of M2 or mix for intonation and other speech features", "high intonation range and frequency of inflection", "more tempo changes through speech", "pitch used as emphasis".
- Typical features of masculine voices: "heavy in vocal weight", "low to medium in resonance", "rumbly, heavy tone", "some voices dipping into M0/fry at bottom of pitch range", "low intonation range and very low intonation frequency", "less tempo changes", "weight and volume used as emphasis".
- "Gendered factors - In very (very) rough order of their effect on vocal gender (when isolated). In-exhaustive." The order given: "vocal weight", "microbehaviours", "resonance", "speech patterns" (with "tempo fluctuation", "intonation frequency", "intonation range" as sub-items).
- A separate list of "Out-of-place factors": "These are not gendered but are uncommon to see and so make a voice more questionable or sound unnatural. In-exhaustive." Listed: "hollowness (such as in M2 or TA deactivation)", "breathiness", "FVF constriction", "too much speech pattern/accent modification", "tongue root retraction", "nasality".

## Bearing on voice training
- The wiki's own ranking puts vocal weight ahead of resonance and pitch — the community's strongest departure from the clinical literature, where F0 explains the largest share of listener judgements [[vocal-weight]] [[gender-perception]] [[leung2018]].
- Separating "gendered factors" from "out-of-place factors" is a distinction the clinical literature mostly lacks: some features make a voice read as unnatural without moving the gender judgement either way, and the wiki lists nasality, breathiness and hollowness among them [[voice-impressions]] [[breathiness]].
- Its model of perception as nearest-category rather than a threshold matches the "gradient" framing in the perceptual literature [[cartei2013]] [[gender-perception]].

## Notes
- Volunteer wiki, "under construction". The ordering of gendered factors is stated by the wiki as rough and inexhaustive, with no evidence given for it. The testosterone list carries the wiki's own unverified marker on the vocal-fold lengthening claim; the vault's clinical sources describe the testosterone effect through F0, vocal tract and fold mass [[hormones-and-voice]] [[irwig2017a]].
