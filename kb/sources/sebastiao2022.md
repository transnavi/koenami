# Transgender women: experiences and perceptions about health, gender dysphoria, voice and society

## What it says
- Cross-sectional qualitative study, semi-structured interviews, participants found by snowball sampling, data analysed by content analysis (abstract, "Method").
- Participants were from the state of São Paulo, most of them studying and/or working. They 「use hormones unsupervised due to the urgency of aligning with their gender identity」.
- On the mechanism of the complaint: 「Negative social experiences generate discomfort and insecurity, showing that the other's point of view impairs self-perception. Thus, they seek passability to avoid harassment.」 The voice was seen as 「a trigger to have their bodies and gender questioned」.
- On what therapy did for them: vocal therapy 「is seen as positive, for working on vocal potentialities, self-perception, and self-acceptance」.
- Peer support mattered: 「Having peer support and positive transgender references provide greater self-confidence, acceptance, and reassurance in gender confirmation.」
- The authors' final position: psychosocial aspects and cisheteronormativity 「should be considered in transgender health care, including vocal therapy」.

## Bearing on voice training
- Self-perception is described here as mediated by the listener, exactly as [[santana2022]] finds statistically and [[nemr2026]] states clinically — the same structure, from three different methods ([[gender-perception]], [[patient-reported-outcomes]]).
- "Self-acceptance" and "self-perception" being named as therapy's value, rather than pitch, is the qualitative counterweight to the F0-centred protocols in [[dornelas2021]] and [[cielo2021]].
- The unsupervised hormone use reported here is the same access fact [[sosthenes2025]] records from Alagoas.

## Notes
- Snowball sample from one state, no acoustic data, no therapy delivered as part of the study; graded low.
- Claims taken from the English abstract; the interview coding and the participant count (not stated in the abstract) were not read.
