## What it says
- Prospective follow-up study: 30 transgender women completed a 10-week gender-affirming voice training program (pitch elevation + articulation-resonance training), then were assessed immediately after, at 3 months, and at 1 year post-training.
- Self-perception and satisfaction measures (Trans Women Voice Questionnaire, visual analogue scale) remained stable across the follow-up period — gains made in training persisted at both 3 months and 1 year.
- 39 listeners rated feminity of voice samples; listener-rated femininity significantly decreased (regressed toward less-feminine) immediately after training and again at 3 months, although ratings stayed within the "gender-ambiguous" zone; no further significant change occurred between 3 months and 1 year.
- Interprets this divergence — stable self-perception vs. declining listener-rated femininity over time — as motivating longer follow-up periods and qualitative research in future studies.

## Bearing on voice training
- Important caution for setting expectations in voice training programs: listener-perceived femininity gains from a 10-week training block are not fully durable without maintenance — ratings drifted toward less feminine over the following months even though the trainee's own satisfaction stayed stable.
- Suggests self-perception and external (listener) perception of trained voice outcomes can diverge over time, which matters for how "success" is measured and communicated in training programs (self-report alone may overstate durability of listener-perceived change).
