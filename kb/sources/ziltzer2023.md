# Needs Assessment of Gender-Affirming Face, Neck, and Voice Procedures and the Role of Gender Dysphoria

## What it says
- Online cross-sectional survey, February–May 2022, of 234 transgender and nonbinary (TNB) adults on need for gender-affirming face, neck and voice procedures, with site-specific dysphoria rated 0–10 (abstract).
- Utilisation was low: TNB participants "infrequently sought gender-affirming care with speech-language pathologists (23%), facial plastic surgeons (8%), or laryngologists (3%)" (abstract).
- Voice produced the strongest dysphoria of any site: median 7/10, against jawline/chin 4/10 and neck 3.5/10 (abstract).
- Want-versus-had gaps: among transmasculine/nonbinary participants seeking masculinization (n = 83), 35% wanted voice therapy and 8% had had it; among transfeminine/nonbinary participants seeking feminization (n = 145), 52% wanted voice therapy and 23% had had it, 45% wanted chondrolaryngoplasty and 5% had had it (abstract).
- Dysphoria ratings predicted desiring the relevant procedure (voice therapy OR = 1.50, chondrolaryngoplasty OR = 1.46, among others) (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The access picture behind [[transgender-care-context]]: voice is where dysphoria concentrates, and it is also the service people most want and least get — demand for voice therapy runs roughly double the uptake in both feminization- and masculinization-seeking groups.
- Quantifies the unmet need that telepractice ([[lin2026]], [[myers2022]]) and service specifications ([[nhse1719]]) are answers to.

## Notes
- US-weighted online convenience sample; "want/had" is self-report, not a service census. The 23% SLP utilisation figure is the survey's headline access statistic.
