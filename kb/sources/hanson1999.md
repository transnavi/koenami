# Glottal characteristics of male speakers: Acoustic correlates and comparison with female data

## What it says
- Abstract: acoustic measurements "believed to reflect glottal characteristics" on 21 male speakers, vowels /æ, ʌ, ɛ/, parameters "related to first-formant bandwidth, open quotient, spectral tilt, and aspiration noise." Compared with 22 female speakers in [[hanson1997]].
- "While there is considerable overlap across gender, the male data show lower average values and less interspeaker variation for all measures."
- "the amplitude of the first harmonic relative to that of the third formant is 9.6 dB lower for the male speakers than for the female speakers, suggesting that spectral tilt is an especially significant parameter for differentiating male and female speech."
- "These findings are consistent with fiberscopic studies which have shown that males tend to have a more complete glottal closure, leading to less energy loss at the glottis and less spectral tilt."
- Some male waveforms suggested "a second glottal excitation within a glottal period."

## Bearing on voice training
- Puts numbers on the source difference the community hears as weight: men, on average, close more completely and have less spectral tilt (weaker H1 relative to higher harmonics). The overlap sentence matters — the distributions are not two islands.
- Open quotient is named as one of the measured correlates, which is the quantity community pages equate with vocal weight ([[sumi-vocal-weight]]).

## Notes
- Abstract only. American English vowels. Graded `medium`.
