# Speech Patterns (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- "Typical behaviours associated with feminine voices": "higher range of intonation", "more frequent intonation", "use of tempo to vary speech", "emphasis with high pitch intonation", "(not always) vocal creak (not fry) at the end of speech segments", "use of pitch and airflow to get more volume".
- "Typical behaviours associated with masculine voices": "intonation is more stable and less range", "less frequent intonation", "not much tempo change", "use of vocal weight and volume for emphasis", "sometimes downward intonation often into vocal fry", "use of vocal weight to get more volume".
- The page presents the two lists as the same features running in opposite directions — intonation range and frequency, tempo variation, and the mechanism used for emphasis and for loudness.
- Training method offered: "You can use an emotive phrase to get into the 'character' of the voice you're going for. Choose something that you can really overact, you can always dial it back later." Example lines given: "I can't believe they said that!"; "Oh my god that is so cute I love it!"; "What!? Why would you say that!?"; "Uhhhg I can't believe this is taking so long. Get on with it already!"; "I'll take two donuts. Actually, why not, I deserve it! I'll take six."; "Where did you get that dress? It's beautiful!"

## Bearing on voice training
- The community's intonation and prosody checklist, stated as behaviour rather than as measurement — the same variables the clinical literature tests under the names intonation range and frequency [[intonation]] [[gender-perception]].
- The emphasis mechanism is a gendered fork worth noting: feminine speech patterns use pitch and airflow for loudness, masculine ones use weight and volume, which places loudness work inside whichever weight strategy the speaker is training [[breathing-and-support]] [[vocal-weight]].
- Overacting emotive phrases is a prosody-training device aimed at range and frequency at once, which is what the Ghent intonation trials measured [[papeleu2025]] [[voice-therapy-techniques]].
