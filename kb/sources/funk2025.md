## What it says
- Longitudinal study of German-speaking prepubertal children (ages 6–9) examining whether spectral characteristics of the sibilant /z/ — center of gravity (CoG) and skewness — relate to gender perception.
- No overall sex differences in CoG or skewness were found in the children's natural sibilant productions.
- Two listening experiments tested gender perception: one using natural stimuli, one using voice-morphed stimuli isolating the effect of sibilant spectra specifically. Sibilant spectra had no effect on gender perception when embedded in natural stimuli, but significant effects emerged in the morphed condition.
- Interpreted as showing that broader acoustic cues (e.g., F0, formants) dominate natural gender perception, but sibilants carry stereotypical gender associations that become perceptible once other cues are controlled/isolated via morphing.

## Bearing on voice training
- Reinforces that sibilant/articulation cues are a secondary, not primary, driver of perceived gender relative to F0 and formants in natural connected speech — consistent with the cue-hierarchy findings in skuk2014 and gelfer2013 — but shows sibilant spectral shape does carry a real, if subtler, gendered social association that becomes audible once dominant cues are neutralized.
- Extends the sibilant-gender association literature (munson2017, hope2023) to prepubertal children, showing the /s/-gender stereotype is present in listener perception even before puberty-driven anatomical voice change, i.e., it is a learned social/acoustic association rather than one dependent on anatomical sex differences in sibilant production.
