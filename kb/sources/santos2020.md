# Transgender man voice therapy: a case report

## What it says
- One 35-year-old trans man, eight months into hormone treatment, whose complaints were 「voice incompatible with his gender and vocal oscillation after hormonal treatment」 (abstract).
- The premise the authors start from: hormone therapy lowers F0, 「but the evidence suggests that it does not modify other female communication characteristics, what may result in insufficient male communication patterns」.
- The plan, listed in full in the abstract, targeted: descending pitch at the end of sentences; less vowel prolongation; "chest resonance"; less pitch variation; costodiaphragmatic breathing; vocal projection and quality; maximum phonation time and pauses; lower and more stable F0; resonance adjustment; less tension at the labial commissures; and male speech-and-language features. Ten weekly sessions of 45 minutes.
- Results as reported: decreased pitch variation in speech, increased pauses, focus on the interlocutor and "chest resonance", which together produced descending sentence-final pitch and less vowel prolongation. 「Even after the hormone-induced vocal changes, he still had complaints about his voice, which improved with the aid of voice therapy.」
- Conclusion: therapy 「provided the development of male vocal markers」; the voice became compatible with his gender and he was recognized as a man by his voice.

## Bearing on voice training
- A rare worked transmasculine protocol, and the target list is the mirror image of the feminisation lists: descending rather than ascending sentence-final pitch ([[intonation]]), less rather than more pitch movement, and a resonance target ([[voice-masculinization]], [[voice-therapy-techniques]]).
- It is also evidence for the claim that testosterone does not finish the job ([[hormones-and-voice]], [[barros2018]]) — the case exists because the participant was still unhappy eight months in.

## Notes
- n = 1, no blinding, no independent listener panel reported in the abstract; graded low. The outcome measures are the therapist's own description.
- Claims taken from the English abstract; the session-by-session record and any acoustic data were not read.
