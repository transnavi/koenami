# Endocrine Treatment of Gender-Dysphoric/Gender-Incongruent Persons: An Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline

## What it says
- Updates the Endocrine Society's 2009 "Endocrine Treatment of Transsexual Persons" guideline; developed by a nine-expert task force with a methodologist using GRADE, commissioning two systematic reviews (abstract).
- "Hormone treatment is not recommended for prepubertal gender-dysphoric/gender-incongruent persons" (abstract) — the endocrine route to voice change therefore begins at puberty at the earliest, via gonadotropin-releasing hormone analogues and later sex hormones.
- A safe and effective regimen must "(1) suppress endogenous sex hormone secretion determined by the person's genetic/gonadal sex and (2) maintain sex hormone levels within the normal range for the person's affirmed gender" (abstract).
- Recommends that clinicians who prescribe gender-affirming endocrine treatment work with appropriately trained diagnosing clinicians (required), a mental health provider for adolescents (required) and a mental health professional for adults (recommended) (abstract).
- Two 2018 errata corrected parts of the published guideline (PubMed record).

## Bearing on voice training
- The endocrinology side of the care pathway this vault's [[transgender-care-context]] describes: hormones are prescribed within a multidisciplinary assessment, and for voice they are one-directional — the masculinizing regimen lowers pitch ([[voice-masculinization]]), while the feminizing regimen does not raise it ([[hormones-and-voice]], per [[leyns2021]] and the Wikipedia feminizing-hormone-therapy article).
