## What it says
- Analyzed multiple progressive voice-change recordings posted publicly online by 21 trans men, each with at least 10 unique recording dates within the first year of testosterone therapy; measured vowel duration, jitter, shimmer, and spectral fricative-skew and fricative-kurtosis changes.
- Six separate linear mixed-effects regression models tested the effect of time point on mean fundamental frequency, vowel duration, jitter, shimmer, fricative kurtosis, and fricative skew.
- Over a year of testosterone therapy: significant decrease in fundamental frequency, significant decrease in shimmer, and significant increase in fricative kurtosis.
- No significant changes were found in vowel duration, jitter, or fricative skew.
- Concludes testosterone therapy affects voice features beyond F0 alone (shimmer, fricative spectral characteristics), and that specialized voice therapy for trans men should consider which features do and do not change with hormones alone.

## Bearing on voice training
- Directly counters an oversimplified "testosterone alone fully masculinizes the voice" narrative — F0 drops robustly, but articulatory/spectral features like fricative production and some voice-quality measures (shimmer) also shift, while others (jitter, vowel duration) do not change with hormones alone, implying these might be legitimate targets for voice therapy in trans men.
- The public-recording longitudinal design (naturalistic, within-subject, dense sampling) is a useful methodological contrast to clinic-based single-timepoint studies like nygren2016 (skipped in this cluster) or maeda2025.

## Notes
- Convenience sample of publicly posted recordings (self-selected trans men documenting their transition online), not a clinical cohort; no control for individual dosing regimens, recording equipment, or background noise across the crowd-sourced videos.
