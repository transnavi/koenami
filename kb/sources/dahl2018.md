## What it says
- Investigated which acoustic features of trans women's voices correlate with self- and listener ratings of voice femininity and with listener perceptions of gender, using speech from 12 trans women and 10 cisgender control speakers.
- Measured speaking fundamental frequency (SFF), SFF variation, intensity, vowel formants, and correlates of breathiness for each speaker.
- Speakers self-rated voice femininity via the Transsexual Voice Questionnaire for Male-to-Female Transsexuals; blind listeners separately rated femininity and classified each speaker's gender in a binary system; correlations used Spearman's rank-order coefficient.
- Moderate-to-strong correlations were found between femininity ratings and mean/maximum SFF, SFF variation, and mean intensity; these same four variables (SFF, its variation, and intensity — i.e., pitch, intonation, loudness) were also moderately correlated with listener gender perception.
- No consistent or significant correlations were found between femininity ratings/gender perception and minimum SFF, vowel formants, or breathiness measures. Trans-vs-cis group differences on these acoustic variables were explored but limited by small sample size.
- Concludes pitch, intonation (pitch variation), and loudness — not formants or breathiness, in this sample — are the acoustic features most supported as evidence-based targets for voice training of trans women.

## Bearing on voice training
- A notable outlier relative to most sources in this cluster: found no significant correlation between formants or breathiness measures and femininity/gender ratings, contrasting with resynthesis studies (skuk2014, cartei2013, neuhaus2026) that isolate formants/spectral tilt as influential. This likely reflects the correlational, small-sample, natural-speech design rather than disproving the causal role of formants — worth flagging as a point of disagreement in the concept notes on formants and voice quality.
- Highlights pitch variation (intonation), not just mean pitch, as independently predictive of femininity ratings — supporting intonation-pattern training, not only static pitch-raising.

## Notes
- Low evidence grade: unpublished master's thesis, small sample (12 trans women, 10 cis controls), correlational (not causal/experimental) design.
- Findings on formants/breathiness not reaching significance may reflect limited statistical power rather than true absence of effect; should not be read as contradicting the larger resynthesis literature without more data.
