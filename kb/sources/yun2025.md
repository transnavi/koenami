# '목소리가 되다' : 트랜스 여성의 목소리 트랜지션과 끝없는 감응의 과정

## What it says
- What it sets out to displace: 「Moving beyond traditional understandings of vocal transition as a linear journey toward a fixed ‘female’ voice, the study conceptualizes it as a process of becoming, defined by interactions between bodies and human/nonhuman actors. By moving beyond transition as a phenomenon confined to medical technology, this research seeks to broaden the understanding of vocal transition and its significance by illuminating its diverse forms.」
- Its theoretical move: 「This study draws on Latour's concept of ‘becoming a nose’, adapting it to conceptualize the vocal transition process as ‘becoming a voice’. To ‘become a voice’, one must learn to be affected by various human and non-human actors… Thus, the ‘female voice’ that vocal transition aims to achieve is not a fixed endpoint but a direction that takes shape through dynamic relational processes.」
- The material analysed: 「This study analyzes medical research on voice, internet discourse on vocal transition, and the experiences of transgender women who have undergone vocal transition.」 And the finding: 「The analysis reveals that the female voice imagined in these sources does not converge into a unified endpoint… These pathways of exploration are not independent; rather, different methods of vocal transition intersect, shaping the very direction of femininity.」
- The actors it names: 「This study examines the ongoing reconfiguration of these boundaries through actors such as telephones, voice chat, recording devices, apps, spectrograms, and listeners.」 — the technical apparatus of voice work treated as participants rather than tools.
- On the route it describes: 「The journey begins with ‘becoming an ear’, developing sensitivity to voice through experience and training. It also includes the search for one's own everyday, default voice, rather than producing any temporary female voice. This process is both constrained and expanded by relations and the physical limits of the body.」 And its conclusion: 「the process of reaching a desired voice never truly ends. Without a fixed endpoint, vocal transitioning is an ongoing process of exploring and searching for direction.」
- On the politics: it argues that 「transitioning is a long-term process that involves not only medical interventions but also various practices that may or may not engage medical technology」, and that by widening the sense of transition it 「challenges the pathologization of trans bodies in medicine and the claim in certain strands of feminism that transitioning conforms to gender norms」.

## Bearing on voice training
- The vault's first sustained argument that the target of voice work is a direction rather than an endpoint, and it says so from a Korean STS department rather than from the community ([[voice-impressions]], [[transgender-care-context]]).
- The list of actors — telephone, voice chat, recording, apps, spectrograms, listeners — is the same apparatus the community uses to judge its own voice, and treating them as co-producers rather than neutral instruments is an argument against scoring tools that assume the voice is an analysable property of an audio file ([[machine-gender-scoring]], [[sumi-spectrograms]], [[kuhn2026]] makes the same refusal from a design side).
- 「becoming an ear」 is the academic statement of what the community calls ear training and mimicry ([[sumi-how-to-practice]], [[sumi-mimicry]], [[community-training]]), and the emphasis on a ‘default’ everyday voice rather than a temporary achieved one matches the working-voice distinction ([[sumi-low-fem-voices]]).
- It also joins the qualitative Korean record to the quantitative one ([[yoo2018]]) and to the survey of views on voice therapy ([[yun2024]]).

## Limits
- A Korean master's thesis in science and technology studies; its sources are medical literature, online discourse and interviews, and it publishes no acoustic measurement or outcome. The English abstract on the RISS record is the only text read here; the thesis itself (102 pp., open access through Seoul National University) was not fetched.
