# Efficacy of vocal function exercises as a method of improving voice production

## What it says
- 35 adult women with objectively normal voices (acoustic, aerodynamic and videostroboscopic measures) were randomly divided into an experimental group doing vocal function exercises, a placebo-exercise group and a no-treatment control.
- After four weeks, "objective measures … demonstrated significant changes in phonation volume, flow rate, maximum phonation time, and frequency range for the experimental group."
- "No significant changes were noted in the measurements of the control and placebo groups."

## Bearing on voice training
- The origin trial for vocal function exercises (the sustained /i/ warm-up, glide up, glide down and sustained low-note series). It shows the exercises change aerodynamic and range measures in *healthy* voices, which is why they are used as conditioning rather than only as rehabilitation.
- Relevant to trans voice work as a way to extend usable frequency range before shaping a habitual pitch, but no study here involves trans speakers.

## Notes
- Women only, normal voices, four weeks, small groups; the abstract reports no listener-perceptual outcome.
