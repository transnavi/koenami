## What it says
- Follow-up study comparing Voice Handicap Index (VHI) scores in trans men before and after testosterone administration, building on the author's earlier finding (see nimura2015 in this vault) that continuous testosterone lowers fundamental frequency (F0) and masculinizes the voice abruptly at 3–6 months from treatment onset.
- VHI scores of trans men were lower (less voice-related distress/handicap) after testosterone administration than before, and were not significantly different from a control group without voice disorders.
- Trans women were found to have a stronger feeling of voice-related discomfort before intervention than trans men had before their own intervention — offered as a likely explanation for why most individuals seeking voice treatment in clinical settings are trans women rather than trans men.

## Bearing on voice training
- Directly supports the claim (echoed in the Nygren longitudinal study, skipped in this cluster as already covered) that testosterone alone is usually sufficient to normalize voice-related distress for trans men, contrasting with trans women, for whom hormone therapy does not affect the voice and dedicated therapy/surgery is typically required.
- Useful comparative-distress data point (VHI, trans men vs. trans women) for framing why clinical voice services are disproportionately oriented toward feminization rather than masculinization.

## Notes
- Japanese-language source; abstract read in English translation as published on J-STAGE. Sample size and precise statistical results for the VHI comparison were not given in the abstract itself (full text not retrieved).
- Population: Japanese trans men clinical sample; findings on "no intervention required" should not be over-generalized — the author's own scoping review counterpart (kalra2026) documents that up to 20% of trans men remain dissatisfied post-hormone-therapy in other populations.
