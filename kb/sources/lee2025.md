# Gender-affirming surgeries and patient-reported outcomes in South Korea: A multicenter cohort survey

## What it says
- Multicenter survey at 8 Korean institutions (Jan–Oct 2024); among 844 transgender and gender-diverse respondents, 382 (45.3%) had undergone at least one gender-affirming surgery (abstract).
- Surgery rates were highest among trans men (75.2%) and nonbinary people assigned female (59.1%), versus trans women (31.7%) and nonbinary people assigned male (22.0%); median age at first surgery 26; 98.9% had had gender-affirming hormone therapy (median 5 years) (abstract).
- Satisfaction was high for gonadectomy (85.1%) and chest/breast surgery (77.8%), and "relatively low for genital surgeries (62.4%) and voice feminization (56.6%)" (abstract).
- Respondents reported improvements in gender expression (94.2%), quality of life (91.0%), self-esteem (90.2%), mental health (88.9%) and gender congruence (88.4%); the conclusion calls for improved accessibility of GAS in South Korea (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The domestic context number for Korean voice work: voice feminization was the least-satisfying gender-affirming procedure category in this survey, sitting under the surgery-plus-therapy outcomes the Korean series report ([[kim2017]], [[kim2024]]) — consistent with pitch-only interventions leaving resonance and phonatory pattern unaddressed [[kim2020]].
- One author is the Korean Initiative for Transgender Health and others are from community clinics (Salim Clinic, Supporting Diversity and Reproductive Health Clinic), so the survey reflects community-network recruitment, not just hospital cohorts (author affiliations in the abstract).

## Notes
- A satisfaction survey across surgery types, not a voice outcome study; the "voice feminization" category mixes surgical and other routes in ways the abstract does not separate.
