# Laryngeal Vibratory Mechanisms: The Notion of Vocal Register Revisited

## What it says
- "Human phonation is characterized by the use of four laryngeal mechanisms, labeled M0-M3", evidenced by electroglottographic study of the transitions between them in men and women, trained and untrained singers (abstract).
- A transition to a mechanism of higher rank shows "a jump in frequency, a reduction of EGG amplitude, and a change in the shape of the derivative of the EGG (which may correspond to a reduction of the vibratory mass)" (abstract).
- The pitches at which M1↔M2 transitions occur, and "the range of the frequency-overlap region", are described in detail: the two main mechanisms overlap in frequency rather than tiling the range (abstract).
- The authors attribute the long-standing confusion about registers to "the heterogeneity of the approaches and methods used to describe the phenomena and to the multiplicity of descriptors", and reorganise register terminology by its relation to the four mechanisms (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- M0 = fry/pulse, M1 = chest/modal, M2 = falsetto/head, M3 = whistle, as tabulated from this paper by [[herbst2020]]. Because M1 and M2 overlap in F0, a given speaking pitch does not tell you which mechanism produced it — the central reason pitch alone is a poor description of a trans-feminine or trans-masculine voice target.
