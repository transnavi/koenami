# Voice quality and tone identification in White Hmong

## What it says
- Native White Hmong listeners identified words in two tasks; in the first, F0 and duration were manipulated while phonation was left alone, showing "phonation is important in identifying the breathy tone, but not the creaky tone."
- In the second task source spectrum slopes were manipulated with F0 held constant, creating a modal-to-breathy continuum. "Results indicate that changes in H1-H2 (difference in amplitude between the first and second harmonics) and H2-H4 (difference in amplitude between the second and fourth harmonics) are independently important for distinguishing breathy from modal phonation."
- The finding is "consistent with the view that the percept of breathiness is influenced by a steep drop in harmonic energy in the lower frequencies."

## Bearing on voice training
- Establishes H1–H2 and H2–H4 as perceptually real dimensions, not just convenient numbers: listeners hear changes in them as changes in breathiness. This is the measurement side of what voice training calls weight and breathiness.

## Notes
- Tone-language listeners judging lexical contrasts, not gender; the perceptual weight of H1–H2 in a gender task is a separate question.
