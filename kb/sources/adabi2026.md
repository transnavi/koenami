# Voice Therapy in Mutational Falsetto: A Narrative Review

## What it says
- "Mutational falsetto is a functional voice disorder identified by the persistence of a high-pitched voice in males after puberty. It causes mild dysphonia and a range of vocal symptoms, including high-pitched falsetto voice, low intensity, cul-de-sac nasality, and breathiness."
- Eleven articles were included from PubMed/MEDLINE, ScienceDirect, SCOPUS and Google Scholar searched on voice therapy, mutational falsetto, puberphonia and speech therapy.
- "The majority of these studies focused on the impact of an eclectic voice therapy approach, combining techniques such as yawn-sigh, glottal fry, laryngeal manipulation, inspiratory phonation, and humming, on multidimensional voice outcomes"; one study used manual therapy.
- "Findings demonstrated the positive effects of voice therapy on multiple aspects of vocal function, with nearly all parameters of voice quality improving following the intervention (P < 0.05)."
- Conclusion: voice therapy is "the first line of intervention", but "further research is needed to determine which approach or technique is most effective."

## Bearing on voice training
- The technique list — glottal fry onset, laryngeal manipulation, yawn-sigh, humming, inspiratory phonation — is the same toolkit used in trans-masculine voice work to find and stabilise a lower, heavier setting, here evidenced in a population that wants a lower voice for a different reason.
- Notice the framing: in this literature, habitual falsetto after puberty is a *disorder* in men. The same production, wanted by a trans woman, is not; the difference is the speaker's goal, not the larynx.

## Notes
- Narrative rather than systematic; eleven heterogeneous studies, mostly small, no meta-analysis. The population is cisgender men.
