# What Contributes to Masculine Perception of Voice Among Transmasculine People on Testosterone Therapy?

## What it says
- The framing: "Voice-gender incongruence, where one's voice does not match their gender identity, can lead to vocal strain, fatigue, emotional distress, and increased risk of suicidality", and many people remain dissatisfied with their voice after testosterone therapy, which "indicat[es] that hormonal voice modification is a complex process not fully understood" (abstract).
- Method: unmodified voice samples from 30 transmasculine individuals undergoing testosterone therapy, analysed with multivariate methods to weigh four acoustic parameters against two measures of gender perception.
- Headline result: "transmasculine individuals' speech is perceived as equally 'masculine' as that of cisgender males, with both groups being statistically categorized as male at similar rates."
- "Although mean fundamental frequency and formant-estimated vocal tract length together account for a significant portion of the variance in gender perceptions, a substantial amount of variance in gender perception remains unexplained."
- The authors' conclusion points at the same gap as the rest of the field: understanding "the acoustic and sociolinguistic factors that contribute to masculine voice presentation can lead to more informed and individualized care".

## Bearing on voice training
- The transmasculine counterpart of the feminisation finding: on the measures used, the voices are read as male as reliably as cis men's, and the standard acoustic pair — F0 and formant-estimated vocal tract length — still leaves much of the judgement unexplained [[voice-masculinization]] [[formants]] [[gender-perception]].
- Whatever else drives the residual variance is not in the F0/formant model; the abstract's own word for it is "sociolinguistic" [[voice-impressions]].
