# Hyperadduction (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Opens with the wiki's bluntest instruction anywhere: "Don't do hyperadduction." Definition: "Hyperadduction is when the vocal folds are pressed too firmly together. The result is a damaging - when done for too long - and grating voice quality."
- Sound: "Hyperadduction has a very harsh and buzzy tone, similar to high resonance vocal weight. It's often mistaken for vocal weight." The page pairs two audio examples, non-hyperadducted heavy vocal weight and hyperadducted heavy vocal weight, so the distinction is made by ear.
- Causes, listed as inexhaustive: "trying to get higher than is comfortable in M2 / basically M2 screaming", "belting with bad technique", "yelling loudly", and "trying to artificially add vocal weight by pressed phonation (which is hyperadduction)".
- Problems: "These mostly come about in the long term. Doing hyperadduction for three seconds probably isn't going to destroy your voice." Listed: "nodules (you don't want this)", "general hoarseness when not hyperadducted", "voice pain", and "loss of closure after being hyperadducted leading to breathiness".
- Avoidance advice: "don't push the voice, especially in M2 or vocal weight past the point of 'slightly fatiguing'"; "make sure you aren't starving your voice of air and then using hyperadduction to get louder"; "avoid vocal fry when going heavier in vocal weight".
- If you already have the problem: "stop training immediately", "rest", "do very light SOVTEs such as humming, (light to the point of almost failing to maintain phonation) for at most 20 minutes a day until the hyperadduction habit is gone", and "learn your limits and stay mostly within them - don't push your voice to be loud or high in pitch, don't do belting until the habit is gone".

## Bearing on voice training
- The community's own safety page, and it names the failure mode that training itself causes: pushing M2 or pressed phonation to manufacture weight [[voice-health]] [[vocal-fatigue]].
- Its central warning is perceptual — hyperadduction "is often mistaken for vocal weight", so a learner chasing a heavier sound by pressing is not doing weight work at all [[vocal-weight]] [[voice-quality]].
- The recovery advice is unusually concrete for community material: total rest from training, capped light SOVTEs, no belting until the habit clears. The vault's clinical sources recommend straw phonation for vocal fatigue and strain on similar reasoning [[voice-therapy-techniques]] [[community-training]].
