# Dynamic 3D MRI of vocal fold oscillations across Estill phonation types

## What it says
- In-vivo case study of a professionally trained singer. Dynamic 3D vocal-fold MRI at sub-millimeter spatial and sub-millisecond temporal resolution, compared with high-speed imaging and electroglottography from the same subject.
- "Six phonation types were produced according to Estill Voice Training® terminology, each intended to elicit different VF thicknesses and supraglottic configurations." For each type, ten phase-binned 3D datasets were reconstructed per cycle. Segmentation yielded fold thickness, vertical and horizontal displacement, contact-area dynamics, glottal area waveforms, open quotient, and supraglottic/subglottic dimensions.
- "These MRI-derived measures showed strong correspondence with those from high-speed imaging and electroglottography."
- "Each phonation type was characterized by distinct VF geometry, supraglottic shaping, and oscillatory behavior. Across types, OQ correlated closely with VF thickness, which systematically covaried with supraglottic adjustments. This suggests supraglottic posturing represents an additional dimension of control."

## Bearing on voice training
- Imaging evidence that Estill's named qualities are not just ear-labels: they come with different fold thickness, contact and open quotient, and the supraglottis moves with the folds rather than as a separate trick. Open quotient tracking thickness is the same "weight" dimension [[lee2023]] measures as H1–H2.
