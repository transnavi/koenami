## What it says
- Integrative review comparing the surgical route ("tiroplastía") and the behavioural route ("terapia vocal") for trans women, searched with the keywords "Transsexual Persons AND Voice Feminization AND Thyroplasty OR Voice Training" (abstract, "Método").
- Twelve studies were retained: nine observational, three quasi-experimental, spanning a wide age range.
- "Ninguno de los estudios informa aspectos económicos de las intervenciones. No existe evidencia de seguimiento a largo plazo" — none of the included studies reported the cost of the interventions and none provided long-term follow-up.
- Concludes that voice therapy with hormonal treatment can be the first line and surgery an option if it does not deliver improvement: "La vía de tratamiento vocal con adición hormonal puede ser una primera línea, y en caso de no reportar mejoras, la cirugía aparece como otra opción."
- Recommends voice therapy first because it carries no significant risk of complications and is a comparatively cheap approach ("un abordaje relativamente económico").

## Bearing on voice training
- A Spanish-language statement of the therapy-first principle that WPATH SOC-8 ([[coleman2022]]) also gives, written for a Latin American readership where surgical access is limited.
- Its cost argument is the one most often repeated in Spanish-language patient material; note that the review itself found no study that measured cost.

## Notes
- Integrative, not systematic: no PRISMA flow, no risk-of-bias appraisal, no meta-analysis, and the search string is reported only as a single keyword line.
- The authors are Chilean/Brazilian-affiliated speech-language pathologists; the journal is a Colombian university title (Universidad Cooperativa / IUE), not indexed in MEDLINE.
- "Tiroplastía" here covers pitch-raising laryngeal framework surgery generally; compare the technique-specific evidence in [[mora2018]] and [[aires2023]].
