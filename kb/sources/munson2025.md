# The Perception of (Trans)masculinity in Speech: Effects of Acoustic Characteristics and Rater Identity

## What it says
- 199 listeners — cisgender heterosexual men (CHM), cisgender heterosexual women (CHF) and a "gender and sexuality expansive" (GSE) group — categorised 240 sentence productions from 20 male talkers in an online experiment, drawn from the Palette of Voices corpus of trans and cis men's speech (abstract, "Method").
- Stimuli included unmanipulated tokens and tokens with F0 and formant-frequency scaling altered.
- As in earlier work, "productions with lower F0 and lower formant frequencies were more likely to be categorized as male".
- But the weighting differed by listener group: the GSE group "weight[ed] these variables less than the CHM and CHF groups when categorizing gender, but more when categorizing gender orientation".
- Conclusion: the link between acoustics and gender categorisation "is highly variable across and within groups", and the study's perception data and speech samples are openly available.

## Bearing on voice training
- Second question built into the design matters for practice: listeners judge *gender* and *whether someone is trans* separately, and different listeners weigh the same acoustics differently for each. Passing is not one threshold [[gender-perception]] [[voice-impressions]].
- The corpus being open makes this the kind of study a learner can hear rather than only read about [[tools]].
