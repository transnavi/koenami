## What it says
- 10 listeners judged perceived masculinity/femininity of 44 talkers (11 heterosexual men, 11 heterosexual women, 11 gay men, 11 lesbian/bisexual women), and these ratings were compared with each talker's independently measured perceived sexual orientation and with acoustic measures of their speech.
- Gay men were judged less masculine than heterosexual men; lesbian/bisexual women were judged less feminine than heterosexual women; masculinity/femininity ratings correlated significantly with perceived-sexual-orientation ratings.
- Regression analyses found that different sets of acoustic measures predicted perceived sexual orientation versus perceived masculinity/femininity, and some acoustic measures were more strongly tied to one perceptual dimension than the other.
- Concludes perceived sexual orientation, perceived masculinity, and perceived femininity are distinct — though correlated — perceptual dimensions with partly separable acoustic bases.

## Bearing on voice training
- Important caution: masculinity/femininity ratings and sexual-orientation impressions share acoustic cues but are not acoustically identical, so training toward a "more feminine" or "more masculine" sound does not have a single, unambiguous target — different cue combinations shift different listener impressions.
- Reinforces that listener perception of gendered voice quality ("vocal weight") is a graded, multidimensional judgment built from a bundle of acoustic cues rather than one parameter.

## Notes
- Small listener panel (n = 10) and speaker panel (n = 44), all from one dialect community; specific acoustic-measure-to-perception mappings are exploratory (regression) rather than causally manipulated.
- Predates later resynthesis work isolating individual cues (e.g., neuhaus2026, cartei2013).
