# Laryngeal adduction in resonant voice

## What it says
- Twelve adult singers and actors — six with healthy larynges, six with nodules — produced repeated tokens of resonant, pressed, normal and breathy voice on sustained vowels; adduction was rated from blinded videoscopic views and electroglottographic closed quotient (EGG CQ) was computed separately.
- "Subjects in both groups produced resonant voice with a barely adducted or barely abducted laryngeal configuration that was distinct from configurations for pressed and breathy (but not normal) voice."
- "Average CQs distinguished resonant from pressed voice, but inconsistently distinguished resonant from breathy voice. Further CQs were reliably different across healthy subjects and subjects with nodules."
- Conclusion: "the utility of this measure to noninvasively estimate resonant voice may be limited, particularly without ongoing subject-specific calibration procedures."

## Bearing on voice training
- Defines "resonant voice" physiologically as the barely-adducted setting between pressed and breathy — the target of resonant voice therapy, and a useful description of the adduction a trans-feminine speaker wants when raising pitch without pressing.
- Warns that closed quotient from EGG is not a reliable read-out of that target without per-speaker calibration; the same caution applies to consumer EGG and CQ displays.

## Notes
- Twelve trained performers; deliberately produced voice qualities, not habitual speech.
