# Questionable Terminology (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Purpose: "These are terms that are commonly used in vague ways or present other problems. They may not be all inherently bad, but just present issues, either because of word use pollution (people using the word in too many different ways) or because they are always involved in risky or ineffective techniques."
- **Chest voice / head voice** ("confusing"): "their definition changes depending on who is asked. Chest voice can mostly be translated as M1 or modal voice. Head voice is usually referring to M2, but specifically for women, then some people use it for men but only when it's not 'falsetto' and has been trained to be more full sounding." Alternatives offered: "M1, M2" and "modal voice, M2 / light M2".
- **Falsetto** ("polluted"): "refers to a light and hollow M2 sound. The term is classically used to refer to 'male head voice', but has been more recently used for all light M2."
- **Buzziness** ("conflation"): only a problem "as an interchangeable term for vocal weight". "There are other sounds that sound buzzy, such as hyperadduction, pressed M0 / fry, twang, some forms of constriction. If trying to make a voice less buzzy in an attempt to make it more feminine, we'll likely sacrifice clarity and brightness in exchange for a dark, smooth, breathy voice ... If we assume vocal weight is what 'buzzy' refers to, then we may become hyperadducted ... while chasing a 'buzzy' sound." Alternative: "heavy", or "describe vocal weight as a clear undercurrent 'rumble'".
- **Forward / throat / chest / head resonance** ("confusing"): "it gets very vague when talking about 'where the resonance is' ... it's based on the assumption that sound can be moved to different parts of the body ... In normal phonation, the source of the sound is always at the vocal folds, in the larynx. We can affect how the voice resonates within our bodies, but not where the sound emits. The problem is when people chase certain feelings instead of sound qualities."
- **R1 / R2** ("conflation") when used to describe resonance or vocal tract size: "Chasing numbers doesn't help, and usually ends up developing some unwanted habits. Also, F1 and F2 should be used in place of R1 and R2". Alternatives: "resonance", "vocal tract size", "F1, F2".
- **Size** ("conflation") where "size" is used without "vocal": "Size, or vocal size is a perceptual quality of voice, similar to how vocal weight is perceptual. If we try to make our voices smaller without the proper knowledge or exercises, it will almost always result in constriction and if not false fold constriction then extraneous muscle tension and fatigue habits." The instruction given: "Do not tell people to make their voices smaller! Instead, tell them to do an actual exercise which results in smaller vocal size."
- **Bright** ("conflation") when used for resonance frequency or size regardless of other factors: "A voice that is high in resonance can sound dark, and a voice that is low in resonance can sound bright ... Brightness is predominantly from resonance multiplied by tone clarity; closure for example. Just increasing closure or pressing will cause a brighter sound for any voice." Alternatives: "high / low resonance", "vocal tract size".
- **Breath support** ("overused"), "in the singing world": "most people do it automatically. It doesn't really solve any problems on it's own ... although breath support is important to voice, so is opening your mouth."

## Bearing on voice training
- A vocabulary audit from inside the community, and the failure it names is practical: a learner chasing "forward resonance" as a *sensation* does extraneous things instead of an exercise, and a learner chasing "buzzy" to be masculine can end up hyperadducted [[voice-quality]] [[sumi-hyperadduction]] [[vocal-weight]].
- Separates two things the popular vocabulary runs together: the resonance of the tract (measurable, F1/F2) and the brightness a listener hears (multivariable, also driven by closure and noise) [[formants]] [[voice-impressions]].
- The register vocabulary reset — M1/M2 in place of chest/head/falsetto — is the same substitution the acoustic literature makes [[vocal-registers]].
