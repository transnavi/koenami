# Transmasculine people's vocal situations: a critical review of gender-related discourses and empirical data

## What it says
- Comprehensive review across seven electronic databases of the gender-related discursive and empirical literature on transmasculine people's vocal situations (abstract).
- Sets out the field's disagreement: some researchers and clinicians "suggest that transmasculine people do not need attention because testosterone treatment leads to a satisfactory masculinization of their voice organs and voices", while others argue transmasculine people are heterogeneous in body type, gender identity and desire for medical transition, so "testosterone-induced voice changes may not necessarily meet the needs and expectations of all transmasculine people" (abstract, background).
- Thirty-one publications met the review's criteria (abstract, methods).
- Concludes the population must be treated as heterogeneous and clinical practice must be client-centred and individualized (abstract); the author's later qualitative and mixed-methods work with the same German-speaking cohort develops this ([[azul2018]]).

## Bearing on voice training
- The citation for refusing the "testosterone settles it" shortcut in [[voice-masculinization]]: hormones do most of the pitch work on average, but the review's whole point is that the average is not every client, and that the voice literature had built its masculinity norms around cis male voices.

## Notes
- Review of discourses as much as of data; the empirical base it found was thin, which is itself the finding. Author position: speech pathology, La Trobe University.
