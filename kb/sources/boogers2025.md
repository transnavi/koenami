# Feminine figures: body image and physical characteristics in trans women after puberty suppression and subsequent estradiol therapy

## What it says
- 42 trans women from the Amsterdam gender clinic who started treatment under 18 (23 with puberty suppression (PS) in early puberty, Tanner G2–3; 19 in late puberty, G4–5), assessed at mean age 20.1 ± 2.0 after 4.2 ± 1.6 years of GAHT, with the Body Image Scale (BIS, 1 = very satisfied to 5 = very dissatisfied), Rosenberg self-esteem and anthropometrics (abstract).
- Mean total BIS 2.4 ± 0.6 (satisfied). Most BIS items (24/27) scored lower (better) in the early-PS group, "particularly voice (β −1.4, 95% CI −1.9; −0.8), Adam's apple (β −0.6, 95% CI −1.2; 0.001) and weight (β −1.1, 95% CI −1.9; −0.4)" (abstract).
- Self-esteem correlated negatively with BIS scores (β −4.5, 95% CI −6.6; −2.5) (abstract).
- Authors' conclusion: early PS is "associated with higher satisfaction with voice, a characteristic strongly influenced by pubertal testosterone exposure" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The patient-reported side of [[puberty-voice-change]]: voice is the body characteristic most improved by having suppressed testosterone puberty, and the one trans women in this cohort were most satisfied with — voice satisfaction here tracks what [[ciancia2026]] measures acoustically.

## Notes
- Small sample (42), single centre, self-selected participants; satisfaction scores, not acoustic measures. Amsterdam cohort; PS policy context is Dutch.
