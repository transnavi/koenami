# How Important is Pitch? (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- The page's own answer: "Very! But, not in the way you think. There is a misconception that pitch is a big part or even the only reason masculine and feminine voices are different ... in fact, pitch isn't really in the equation at all."
- The claim stated flatly: "Pitch is not a gendered factor. On its own it does not modify the gender presentation of a voice. In fact, there are plenty of voices as low as 120hz or even 90hz that are unmistakably feminine and voices as high as 250hz that are unmistakably masculine."
- Pitch's real role is as a training lever: "a lot of exercises use pitch as a method of changing other factors. When we go up in pitch we usually lower vocal weight, which is useful in a pitch slide. So in essence, while pitch is not important to the way a voice is perceived, it is important to the way we train our voices."
- A recommended practice pitch is given: "A good pitch that is usually not too high for most people, but still gets the benefits of lowering vocal weight and often raising resonance too, is 200hz or G3. For most people this is the sweetspot. Too much higher is straining or flipping into M2 / falsetto, too much lower risks becoming heavier in vocal weight and darker in resonance." And: "Learning vocal weight without going above 150hz is nearly impossible".
- On why low-pitch feminine voices are hard: "the short answer is microbehaviours (and vocal weight mastery) ... When we go to a lower pitch our configuration can be so fragile that it breaks up completely, or we could cover up the lack of coordination with things like breathiness." And: "The more stress on the configuration we add, by going low in pitch or higher in vocal weight or lower in resonance, the more these features (like speech patterns and microbehaviours) matter" (the longer version of this argument is on the low-fem-voices page).
- The page closes by linking audio examples of low-pitch bright feminine voices (Jackie Liu, imawonder).

## Bearing on voice training
- The clearest statement of the community's position that F0 is a means rather than an end — a direct contrast with the meta-analytic finding that F0 accounts for the largest share of variance in listener judgements [[fundamental-frequency]] [[leung2018]] [[gender-perception]].
- The 200 Hz / G3 practice-anchor advice is a concrete, checkable convention: train where weight is light and resonance high, not where the target voice will end up [[vocal-weight]] [[community-training]].
- The warning that a low-pitch feminine target is fragile "as soon as we add intonation, tempo changes and other speech patterns" names the interaction between prosody and glottal control that the acoustic literature rarely tests [[intonation]] [[sumi-microbehaviours]].
