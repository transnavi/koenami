# Measures of the glottal source spectrum

## What it says
- Abstract purpose: "it remains unclear which aspects of the source should be quantified and how measurements should be made." They took 78 measures of source spectral shape on 70 speakers, ran principal components, and compared the resulting factors with factors from oral speech spectra and glottal pulses.
- "Results revealed high levels of duplication and overlap among existing measures of source spectral slope." Existing measures "were not well aligned with patterns of spectral variability" and "do not appear to model the higher frequency parts of the source spectrum adequately."
- Conclusion: "The failure of existing measures to adequately quantify spectral variability may explain why results of studies examining the perceptual importance of spectral slope have not produced consistent results." "most existing measures of source spectral slope are unlikely to be good predictors of voice quality."

## Bearing on voice training
- A caution on treating H1–H2 (or any one tilt number) as *the* measure of the quality community training calls weight. The community's ear-training approach ([[erose2022]], [[erose2022a]]) is doing a different job from a single spectral slope statistic.
