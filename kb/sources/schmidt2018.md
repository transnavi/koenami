# Voice challenge in transgender women: trans women self-perception of voice handicap as compared to gender perception of naïve listeners

## What it says
- 31 trans women at the Hospital de Clínicas de Porto Alegre gave a vocal acoustic assessment (F0 and SD measured on recordings), answered the Voice Handicap Index with items selected for this population's daily demands, and had their recordings played to 50 naïve blindfolded listeners who labelled each voice male, female or undefined (abstract, Methods).
- Mean F0 was 172.40 Hz (SD 4.8 Hz). "One third of the transgender women reported being satisfied with their voices."
- Correlation between self-rated handicap and listener labelling was "moderate positive significant … in voices considered as males, and strongly significant and negative in voices considered as females". For voices judged *undefined* there was no significant correlation at all.
- Conclusion: "transgender women's self-perception of voice handicap is directly related to naïve listeners' perception of their voice gender."

## Bearing on voice training
- The self-report and the listener judgement move together at the two extremes and stop moving together in the middle. That middle is where training lives: a speaker can improve without their self-rated handicap budging, and vice versa, until the voice lands clearly on one side ([[gender-perception]], [[patient-reported-outcomes]]).
- Only one third satisfied at a mean F0 of 172 Hz is the Brazilian counterpart of the practical ceiling the vault carries from English and Japanese data ([[leung2018]], [[sakuraba2009]]). Sustained F0 targets above the cis mean do not by themselves produce satisfaction.

## Notes
- Abstract read; the item selection for the adapted Voice Handicap Index, the listener panel's composition and the choice of a three-way (male / female / undefined) labelling scale were not read in full.
- Sample drawn from a single southern Brazilian clinic; no therapy was given as part of the study, so this is a baseline picture, not an outcome.
