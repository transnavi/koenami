# Growth of the Human Prepubertal and Pubertal Larynx

## What it says
- Extends [[kahane1978]] by comparing the 20 prepubertal and pubertal cadaveric larynges with adult measurement data from a separate study of 20 male and 20 female larynges aged 37–70, the protocols being similar enough for direct comparison (abstract).
- "Results highlight specific quantitative characteristics and trends in circumpubertal growth of the laryngeal cartilages and the vocal folds for each sex and with respect to sex differences" (abstract).
- The findings are discussed against the literature on postnatal laryngeal development and on adolescent voice change (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- Establishes that the adult male–female difference in the larynx is laid down around puberty rather than accumulating slowly through adulthood — which is why a voice changed after puberty must be changed by use, not by waiting.

## Notes
- Cadaveric samples of 20 circumpubertal and 40 adult larynges; the specific growth figures are in the paper's tables, not in the abstract, so quote them from the paper rather than from this note.
