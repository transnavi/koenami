# Spectrograms (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- The whole page is a warning and a scope note: 「Spectrograms are cool! It's best not to rely on them. Use them to train your ears, but don't use them in place of your ears!」
- What they are for, in the wiki's account: 「The most common use of a spectrogram is to monitor pitch and sometimes formants.」
- The section 「How To Read A Spectrogram」 is present as a heading; the page links out to the explanation rather than carrying it, and its body is a stub at the time of the fetch.

## Bearing on voice training
- The position stated here is the one the wiki applies to all its measurement: a display is a training aid for the ear, not a substitute for listening, and the thing it shows most easily is pitch ([[sumi-in-site-spectrogram]], [[praat]]).
- It is also the wiki's answer to the gender-scoring-app question, and it lines up with the caution in the community's own instruments: resonance is the hard part to see, and the tools that claim to score it are not neutral ([[machine-gender-scoring]], [[transvoice-app]], [[note-formant-app]]).

## Limits
- A two-sentence page. Nothing on it explains how to read a spectrogram, and no source is cited; the linked explanation was not fetched.
