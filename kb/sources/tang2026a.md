# The Clinical and Surgical Landscape of Gender Affirming Vocal Care: A Scoping Review

## What it says
- PRISMA-ScR review of PubMed, Embase, CINAHL and Web of Science from inception: "Of 3231 articles screened, 290 met inclusion criteria."
- Growth: primary publication rates "increased from an average of 2.7 articles/year (2000-2009) to 31.3 articles/year (2022-2024)".
- Design: most studies "were cross-sectional (n = 106) or retrospective (n = 70), nonrandomized (n = 181), and lacked a comparison group (n = 92)". Median sample sizes 20 for all primary literature and 18 for surgical primary literature.
- Populations: trans women (n = 119), combined cohorts (n = 71), trans men (n = 32), non-binary/gender-diverse individuals (n = 4).
- Interventions: nonsurgical (n = 180), surgical (n = 74), combined (n = 36). Among surgical papers on trans women, "anterior web glottoplasty (n = 46) and cricothyroid approximation (n = 21) predominated".
- Outcomes: "acoustic measures (75.3%) or quality of life metrics (56.8%), with limited information on functional impairment".
- Conclusion: the literature is "characterized by small size, lack of randomization, and no comparison groups", and the named gaps are "underrepresented populations, such as trans men and nonbinary/gender diverse individuals", and the impact of vocal gender dysphoria on function.

## Bearing on voice training
- The single best description of the evidence base's shape: 290 primary studies, mostly cross-sectional, no control group, median 18–20 participants. Useful for calibrating how much weight any single finding can carry [[clinical-guidelines]].
- Counts the under-representation in one line: 119 studies on trans women, 32 on trans men, 4 on non-binary people [[nonbinary-voice]] [[transgender-care-context]].
- Acoustic measures appear in three-quarters of studies, quality of life in just over half, and functional impairment rarely — which is the measurement gap this vault's source notes keep running into [[patient-reported-outcomes]].
