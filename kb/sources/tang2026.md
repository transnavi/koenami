# Evaluating Components of Vocal Effort in Transgender Women

## What it says
- Cross-sectional study at the University of Washington with four groups: 15 trans women and 16 cisgender people without laryngeal pathology, plus two positive controls — 12 patients with adductor laryngeal dystonia (ADLD) and 9 with unilateral vocal fold paralysis (UVFP), conditions known to raise vocal effort (abstract, "Methods").
- After a spontaneous speaking task, participants completed the NASA Task Load Index (NASA-TLX) — used here as a novel measure of the mental and physical demand components of vocal effort — and the VHI-10.
- Trans women reported "significantly increased mental demand" compared with both cisgender participants (p < 0.01) and, notably, with ADLD patients (p < 0.01).
- Trans women, ADLD and UVFP participants all showed increased physical demand against cisgender participants (p < 0.0001, p < 0.001, p < 0.01); VHI-10 was elevated in all three groups (p < 0.0001).
- Conclusion: trans women "experience greater vocal effort, including both physical and mental components, compared to cisgender individuals".

## Bearing on voice training
- Puts a number on the cognitive load of running a consciously produced voice: the mental demand reported by trans women exceeded that of a clinical dystonia group with no laryngeal pathology present [[vocal-fatigue]] [[voice-health]].
- Supports measuring effort and load, not only F0 and formants, when judging whether a voice programme is working [[patient-reported-outcomes]].

## Notes
- n = 15 trans women, recruited at one US academic centre (the abstract does not describe the sampling), cross-sectional; NASA-TLX is borrowed from human-factors research and has not been validated as a voice measure — the paper calls this exploratory. Level of evidence 3, as stated in the abstract.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
