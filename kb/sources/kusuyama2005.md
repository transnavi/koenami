# ホルモン音声障害に対する音声治療の経験―声帯粘膜波動に注目して―

Hormonal dysphonia here means the voice disorder a woman develops after being given a virilising drug — the same clinical entity as [[miyata2013]] and [[kobayashi2012]], reported here with laryngostroboscopy alongside the therapy outcome.

## What it says
- Definition as the authors give it: 「"Hormonal dysphonias" are induced by hormonal supplements containing virilizing agents, which are mainly androgens and anabolic steroids」, and such prescriptions had been becoming more common.
- Sample: 「We have treated 8 cases diagnosed as hormonal dysphonia in the past 2.5 years.」 On laryngostroboscopy, 「reduction of the mucosal wave of the vocal fold in 4 cases, in all of whom speaking pitch level and voice range (highest and lowest tone) became lower.」
- Treatment: voice therapy along the lines of vocal function exercises, given to the five cases who requested it. 「All exhibited marked improvement in voice.」
- The finding that makes the paper worth keeping: 「The mucosal wave of the vocal fold recovered with voice therapy in 3 of the 4 cases after withdrawal from hormonal medication.」 The mucosal wave came back in three of four — but only after the drug was stopped.

## Bearing on voice training
- A concrete picture of what the androgen effect does to the vocal folds and what it takes to reverse any of it: the mucosal wave reduction tracks the pitch and range loss, and it recovered only when the medication stopped, with therapy alongside ([[vocal-folds]], [[hormones-and-voice]]).
- That is the same structure as [[kobayashi2012]]'s 30-year follow-up, where the drop persisted despite the patient's own training — the drug has to go, and even then the recovery is not guaranteed ([[voice-health]]).

## Notes
- A case series of eight with no control group, and the therapy was given to those who asked for it, so the "marked improvement" is not compared against untreated cases; graded low. Only the English abstract was read.
- The indication for the virilising prescription is not given in the abstract.
