# 両声類（ピクシブ百科事典）

The second of the two Japanese wiki encyclopedias with an article on 両声類. Read this note against [[nicodic-ryoseirui]]: the pixiv text is a copy of the ニコニコ大百科 article, and its last paragraph says so.

## What it says
- Headword line: 「両声類（りょうせいるい）とは、性別を逆転させたような声で歌唱する人達への敬称である。」 — the same first sentence as ニコニコ大百科 ([[nicodic-ryoseirui]]), and likewise calls the word a 敬称.
- The body reproduces the niconico article's scope, its 声質-only tagging rule, the 「音痴でもタグがつく」 point, the second sense (someone who commands 男声 and 女声 both), the 性別不明 performers, and the subjective-judgement caveat — and then closes the section with an attribution: 「（以上ニコニコ大百科（https://dic.nicovideo.jp/a/両声類）より）」.
- One line is pixiv's own addition and not on the niconico page: 「ちなみに、男性の両声類のことを「男の娘」という呼び方をすることもある。」
- The 関連タグ list is pixiv-specific and names the scene: ASK, 赤飯, vip店長, ピコ（ﾋﾟωﾟｺ）, 96猫, ろん, and the unit 赤ピコ飯まー☆ — the singers whose videos carry the tag ([[nicovideo-akapiko2009]]).
- pixiv's own user content shows the tag's reach beyond singing: the 小説 section carries 「フリー台本（両声台本）」 (scripts written for a single performer doing both voices), a 「【両声類】私流！男声の出し方講座」-style script, and a 「両声類VTuberさんの一人二役台本」 — scripts for VTubers performing two roles alone.

## Bearing on voice training
- Together with [[nicodic-ryoseirui]] this is the community's own definition of the word; both define it by 声質 and by what listeners accept, not by identity, and both treat 裏声 as the name of a voice that fails to convince.
- The 台本 and VTuber台本 material shows the practice is a performance convention with a working vocabulary of its own, which is the tradition Japanese trans guides inherit the words from ([[brushvoice-joseigoe]]).

## Limits
- A community encyclopedia whose 両声類 text is explicitly copied from another wiki; the only original content is the 男の娘 remark and the tag/illustration lists. Figure and tag counts on the page are wikia-local and not meaningful here. The live page was not directly retrievable on 2026-09-17 (HTTP 403), so the text was read from a Wayback capture.
