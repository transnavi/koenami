## What it says
- Reanalyzes fundamental-frequency and formant data from Lee, Potamianos & Narayanan's (1999) developmental acoustic study of children's and adults' speech for ten monophthongs, using both raw formant comparisons and a Bark-scale acoustic-phonetic analysis (vowel-space size, distance from speaker centroid, male–female formant differences, vowel openness/frontness, vocal effort, formant range).
- Finds age- and sex-specific developmental patterns in both F0 and formant frequencies, and shows these patterns differ depending on whether a vowel is peripheral or non-peripheral in the vowel space.
- Discusses the emergence of adult sex differences with reference to anatomical, physiological, sociophonetic and cultural factors, and proposes further lifespan research on sex-linked acoustic-phonetic development.

## Bearing on voice training
- Establishes that male/female formant differences are not simply a fixed offset scaled from vocal-tract length; the size and even direction of the difference vary by vowel identity and vowel-space position, which is relevant when choosing which vowels to target in resonance/formant training.
- Frames sex differences in voice acoustics as partly sociophonetic/cultural rather than purely anatomical, supporting the general finding (parallel to leung2018, skipped) that gendered voice is behaviorally as well as physically shaped.

## Notes
- Secondary reanalysis of Lee et al. (1999) data, not new recordings; cross-sectional (not longitudinal) so developmental claims are inferred from age groups, not tracked individuals.
- Speaker population is the original Lee et al. American English sample.
