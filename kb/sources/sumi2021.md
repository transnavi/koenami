# Transvoice: Vocal weight - Pitch slides

## What it says
- oEmbed title as above, author Sumi. Watch-page date text: 2021/08/29. Description is English.
- "In this video I'll go over a particularly useful exercise called a pitch slide. This is commonly used to decrease vocal weight which is one of the more powerful gender determining factors of voice."
- "Vocal weight is often also referred to as thick/thin vocal fold configuration or open and closed quotient. While they refer to slightly different and nuanced things, the exercises used basically are designed to change all those factors and result in the sound quality we hear that is sometimes called 'harsh' 'buzzy' or 'heavy' in tone."
- "Changing vocal weight alone can in some cases result in an androgynous voice, and the only other major factors (aside from clarity related things) are resonance and speech patterns."
- Caveat in the description (not in the video, the author says): "I forgot to mention not to use volume to imitate low vocal weight. If you get really quiet it will sound similar, but you won't be changing vocal weight. Try to get loud with low weight when doing these exercises." If loudness only comes with heaviness, "try to keep your airflow (subglottal pressure) the same during the slide."

## Bearing on voice training
- Explicit community equation of vocal weight with CQ/OQ and thick/thin, plus the warning that quietness is not lightness — which matches Holmberg's finding that soft voice shrinks the male–female glottal difference ([[holmberg1988]]).
- "Weight alone can androgynize" is the author's claim, not a listener study.
