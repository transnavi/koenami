# Effects of native language on perception of voice quality

## What it says
- Abstract: native speakers of Gujarati, Thai and English discriminated voice pairs that "differed only in the relative amplitudes of the first versus second harmonics (H1-H2)."
- "speakers of Gujarati (which contrasts H1-H2 phonemically) were more sensitive to changes than are speakers of Thai or English."
- Gujarati sensitivity "was not affected by the overall source spectral slope," unlike Thai and English speakers, "who were most sensitive when the spectrum fell away steeply."
- Combined with prior Mandarin findings, "a continuum of sensitivity to H1-H2": phonemic (Gujarati, independent of spectral context) > allophonic/tonal (Mandarin, context-conditioned) > post-lexical (Thai, English — "sensitivity is both lower and contextually-determined, reflecting the smaller role of H1-H2 in these languages").

## Bearing on voice training
- H1–H2 is a real perceptual dimension, but English listeners are on the least-sensitive end of this continuum. Community ear training for "weight" is teaching English speakers to hear a cue their language does not contrast phonemically ([[garellek2013]] is the complementary finding that H1–H2 and H2–H4 shift breathiness for listeners who do use phonation contrastively).

## Notes
- Abstract only. Not a gender-perception task. Graded `medium`.
