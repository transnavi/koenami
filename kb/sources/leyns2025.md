# Virtual Reality as a Tool in Gender-Affirming Voice Training: A Pilot Study

## What it says
- Ghent pilot with the hospital's Smart Place Rehabilitation unit and the withVR company: eleven Dutch-speaking trans women, six trained with Meta Quest 2 virtual-reality speaking situations (withVR web app) and five with traditional training, four weekly 30-minute one-to-one sessions each (abstract, "METHODS").
- Aim: generalise elevated pitch to spontaneous speech and reduce speaking anxiety.
- Willingness-to-Communicate scores rose across contexts in the VR group — "particularly in interactions with strangers and public speaking" — while the traditional group stayed flat; TWVQ improved slightly in both; median F0 rose in both with high between-participant variability (abstract, "RESULTS").
- Conclusion: VR "might potentially increase pitch and WTC", but improvements in voice-related quality of life were modest and larger, mixed-methods, long-term studies are needed.

## Bearing on voice training
- Tests the transfer problem every training programme faces — clinic voice versus street voice — with simulated speaking situations; a delivery mechanism, not a new exercise set [[tools]] [[community-training]].
- The Willingness-to-Communicate measure names the social-anxiety half of voice dysphoria that acoustic F0 does not capture [[patient-reported-outcomes]].

## Notes
- Eleven participants, descriptive analyses only; co-author Walkom is affiliated with the VR vendor (declared in the byline), so the pilot is a proof-of-concept with a conflict-of-interest shape.
