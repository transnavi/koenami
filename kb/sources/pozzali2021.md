# Effectiveness of SOVTEs in patients with dysphonia: a systematic review and meta-analysis

## What it says
- PRISMA systematic review and meta-analysis of SOVTEs for dysfunctional and organic dysphonia, covering acoustic, perceptual, aerodynamic and self-perception outcomes. Five databases were searched for RCTs and non- or quasi-RCTs; Cochrane RoB and ROBINS-I were applied; GRADE rated the quality of evidence.
- "Eight articles were included. Studies investigated several SOVTEs, alone or in combination. None of the study was completely judged at low RoB. The quality of evidence resulted very low for each analysis."
- SOVTEs were "statistically more effective than control interventions in improving F0" (effect size −14.42 Hz; 95% CI −27.16 to −1.69; P = 0.03). Shimmer did not change significantly. Jitter and overall gravity on perceptual-auditory evaluation showed non-significant changes in favour of control groups.
- Secondary outcomes: SOVTEs reduced subglottic pressure more than controls (ES −1.47; 95% CI −2.84 to −0.10; P = 0.03). Self-assessment (VHI/VRQoL and VTDS/VDSI) was not significantly in favour of SOVTEs.
- Conclusion: "voice therapy based on SOVTEs is not to consider significantly superior if compared to other treatments, even if a favorable trend was detected"; further high-quality RCTs on specific SOVTEs are recommended.

## Bearing on voice training
- The broadest outcome review of the exercise family trans voice work uses as warm-up and as a first-session tool. The physiology ([[titze2006]]) is clearer than the clinical superiority: SOVTEs move F0 and lower driving pressure, but they do not beat other treatments on quality-of-life scores, and the evidence grade is "very low".
- Narrower than [[asadollahpour2025]], which restricted inclusion to free-end-tube (straw-in-air) work and found only three trials.
