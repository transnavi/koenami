# How to Develop a Female Voice

## What it says
- Byline "By Melanie Anne" (part one) / "Melanie Anne Philips" (part two, one l). Opening: "Nothing gets you read faster than a voice that doesn't match your appearance." Distinguishes "a truly FEMALE voice - not just a feminine one."
- Rejects "raising my pitch artificially, arriving at that bad falsetto that forms the stereotype" and treats vocal-fold surgery as a last resort she did not take. Claims an accidental "TIMBER" shift: "the secret I found is not in the way one speaks but the way one sounds."
- Seven "tools": Pitch, Resonance, Dynamic Range, Enunciation, Vocabulary, Grammar, Body English. "Six of them work on the femininity but the seventh is the 'secret' that actually makes one SOUND female" — Resonance.
- Pitch section: "the difference between the average male voice and the average female voice is only about 1/2 octave." Names low-pitched women (Suzanne Pleshette, Marlene Dietrich, Cher, Bea Arthur) who "we would never mistake for men" because "it is not the pitch that makes them sound female, but the Resonance." Her own pitch "is only 2 or 3 notes higher on the scale than it was before."
- Resonance: orchestra metaphor (same note, different instruments). "Men have a larger voice box… Women have a smaller voice box and as a result have less low end resonance." "The whole trick is to learn to use LESS of your voice."
- Part two (written "some years ago" after the first article, per its opening): "the male voice box is about twice as large"; falsetto "tighten[s] all the muscles around your voice box, killing ALL the harmonics"; the intended trick is to "clamp down on half of the voice box, effectively cutting the resonance chamber to half size and deadening the low-end harmonics." Palpation test: "Speak normally, both will vibrate. Speak in falsetto, neither will vibrate. If you can get the bottom to vibrate while the top does not, you'll have the voice."
- Safety in part two: "these kinds of vocal exercises can easily strain your voice"; "Don't practice any longer than a minute or two to start"; "never even approach the point at which you might get hoarse."
- Dynamic range is described as men using a "very narrow" range and women a "much WIDER" "sing/song" range — training, "not physiology."

## Bearing on voice training
- Historical root of English-language self-teaching: pitch is secondary, "female resonance" is the work, falsetto is the failure mode. That split is still the community's (see [[erose2020]], [[transvoice-wiki]]).
- The mechanism she names — shrinking a "voice box" chamber by clamping laryngeal muscles — is not the tract-length / formant account in [[formants]]. Treat it as her model, not as anatomy.
