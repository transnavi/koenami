## What it says
- Single-case follow-up of an FTM/GID patient over 143 days of testosterone administration, tracking speaking fundamental frequency, vocal range, subjective voice-use complaints, and Voice Handicap Index (VHI).
- Speaking pitch fell from 187 Hz pre-treatment to 108 Hz at day 143.
- Vocal range temporarily widened at day 48, then narrowed again.
- By day 48 the patient reported voice breaks ("翻転", pitch cracks) during conversation; by day 143 the patient also reported difficulty producing falsetto while singing and a sense of vocal strain/tension during speech.
- VHI improved on the emotional subscale but worsened on the physical subscale.
- Authors attribute the symptoms to thyroarytenoid muscle-fiber hypertrophy (lowering speaking pitch) alongside laryngeal structural changes causing laryngeal-muscle coordination problems, and recommend monitoring voice during hormone administration so dose/duration can be adjusted to prevent these complications.

## Bearing on voice training
- A cautionary data point: testosterone-driven pitch lowering in trans men is not symptom-free — pitch breaks, loss of falsetto, and conversational vocal strain can appear alongside the expected pitch drop, and self-rated physical voice handicap can worsen even as emotional handicap improves.
- Supports monitoring/voice-therapy involvement during masculinizing hormone therapy, not just after.

## Notes
- n=1 case report — low evidentiary weight, illustrative only, not generalizable.
- Japanese-language clinical population.
