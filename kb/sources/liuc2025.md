# Analysis of factors related to voice training compliance（Liu C et al. 2025）

## What it says
- The Chinese abstract's own terms (PubMed publisher block): the outcome is 嗓音训练依从性, staged with the URICA-Voice scale into 意向期 (contemplation), 行动期 (action) and 维持期 (maintenance): 「女性较男性更容易处于依从性分期的意向期，OR=0.22」, 「家庭人均月收入>6 000元的患者更容易处于意向期，OR＝13.94」, 「医院的距离<30 min的患者更容易处于行动期或维持期，OR＝7.14」, 「职业对嗓音要求高的患者…更容易处于行动期+维持期，OR＝16.20」.
- Question and setting: to explore "the factors influencing adherence to voice therapy among patients with voice disorders in China", among patients attending the Voice Therapy Center at Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hospital, Sun Yat-sen University, February–May 2022 (abstract).
- Instruments: adherence assessed with the URICA-Voice scale (a stage-of-change instrument), influencing factors assessed with the Voice Handicap Index (VHI) and a general information questionnaire; univariate and multivariate logistic regression.
- Sample: 247 patients — 57 male (23.08%) and 190 female (76.92%).
- The five results the abstract lists: female patients were more likely to be in the contemplation stage than males (OR = 0.22); patients with household monthly income per capita above 6,000 yuan were more likely to be in contemplation than those below 3,000 yuan (OR = 13.94); high vocal-demand occupations increased contemplation-stage probability (OR = 7.70) versus moderate-demand occupations; living within a 30-minute commute predicted the action/maintenance stages (OR = 7.14) versus a commute of 60 minutes or more; and high vocal-demand occupations made action-plus-maintenance stages more likely than average vocal demands (OR = 16.20).
- Conclusion: "Gender, monthly family income per capita, occupational voice demands, and distance to the hospital significantly impact the URICA-Voice compliance stages of patients. Patients who are female, have higher family income, have occupations with high voice demands, and live closer to the hospital exhibit higher compliance with voice training."

## Bearing on voice training
- The Chinese measurement of the adherence problem the trans voice literature describes qualitatively: voice training only works if it is done, and here the predictors are income, occupation, gender and travel distance — four variables with nothing to do with vocal technique ([[voice-therapy-techniques]], [[patient-reported-outcomes]]).
- The travel-distance result is the same barrier that the remote-therapy study from the same hospital group tests and partly removes [[chenw2026]], and the same one the Belgian survey reports as an access barrier for non-binary people presumed female at birth [[papeleu2025b]].
- Note the sample's sex skew (77% female) and what it implies for a reader asking about trans patients: this is a general voice-disorder clinic population, and the Chinese-language evidence on adherence is built on it rather than on trans cohorts.

## Notes
- Cross-sectional study in the Chinese journal of clinical otorhinolaryngology; graded medium. Single-centre, one three-month recruitment window; association only, no intervention.
- Only the abstract (English and Chinese) was read; the original Chinese title is not carried by PubMed, Europe PMC or OpenAlex, so the translated title is used. Full text not accessed.
