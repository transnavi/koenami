# Voice Disorder Prevalence and Vocal Health Characteristics in Adolescents

## What it says
- Probability sample of 502 US adolescents aged 13–17 (248 female, 254 male, 7 nonbinary, 4 transfemale, 3 transmale), surveyed March–June 2023 with the Vocal Fatigue Index (VFI).
- "The lifetime prevalence of voice problems was 24.3%, and current prevalence was 7.4%." Diagnoses included dysphonia with acute illness, vocal hyperfunction, benign lesions and muscle tension.
- Adolescents with voice complaints had significantly higher VFI scores (Cohen d 0.78 / 0.59 / 0.79 for parts 1–3).
- Voice-use patterns associated with problems: "frequent loud voice use, coughing, and/or throat clearing." Risk factors included childcare work (OR 6.4), singing (OR 2.08), musical theatre (OR 2.67), karaoke (OR 3.08), vaping (OR 1.99) and secondhand smoke (OR 1.83).
- "Adolescents identifying as transgender were more than 4 times (OR, 4.44; 95% CI, 0.63-31.2) as likely to report voice problems compared with cisgender adolescents."

## Bearing on voice training
- Throat clearing and habitual loud voice are the concrete behaviours that vocal-hygiene advice targets, with an effect size attached.
- The transgender odds ratio is the most direct published signal that trans adolescents report more voice problems — but its confidence interval crosses 1 (7 trans participants in total), so it is a reason to ask, not a finding to quote as established.

## Notes
- Self-report survey; diagnoses are reported, not verified by examination. The trans subgroup is 7 of 502.
