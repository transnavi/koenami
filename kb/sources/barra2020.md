## What it says
- Cross-sectional study of 60 people attending a Brazilian reference outpatient centre for LGBT health; self-administered profile and voice self-assessment questionnaires.
- "Most of the interviewees were young people who had never visited a speech-language-hearing therapist with the Public Health System (Sistema Único de Saúde); in general, they expressed interest in having such attention" (abstract, "Results").
- Impact was reported on three axes: "use of the voice in social life", "mischaracterization of gender, due to the voice", and "indication of dysphonia"; voice symptoms characteristic of dysphonia were identified in some participants.
- Concludes that transgender people are dissatisfied with their voices, that this hinders perception of the identified gender and has social, emotional, occupational and interpersonal repercussions, and that speech-language therapy should be included in the "transgendering process" (processo transexualizador) desired by most participants.

## Bearing on voice training
- Documents an SUS access gap directly: a majority of a 60-person clinic sample had never seen a fonoaudiólogo despite wanting to, which is the demand side of the service model described in [[dornelas2021]].
- The dysphonia signal in some participants supports screening for voice health before any pitch-raising practice ([[voice-health]]).

## Notes
- Published in English in a Brazilian speech-language journal; n = 60 at one reference centre, self-report only, no acoustic or laryngeal examination.
- The questionnaire used is described only as a "voice self-assessment questionnaire" in the abstract, so the instrument cannot be identified from the record fetched.
