## What it says
- Speech stimuli came from a single transmasculine speaker recorded repeatedly over ~1 year of testosterone hormone replacement therapy, during which his voice changed substantially.
- Three groups of 10 listeners each — cisgender young adults, cisgender older adults, and gender-diverse individuals — rated each stimulus on a visual analogue scale from "definitely male" through "guessing male/guessing female" to "definitely female."
- All three listener groups rated the speaker as consistently female through week 14 of hormone therapy and consistently male after week 28; the three groups' mean ratings were highly correlated with each other (all Pearson's r > 0.97).
- Concludes that, at reasonable group sizes, average voice-based gender-perception ratings of a transmasculine speaker are not strongly influenced by listener age or gender-minority status.

## Bearing on voice training
- Reassures that listener demographic composition (age, cisgender vs. gender-diverse) does not meaningfully change the group-average gender-perception judgment of a changing voice — a methodological finding useful for designing or interpreting listener panels in voice-training outcome research.
- Documents, as a side finding, a fairly sharp perceptual transition window (roughly weeks 14–28 of testosterone therapy) during which a transmasculine voice's perceived gender flipped from consistently female to consistently male — a concrete acoustic-to-perceptual timeline data point for masculinizing voice change under HRT.

## Notes
- Single-speaker design (n=1 transmasculine speaker tracked longitudinally) — strong internal validity for the listener-comparison question but no generalization across speakers/voices.
- Small listener groups (n=10 each); "high correlation between group means" does not rule out individual-level differences within or between groups.
