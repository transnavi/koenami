## What it says
- 20 speakers (10 male, 10 female) recorded six sustained isolated vowels normally and whispered; a third "filtered" tape low-pass filtered the voiced tape at 255 Hz to remove F0 while keeping resonance information.
- 15 listeners judged speaker sex and rated their own confidence across the three tapes (1800 judgments per tape).
- Accuracy: 96% correct on the voiced tape, 91% on the 255 Hz-filtered (resonance-only) tape, 75% on the whispered (F0-absent) tape; confidence followed the same ordering.
- The authors conclude laryngeal fundamental frequency is a more important cue to speaker sex than vocal-tract resonance characteristics, though resonance alone (filtered condition) still supported well-above-chance identification.

## Bearing on voice training
- Classic quantification that F0 dominates listener sex categorization but formant/resonance cues carry real weight on their own (91% correct with F0 removed) — supports training approaches that target both pitch and resonance rather than pitch alone.
- The whispered condition (F0 absent, some resonance cues degraded) still yielded 75% accuracy, showing listeners fall back on other cues (e.g., breathiness, articulation) when F0 and clean formant structure are both compromised.

## Notes
- Isolated sustained vowels, not connected speech — cue weighting can differ in running speech (compare gelfer2013).
- Small, homogeneous speaker/listener samples typical of 1970s phonetics; binary male/female forced-choice paradigm only.
