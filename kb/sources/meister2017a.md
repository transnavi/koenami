# Pitch Elevation in Male-to-female Transgender Persons — the Würzburg Approach（Meister et al. 2017）

## What it says
- What was done: "A total of 21 patients underwent Wendler's glottoplasty modified by Hagen" — the Würzburg modification of the standard pitch-raising operation (abstract, Methods). It is an outcomes-research study, not a comparison.
- Follow-up assessment: "laryngoscopy, voice range profile, Voice Handicap Index, Life Satisfaction Questionnaire, and a visual analog scale for individual satisfaction with the voice."
- The pitch result: "The fundamental frequency was elevated into the typical female fundamental frequency range. Furthermore, an elevation of the lower frequency limit was shown without a reduction of the frequency range" — the floor rose with the ceiling, so the range itself was preserved.
- The costs recorded: "About one third of the population feels affected by the restricted dynamic range", and "The Dysphonia Severity Index as a marker for voice quality was unchanged."
- The satisfaction structure: "Subjective satisfaction with the voice showed a strong correlation with the individual elevation of the pitch", while "elevated Scores of the Voice Handicap Index indicated that in everyday life, transgender persons continue to feel handicapped because of their voice."
- The social finding, stated as an indicator: "Another indicator for the lack of social acceptance and integration is the reduced general life satisfaction in the Life Satisfaction Questionnaire especially in the domain 'friends, acquaintances, relatives.'"
- Conclusion: Wendler's glottoplasty modified by Hagen is "an effective and low-risk method of raising the vocal pitch of male-to-female transgender persons", but "a better multidisciplinary therapy concept for voice feminization is necessary."

## Bearing on voice training
- Supplies the objective counterpart to the Würzburg group's satisfaction study [[meister2016]] on the same 21 patients: the operation reliably moves F0 into the female range without shrinking the range, keeps the Dysphonia Severity Index unchanged, and still leaves a voice handicap in daily life for many patients.
- The two findings that bear hardest on training practice: the strong pitch–satisfaction correlation is about *how much* pitch rose for that individual, not about everyone reaching a target; and a third of patients felt the loss of dynamic range, which is a loudness/expression constraint a training programme may have to work around rather than a pitch problem ([[vocal-weight]], [[voice-quality]]).
- The life-satisfaction result in the "friends, acquaintances, relatives" domain is the clearest statement in this vault that voice outcome and social outcome are separate measurements ([[transgender-care-context]], [[patient-reported-outcomes]]).
- Its own conclusion — that a better multidisciplinary concept is needed — is the argument for the therapy-and-surgery pathway the German sources describe ([[charite-stimme-trans]], [[dgti-stimmanpassung]]).

## Notes
- Single-centre retrospective outcomes study of 21 patients at the Würzburg ENT department; graded medium. No control group and no comparison against voice therapy alone.
- The cohort overlaps the German-language paper [[meister2016]]; the telephone-test study [[meister2017]] uses 18 of the operated speakers from the same programme.
