# Voice and Speech Training for the Transgendered Patient: What the Otolaryngologist Should Know（Antoni 2022）

## What it says
- The starting point: "Vocal dysphoria is present for many transgender individuals." The goals are not the clinician's to set — "Voice goals are defined by each individual client" (abstract).
- On the nature of the work: "Voice modification work can be challenging for both clinicians and clients, involving both direct and indirect aspects of intervention", and it adds a claim about which parts the surgeon does not see — "The latter are likely to be less known to otolaryngologists but can form a significant part of clinical practice for experienced speech pathologists."
- A stated source of outcome variation: "Varying experience levels of treating clinicians can in turn have a bearing on the vocal outcomes achieved by clients."
- The surgical pathway, as this author states it: "Those who do seek surgical assistance benefit from appropriate presurgery speech and language therapy evaluation and postsurgical voice rehabilitation and treatment."
- Author affiliation as printed: University College London (UCL); Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK); Voice & Speech Services, Harrow, Middlesex.

## Bearing on voice training
- A practising UK voice clinician writing for surgeons, and the three claims worth keeping are about the clinician rather than the client: goals are set by the client, the indirect parts of the work (the parts that are not drills) are the parts otolaryngologists do not see, and the treating clinician's own experience level is a variable in the outcome ([[voice-feminization-therapy]], [[speech-language-pathology]]).
- Its presurgery-evaluation/postsurgery-rehabilitation sequence is the same ordering the surgical series in this vault build into their protocols [[sun2025]], [[scarpa2025]] and the consensus statements recommend ([[clinical-guidelines]]).
- The author is the speech pathologist the vault already holds as the third-party voice clinician in the community-accessible material — the Christella voice resources and the Free Your Voice toolkit [[christella-voiceup]], [[davies2015]] — which makes this the peer-reviewed statement of the same practice.
