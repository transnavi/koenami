# Type III Thyroplasty for Vocal Masculinization in Transmasculine Patients: A Scoping Review

## What it says
- Searched MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, CINAHL, CENTRAL, ClinicalTrials.gov and Google Scholar from inception to 10 August 2025 for adults having type III thyroplasty for vocal masculinisation.
- "Of 1042 studies screened, 4 met the inclusion criteria (n = 23)" — twenty-three patients in total across the whole literature.
- Reductions in fundamental frequency after surgery were reported, "although surgical techniques, outcome measures, and follow-up durations varied substantially".
- Complications: "One patient (4.3%) developed transient subcutaneous emphysema that resolved without sequelae."
- Patient-reported outcomes "were inconsistently assessed, with limited reports of improved voice-related quality of life, reduced dysphoria, and decreased misgendering".
- Conclusion: evidence "is insufficient to determine long-term effectiveness, durability, functional voice outcomes, or safety", and prospective studies with standardised measures are needed before clinical recommendations.

## Bearing on voice training
- The surgical option for masculinisation is essentially unevidenced at the moment: 23 patients, four studies, heterogeneous technique. Anything said about it in a guide or a forum is ahead of the data [[voice-surgery]] [[voice-masculinization]].
- Contrasts with the feminisation side, where pooled glottoplasty series cover several hundred patients [[chowdhury2025]] — the asymmetry is a fact about where surgical effort has gone [[clinical-guidelines]].

## Notes
- The review registers no language or country limits in the abstract but reports no geography of the four included studies. Fundamental frequency is the outcome most often reported; intensity, projection and quality of life are named as gaps.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
