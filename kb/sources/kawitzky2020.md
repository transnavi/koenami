## What it says
- Investigated whether visual-acoustic biofeedback can help transgender women achieve second-formant (F2) targets typical of cisgender women, and whether such shifts affect perceived femininity of speech.
- Transgender women and a comparison group of cisgender males were trained to produce vowels while attempting to match a visual F2 target shifted up (feminized target), shifted down, or unshifted (control conditions) relative to their own baseline F2.
- Despite the short-term nature of training, both groups showed significant F2 differences across the shifted-up, shifted-down, and unshifted conditions.
- Blinded listeners' gender-typicality ratings showed higher F2 values were associated with increased perceived femininity of speech.
- Consistent with prior literature, the study found fundamental frequency and F2 jointly contribute to gender perception.
- Concludes biofeedback may be a useful tool in voice modification therapy for transgender women but that larger studies and information about generalization to spontaneous speech are needed.

## Bearing on voice training
- Direct experimental evidence that formant biofeedback is trainable even in short sessions and that F2 shifts independently move listener femininity ratings — supports resonance/formant-focused training methods, not just pitch (F0) training.
- Reinforces that F0 and formants act jointly on gender perception, relevant to any concept page distinguishing pitch training from resonance training.

## Notes
- Short-term training paradigm (single-session biofeedback), not a full therapy-course outcome study; generalization beyond the trained vowel context to conversational speech is explicitly flagged as unresolved by the authors.
