# Thai Voice Handicap Index-10: A Validity Study

## What it says
- Prospective validation at the Department of Otolaryngology, Pramongkutklao Hospital, Bangkok: 40 patients diagnosed with voice disorders and 40 people without, each completing both the full Thai VHI and the new Thai VHI-10 (abstract, "Methods").
- "The Thai VHI-10 demonstrated excellent internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha = 0.965) and a strong correlation with the full Thai VHI (r = 0.969, P < 0.001)."
- "ROC analysis identified a sensitivity and specificity of 92.5% at a cutoff score of 7"; the short form distinguished patients from controls (P < 0.001).
- Completion time fell from "mean: 4.2 vs. 14.3 minutes" — the short form against the full Thai VHI.
- The authors' stated purpose: the 30-item VHI "can be time-consuming", and the VHI-10 "provides a concise alternative, maintaining reliability and validity".

## Bearing on voice training
- Gives Thai-language voice work a validated, quick self-report instrument with a published cut-off (7) and completion time, which is what a Thai clinic or trainer needs to track a client's own sense of handicap before and after voice work [[thai-voice]] [[patient-reported-outcomes]].
- The instrument is general, not trans-specific — no item set for gender-related voice concern is reported here — so it measures vocal handicap rather than gender congruence, unlike the trans-specific questionnaires used in other languages [[patient-reported-outcomes]] [[voice-across-languages]].

## Notes
- Validation study with a modest sample (40 + 40) at one Bangkok hospital; the sample is a general voice-disorder clinic population, and no transgender participants are described.
- The article is in English; the Thai-language instrument it validates is the object of interest for this vault, not the article's language.
