## What it says
- Recorded 37 adult heterosexual men across several speech tasks and had 20 adult heterosexual women listeners rate the speakers' masculinity from voice alone.
- Used two-level (speaker-within-listener) path analysis to relate physiological measures (salivary testosterone, height), acoustic measures (F0, formant spacing ΔF), and listener masculinity ratings.
- Taller men with higher salivary testosterone had lower F0 and lower ΔF, and were in turn rated more masculine by listeners.
- The testosterone → perceived-masculinity relationship was essentially mediated by F0; the height → perceived-masculinity relationship was partially mediated by both F0 and ΔF.

## Bearing on voice training
- Quantifies the causal chain from physiology (testosterone, height) through acoustics (F0, formant spacing) to listener-perceived masculinity, showing F0 is the dominant acoustic mediator of hormonally-driven masculinity impressions — informative for masculinizing voice training, where F0 lowering is likely to move perceived masculinity more directly than resonance work alone.
- Because ΔF (formant spacing/perceived vocal tract length) partially mediates the height-masculinity link independently of F0, formant/resonance work retains value even once pitch has been adjusted.

## Notes
- Correlational path model, not an experimental manipulation (contrast with cartei2012/cartei2013 resynthesis studies); causal directionality is inferred, not proven.
- Speaker sample restricted to heterosexual men rated by heterosexual women listeners — generalizability to other populations untested.
