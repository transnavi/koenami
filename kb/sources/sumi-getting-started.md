# Getting Started (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- The wiki's front door is a plan rather than an explanation. For feminisation: 「If you're new to voice feminisation voice training, the recommendation is to learn using Pitch Naturalisation, which is an intuitive method to get you the foundation and probably 70% of the skills and changes you will need to learn. If you don't have existing habits, this should be far more effective and get better results than most other exercises.」
- Its opening claim about pitch: 「Pitch is not that important! Well, it's actually really important but not in the way you might think. It's very useful for training, intonation and a few other things, but it's not a gender factor in and of itself.」
- Two pieces of standing advice: 「It's important to be aware at every stage of the possibility of falling into bad habits so be aware of what can go wrong in each exercise. You don't need to worry about it, just know that it can happen and then it won't.」 and 「Finding a voice example you think is an improvement on your current voice, or in other words is closer to your goal is really helpful for having a sense of direction.」
- The feminisation plan, in the order given, with the reasoning attached to each step:
  - **Lower vocal weight and raise pitch using pitch naturalisation** — 「a relaxed high pitch will usually be light in vocal weight provided it isn't forced in some other way (i.e. breathiness)」; 「using "awwww" as if talking to a cute puppy is a good behavioural trigger for getting light vocal weight」; 「just sliding up to a higher pitch and then down half as far (e.g. C3 to C4 to G3) will help a lot with this too」; 「avoid breathiness」; 「M2 / falsetto is fine to break into, but it must come back down to a normal voice again」.
  - **Raise resonance and brighten as needed** — 「try to think of resonance as just an aspect of sound, don't focus on physical aspects like raising the larynx except for context」; 「avoid FVF and constriction ("old voice")」; 「avoid unvoiced / whisper slides if you can, or at least prioritise doing voiced ones … don't slide from a whisper directly into speech - make sure there's a pause between」; 「clean the sound up before using it. Don't use a configuration that is rough or efforful, make it smooth first even at the cost of lowering resonance slightly」.
  - **Clean up the voice** — 「If you're at this stage and you have any roughness in your voice, now's the time to sort it out. Going further will make it extremely difficult to unlearn later」; the steps are reducing breathiness, 「practice lighter onsets with glottal strikes」, reducing FVF or roughness, eliminating tension, and 「find or create your strategically lazy configuration」.
  - **Practice speech patterns** — 「find an example of a voice you like」; 「avoid emphasis with volume for feminine voices」; 「vary tempo」; 「mimic someone's speech patterns」; 「intonation doesn't need to be super high, but it's worth practicing」.
  - **Get more pitch range** — 「at first this means just using the voice at a slightly higher pitch sometimes to stretch your range over time. Fatigue or strain is to be avoided entirely; if you feel tension in the neck you are trying too hard or are too high」; 「blending into m2/falsetto and making it sound more full」; 「practice intonating progressively higher」.
  - **Start mimicry and learn microbehaviours** — 「you can do this at any time, but make sure you can hit the pitch and resonance of the voice you're mimicking or else it might be quite difficult」, then the clip procedure ([[sumi-mimicry]]).
- The masculinisation plan, marked 「(under construction)」, runs in three steps: **Expand your lower range** — 「low pitch and heavy vocal weight are linked, and to achieve one you need the other」; 「Vocal Weight Layering slides are a good exercise to start with」; 「avoid fry and pressed phonation, as they don't help but can sound lower in pitch」; 「the goal is to get a sense of "momentum" or power, when lowering pitch - if it feels forced or weak, it's not helping you get more weight」. **Develop a lower resonance and darker voice** — 「mouth space and articulation are extremely important, so look at some methods that focus on that like hah/haw」; 「it may be needed to lower overall resonance too, not just mouth space; whispering high to low as a guide, or doing a yawn, can make that happen」. **Practice speech patterns** — 「you don't need to be monotone to sound masculine, but you do need to keep the voice heavy if you do intonate」; 「masculine voices typically have less intonation frequency, meaning they may intonate up once and let it slowly drop, rather than going up and down for every word (but, again it's not always the case)」.
- It states that the plan is a generalisation: 「Below is a typical plan assuming an average masculine voice trained to be feminine. This isn't always accurate because everyone is different but it can give some direction to start… You can do any of these things at any time, it's more a generalisation. Often it's most effective to be trying several things while working on one main concept.」

## Bearing on voice training
- This is the wiki's curriculum, and it is the clearest statement of the order the rest of the pages assume: weight and pitch first through intuitive practice, resonance second, cleaning up before going further, then speech patterns, then range, then mimicry ([[sumi-pipm]], [[sumi-vocal-weight]], [[sumi-resonance]], [[sumi-speech-patterns]], [[sumi-mimicry]]).
- The two instructions that differ from a purely acoustic account are both about behaviour: resonance is to be treated as a property of the sound rather than as larynx position, and the whole first step is to be reached by leaning rather than pushing ([[sumi-resonance]], [[sumi-how-to-practice]]).
- The masculinisation half is the wiki's only substantial treatment of the other direction, and it ties pitch to weight rather than to fry or pressed phonation ([[voice-masculinization]], [[vocal-weight]]).

## Limits
- Volunteer wiki, "under construction"; the masculinisation section says so explicitly. Claims such as the "70%" figure and the "strategically lazy configuration" are the wiki's own framing and cite nothing. This page restates material held in the wiki's individual pages; the fetch read the introduction, both plans and their reasoning, not any linked page.
