# Wendler glottoplasty: an effective pitch raising surgery in male-to-female transsexuals（Mastronikolis et al. 2013）

## What it says
- Cohort and design: a retrospective case series of 31 male-to-female patients treated with a Wendler glottoplasty (abstract, Methods).
- The procedure as described: "CO2-laser de-epithelialization of the anterior commissure along with the anterior third of the two vocal folds, the suturing of the two vocal folds with two 3.0 resorbable threads, and next, the application of fibrin sealant to strengthen the stitches."
- Measures: "fundamental frequency, frequency range, maximum phonation time, phonation quotient, estimated subglottic pressure (ESGP), grade of dysphonia (G), and voice handicap index", measured preoperatively and at the last follow-up visit. Mean follow-up was 9.2 months.
- Groups: 19 patients under 40 (mean age 28.6, range 16–39) and 12 aged 40 or over (mean 51.9, range 45–59), "for assessing the influence of the age of treatment on the results". Three cases had previously undergone cricothyroid approximation elsewhere.
- Pitch results: mean F0 rose "from 135.8 to 206.3 Hz in total (P = 0.001) and also in both groups, especially in group A (mean F0-postop = 213.8 Hz)" — a mean gain of about 70 Hz, larger in the younger group.
- The trade-offs recorded: "The mean frequency range had a tendency to decrease postoperatively, whereas the ESGP was significantly higher in both total sample and group A (P = 0.001, respectively). G was increased postoperatively and presented a statistical significance in group B (P = 0.035)" — dysphonia grade worsened and subglottic pressure rose, the latter significantly in the younger group.
- Revision rate and causes: "A revision Wendler procedure was necessary for three individuals (9.7%); two of them presented a suture's line breakdown because they did not follow the postoperative recommendations for voice rest and the third one had an insufficient web due to an insufficient estimation of the necessary correction."
- Conclusion: "Wendler glottoplasty seems to be an effective technique to feminize the voice in MFTs with better results when performed in younger individuals."

## Bearing on voice training
- The clearest account in this vault of what the operation's success hangs on after the surgeon is finished: two of the three revisions followed failure to observe the postoperative voice-rest recommendations, which makes adherence to the rest protocol a surgical-outcome variable rather than an aftercare detail ([[voice-health]], [[vocal-fatigue]]).
- Its trade-off list — frequency range tending down, subglottic pressure up, dysphonia grade up in the older group — is the same "pitch moves, other parameters pay" pattern the later series report [[meister2017a]], [[wang2009]], with the age split added as a possible modifier of both gain and dysphonia.
- A Belgian–Italian–Greek collaboration from the Liège group (Remacle, Lawson) with Italian (Biagini) and Greek authors, which is why it is grouped here with the Francophone and Italian material; it predates the Italian national cohort by twelve years [[scarpa2025]].

## Notes
- Retrospective single-centre case series of 31 patients; graded medium. No control group; the age subgroups are observational, not randomised; follow-up is short (mean 9.2 months) for a claim about durability.
- Only the PubMed abstract was read; the full text is paywalled and was not accessed.
