# Differences Among Mixed, Chest, and Falsetto Registers: A Multiparametric Study

## What it says
- Twelve healthy singers (six men, six women) phonated in chest, mix and falsetto; three of them were additionally measured with high-speed digital imaging and electroglottography. The authors describe mix as "an important technique for singers, as it can help bridge from the chest voice to falsetto without noticeable voice breaks."
- Subglottal pressure was highest for chest; mean flow rate was highest for falsetto. "The PSub of the mix was as low as the falsetto, and the MFR of the mix was as low as the chest" — mix is the low-pressure, low-flow combination.
- Open quotient differed significantly among registers *even at the same fundamental frequency*; "the OQ of the mix was higher than that of the chest but lower than that of the falsetto."
- "The first and second harmonic (H1-H2) difference of the voice source spectrum was the greatest for the falsetto, then the mix, and finally, the chest."
- Maximum volume velocity was lowest in mix at every F0. The authors conclude mix "proved to have a distinct voice-production mechanism, which can be differentiated from those of the chest or falsetto registers."

## Bearing on voice training
- Puts a number on the thing trans voice teachers call "weight": H1–H2 ranks chest < mix < falsetto, so a lighter-sounding voice is one with a larger H1–H2 at the same pitch. It also shows a middle setting exists and is measurable, which is the target most feminisation work is actually aiming at rather than falsetto.

## Notes
- Trained singers only; only three subjects contributed the imaging and EGG data. Registers were self-produced on instruction, so the register labels are the singers' own.
