# Effects of Intensive Intonation Training in Transgender and Gender Diverse People Aiming for a More Masculine-Sounding Voice: A Randomized Clinical Trial

## What it says
- Ghent University randomized clinical trial (with La Trobe's Oates): 23 Dutch-speaking transgender and gender-diverse people presumed female at birth — 17 nonbinary, six transmasculine — aiming for a more masculine-sounding voice; four weeks of intonation training versus four weeks of sham training first, with recordings before, between and after, and at four-week follow-up (abstract, "METHOD").
- Significant desired decreases in all acoustic and perceptual intonation parameters — general and final intonation shifts, F0 range, F0 variation index — across yes/no questions, question-word questions and declaratives.
- Naive listeners rated the voices significantly more masculine after training; participants reported a voice "significantly more masculine and more aligned with their gender identity", noticing more downward final intonation shifts, less pitch variation and slower pitch changes.
- Conclusion: findings "support including intonation in voice and communication training for TGD individuals seeking a more masculine-sounding voice".

## Bearing on voice training
- The first controlled intervention this vault holds for behavioural masculinization without testosterone — previously flagged as an evidence gap. Intonation alone moved both listener ratings and self-perception [[voice-masculinization]].
- Masculinization here means flatter, falling, less variable pitch — the inverse of the feminine profile [[hancock2014]] [[leung2018]].

## Notes
- Dutch-language material; intonation patterns are language-specific, so the exact parameters transfer to other languages only in shape [[voice-across-languages]].
- 23 participants, waitlist rather than inactive control; therapy intensity (four weeks) is short against the year-plus courses of [[leyns2023]].
