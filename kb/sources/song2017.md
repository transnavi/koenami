## What it says
- Systematic review and meta-analysis (CINAHL, Cochrane, Embase, Medline, PubMed, Science Direct) of pitch-elevating phonosurgical techniques evaluated in live humans with pre- and postoperative acoustic analysis.
- 29 studies were identified; after applying inclusion/exclusion criteria, 13 studies were included in the meta-analysis.
- Techniques grouped by mechanism: increasing vocal-cord tension (cricothyroid approximation), shortening vocal-cord length (cold-knife glottoplasty, laser-shortening glottoplasty), and decreasing vocal-fold mass (laser reduction glottoplasty). Shortening techniques and cricothyroid approximation were the most commonly studied (6 studies each).
- The largest increase in fundamental frequency was seen with vocal-cord-shortening techniques.
- Preoperative speech therapy, postoperative voice rest protocols, and reporting of patient satisfaction were inconsistent across the included studies; many studies were limited by low statistical power and short follow-up.
- Conclusion: multiple pitch-elevation techniques exist, but vocal-cord-shortening procedures (e.g., glottoplasty variants) appear to produce the largest F0 increases.

## Bearing on voice training
- One of the earliest formal meta-analyses in this space; establishes the general mechanism-based taxonomy of surgical techniques (tension-increasing vs. length-shortening vs. mass-reducing) that later reviews (chowdhury2025) build on and refine with PROM data.
- Its core finding — shortening procedures give the largest F0 gains — is consistent with, and now supplemented by, more recent studies' quantified numbers (yilmaz2021, mora2018, titze2020's mechanistic explanation).

## Notes
- Predates the widespread use of validated PROMs (TVQ/TWVQ) in this literature — outcome measure is F0 only, not patient-reported quality of life; the review's own text flags inconsistent reporting of therapy protocols and satisfaction, limiting comparability across included studies.
