# Voice and Stroboscopic Characteristics in Transgender Patients Seeking Gender-Affirming Voice Care

## What it says
- Retrospective review of 61 trans female patients (mean age 32 ± 11) at a tertiary laryngology centre, February 2018 – February 2019; mean 41.5 months since starting transition, 69% presenting full-time female in all contexts.
- "Pathologic stroboscopy findings were found in 5%." The authors state this "is the first to report stroboscopy findings in transgender individuals" and conclude "Stroboscopy is encouraged before initiating voice intervention in transgender patients."
- Mean questionnaire scores: VHI-10 16 ± 10, Singing VHI-10 19 ± 13, TVQ^MtF 81 ± 22. Correlation between VHI-10 and TVQ was weak (R = 0.47, P = .053), and between SVHI-10 and TVQ weaker still (R = 0.35, P = .17).
- "Trans women report perceived voice handicap in speaking and singing, which appears separate from gender-related voice/communication concerns."

## Bearing on voice training
- Two distinct complaints can coexist: the voice is not gendered as wanted (TVQ), and the voice is not working well (VHI). They correlate weakly, so a satisfied TVQ score does not mean the voice is healthy — and singing handicap was reported independently again.
- The 5% figure is the concrete argument for laryngeal examination before a pitch-raising programme.

## Notes
- Single tertiary centre, treatment-seeking sample, retrospective; level 4 evidence by the authors' own grading. No trans masculine patients.
