# Is Your Voice Too Buzzy or Too Hollow?

## What it says
- oEmbed title as above, TransVoiceLessons. Watch-page date text: 2022/04/10. Description is English on this fetch.
- "Vocal Fullness is a pedagogical concept developed by Clover and I to describe the relationship between resonance and weight. It's a perceived sense of 'saturation' or 'fullness'."
- Quoted definition in the description: "Fullness is the perceptual description of how vocal weight and size interact with each other." Three named states: sufficiently full, underfull, overfull. "The larger the size, the more weight is needed to create an equally Full sound" and the inverse. "Overfull is a harsh, saturated, twangy sound formed by a heavy—small relationship. Underfull, on the other hand, is a hollow, dull, empty sound formed by a light—large relationship."
- "One of the most common issues in transvoice is a lack of balance between weight and size." Transmasculine voices "a bit 'buzzier' than average" when they have "enough weight but too small a vocal tract (thus too high of resonance)" — Overfull. Feminizing voices "too buzzy or too hollow (Overfull or Underfull)."
- Full: "sounds average, human, typical, 'normal'"; "occurs when weight and size are balance[d]"; "sufficient fullness is responsible for typical masculine and feminine voices." Overfull: "twangy, gnome-like, duck-like, metallic, and bright."
- Chapter titles name a "Boomerang" mistake, "Plateauing," "Androgenization Space," and a viewer question "What is Size??"

## Bearing on voice training
- The English community's working model of why a high-pitched small tract still "clocks" as male (too much weight for the size: buzzy/overfull) or why a light large tract sounds empty (hollow/underfull). Size here is resonance / implied tract length ([[formants]]); weight is the source ([[vocal-weight]]).
- "Twangy" as overfull matches Sundberg's finding that twang raises F1/F2 *and* closed quotient ([[sundberg2010]]).
