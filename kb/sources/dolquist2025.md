# The Bound Breath: Transmasculine and Gender Nonconforming Singers' Perceived Effects of Chest Binding on Singing

## What it says
- Described by the authors as "the first of its kind": a survey of 38 Twin Cities-based singers of varying levels who have worn or currently wear chest binders while singing (abstract).
- Chest binding is "a gender-affirming practice used by many transmasculine and gender nonconforming individuals to achieve the appearance of a flatter chest without surgical intervention"; its harmful effects are "relatively well-established", but its effects on singing were "under-explored".
- Participants "reported that binding gave significant mental health benefits while singing, as well as perceptions of physical limitations such as difficulty with inhalation, loss of singing stamina, and decreased voice volume".
- "Despite the physical limitations, participants reported a preference to bind while singing."
- The authors call for "binding-focused, gender-inclusive singing pedagogy" and for research into compression garments for singing.

## Bearing on voice training
- Puts the two sides of binding in one finding: reported breathing and stamina costs alongside a stated preference to keep doing it, which is why advice to simply stop binding is not usable [[breathing-and-support]] [[voice-health]].
- The three named limitations — inhalation, stamina, volume — are all respiratory-support variables, so a singing teacher's usual tools (breath management, dynamic control) are the ones being constrained [[singing-and-voice]].

## Notes
- n = 38, single metropolitan area, self-report survey, no spirometry or acoustic measurement; "perceptions" is the paper's own word for the physical findings. Published as a Journal of Voice article; first author at the University of Minnesota.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
