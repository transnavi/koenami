## What it says
- States plainly that FTM/GID patients need no voice therapy since testosterone naturally lowers pitch, but MTF/GID patients' feminizing hormones do not raise pitch — hence surgical and behavioral intervention is needed for trans women.
- Since 2014 the authors' clinic has performed type IV thyroplasty (甲状軟骨形成術Ⅳ型, Isshiki type 4) with pre- and postoperative voice therapy (VT) for MTF/GID patients, and this paper reports the VT outcomes.
- Speaking fundamental frequency increased after the surgery+VT protocol.
- On the Voice Handicap Index: functional and physical subscale scores decreased slightly; the emotional subscale decreased significantly (i.e., improved).
- Postoperative complaints included difficulty speaking, difficulty "passing" vocally as female, and a mismatch between the patient's subjective image of their eventual female voice and the outcome.
- Conclusion: pre/postoperative voice therapy is important, but so is coaching on speech style, clothing, and makeup — voice alone doesn't determine passing.

## Bearing on voice training
- Direct evidence that hormone therapy alone does not feminize pitch in trans women — surgery (or behavioral training) is required, unlike the masculinizing case.
- The "expectation mismatch" complaint (patients' internal image of their target voice vs. the achieved voice) is a recurring theme worth flagging in any voice-training guidance: SFF gains do not guarantee subjective satisfaction.
- Emotional VHI improving while functional/physical improves only slightly suggests psychological benefit can outpace acoustic/functional change.

## Notes
- Single-institution Japanese case series; exact n not given in the abstract.
- Population: MTF/GID patients undergoing type IV thyroplasty with structured VT, Japan, from 2014 onward.
