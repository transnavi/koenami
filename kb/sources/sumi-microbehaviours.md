# Microbehaviours (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Definition: "You can call it a voice print. Microbehaviours are how to describe aspects of voice that are too small to individually explain or consciously train. When we think about glottal behaviour ... it contains things as big as vocal weight and as small as onsets. So what's smaller and harder to affect than something as tiny as onsets? Well, microbehaviours."
- The problem they are invoked to explain: "When you take a feminine voice, lower the pitch, make it heavier in vocal weight and drop the resonance a bit, you expect to hear a typical masculine sound. So, why is it then that sometimes we hear voices just like this that are feminine? ... The answer is - probably - microbehaviours."
- On trainability: "Microbehaviours are an aspect of voice that is near impossible to train consciously. Usually we just practice for so long that we get really used to that configuration and learn new microbehaviours and develop more consistent glottal behaviour over time. That's the problem though: it takes a long time - in the order of years."
- The stated route: "There are ways to train it however and the main one is by finding a voice that displays the characteristics you want and mimicking it intuitively." The page's table of contents links a "List of Microbehaviours" and a "Mimicry" page, and embeds an audio introduction ("What Are Microbehaviours").
- The concept is also ranked second in the wiki's own ordering of gendered factors, behind vocal weight and ahead of resonance [[sumi-vocal-gender]].

## Bearing on voice training
- Names the residual directly: after pitch, weight and resonance are matched, some voices still read as the wrong gender, and the wiki attributes the difference to features too small to teach one at a time [[voice-impressions]] [[gender-perception]].
- Prescribes mimicry of a target voice as the training method, on the argument that conscious control cannot reach this level — a very different instruction from isolating one variable at a time [[community-training]] [[voice-therapy-techniques]].
- The stated timescale ("in the order of years") is the wiki's own estimate, and it is the reason the site treats these as a long-game rather than an exercise.
