# The Case for Acoustic Registers

## What it says
- Quotes García II's historic definition — "a series of consecutive and homogeneous tones going from low to high, produced by the development of the same mechanical principle" — and extracts its two requirements: homogeneity of timbre, and an observable mechanical principle.
- Quotes Hollien's expansion of García (see [[hollien1974]]) and notes that pedagogy has remained preoccupied with laryngeal biomechanics as the mechanism of register change.
- Quotes Titze (2021): "a register is a plateau of vocal timbre (voice quality) as pitch, loudness, or vowel is changed", and "registration involves the whole instrument, the entire airway, not only the larynx." Two mechanisms can produce the sudden harmonic change that signals a register: a change in vocal-fold medial surface contact, or vocal-tract resonances enhancing or de-enhancing particular harmonics.
- Describes the laryngeal binary as "a bulkier vibration that involves the cover, body, and muscle layers of the vocal folds (thyroarytenoid involvement — i.e., a chest, modal, or mode 1 registration)" versus "a longer, thinner mode that involves vibration only of the edge of the cover (the cricothyroid or 'stretched' vocal ligament — i.e., head, falsetto, or mode 2 registration)", whose toggle produces "a sudden shift in voice source contribution from a shallow spectral slope to a steep spectral slope".
- **The voice source contributes only two things**, on his account: fundamental frequency, and spectral slope (spectral tilt, "roll-off"). Figure 1 contrasts −3 dB/octave ("a 'shallow' slope with many strong higher harmonics and a brassier timbre, more characteristic of mode 1") with −12 dB/octave ("a 'steep' spectral slope with fewer, weaker higher harmonics and a warmer, flutier timbre, more characteristic of mode 2").
- Argues that in a voice trained out of the binary flip, the remaining perceptible register changes are *acoustic*: rising harmonics passing through stable vocal-tract resonances, occurring "at locations that are completely predictable per voice type and vowel, since they occur relative to first formant locations". Historic pedagogy named these by feel: "turning over, turning, tipping, or covering".

## Bearing on voice training
- The clearest statement that "weight" and "brightness" are two different things: spectral slope belongs to the source (how the folds close), formant-harmonic relationships belong to the tract. A trans-feminine voice worked only on resonance can still sound heavy, and one worked only on the source can still sound dark.
