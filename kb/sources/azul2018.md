# Do Transmasculine Speakers Present With Gender-Related Voice Problems? Insights From a Participant-Centered Mixed-Methods Study

## What it says
- Participant-centred mixed-methods study: qualitative content analysis of semistructured interviews plus acoustical voice analyses and an examination of gender attributions to voice, with 14 German-speaking transmasculine people, 14 cisfemale and 7 cismale controls (abstract).
- "Eleven participants (79%) presented with indications of gender-related voice problems" — dissatisfaction with gender-related voice features, difficulty controlling vocal gender presentation, and mismatch between desired gender attribution and the attributions received from others (abstract, results).
- Self-evaluations and researcher-led examinations disagreed in a number of cases; the authors treat negotiating that discrepancy as part of participant-centred interpretation (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The empirical counterweight to "trans men do not need voice services": most of this cohort showed gender-related voice problems of some kind, and the problems include perception by others, not only acoustics ([[voice-masculinization]], [[gender-perception]]).
- The self-versus-examiner disagreement argues for asking the client what the problem is before measuring anything ([[patient-reported-outcomes]]).

## Notes
- Small qualitative-led sample (14 transmasculine participants), German-speaking, recruited through the authors' clinical network; the percentage is descriptive, not a prevalence estimate.
- Companion papers from the same corpus: the 2015 critical review ([[azul2015]]), a 2016 qualitative analysis of gender-related aspects, a 2017 review of voice function, and a 2021 interview study of forces affecting voice function (not in this vault).
