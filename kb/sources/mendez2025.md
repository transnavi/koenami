## What it says
- Qualitative narrative study of 19 travesti, trans and non-binary people ("personas TTNB") living in Córdoba, Argentina; semi-structured interviews analysed with qualitative content analysis in Atlas.ti.
- Introduces "voz migrante" — the migrant voice — as a "práctica vocal comunicacional situada (PVCS)", a situated communicative vocal practice, rather than a set of acoustic targets.
- Central finding: "la migración corporal no siempre implica una migración vocal" — bodily transition does not always imply vocal transition, and the life course determines whether vocal migration is experienced as imperative, conditioned or optional.
- Reports that integral speech-language interventions promoted wellbeing and autonomy, but warns that "el énfasis centrado en la técnica vocal desvinculada del contexto social puede generar malestar y dependencia" — a focus on vocal technique detached from social context can produce distress and dependence.
- Frames voice work as part of "el derecho a la comunicación" (the right to communication) and argues that contextualised approaches reduce inequities in health care for gender-dissident people.

## Bearing on voice training
- The strongest Spanish-language statement that a training programme built only on pitch and resonance targets can harm; a counterweight to the technique-only framing of most self-training material ([[community-training]]).
- Supplies Argentine vocabulary — travesti, género-disidente, voz migrante — that Spanish-language material from Spain does not use.

## Notes
- Qualitative, 19 participants in one Argentine city; no acoustic or outcome data, and the categories are the authors' analytic constructs.
- Argentina's Ley 26.743 de Identidad de Género is the legal backdrop for this cohort but the abstract does not name it; see [[spanish-voice]] for what is and is not sourced on coverage.
