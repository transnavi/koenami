# Transgender voice and communication treatment: review of the literature（Lechien et al. 2014）

## What it says
- The abstract's premise: transgender people are "a small but growing population in ENT department", and "many voice parameters significantly contribute to the perception of gender (fundamental frequency, supraglottic resonance patterns, etc.)" — resonance is named beside pitch from the first sentence.
- It frames the transition pathway as optional in its intervention: people in transition "may therefore aim at changing their own voice properties, either by means of speech therapy or by medical intervention (hormonotherapy and/or surgery)".
- The paper's own gap claim is about measurement, not technique: "The current voice assessment and outcome measures for this population before and after treatment are nevertheless still lacking validity", and "A well-accepted general framework including self-perception, subjective assessment of the practitioner and objective measures is not well documented."
- Stated purpose: "this review is therefore meant as a contribution to the development of a state of the art in the field."

## Bearing on voice training
- The Belgian French-language statement of the three-armed assessment requirement — self-perception, clinician's perceptual judgement, objective measures — that the trans voice literature keeps rediscovering; it is the same triad the outcome reviews formalise and the community guides translate into self-recording practice ([[patient-reported-outcomes]], [[gender-perception]]).
- Its authors sit in the Francophone Belgian voice network (Lechien, Harmegnies and the Liège/Ghent groups) whose later work fills in exactly the instruments and therapy trials this review says are missing: the French TWVQ [[morsomme2019]], the ambulatory biofeedback case [[morsomme2022]], and the Belgian needs survey [[papeleu2025b]].

## Notes
- Narrative review in a Francophone ENT journal, no new data; graded medium.
