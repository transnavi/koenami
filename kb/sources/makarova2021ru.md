# Проблема применения гормональной терапии, направленной на коррекцию пола, трансгендерными лицами по собственной инициативе

## What it says
- Data from 1117 transgender patients who applied to the «Научный центр персонализированной медицины» were analysed: 44.01% (n=515) trans women and 55.99% (n=630) trans men (abstract).
- "Half of transgender people (53.6%) are already taking hormonal drugs. More often transgender women (76.7%), less often transgender men (32.3%)" (abstract).
- «К эндокринологам за назначением терапии при этом обращались только 8,6% из них» — only 8.6% of those taking hormones had consulted an endocrinologist for the prescription (Russian abstract).
- "Many patients used unlicensed drugs, use irrational schemes and combinations, often overdose" (abstract).
- The authors conclude that a significant share of transgender people start hormone replacement on their own initiative without medical supervision, and propose better-informed doctors and patients plus counselling centres inside state medical institutions (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- Documents the Russian care context in which a trans woman's voice is worked on: for roughly nine in ten of the hormone users in this cohort the regimen was self-directed, so a trainer cannot assume any clinical supervision behind what the client is taking [[transgender-care-context]] [[hormones-and-voice]].
- The contrast between 76.7% of trans women and 32.3% of trans men taking hormones already reflects the asymmetry that makes voice work urgent for trans women: the masculinising voice change is a drug effect while no equivalent feminising drug exists [[hormones-and-voice]].

## Notes
- Single-centre cross-sectional analysis; the sample is people who sought care at one Moscow centre, not a population sample.
- The Russian and English abstracts are both in the PubMed record; the full text was not read.
