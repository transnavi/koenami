## What it says
- Tracked speaking fundamental frequency (SFF) longitudinally in 23 FTM/GID cases undergoing testosterone administration, recording relaxed vowel phonation at start, 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months.
- F0 dropped especially rapidly between month 1 and month 3; the decline remained significant through month 6, with only a slight, non-significant further decline from month 6 to month 12.
- Baseline SFF and the SFF at 6 months were significantly correlated.
- As pitch dropped, vocal folds showed redness and hypertrophy, and the laryngeal prominence (Adam's apple) became more prominent.
- The authors attribute the pitch drop to vocal-fold hypertrophy and enlargement of the laryngeal framework under androgen exposure.

## Bearing on voice training
- Gives a concrete timeline for testosterone-driven pitch lowering in trans men, useful for setting expectations: rapid change in months 1–3, most of the effect complete by 6 months.
- Confirms this is a hormone-only, endocrine/structural effect (framework and fold changes), not a trained behavior — contrasts with MTF voice feminization, where hormones alone do not raise pitch (see kobayashi2022, nakamura2007).

## Notes
- n=23, single-institution Japanese clinical sample; only vowel phonation was measured (no connected speech/prosody).
- Population: adult FTM/GID patients in Japan on standard testosterone regimens; findings may not generalize to other dosing protocols.
