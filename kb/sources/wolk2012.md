# Habitual use of vocal fry in young adult female speakers

## What it says
- 34 female college students aged 18–25, native Standard American English speakers, recorded on a sustained /a/ and a sentence-reading task, rated perceptually and analysed acoustically.
- "Approximately two-thirds of this population used vocal fry and that it was most likely to occur at the end of sentences."
- Fry differed significantly from modal register in mean F0 minimum, F0 maximum, F0 range and local jitter.
- The authors conclude that "use of the vocal fry register may be common in some adult SAE speakers."

## Bearing on voice training
- Fry at phrase ends is an ordinary feature of many young women's speech, not by itself a fault to be trained out. For a trans-feminine speaker it is one of the things that makes an English voice sound like a peer's rather than like a textbook.

## Notes
- Small, single-dialect, female-only sample; prevalence figures are not generalisable beyond young American college students. Compare [[yuasa2010]], who reads the same habit sociolinguistically.
