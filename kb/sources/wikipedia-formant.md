# Formant (English Wikipedia)

## What it says
- Summary endpoint, 2026-09-17: "In speech science and phonetics, a formant is the broad spectral maximum that results from an acoustic resonance of the human vocal tract. In acoustics, a formant is usually defined as a broad peak, or local maximum, in the spectrum."
- Two definitions, as the same extract distinguishes them: whether "formants" name the production mechanism (a tract resonance) or the produced sound (a peak in the spectrum). "In practice, the frequency of a spectral peak differs slightly from the associated resonance frequency, except when, by luck, harmonics are aligned with the resonance frequency, or when the sound source is mostly non-harmonic, as in whispering and vocal fry."
- For harmonic sounds, "the formant frequency is sometimes taken as that of the harmonic that is most augmented by a resonance."

## Bearing on voice training
- The sentence a spectrogram reader needs: a dark band on a wideband spectrogram is a formant (a tract resonance), not a harmonic. Harmonics move when F0 moves; formants move when the tract changes shape. Alignment of a harmonic with a formant is "by luck" in the article's wording — which is why pitch and 響き are trained as separate levers.
