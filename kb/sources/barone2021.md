# Acoustic and aerodynamic comparisons of voice qualities produced after voice training

## What it says
- "Characteristics of true vocal fold vibration such as the proportion of closed phase of vibration to open phase, longitudinal tension, and the amount of medial compression are used to define four conditions during Estill Voice Training. However, it is unknown whether trainees achieve these phonatory differences after training."
- 24 female speech-language pathology graduate students received training in perceiving and producing Slack, Thick, Thin and Stiff, volunteered 3–5 months later, had a 20-minute refresher, and were recorded on the Phonatory Aerodynamic System with electroglottography. Four EVT experts independently categorised the tokens; only productions agreed by at least three of four experts entered the acoustic/aerodynamic analysis.
- Slack: low F0, low SPL, high vibratory instability. Thick: high subglottal pressure, high SPL, high vibratory stability. Stiff: high airflow. Thin: lower subglottal pressure than Thick.
- "Seven measures differentiated the four qualities with 88.1% accuracy while only Psg, airflow, and jitter were required to differentiate Thick, Stiff, and Thin with 88.7% accuracy."
- Conclusion: the measures "differentiated among voice qualities and supported the theoretical physiological characteristics used in training."

## Bearing on voice training
- Puts numbers on Estill's source options: Thick versus Thin is a pressure-and-stability contrast (the "weight" dimension), Slack is the fry-like low-F0 unstable setting, Stiff is a high-flow setting. A trans-feminine target described as "thin folds" is, on this evidence, a lower-pressure, still-periodic production, not falsetto and not slack.

## Notes
- Women only, SLP students, productions classified by EVT experts so the labels are the model's own. Print January 2021, epub 3 September 2019.
