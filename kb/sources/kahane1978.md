# A morphological study of the human prepubertal and pubertal larynx

## What it says
- Ten male and ten female cadaveric larynges, ages 9–18, dissected and measured for linear and angular dimensions of the laryngeal cartilages and the weight of each cartilage (abstract).
- "With few exceptions, pubertal laryngeal measurements were significantly larger than prepubertal counterparts in both sexes" (abstract).
- Before puberty there is no clear dimorphism: "No clear sexual dimorphism was found between male and female prepubertal larynges though measurements of the prepubertal female larynx were closer to adult counterparts in size and weight than were those in the male. Thus the female larynx requires less growth per unit time to reach maturity than the male" (abstract).
- After puberty there is: "Linear and weight measurements of the pubertal male larynx were significantly larger than in the female" (abstract).
- On the laryngeal prominence: "Though the angle of the thyroid laminae was not significantly different in pubertal male and female cartilages, the thyroid eminence was clearly more prominent in the male" (abstract).
- On the folds: "The vocal folds in both sexes reached essentially their adult length by puberty; however, the absolute length of the male vocal folds had increased by over two times that of the female" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The anatomical basis of the asymmetry between transfeminine and transmasculine voice work: the male pubertal larynx is not a differently shaped organ so much as the same organ grown much further, and the growth in fold length is what the pitch difference rests on.
- The finding that thyroid-lamina *angle* did not differ significantly while the *eminence* did is worth keeping in mind when reading claims that the "Adam's apple" is simply a sharper cartilage angle ([[wikipedia-adams-apple]]).

## Notes
- Twenty cadaveric specimens, all described as Caucasian; small sample, one population, 1978 measurement methods.
