# Vocal tract area functions from magnetic resonance imaging

## What it says
- Acquired an inventory of three-dimensional vocal-tract air-space shapes by MRI for one male subject who vocalised while being scanned: 18 shapes covering 12 vowels, 3 nasals and 3 plosives (abstract).
- The 3-D shapes were reduced to **area functions** — cross-sectional area measured in planes always perpendicular to a centreline "extending from the glottis to the mouth" (abstract).
- Formants simulated from the measured area functions were reasonably similar to formants from the same speaker's recorded speech, "but suggest that the imaged vocal tract shapes may be somewhat centralized" (abstract) — an artefact of holding a posture inside a scanner.
- Comparison with four earlier studies showed general agreement in shape with obvious differences attributable to imaging technique, image processing and the anatomy of the individual imaged (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The measured link between tube shape and formant pattern: the tract is a series of cross-sections from glottis to lips, and every articulatory move a learner makes — larynx height, tongue position, lip spreading — is a change to that series.

## Notes
- One male speaker. The area functions are a catalogue for modelling, not population norms, and the paper says so.
