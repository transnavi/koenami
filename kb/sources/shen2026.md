## What it says
- Cross-sectional online survey (Prolific platform) of 313 transgender and gender-diverse (TGD) adults with voice concerns (mean age 26; 40% nonbinary, 28% trans women, 23% trans men) who completed the Trans Woman Voice Questionnaire (TWVQ), adapted TVQ Female-to-Male (TVQFtM), and/or Voice-related Experiences of Nonbinary Individuals (VENI); psychological well-being measured with the Scale of Positive and Negative Experience and Flourishing Scales.
- Gender-affirming care variables included mental health therapy (n=197), hormone therapy (n=118), counseling (n=87), speech therapy, and surgery (phonosurgery was rare, n=1).
- Participants who had received gender-care counseling had lower (better) TVQFtM scores than those still planning it (P = 0.03).
- Participants on hormone therapy had lower TWVQ and TVQFtM scores than those planning hormone therapy (P = 0.03 and P = 0.0002 respectively).
- Speech therapy engagement was associated with lower TWVQ and TVQFtM scores compared to those who only planned to pursue it.
- Participants interested in phonosurgery had higher (worse) TWVQ and TVQFtM scores than those not interested (P = 0.02 and P = 0.0004), suggesting these scores reflect motivation for surgery rather than an effect of surgery itself (given only 1 participant had actually had phonosurgery).
- Conclusion: TGD adults who had completed hormone therapy, counseling, and speech therapy reported better voice-related quality of life than those still deciding or planning; those planning phonosurgery had worse scores, consistent with unmet need motivating surgical interest.

## Bearing on voice training
- Notably uses adapted PROMs across the full range of gender-affirming voice goals (TWVQ for trans women, TVQFtM for trans men, VENI for nonbinary people), directly supporting a nonbinary/gender-diverse concept page, not just binary MTF/FTM framing.
- Confirms across a large (n=313), mixed TGD sample that hormone therapy, counseling, and speech therapy are each independently associated with better voice-related QoL — while phonosurgery interest (not completion, given n=1) marks unmet need rather than treatment failure.

## Notes
- Cross-sectional design cannot establish causation; associations may reflect selection (e.g., people who feel better already may be more likely to complete counseling/therapy) rather than treatment effect.
- Recruited via Prolific (online convenience panel), which may not represent clinical populations; one author (Misono) reports NIH funding as a declared interest.
