# Bilateral in-office injection laryngoplasty as an adjunctive treatment for recalcitrant puberphonia

## What it says
- "Puberphonia or mutational falsetto is a voice disorder seen in male adolescents. It is defined as the failure of the voice to change from the high pitch of early childhood to the low pitch of adulthood."
- "Puberphonia is usually treated with voice therapy (with or without adjunctive laryngeal manipulation) and psychological counseling. Small series of surgical treatments have also been described."
- Case: a 22-year-old man with puberphonia that had not responded to voice therapy received bilateral in-office injection laryngoplasty with hyaluronic acid plus continued voice therapy. Speaking F0 was 152 Hz at presentation, 102 Hz immediately after injection, and 108 Hz at 24 months.

## Bearing on voice training
- A documented instance of the first-line rule in [[adabi2026]]: therapy first, a procedure only when therapy has failed. The 50 Hz drop was produced by adding mass and closing the glottis, not by teaching a new register — so recalcitrant puberphonia is not always "just" an M2 habit.

## Notes
- One patient. Graded `low`. The authors call it the first report of this adjunct. Claims from the PubMed abstract.
