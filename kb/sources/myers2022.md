# Telepractice Considerations for Evaluation and Treatment of Voice Disorders: Tailoring to Specific Populations

## What it says
- Clinical focus article from the University of Utah Voice Disorders Center on implementing telepractice for voice evaluation and treatment, with three case examples: a pediatric client, a transgender client and a chronic cough client (abstract).
- The transgender client "showed increased habitual speaking fundamental frequency (pitch) and quality of life" after telepractice voice therapy; the pediatric client showed decreased perceptual strain and increased intelligibility; the chronic cough client improved vocal hygiene and cough severity (abstract).
- "A review of the literature shows comparable outcomes for in-person and telepractice voice therapy, but special considerations must be made to ensure therapeutic success"; in all three cases the clients' personal goals were met without travelling to clinic, and asynchronous practice opportunities emerged as a positive byproduct (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The service-delivery argument for remote gender-affirming voice work: same outcomes as in-person, and it removes the travel barrier that limits access in the survey data ([[ziltzer2023]]); [[lin2026]] supplies the controlled-protocol counterpart.
- Frames telepractice as a tailoring decision per population, not a blanket substitute — relevant to how [[speech-language-pathology]] services reach people outside clinic catchments.

## Notes
- Case examples, not a trial; the transgender case is a single client. The "comparable outcomes" claim is the authors' reading of the literature, cited at abstract level here.
