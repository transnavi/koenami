# Questionable Exercises (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Purpose stated on the page: "Below is a very incomplete list of just some of the vocal training methods that are either extremely questionable or downright dangerous. Each is ranked and information about how to avoid the risks if possible is included."
- Entry 1 — "Swallow and hold", rated "dangerous", "often used to raise the larynx": it "risks extreme constriction of muscles not associated with phonation including FVF", and can cause "strain, loss of vocal control, loss of closure, pain". The page adds that it "was recommended by some very old resources and occasionally SLPs".
- The advice for swallow-and-hold is categorical: "don't do it at all, ever". The distinction drawn: "swallowing is fine, holding is not. Learning what a larynx is with swallowing is fine, but it is absolutely paramount that you don't hold it and do not base your understanding of how the larynx can be moved on the sensations being felt or muscles used when swallowing."
- Entry 2 — "Glottal stop slide or any slide toward full closure", rated "risky": it "can encourage a conflation between FVF closure and true fold closure" and is "very, very hard to untrain if habitualised". Mitigations offered are "only do it under supervision of someone who knows what FVF closure glottal stops and strikes sound like", "don't add more pressure", and "use extremely light glottal taps to gain understanding of unconstricted true fold closure" — closing with "don't do it".

## Bearing on voice training
- Names a specific exercise the older literature and some clinicians have recommended for raising the larynx and marks it dangerous, which is a community source contradicting handed-down practice rather than repeating it [[voice-health]] [[larynx-anatomy]].
- The FVF-versus-true-fold-closure confusion is the wiki's recurring distinction — it defines "the" problem with glottal exercises as teaching the wrong closure, and adds that the error is hard to reverse once habitual [[voice-therapy-techniques]] [[vocal-fatigue]].
- The swallow test survives in a restricted form: swallow to learn *where* the larynx is, never as a posture to hold.

## Notes
- Volunteer wiki, "under construction"; the page is described as "a very incomplete list" and "an evolving document". Ratings ("dangerous", "risky") are the wiki's own, not a clinical grading. The vault's clinical sources treat larynx-raising exercises as ordinary technique without this warning [[carew2007]]; the two can be read together but they are not the same claim.
