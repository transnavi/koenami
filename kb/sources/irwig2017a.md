# Effects of testosterone on the transgender male voice

## What it says
- Prospective 12-month study at an academic endocrinology clinic and speech and voice center: seven transgender men naïve to testosterone, two baseline voice assessments and one each at 3, 6, 9 and 12 months on intramuscular testosterone esters, with serum testosterone and estradiol measured every 3 months (abstract).
- "All seven transgender men reached a cisgender male mean fundamental frequency (MF0) within 6 months of testosterone therapy and four continued to experience a decrease after 6 months. The mean decrease in MF0 after 12 months of testosterone therapy was 6.4 semitones (49 Hz)" (abstract).
- Timing varied sharply between individuals: some showed no MF0 decrease in the first 3 months while others showed their greatest decrease then (abstract).
- Counselling implication drawn by the authors: trans men "may or may not experience voice lowering within the first 3 months of testosterone therapy and … the majority of voice deepening will occur within 6–9 months" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The cleanest small-sample time course for testosterone's pitch drop: most of the deepening lands in months 3–9, and silence in the first three months is not treatment failure — useful expectation-setting in [[voice-masculinization]].
- Six semitones is the order of magnitude of the drop; consistent with the abrupt 3–6 month fall in [[nimura2015]] and the group-level 12-month male range in [[nygren2016]].

## Notes
- n = 7, single centre; individual trajectories are the point of the paper, not population estimates (those come from [[ziegler2018]]).
