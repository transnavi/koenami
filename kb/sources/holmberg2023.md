# Exploring Motives and Perceived Barriers for Voice Modification: The Views of Transgender and Gender-Diverse Voice Clients

## What it says
- Swedish interview study (Umeå University with the Karolinska Institutet speech-language pathology group): semistructured individual interviews with 15 transgender and gender-diverse voice clients considering voice therapy, analysed by qualitative content analysis (abstract, "METHOD").
- The abstract opens from a gap: clients' perceptions and individual goals "have been missing in discussions and research on gender-affirming voice therapy", so little is known about expectations of outcomes "developed from views of vocal gender as perceived by cisgender persons".
- Three themes: "the incongruent voice setting the rules" (voice and gender dysphoria), "to reach a voice of my own choice" (anticipated gains), and "a voice out of reach" (worries and restricting factors).
- Conclusion: a need for person-centred voice therapy that starts from the client's expressed motives while being "affirmative of anticipated difficulties", with the themes proposed as the basis of the pre-therapy joint discussion between client and speech-language pathologist.

## Bearing on voice training
- The Swedish line this vault's notes contrast with Brazil's explicit "passabilidade" goal [[dornelas2021]]: therapy goals are the client's own choices, not a passing standard set by cisgender listeners.
- Gives the qualitative counterpart to the person-centred outcome measures argued for in [[patient-reported-outcomes]].

## Notes
- Qualitative study of 15 clients in northern Sweden who were still considering therapy — motives before therapy, not experiences after it; no claims about acoustic outcomes.
