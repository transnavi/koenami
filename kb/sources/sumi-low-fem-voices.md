# How to do Low Fem Voices (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Premise: "While pitch itself is not a gendered factor there are several things that are conflated with it that we need to control to achieve a low pitch feminine voice."
- **Vocal weight** — "much harder to keep light at a low pitch. To achieve a low pitch feminine voice, we need a very high level of control over vocal weight. This can be done by slowly creeping the pitch down while keeping an eye on the vocal weight." The protocol given: "start at 200hz, slide down to 180 and speak, then slide down to 160 and speak, then 140 and then 120. It's important to speak at each step because a solid tone like 'aaaa' can sound ambiguous, but speech contains much more intricacy and will be more obvious if we were to get heavier."
- **Resonance** — "it is often the case that when coming down to a low pitch, we go too low in resonance as well - the larynx drops down. A good way to combat this is to simply redrill resonance at the low pitch, and try to keep the larynx fairly high when dropping in pitch." The page adds that unvoiced resonance slides tend to drift back up in pitch, so "it can be best to redrill resonance voiced", and warns that "some people link resonance with vocal weight as well, meaning that when we add resonance at a low pitch we're more likely to add vocal weight as well".
- **Microbehaviours** — "At a high pitch, things like microbehaviours and speech patterns are slightly less important because all the other large scale components of voice, like vocal weight and resonance are easier to maintain and somewhat cover up any inconsistencies. That is to say, a typically feminine low vocal weight high resonance voice at a high pitch with masculine speech patterns and microbehaviours ... will still pass as feminine. The more stress on the configuration we add, by going low in pitch or higher in vocal weight or lower in resonance, the more these features (like speech patterns and microbehaviours) matter." Concluded as: "while microbehaviours don't matter much in typically feminine voices, they do matter a lot in atypical voices."
- An upside is claimed for the difficult case: "Learning to do a low pitch feminine voice or a high vocal weight one can actually help significantly with getting a more natural configuration, because we are forced to learn more feminine speech patterns and microbehaviours."
- Two reference voices are given with the wiki's own five-point rating scale (vocal weight, resonance, pitch, difficulty): **imawonder** — "very bright, slightly heavy, low pitch", pitch "low (150hz - 250hz intonation, ~500hz peaks)", difficulty "medium hard (bright, buzzy, high intonation peaks)"; **Nurse** — "very low pitch, smooth, deep fem, low resonance", "very low (120 - 200hz)", difficulty "hard (good glottal behaviour at very very low pitch)".

## Bearing on voice training
- Sets out the community's diagnosis of why low-pitched feminine voices are hard: weight and larynx height are conflated with pitch, so descending drags the other variables down with it [[vocal-weight]] [[formants]].
- The claim that the small cues matter *more* in atypical voices is the wiki's explanation for why some people pass easily at 200 Hz and struggle at 130 Hz with the same technique [[intonation]] [[sumi-microbehaviours]] [[female-voice]].
- The rated voice examples are a community practice worth recording: real speakers used as reference targets with named acoustic ranges rather than as ideals [[community-training]].
