# Perceptual-Auditory and Acoustical Analysis of the Voices of Transgender Women

## What it says
- Case-control study at the Gender Identity Program of the Hospital de Clínicas de Porto Alegre, participants aged 19–52 (abstract). Recordings were a sustained /a:/ and six CAPE-V phrases, edited, randomly coded and stored before rating.
- The /a:/ recordings were rated by consensus on the same day by two speech therapists with more than ten years in voice, using the GRBASI scale; acoustic analysis used the advanced Multi-Dimensional Voice Program. Resonance focus and degrees of masculinity and femininity were judged on the CAPE-V phrases by the same two raters.
- Significant differences: more trans women than controls had F0 between 80 and 150 Hz (P = 0.003); hypernasal resonance focus was more frequent in the trans women (P < 0.001); absence of roughness was more frequent in the control group (P = 0.031).
- Summary: 「The MtF group of individuals showed altered vertical resonant focus, more masculine voices, and lower fundamental frequencies.」
- The paper's opening framing: voice is 「an important gender marker in the transition process as a transgender individual accepts a new gender identity」.

## Bearing on voice training
- The resonance finding is the one to carry: altered *vertical* focus and hypernasality, not only low pitch, distinguished this group perceptually ([[formants]], [[voice-quality]]). It is the Brazilian counterpart of the resonance-first emphasis in [[carew2007]] and [[leyns2023]].
- Consensus rating by two experienced therapists rather than independent raters means no inter-rater reliability is reported — the same limitation the vault notes for [[irineu2025]].
- Together with the acoustic-only analysis of a same-sized sample from the same programme, [[villasboas2021]], this is the fullest Brazilian baseline for what a trans-women clinical cohort sounds like: low F0, altered resonance, but *less* roughness than controls.

## Notes
- Single centre, southern Brazil; participants were current attenders at a gender programme, so treatment status (hormones, prior therapy) is not held constant in the abstract. No therapy was given as part of the study.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract; the full text's tables were not read.
