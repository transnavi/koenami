## What it says
- Retrospective analysis of 296 patients (293 transgender women, 3 cases of pseudohermaphroditism) who underwent transoral microscopic vocal cord shortening with anterior commissure retrodisplacement at the Voice Center of Nanjing Tongren Hospital, November 2014–August 2023. Ages 18–50, mean 28.3±14.5.
- Procedure: resection of the superficial lamina propria of the anterior vocal cords and subglottic tissue at the anterior commissure, followed by vocal ligament plication to shorten the cords and retrodisplace the anterior commissure. One month of voice rest followed by speech rehabilitation training.
- At 1-year follow-up, 94.3% (279/296) of patients reported satisfaction.
- For effective cases, fundamental frequency (F0) increased by 57.42±16.72 Hz compared with baseline.
- Postoperative acoustic/aerodynamic parameters and stroboscopic vocal-fold mucosal wave findings remained within normal ranges.
- Conclusion (authors'): the combination of vocal cord shortening, anterior commissure retrodisplacement, and postoperative pronunciation training is an effective surgical strategy for a natural feminine voice.

## Bearing on voice training
- Documents a large single-center Chinese surgical case series pairing phonosurgery with mandatory postoperative speech rehabilitation — surgery alone was not the endpoint; training was built into the protocol.
- The 57.42±16.72 Hz mean F0 gain gives a concrete magnitude for what this specific technique adds on top of (presumably) any voice training effect, though the study does not isolate surgery-only vs. training-only contributions.

## Notes
- Single-center retrospective series, no control group, no separate reporting of surgery-only vs. surgery+training subgroups; "94.3% satisfaction" is unspecified in the CSL/abstract as to instrument used.
- Population: Mandarin-speaking, mainland China (Nanjing).
- Abstract read via PubMed (English translation + original Chinese abstract as published by the journal); full text not accessed.
