# Real-Time Resonance Biofeedback for Gender-Affirming Voice Training: Usability Testing of the TruVox Web-Based Application

## What it says
- The resonance module of TruVox, a project to build free gender-affirming voice training software; it "represents resonant frequencies of the vocal tract as peaks in a real-time linear predictive coding spectrum", with built-in targets for English vowels, a practice routine of utterances of increasing length, and video tutorials (abstract, "Materials and Methods").
- Motivation stated in the abstract: training "can reduce voice dysphoria for some transgender/gender-diverse individuals, but access is often limited by factors such as high cost and a shortage of well-trained providers", and "existing applications are limited in functionality, particularly in the area of resonance".
- Online and in-lab usability testing; participants completed the System Usability Scale (SUS), qualitative feedback, and in the lab the Intrinsic Motivation Inventory plus vowel targets recorded at the start and end of one session.
- In-lab SUS averaged 75.25 — "acceptable-to-good usability". Users reported the visual-acoustic feedback "helped them understand and control their vocal resonance" and named usability problems.
- "While little acoustic change was expected in light of the short duration of the session, 8/10 participants showed a trend in the expected direction for the vowel that was practiced most extensively."
- Stated future work: refining the automatic resonance tracking algorithm, letting users set individual targets from their own baseline, and "adding targets for nonbinary and transmasculine users".

## Bearing on voice training
- A free, browser-based resonance visualiser built on LPC spectra — the acoustic display that community practice usually has to infer by ear [[tools]] [[formants]].
- The research team's own framing admits the gap the tool fills: resonance feedback is the part of the market existing apps lacked [[community-training]].

## Notes
- Usability, not efficacy: one session per participant, 10 in-lab, and the acoustic result is a trend in 8/10, not a controlled comparison. The abstract does not give the online participant count, the participants' languages, or their prior training.
- Competing-interest note in the record: co-author McAllister is a member of Sonority Labs LLC, which explores commercialisation of a related biofeedback app (staRt); the record states the TruVox code base is not licensed to that company and no GAVT commercialisation is planned.
