# Pitch Naturalisation / PIPM (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Described on the page as "A method for reducing vocal weight and achieving overall more consistent and clear glottal behavior." The vocal-weight page lists it as "PIPM / Pitch Naturalisation"; the page itself uses the name "Pitch Naturalisation" and does not expand the acronym.
- The method's central instruction is to stop controlling individual features: "Forget singular aspects such as vocal weight, resonance etc. The more you focus on one aspect and try to achieve it, the less this method will work ... Your conscious mind will ruin it, so instead, let your voice naturally and automatically adjust and naturalise. The only parts that are consciously controlled are the very beginning and the times we change pitch intentionally, the rest should be on autopilot."
- Stated rationale (in a collapsed background section the wiki marks as optional reading): "Pitch happens to be conflated positively with almost every aspect of voice feminization to a small degree ... It's important to note that pitch itself is mostly irrelevant to the 'quality' or the gender of the voice. Instead it is a powerful tool to be used to make the changes we want. The goal is not to 'raise pitch', but to improve the voice at every pitch."
- The goal is framed as a sound, not a sensation: "Your goal is a sound! Only focus on 'does this sound closer to my goal'. Physical feelings only happen when there's a problem such as constriction or pressing, so avoid trying to make physical movements."
- The main exercise ("floating"): speak at your relaxed, not-doing-anything pitch, "possibly a little bit louder and heavier than normal"; hold the last word of each phrase longer and monotone so the base pitch is audible; when comfortable, "let that last word and note float up a tiny bit, raising the whole voice"; repeat but "don't push the pitch too high - the goal is to naturalize all of your range, not raise pitch". Warnings attached: "be extremely patient; if you rush or try too hard to control things, it won't work"; "lean toward less airflow but moderately loud"; and — "going up in pitch should be extremely easy, suspiciously so; if not, spend more time letting it naturalise it here or start again".
- A second exercise, "pitch naturalisation at a set pitch", picks a base pitch that "should be easy to do in your heavier, more masculine voice", slides up to it and speaks a monotone phrase, then the next phrase starts higher and arrives back at the base pitch; it is marked as more advanced and also "can be used to bring a lighter voice down low in pitch for deep feminine voices, once enough skill is learned".
- Part two adds a brightness exercise, "dark to bright naturalisation": start at a medium pitch with a deep, dark, yawn-like voice, speak a passage and let it become brighter; "making changes slowly is better than having it jump suddenly", and if there is not enough range, the wiki sends the reader back to the unvoiced and voiced resonance exercises first.

## Bearing on voice training
- The community's attempt at a whole-voice coordination exercise instead of a single-feature drill, on the argument that pitch is conflated with the other feminisation features [[voice-therapy-techniques]] [[fundamental-frequency]] [[community-training]].
- Its stated purpose is weight reduction, not pitch elevation — "the goal is not to 'raise pitch', but to improve the voice at every pitch" — which is why it is listed among the lightening tools on the vocal-weight page [[vocal-weight]].
- The "goal is a sound, not a feeling" rule is the wiki's own answer to the problem its terminology page describes, where learners chase physical sensations and add constriction [[sumi-questionable-terms]] [[voice-health]].
