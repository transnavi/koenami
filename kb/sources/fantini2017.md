# Estill Voice Training and voice quality control in contemporary commercial singing

## What it says
- "Estill Voice Training (EVT) is a widely known programme for developing vocal skills based on partitioning the process of vocal production in order to reach control of specific structures in the vocal mechanism."
- Retrospective exploratory comparison: 35 contemporary commercial (pop and rock) singers with no vocal complaints. Twenty had studied EVT and held a Certificate of Figure Proficiency; fifteen had studied at Italian contemporary popular-music institutions but were not familiar with EVT.
- Voice-quality control was tested on "a specific vocal exercise requiring sound pitch, perturbation and spectral energy distribution control."
- "The acoustic analysis showed some significant differences between the two groups of singers both in sound perturbation control and spectral energy distribution control, suggesting a higher voice quality control ability for the experimental group. The perceptual evaluation confirmed a higher ability for the experimental group to produce recognizable voice qualities in this specific task."
- Conclusion: the results "seem to suggest EVT as an effective educational system for developing voice quality control ability in contemporary commercial singers."

## Bearing on voice training
- The first peer-reviewed comparison of EVT-trained versus otherwise-trained CCM singers on a quality-control task. It supports the claim that the figures can be heard as distinct qualities, which is why trans voice teachers borrow thick/thin, twang and larynx-height as isolable options rather than as a single "feminine setting".

## Notes
- Not randomised; EVT holders versus a convenience control; one task; Italian CCM singers only. Graded `medium` as a controlled comparison with no random assignment. Print 2017, epub 30 September 2016.
