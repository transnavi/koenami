## What it says
- Prior research (cited within the paper) showed listeners' identification of English anterior sibilant fricatives (the /s/–/ʃ/ boundary) shifts depending on whether listeners are primed to believe the talker is a woman or a man — i.e., the same acoustic sibilant is categorized differently depending on assumed speaker gender.
- This article tests how stable that gender-priming effect is across two different priming methods (the crossref/openalex abstract text available is truncated after describing a nine-step sibilant identification continuum; full method/results were not retrievable from the indexed abstract).
- Established finding referenced: sibilant categorization is not purely acoustic — listener beliefs about speaker gender shift the perceptual boundary between /s/ and /ʃ/.

## Bearing on voice training
- Demonstrates listener gender-priming can shift phoneme-level sibilant perception, which is directly relevant to sibilant/articulation training in gendered voice work: how a trainee's /s/ is categorized by listeners may depend partly on other gendered cues (pitch, visual presentation) the listener has already picked up on, not solely on the acoustic sibilant itself.
- Motivates caution in trainer feedback — a sibilant judged "too masculine-sounding" in isolation might be perceived differently once embedded in a voice already carrying other feminine cues.
