# 声のはなし

A self-taught speaker describing how they learned to keep a high 地声 alongside a low male voice, starting from childhood.

## What it says
- The origin, in the writer's own account: from kindergarten they were 「高い声を出そう出そうとして」, continuing to cling to a high voice through 変声期 — 「小学生、中学生と年齢が上がり声変わりが始まって高校生になっても、高い声を出し続けることに執着してました」. The result claimed: 「現在は、低い男性の声と高い女声の両方を出せるようになり、日常生活で声で困ることがない」.
- The method, offered as self-taught and unverified: 「まず最初に、裏声を完璧に出せるようにひたすら練習します。喉仏を高い位置に固定する感覚を覚えたり、喉の筋肉を鍛えるためです。」 Then «次に裏声を出せるようになったら、徐々に声を低く出していって、裏声と地声になる境目、つまり裏返る瞬間を覚えます。この裏返る瞬間があなたの出せる、地声の一番高い音程になると思います。» And finally «あとは裏返る声をひたすら出して、なるべく自然な女声に聞こえる音程を探します» — with recording yourself as the recommended check.
- The stated cost of the method for singing: 「高い声を裏声ではなく女の地声で出そうとすると、音域がかなり狭くなるため歌えない」 — the high 地声 is usable for conversation but not for melody, and the writer declines to train the low voice for singing because 「低い声で歌えるようになっても嬉しくない」.
- Where the wide range is useful, as reported: disclosing in LGBT bars by using the low voice so others recognise them as MTF, and being received well by dates.

## Bearing on voice training
- A first-person account of the route the Japanese community calls keeping a high 地声: train M2 to stability, find the break, then press down into it until it becomes a modal-quality high register [[vocal-registers]] [[japanese-voice]].
- Its own limitation is the interesting part — the trained high register has a narrow range and cannot carry melody, which is the register/range trade-off the singing literature also describes [[singing-and-voice]] [[pitch-and-scales]].
- The writer's practice of recording to check how it sounds to others matches the advice the English wiki gives for the same reason [[sumi-how-to-practice]] [[community-training]].
