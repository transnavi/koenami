# Perceiving physiology from the voice: laryngeal and epilaryngeal configurations in the Estill Voice Model

## What it says
- Motivation: behavioural voice therapy "often rel[ies] on auditory perceptual prompts and oral sensations"; because those "vary depending on listeners' experience", the authors propose describing voices by "the underlying anatomical and physiological configurations that are used to produce them" in the framework of the Estill Voice Model.
- Result, as stated in the abstract: "with training focused on both physiology and auditory perception of the voice, listeners are able to perceive laryngeal and epilaryngeal configurations with an accuracy much higher than the chance level. However, our results also show that some physiological combinations are more challenging to perceive than others, which implies either anatomical or physiological constraints or conflicting/redundant acoustic consequences."
- Competing-interest note on the PubMed record: Kimberly Steinhauer reports financial support from Estill Voice International; Zhaoyan Zhang reports NIDCD support.

## Bearing on voice training
- Evidence that Estill's structure labels (larynx height, epilarynx narrowing, and so on) can be heard, after training, at above chance — which is the claim that makes "twang the aryepiglottic sphincter" a teachable instruction rather than a metaphor. Some combinations remain hard to hear, so not every figure is equally available as an ear-target.
