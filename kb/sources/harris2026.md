# First-Person Perspectives of Gender-Affirming Voice Feminization Training: A Mixed Methods Approach

## What it says
- Numeric and qualitative survey data from 21 people who had done gender-affirming voice training for feminisation, plus follow-up interviews with five of them (abstract, "Methods").
- Clients "are satisfied with GAVT supporting voice feminization, perceive their clinicians as being culturally inclusive, and identify oral/forward resonance work as one of the most helpful voice training methods".
- The counterweight the study reports against itself: "frequency of work on oral/forward resonance during training did not predict voice satisfaction post-training".
- The authors argue the result "emphasize[s] the importance and value of client-led outcome measures" in this field.

## Bearing on voice training
- Clients themselves rank forward/oral resonance first among what helped, which is the community's ranking too [[community-training]]; the same study shows how often it was practised does not predict satisfaction, so dose is not the mechanism anyone has demonstrated [[voice-therapy-techniques]].
- Mixed-methods design gives the field a template for pairing acoustic outcomes with what clients say mattered [[patient-reported-outcomes]].

## Notes
- n = 21 survey, 5 interviews; the first and third authors declare having provided gender-affirming voice services to some participants (the record gives conflicting counts, three and five), and those participants were excluded from the interviews. Emerson College affiliations, so presumably a US sample; self-report measures only, no acoustic data.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract (J Voice 2026 Jul;40(4); Epub 2024 Apr 30).
