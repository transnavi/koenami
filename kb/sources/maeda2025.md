## What it says
- Retrospective study of 15 assigned-male-at-birth patients who underwent cricothyroid approximation (CTA) under local anesthesia with intraoperative pitch monitoring at a single Japanese institution (2019–2024), all by the same surgeon.
- Standard fundamental frequency (SFF) was measured preoperatively, intraoperatively, and at 1, 3, and 6 months postoperatively: 152.1 ± 25.2 Hz preoperatively, 343.4 ± 62.8 Hz intraoperatively, 249.9 ± 40.1 Hz at 1 month, 225.9 ± 26.4 Hz at 3 months, and 231.3 ± 29.8 Hz at 6 months.
- Intraoperative SFF was significantly higher than preoperative SFF (P < 0.05); SFF fell significantly from intraoperative to 1 month, and again from 1 month to 3 months, but not significantly from 3 to 6 months (P = 0.26).
- Compared with preoperative baseline, SFF remained significantly elevated at 6 months (P < 0.05), with the intraoperative value settling to roughly 112 Hz lower by 6 months postoperatively.
- Concludes that performing CTA under local anesthesia with intraoperative voice monitoring is useful because the surgeon can deliberately target an intraoperative SFF higher than the desired final SFF, anticipating the expected postoperative decline (approximately 100 Hz).

## Bearing on voice training
- Gives a specific, quantified account of how much CTA pitch gain is lost between the operating table and 6-month stabilization (roughly 100–112 Hz drop from the intraoperative peak), which is directly useful for setting patient expectations and explains variability in "CTA outcome" numbers across different studies depending on measurement timing.
- Local-anesthesia, awake-monitoring technique is a distinct procedural variant worth distinguishing from general-anesthesia CTA when comparing outcome studies in this cluster (e.g., mora2018, vanborsel2008).

## Notes
- n=15, single surgeon, single institution in Japan; retrospective design, no comparison group operated under general anesthesia within the same study.
