# What more can be done to popularize phonosurgical ideas in everyday handling of vocal folds?

## What it says
- States the aim as translating "the layered structure and physiology of the vocal fold described in Hirano's classic body-cover model into the graphic illustration of the 'fluttering sleeve' created by Dikkers" (abstract) — a teaching image in which the pliable cover moves over a comparatively fixed body like a loose sleeve over an arm.
- Argues that in countries where phonosurgery is new, "simple patterns and plain associations will serve the best in popularizing sophisticated vocal fold anatomy and preservation surgery" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- Cited here for one thing only: a fetched, refereed statement attributing the body–cover description of the vocal fold to Hirano ([[hirano1974]]), whose own text is paywalled and abstract-less. The "fluttering sleeve" is also the most usable image for explaining the cover to a beginner.
