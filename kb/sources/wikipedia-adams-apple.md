# Adam's apple (English Wikipedia)

## What it says
- Summary endpoint, 2026-09-17: "The Adam's apple is the protrusion in the neck formed by the angle of the thyroid cartilage surrounding the larynx, typically visible in men and less so in women. The prominence of the Adam's apple increases in most men as a secondary sex characteristic during puberty."
- Short description: "Feature of the neck."

## Bearing on voice training
- The visible landmark most people use to find their own larynx, and the one whose growth is the outward sign of the change described in [[puberty-voice-change]].
- The article attributes the protrusion to "the angle of the thyroid cartilage". Measured data complicate that: in pubertal cadaveric larynges the thyroid-lamina angle did not differ significantly between the sexes while the eminence itself was clearly more prominent in males [[kahane1978]]. Prefer the measurement over the encyclopedia's one-line explanation.

## Limits
- Community-edited; the anatomical explanation in the summary is simplified, as noted above. The French title uses the clinical term (proéminence laryngée) rather than the folk name.
