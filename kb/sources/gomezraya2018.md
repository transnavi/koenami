# Intervención logopédica en la feminización de la voz en transexuales: revisión bibliográfica

## What it says
- Descriptive bibliographic review of speech-therapy work in the feminisation of the voice for trans women, searched in PubMed and Scopus; 「a list of 25 articles is obtained from each of them」, then filtered, leaving seven final articles (abstract).
- The framing is explicit that therapy is being reviewed 「as a single treatment not complementary to surgical intervention」 — as a treatment on its own, not an adjunct to surgery.
- Its premise about hormones: 「The transsexual woman will not achieve the feminization of her voice through the hormonal treatment」, leaving vocal-cord surgery and/or speech therapy as the options.
- Results: 「all the articles conclude with positive results of the vocal therapy in transsexual women, especially emphasizing the objectives related to the increase of the fundamental frequency and the increase of the forming frequencies [formant frequencies]」.
- The stated conclusion keeps its own scale in view: 「there is enough information to show that speech therapy can be effective in the feminization of voice in transsexual population. However, the limited number of articles hinder universal generalizations.」

## Bearing on voice training
- The Spanish-language review of therapy-alone, and it names both levers — F0 **and** formants — as the goals the included studies reported ([[fundamental-frequency]], [[formants]]). That is the source-filter pairing the Spanish clinical lineage in [[casado2016]] and [[casadomorente2024]] works both sides of.
- Seven articles is a small base, and the author says so. Registered here as the Spanish counterpart of [[leyns2021]] and the older [[dacakis2013]].

## Notes
- A review with a stated search but no formal appraisal instrument or quality rating; graded medium for the peer-reviewed design and the transparent screening, not for the strength of the underlying studies.
- Claims taken from the abstract; the seven included studies, and whether any measured listener outcomes rather than acoustic targets, were not read.
