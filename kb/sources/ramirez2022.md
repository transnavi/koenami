## What it says
- Mexican cross-sectional study of 26 trans women seeking voice feminisation; they completed quality-of-life questionnaires including the Trans Woman Voice Questionnaire and stated their reasons for seeking treatment, and their fundamental frequency was measured (abstract).
- 77% were legally recognised as women, 96% were on hormone therapy, 27% had had gender-affirming surgery.
- Median f0 was 131 Hz; f0 correlated poorly with the quality-of-life measures. Mean TWVQ score was 95 (SD 14.3).
- "None of the participants perceived their voice as feminine while 97% described their ideal voice as feminine"; achieving a feminine voice that allows gender conformity was the main stated reason for seeking treatment.
- States that "hormonal replacement treatment (HRT) in TW does not play a role in voice feminization" and that "access to voice feminization procedures is limited while the population demanding transgender healthcare is increasing".
- Concludes that "improving access to affirmation procedures in public institutions without pathologization of transgender people is a pending agenda" and that it remains difficult to define how to measure success.

## Bearing on voice training
- One of the few Latin American datasets pairing a measured f0 with a validated voice questionnaire; the poor f0–quality-of-life correlation echoes [[hancock2011]] and [[quinn2021]] and argues against pitch as a sole outcome.
- Documents a public-provision gap in Mexico, relevant when reading Spanish-language self-training material as a substitute for clinical care ([[community-training]]).

## Notes
- n = 26, single clinic, self-selected for wanting voice feminisation, so not a population sample of trans women in Mexico.
- The abstract does not report which Spanish TWVQ version was used or how it was translated.
