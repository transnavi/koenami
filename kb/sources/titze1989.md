# Physiologic and acoustic differences between male and female voices

## What it says
- Compares male and female larynges by overall size, membranous vocal-fold length, tissue elasticity and prephonatory glottal shape.
- Proposes two scale factors: fundamental frequency scales primarily with membranous vocal-fold length (factor 1.6); airflow, sound power, glottal efficiency and vibration amplitude include a second factor (1.2) tied to overall larynx size.
- Modelled contact area suggests the female glottis converges more linearly from bottom to top; the male vocal folds bulge medially.

## Bearing on voice training
- Why pitch differs by sex is mostly vocal-fold length; why "weight"/closure differs relates to fold shape — two separate things to train.
