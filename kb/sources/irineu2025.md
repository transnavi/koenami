# Vocal Markers in the Gender Perception of Trans Women and Trans Men

## What it says
- Brazilian observational cross-sectional study: 30 trans women and 23 trans men aged 18–43, recorded on a sustained /a/ and on connected speech (counting, days of the week). Two judges rated by consensus using GRBASI plus pitch, loudness, resonance, articulation, intonation and gender perception; Praat extracted f0, f0 deviation, f0min/f0max and F1–F4 (abstract, "Methods").
- Average f0 was 146.289 Hz for the trans women and 157.409 Hz for the trans men — the two groups overlapped heavily, i.e. many trans men were speaking above the trans women's average.
- For trans women, gender perception was related to pitch (P = 0.013), articulation (P = 0.017) and intonation (P = 0.000). "Ascending intonation was an auditory-perceptual predictor (P = 0.001)" of gender congruence in connected speech.
- For trans men, gender perception was related to hormone use (P = 0.016), the GRBASI tension parameter (P = 0.028), pitch (P = 0.001), loudness (P = 0.033), intonation (P = 0.001), f0 (P = 0.034), f0min (P = 0.029), f0max (P = 0.018) and F1 (P = 0.038); F1 was the acoustic predictor (P = 0.050), again in connected speech.
- "Most of the trans women's voices were perceived as feminine, even when they had a low pitch."
- Masculine markers named for the trans men: "more tense vocal quality, descending intonations, and average f(o) in the range considered masculine".

## Bearing on voice training
- Intonation direction — ascending for feminine, descending for masculine — is a predictor here, which is the perceptual counterpart of the Ghent intonation trials [[papeleu2025]] [[papeleu2026]] [[intonation]].
- The trans-women result contradicts a simple pitch threshold: low-pitched voices still read feminine. Articulation and intonation carried the judgement [[articulation]] [[gender-perception]].
- For trans men, voice *quality* (GRBASI tension) appears as a marker, not only F0 — a reminder that testosterone changes more than pitch [[male-voice]] [[hormones-and-voice]].

## Notes
- n = 53, single centre in Sergipe/Paraná, Brazil; judges rated in consensus rather than independently, so no inter-rater reliability is reported in the abstract. Trans men on hormone therapy were included, and hormone use itself predicts their ratings — an unavoidable confound in a cross-sectional design.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
