# TruVox Web-Based Software for Vocal Pitch Training in Transgender Women

## What it says
- Free web platform (University of Cincinnati) combining "real-time feedback about the user's voice with structured vocal pitch exercises for transfeminine people"; the prototype has 5 structured pitch exercises with real-time pitch visualisations, plus supporting videos and text (abstract, "Methods").
- Motivation: distress from a mismatch between gender and how the voice is perceived "significantly reduces their mental health and quality of life", and training "is often inaccessible due to factors such as price and geographical constraints".
- Two evaluation stages: remote usability testing with iterative bug fixes, then a single-session in-person evaluation with 21 transfeminine participants supervised by two researchers.
- Results: SUS mean 79.8 (SD 12.8) out of 100; Intrinsic Motivation Inventory scores high ("interest/enjoyment over 11/14"); "exercise performance significantly improved in all but 1 exercise (P values ranging from below .001 to .095)" across 10 repetitions of each exercise.
- Qualitative requests: ability to use the software "without much preparation", and future features such as performance tracking and goal-setting.
- The authors position it as "the first GAVT software that combines real-time voice visualization with structured exercises" and "the first quantitative human subjects evaluation of GAVT software"; they state plainly that the pitch module "should not be considered a complete GAVT package".

## Bearing on voice training
- Single-session *practice* improvement is what this measures — within-session repetition, not training transfer — so it is evidence about a practice tool's usability, not about whether the voice feminises [[tools]] [[fundamental-frequency]].
- The fairness point is the motivation: cost and geography are named as the access barriers that free software can address [[community-training]] [[voice-feminization-therapy]].

## Notes
- n = 21 in the in-person evaluation; developers evaluated their own software (co-authors are TruVox developers, and one acted as evaluator). No control group, no follow-up, no acoustic outcome beyond exercise performance.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract (JMIR Form Res 2025 Sep 15;9:e73841).
