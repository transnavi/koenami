# Falsetto (English Wikipedia)

## What it says
- Summary endpoint, 2026-09-17: "Falsetto is the vocal register occupying the frequency range just above the modal voice register and overlapping with it by approximately one octave. It is produced by the vibration of the ligamentous edges of the vocal cords, in whole or in part. Commonly cited in the context of singing, falsetto, a characteristic of phonation by both male and female sexes, is also one of four main spoken vocal registers recognized by speech pathology."
- Two facts the training literature needs: falsetto overlaps modal rather than sitting cleanly above it, and it is available to any larynx, not a male-only trick.

## Bearing on voice training
- The overlap of about an octave is the same claim [[roubeau2009]] makes for M1 and M2: a speaking pitch can be produced in either mechanism. "Speak in falsetto" is therefore not a pitch instruction.
- "Both male and female sexes" is the encyclopedia's wording of the point [[herbst2020]] argues from anatomy: producing M2 does not make a voice feminine.

## Limits
- Community-edited. The "ligamentous edges" mechanism is the article's, standing in for the imaging papers ([[echternach2010]], [[herbst2020]]).
