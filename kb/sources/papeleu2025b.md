# Voice-related challenges experienced by transgender and gender diverse individuals in Belgium（Papeleu et al. 2025）

## What it says
- Design and method: "Using a community-based participatory approach, a questionnaire was co-developed with TGD individuals, assessing voice satisfaction, social impacts, and access to voice services" (abstract). The instrument was built with the population rather than for it.
- Who answered: 197 TGD individuals — transfeminine 108, transmasculine 29, non-binary presumed male at birth 11, non-binary presumed female at birth 40, and "other or questioning" 4.
- The core prevalence finding: "Half of the participants (48.2%, 95/197) expressed dissatisfaction with their voices and felt their voices did not align with their gender expression", and "Misattributions and voice-related judgments negatively affected well-being and social lives."
- Service use: "More than half (57.4%; 113/197) had engaged in VCT, reporting positive outcomes, particularly (trans)feminine individuals." All of those on masculinising hormone therapy (21.8%; 43/197) "experienced voice changes". Ten participants — printed in the abstract as "(0.05%; 10/197)" — "underwent gender-affirming pitch changing surgery with mixed results".
- The access finding the paper foregrounds: "Non-binary participants, especially those PFAB, faced more barriers accessing VCT."
- Conclusion: the study "underscores the importance of voice in gender expression and well-being"; it credits VCT and gender-affirming hormone therapy with "reducing misattribution and increasing voice satisfaction" while "identifying significant barriers to care in Belgium, especially for non-binary PFAB individuals."

## Bearing on voice training
- The Belgian numbers behind the pathway the German and Swiss sources describe: how many people are dissatisfied, how many reach voice training at all, and who does not — with non-binary people presumed female at birth named as the group least served ([[nonbinary-voice]], [[transgender-care-context]]).
- It is the survey counterpart to the same Belgian research network's intervention work [[papeleu2025]] and to the Liège therapy studies [[morsomme2022]]; the "positive outcomes, particularly (trans)feminine individuals" line is self-report from users of the service, not measured outcomes.
- The surgical figure — ten participants out of 197, with "mixed results" — is the patient-side version of what the surgical series report clinically [[scarpa2025]], [[meister2017a]].

## Notes
- Cross-sectional survey in a peer-reviewed trans health journal; graded medium. The abstract describes a community-based participatory design and does not state the sampling method, so the proportions are not population estimates.
- The abstract prints the surgical proportion as "(0.05%; 10/197)", which does not match the accompanying fraction (10/197 ≈ 5%); the figure is reproduced as published.
- Only the abstract was read (via OpenAlex; the doi.org record carries no abstract); the full text is paywalled and was not accessed.
