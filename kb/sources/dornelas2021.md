## What it says
- Presents two Brazilian instruments developed for a public Ambulatório Trans: the PAV-trans (Protocolo de Atendimento Vocal) and the PRV-trans (Programa de Redesignação Vocal), described as tools "que podem subsidiar o atendimento fonoaudiológico às pessoas trans no Brasil".
- The protocol runs in four stages (abstract, "Método"): (1) referral of the service user; (2) speech-language assessment, self-perception questionnaires, referral for ENT assessment, and a decision between individual therapy for the sound source ("fonte sonora") or the PRV-trans programme for the vocal filter ("filtro vocal"); (3) the PRV-trans itself, containing 12 vocal parameters worked monthly, individually and in groups; (4) re-assessment by speech-language pathology and ENT to see whether the goal of vocal reassignment was reached.
- The source/filter split ([[source-filter-model]]) is used as the triage rule: laryngeal-source work is done individually, resonance-filter work in the group programme.
- States the programme is designed to be flexible and to address the complaints of a population "no qual apresenta dificuldades de acesso ao serviço de saúde" — with difficulty accessing health services.
- Frames the aim as "maior segurança às pessoas trans em busca de uma passabilidade social por meio da voz, minimizando a transfobia" — greater safety through voice-based social passability, minimising transphobia.

## Bearing on voice training
- A rare published description of a group-plus-individual public-service pathway rather than a one-to-one clinic model; the 12-parameter monthly structure is the kind of curriculum a self-training tool can mirror ([[community-training]]).
- Uses "passabilidade" (passability) as an explicit programme goal, a framing the Swedish and Ghent literature increasingly avoids ([[holmberg2023]]).

## Notes
- A protocol presentation, not an outcome study: no participants, no acoustic or questionnaire results are reported in the abstract, which is why it is graded low.
- The 12 parameters are not enumerated in the abstract; the full text would be needed to reproduce the programme.
