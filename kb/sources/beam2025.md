# Patient-Reported Outcome Measures of Transmasculine Voice: A Scoping Review

## What it says
- Databases searched for transmasculine participants engaged in voice therapy with at least one patient-reported outcome measure of quality of life, participation or gender congruence; five studies met the criteria (abstract, "Methods", "Results").
- Outcome measures "varied widely and included standardized and non-standardized questionnaires".
- Kennedy and Thibeault (2020): Voice Handicap Index scores were lower for masculine than for feminine participants but higher than for gender-neutral participants, and "masculine participants reported greater barriers to seeking help".
- Myers et al. (2024): gender incongruence scores on the Utah Gender Presentation Scale for Communication fell after gender-affirming voice care for transgender men, "but the sample size was too limited for statistical analysis".
- On instrument fit: the Communicative Participation Item Bank and the Transsexual Voice Questionnaire Female to Male "were reportedly not representative" in their original forms, and "the validity of the VHI in the transmasculine population remains to be determined".
- Conclusion: transmasculine PROMs "have limited validity and sample sizes", and barriers to seeking care may themselves affect participation and quality of life.

## Bearing on voice training
- States the measurement gap plainly: the instruments in use for transmasculine voice are borrowed, and none has established validity for the group [[patient-reported-outcomes]] [[voice-masculinization]].
- The Kennedy and Thibeault finding — transmasculine people report more barriers to seeking help and a middle level of handicap — is the reason transmasculine voices are under-represented in the therapy literature [[hormones-and-voice]] [[speech-language-pathology]].

## Notes
- Five studies is a small evidence base; the review's own conclusion asks for prospective validation, representative samples and measures developed with community input. Published 2025; the included studies date from 2020 and 2024.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
