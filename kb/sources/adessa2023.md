# Gender-Affirming Voice Modification for Transgender Women: Characteristics and Outcomes

## What it says
- Retrospective series from the Cleveland Clinic Voice Center: 16 patients assigned male at birth and identifying as female, seen over 30 months, comparing behavioral voice training alone, surgery alone, and training plus surgery (abstract).
- Most patients were on hormone replacement therapy at treatment initiation, had been presenting in public as female for over 2 years, and had a history of psychological disorders including gender dysphoria, anxiety, depression and ADHD (abstract).
- "Increased f0 and decreased TWVQ scores were evident and statistically significant within behavioral voice training cohort and behavioral voice training+surgery cohort"; treatment groups looked similar in average change, and pre/post data were missing for the surgery-only patients (abstract).
- "Greater change in f0 was directly correlated with increased number of voice training sessions" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- A dose–response datum for [[voice-feminization-therapy]]: more sessions, more pitch change — the closest this vault has to a sessions-versus-outcome curve, from routine clinical care.
- The cohort description is the care-context detail: patients typically arrive already on hormones and socially transitioned, and HRT is not what moved their f0 ([[hormones-and-voice]]).

## Notes
- n = 16, retrospective, single centre; no pre/post data for the surgery-only arm, so the three-way comparison is incomplete by the authors' own statement.
