# Changes in the male voice at puberty

## What it says
- Twenty-six boys followed longitudinally; speaking and singing fundamental frequency analysed against Tanner pubertal staging, saliva testosterone and the Cooksey classification of the changing voice (abstract).
- "There were abrupt changes in voice characteristics between Tanner stages G3 and G4 and more gradually from stages C3 to C5 of Cooksey" (abstract).
- "Although testosterone concentrations were not predictive of the changes, there was a correlation with testis volume" (abstract).
- "Voice fundamental frequencies were seen to change abruptly in late puberty, in contrast with previous studies" (abstract).
- Tanner and Cooksey classifications agreed well through male puberty (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The pitch drop in male puberty is not a slow slide: it lands in a short window late in puberty. The salivary-testosterone result is a caution against reading a single hormone level as a predictor of where a voice will end up — compare the adult testosterone-therapy literature ([[nygren2016]], [[nimura2022]]).

## Notes
- 26 boys, one UK voice clinic. Saliva testosterone is a coarse measure.
