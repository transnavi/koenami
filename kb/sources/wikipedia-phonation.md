# Phonation (English Wikipedia)

## What it says
- Summary endpoint, 2026-09-17: "The term phonation has slightly different meanings depending on the subfield of phonetics. Among some phoneticians, phonation or phonating is the process by which the vocal cords, or vocal folds, produce certain sounds through quasi-periodic vibration. This is the definition used among those who study laryngeal anatomy and physiology and speech production in general. Phoneticians in other subfields, such as linguistic phonetics, call this process voicing, and use the term phonation to refer to any oscillatory state of any part of the larynx that modifies the airstream, of which voicing is just one example. Voiceless and supra-glottal phonations are included under this definition."
- The article states the myoelastic–aerodynamic account this vault uses: vocal-fold vibration is sustained by the interaction of tissue elasticity and muscular tension with the aerodynamics of the air passing through the glottis, rather than by a nerve impulse per cycle — the theory named by [[vandenberg1958]].

## Bearing on voice training
- Worth knowing that the word is ambiguous: in a phonetics paper "phonation type" may mean breathy/creaky/modal register, while in a voice-science paper "phonation" is simply the act of making the folds vibrate. The vault uses the second sense in [[phonation]] and the first in [[voice-quality]].
