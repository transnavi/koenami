# Gender-Affirming Voice Therapy Duration and Satisfaction: Experiences from a Single Institution

## What it says
- UCSF Voice and Swallowing Center retrospective cohort: 82 transgender and nonbinary patients (65 trans female, 5 trans male, 12 nonbinary; mean age 33.6) seeking gender-affirming voice care (abstract).
- In the first year, patients had a mean 5.7±3.1 gender-affirming voice therapy (GAVT) sessions (range 1–16, median 5), biweekly for 44% or monthly for 15% (abstract).
- 62% were satisfied/very satisfied and 23% fairly satisfied; the SLP impression was more variable — 15% of patients fully met voice goals, 39% "making good progress, with further treatment recommended"; the SLP referred 11% for surgery consideration and 9% underwent Wendler glottoplasty (abstract).
- Conclusion: gender-affirming voice therapy "often requires more sessions (mean 5.7) than the literature-reported average (∼4) for other voice diagnoses", and expectations should be set accordingly (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- Direct evidence on GAVT dosage — a realistic session count and cadence for a feminization-training program [[voice-feminization-therapy]] [[community-training]], complementing the intensive-programme trials [[leyns2023]] [[lin2026]].
- Included here from the ko-asia brief because the first author is an otolaryngologist of the Faculty of Medicine, Khon Kaen University, Thailand — a Thai clinician-researcher publishing on trans voice care, while the data themselves are US.

## Notes
- US single-institution cohort; satisfaction is patient-reported at one centre; not Thai speech or Thai patients.
