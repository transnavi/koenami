# Acoustic Measures of Brazilian Transgender Women's Voices: A Case–Control Study

## What it says
- 30 trans women aged 19–52 and 31 cis women aged 20–48, all recorded on a sustained /a/ and analysed with the Multi-Dimensional Voice Program (abstract, Methods). Single centre — the Gender Identity Program at the Hospital de Clínicas de Porto Alegre — and an author list overlapping almost entirely with [[schwarz2018]], which reports perceptual ratings on a sample of the same size from the same programme; whether the participants are the same people is not stated in either abstract.
- 14 measures differed significantly between the two groups: F0, F0max, F0min, F0 SD, absolute and relative jitter, F0 relative average perturbation, F0 perturbation quotient and smoothed F0 perturbation quotient, F0 variation, absolute and relative shimmer, voice turbulence index (lower in the trans women) and soft phonation index (higher in the trans women).
- Mean F0 was 159.046 Hz in the trans women and 192.435 Hz in the cis women — a 33 Hz gap, with a wide spread among the trans women.
- The conclusion the authors draw: "Through glottal adaptations, the group of transgender women managed to feminize their voices, presenting voices that were less aperiodic and softer than those of cisgender women."

## Bearing on voice training
- The perturbation profile matters for practice: these trans women were *less* aperiodic and *softer* than cis women, which is the acoustic face of the breathiness the vault records as a contested cue ([[breathiness]], [[voice-quality]], [[leung2018]]) and of the "softer voice" warning in Brazilian clinical writing ([[viana2025]]).
- Group means hide the individual range. Mean F0 159 Hz with individual values reaching into the masculine range matches the mixed-report pattern of [[schmidt2018]] and the pitch-ceiling result of [[sakuraba2009]]: an acoustic measure being "feminised" on average says nothing about one speaker.

## Notes
- Sustained-vowel measures only; no connected speech and no listener ratings in this paper. The associated paper [[schwarz2018]] adds GRBASI and resonance focus for a sample of the same size from the same programme.
- Claims taken from the abstract; the Statistics section and the individual participant table were not read.
