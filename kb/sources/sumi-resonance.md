# Resonance (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Definition: "The space in a container is it's internal volume, like the air in a pipe. Resonance is how sound is affected by the shape and amount of that space. We hear this quality as low and high resonance or as brightness, which is the more perceptual equivalent."
- The wiki's own instruction about how to hold the concept: "it's best not to think about resonance as formants or physical space changes but just as how the voice sounds. We're much better equipped to change sound color than we are to change physical aspects of our vocal tract."
- Formant definitions given for the training reader: "Formants are frequencies of the voice that are highlighted because of the shape of the vocal tract. If a resonance is a property of the container, or space, then a formant is a property of the sound." F1 is the lowest formant, F2 the second lowest; "All resonances (and so, formants) will be raised by shortening the vocal tract, and lowered by extending it."
- Raising (brightening) tools: *voiced resonance stepping* (start at a low yawn-like position, say "one two three", step to neutral, then brighter, then into a passage), *whispers* ("whisper a word over and over, and change how high or low it is. This isn't pitch, but it's the equivalent for unvoiced sounds"), *vampire slides* (unvoiced slides to the top of the range, then a voiced "wa" or "la").
- Stated pitfalls for the brightening exercises: "changing pitch too much", going "buzzy or overbright", and "don't smooth it over or add breathiness"; on whisper work, "never do whisper based exercises on their own; do voiced ones at a set pitch immediately after".
- Lowering (darkening) tools: "uhh and iii" mouth-shape pairs, and "haah and haww" ("the more you keep the mouth in roughly this haww shape, the darker your overall speech will be"); a warning attached — "be sure to keep the larynx in vaguely the same position, since otherwise you might drop the larynx instead of making a large mouth space!"
- Four named mechanisms of resonance modification: "Larynx Height" ("moving it up shortens the vocal tract and raises resonance ... considered the larger modification in resonance, where vowel would be smaller"); "Vowel Shape" (shifting toward a brighter vowel, "usually called macrovowel"); "OPC - Oropharyngeal closure" (the palatopharyngeal arch constricting, which "can also encourage tongue root retraction which contributes to a cartoonish quality (see: Stitch voice)"); "OPE - Oropharyngeal expansion"; and "Mouth Space" (corner tension with the expression removed).
- On twang: "It highlights parts of the voice around 2000-4000hz which is in the F2 range ... especially useful when imitating low pitch bright voices, and getting loudness at medium to high pitch without adding vocal weight", with the caveat that "It can encourage nasality".
- Muscle note: raising pitch raises the larynx through the thyrohyoid muscles alone, whereas resonance training "often ... use[s] both the thyrohyoid muscles and the stylohyoid muscles"; "If we have a situation in which the resonance cannot go above around 700hz F1 (/.ae/ vowel) then it's highly likely that only the stylohyoid muscles are being activated."

## Bearing on voice training
- The community's fullest account of resonance as a trainable target, and explicitly a *perceptual* target rather than an anatomical one — the opposite of the instruction a formant-number display gives [[formants]] [[voice-therapy-techniques]].
- Names exercises the clinical literature largely does not test: whisper slides, vampire slides, macrovowels, oropharyngeal closure. Clinical resonance work is described as forward tongue carriage and lip spreading [[carew2007]] [[formants]].
- The larynx-height-versus-mouth-space distinction and the muscle note give a physical reason why a learner can plateau: raising F1 by mouth shape alone has a ceiling [[larynx-anatomy]] [[vocal-tract-anatomy]].
