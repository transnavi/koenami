# On the relation between subglottal pressure and fundamental frequency in phonation

## What it says
- Quantifies the F0 change with subglottal pressure from the ratio of vibrational amplitude to vocal-fold length. That ratio "is typically very small in stringed instruments, but becomes quite appreciable in vocal fold vibration" (abstract).
- Because tissue tension therefore is not constant across the vibratory cycle, a dynamic tension produces amplitude–frequency dependence (abstract).
- "The typical 2-6 Hz/cm H2O rise in fundamental frequency with subglottal pressure observed in human and canine larynges is a direct and predictable consequence of this amplitude-frequency dependence" (abstract).
- Results are limited to chest-register phonation (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- A number to keep: pushing more air raises pitch, but only by a few Hz per cm H₂O. Pitch changes of tens of Hz come from the larynx, not from breath pressure — so "speak louder to sound higher" is a small effect bought at a high effort cost.
