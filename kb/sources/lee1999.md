# Acoustics of children's speech: developmental changes of temporal and spectral parameters

## What it says
- Duration, F0, formant frequencies and spectral envelope analysed in 436 children aged 5–17 plus 56 adults (abstract).
- "Differentiation of male and female fundamental frequency and formant frequency patterns begins at around age 11, becoming fully established around age 15" (abstract).
- "During that time period, changes in vowel formant frequencies of male speakers is approximately linear with age, while such a linear trend is less obvious for female speakers. These results support the hypothesis of uniform axial growth of the vocal tract for male speakers" (abstract).
- Magnitude and within-subject variability of temporal and spectral parameters fall with age; segmental durations converge to adult levels around 12, while F0 and formant variability "may reach adult range about 2 or 3 years later" (abstract).
- Reports "an apparent overshoot in acoustic parameter values, somewhere between ages 13 and 15, before converging to the canonical levels for adults" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- Locates the split in both cues — pitch *and* resonance — in the same window, roughly 11 to 15. The formant divergence is a tract-length effect, which is why formants and F0 are learned as two separate levers ([[formants]], [[fundamental-frequency]]).

## Notes
- American English; formant tracking in children's speech is harder than in adults' and the authors flag high within-subject variability.
