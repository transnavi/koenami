# Taiwanese speech-language therapists' awareness and experiences of service provision to transgender clients (Litosseliti & Georgiadou 2019)

## What it says
- A cross-sectional web survey of 140 Taiwanese speech-language therapists (abstract, PubMed, 2026-09-17): it is described by its authors as among the first studies of SLT transgender-service provision in Asia.
- Findings: the SLTs were more familiar with LGB terminology than with transgender terminology; generally positive attitudes toward transgender individuals; comfortable providing clinical services to trans clients — but the majority did not feel sufficiently skilled in working with transgender individuals, even though most believed voice and communication services for them fall within the SLT scope of practice.
- The framing premise the authors state: whether a trans person can "pass" in their felt gender is one of the most influential factors for quality of life, and few trans individuals access voice therapy because of stigmatization concerns or negative healthcare experiences.
- Conclusion: clinicians need both transgender-voice skill and cultural competence; the authors recommend adding gender/sexual-minority cultural competence to SLT university curricula and continuing education in Taiwan.

## Bearing on voice training
- The supply-side reason the Chinese-speaking world relies on community material ([[chinese-community]]): where 140 surveyed SLPs mostly feel insufficiently skilled, mtf.wiki's directories ([[mtfwiki-vocal-confirmed]]) and 伪声 videos fill the gap — a documented mismatch between scope-of-practice acceptance and actual readiness.
- Taiwan context for [[transgender-care-context]]: service access barriers named are stigma and prior negative experiences, matching the uptake gaps in [[hancock2011]].

## Limits
- Self-report survey with a volunteer web sample (n = 140), authors based in the UK and Nottingham (UK) transgender health service; English-language publication of a Taiwan study — the Chinese-language clinical discussion it prompted is not captured here.
