## What it says
- Cross-sectional study of 47 transsexual women (aged 18–50) in the Gender Identity Program (PROTIG) at Hospital de Clínicas de Porto Alegre, Brazil, who completed the Brazilian Portuguese Transsexual Voice Questionnaire for Male-to-Female Transsexuals (TVQMtF), analyzed against demographic data and visual-perceptual/auditory-perceptual judgments from the program database.
- 55.32% had completed secondary education; 72.34% had not undergone sex reassignment surgery; 4.2% used no hormones; 23.40% had undergone speech therapy; average time living in the female role was 13.98 years (range 5–34 years).
- Most participants had low TVQMtF scores, but significant differences were found by age (higher/worse scores in those <30 and ≥40 years old) and by visual-perceptual facial evaluation (higher/worse scores in those rated with a more masculine face).
- No significant correlations were found between total TVQMtF score and other demographic variables.
- Concludes that although most participants scored low (less distress) on the TVQMtF, some individuals still experienced distress from being perceived as their sex assigned at birth or dissatisfaction with their voice.

## Bearing on voice training
- Provides the Brazilian Portuguese validation/use data for the TVQ(MtF) instrument (companion to dacakis2013's original and salm2020/robotti2022's German/Italian translations), useful for cross-linguistic PROM comparison.
- The finding that facial masculinity perception correlates with worse voice-questionnaire scores is a useful data point on how visual gender perception and voice-related distress interact, relevant to whole-person (not voice-only) approaches to care.

## Notes
- n=47, single clinical program in Brazil; only 23.40% had received speech therapy, so this is largely a pre-/non-treatment distress snapshot rather than a treatment-outcome study.
