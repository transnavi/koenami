# Defining Goals of Transfeminine Individuals Seeking Gender-Affirming Voice Therapy: A Qualitative Study

## What it says
- Fifteen transfeminine people were interviewed before starting gender-affirming voice care; four SLPs with gender-affirming voice experience coded the transcripts line-by-line with a grounded-theory approach (abstract, "Method").
- Four themes, 12 subthemes. The first: "individuals hope that voice therapy will reduce adverse voice-related experiences" — past harms included "being misgendered or feeling isolated due to vocal function".
- The others: success should "facilitate specific voice goals", "facilitate communication across varying contexts", and people "want specific guidance in voice therapy".
- The named voice goals: a voice "perceived as more feminine, higher in pitch, and produced with less mental and phonatory effort" — effort reduction is asked for alongside pitch.
- The named contexts: "occupational voice use, phone use, and interactions with strangers".

## Bearing on voice training
- Written by patients, not clinicians, as the definition of success: less misgendering, less effort, and a voice that survives the phone and the stranger [[voice-feminization-therapy]] [[patient-reported-outcomes]].
- "Less mental and phonatory effort" as a stated goal is the same complaint quantified elsewhere as elevated vocal effort [[tang2026]] and vocal fatigue [[godoy2026]].

## Notes
- n = 15, pre-treatment interviews at a US academic voice centre (USC, with Wisconsin and MGH co-authors); qualitative, no outcome measures. "Future study should continue to clarify the manner in which patient experiences drive voice outcomes."
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
