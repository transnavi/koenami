# Dynamics of the chest wall during speech production: function of the thorax, rib cage, diaphragm, and abdomen

## What it says
- Measured anteroposterior diameters of the rib cage and abdomen together with esophageal and gastric pressures, upright and supine, during breathing manoeuvres and speech (abstract).
- Chest-wall behaviour "depend[s] upon lung volume, utterance loudness, body position, and utterance task" (abstract) — there is no single "correct" breathing pattern independent of what is being said.
- For utterances spanning most of the vital capacity, "chest wall effort was at first net inspiratory and later net expiratory" (abstract): near the top of the breath the muscles are holding the recoil back, not pushing.
- "For conversational speech, chest wall effort was continuously expiratory, control being vested in the rib cage and the abdomen in the upright body position and typically in the rib cage alone in the supine position" (abstract).
- "The abdomen occupies an especially important role in running conversational speech in that it mechanically tunes the diaphragm to increase the latter's inspiratory efficiency and thus enables man to minimally interrupt his ongoing speech for needed inspiratory pauses" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The measured version of what teachers call support: the abdominal wall's job in speech is to set the diaphragm up for a fast, small refill between phrases, and the rib cage does most of the pressure control when upright. It also shows why breathing advice given lying down does not transfer unchanged to standing.

## Notes
- Small number of normal adults, 1976 instrumentation; the paper is a 60-page methods-heavy monograph and the abstract is all that was read here.
