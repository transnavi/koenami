# La Voz de la Mujer Transexual y El Efecto de Doble Mirada

A short Spanish professional article that names a mechanism the rest of this literature treats as a background fact — the double gaze, being seen and heard at once, and the way a heard voice overrides a seen body.

## What it says
- Its premise about demand: with trans people's increased visibility and more logopedas specialising in logofoniatría, 「empieza a ser habitual la demanda de nuestros servicios de reeducación vocal por parte de personas transexuales」 — requests for vocal re-education have become ordinary.
- The mechanism, in the author's words: 「Por el efecto de la doble mirada, estas se sienten más vulnerables y se acentúa la sensación de estar lejos del género sentido. Aun cuando ya puedan haber conseguido la imagen física deseada, ven cómo esta imagen física adquirida no les sirve si utilizan la voz.」 Through the double-gaze effect they feel more vulnerable and further from the gender they feel themselves to be; even with the physical image they wanted, that image stops working the moment they speak.
- It is presented as a reflection for practitioners, not as a study; the abstract carries no data.

## Bearing on voice training
- A compact statement of why voice carries disproportionate weight in how trans women are read, and why the complaint often appears *after* the physical transition ([[gender-perception]], [[voice-health]]). The same structure is measured in [[schmidt2018]]'s listener correlation and described qualitatively in [[sebastiao2022]].
- The phrase 「estar lejos del género sentido」 — being far from the felt gender — is the Spanish-language vocabulary for incongruence, useful beside the Brazilian 「harmonização vocal」 ([[voice-across-languages]], [[spanish-voice]]).
