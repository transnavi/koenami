## What it says
- PRISMA systematic review searching CENTRAL, MEDLINE (PubMed) and Embase for studies measuring effects of speech therapy in transgender women, with risk-of-bias assessment and levels-of-evidence rating for each included study.
- 14 studies were identified and narratively synthesized; results were positive for pitch elevation, oral resonance, self-perception, and listener perception.
- The review states gender-affirming hormone treatment does not affect trans women's voices, framing voice therapy as the primary intervention route.
- Concludes methodological issues (small samples, lack of blinding, short follow-up, inconsistent outcome measures) limit generalization and reproducibility across the 14 studies.
- Calls for RCTs with larger samples, multidimensional voice assessment, well-described therapy protocols, blinded assessors, and longer-term follow-up.

## Bearing on voice training
- The highest-grade evidence source in this cluster for "does voice therapy work" — a positive but qualified answer: therapy shifts pitch, resonance and perception, but the underlying evidence base is still weak by RCT standards.
- Useful as the citation for stating that hormone therapy alone will not feminize an MTF/trans woman voice, motivating why therapy (and/or surgery) is needed.

## Notes
- Population: transgender women (MTF) only; does not cover masculinization.
- Open access via PMC (PMC10553375).
