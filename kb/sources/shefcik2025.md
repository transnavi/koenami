# Reliability and Severity Levels of the Voice-Related Experiences of Nonbinary Individuals

## What it says
- The VENI (Voice-related Experiences of Nonbinary Individuals) is described as "the only published questionnaire exclusively for nonbinary clients" — 17 items on self-perception of voice and voice-related concerns, whose content validity was rated good to excellent in the authors' 2023 work (abstract, "Objectives").
- This study tested reliability: 55 nonbinary participants who wanted to modify their voice and were *not* receiving gender-affirming communication services completed the VENI plus a self-report of how much their voice adversely affected daily life; 30 retook it 3–5 weeks later.
- Internal consistency α = 0.85, item–total correlations r = 0.11 to 0.72. Severity bands were created as standard deviations from the mean, giving five classifications from "mild" to "severe".
- Test–retest: Pearson's r = 0.89, P < 0.001; standard error of measurement 3.03; intraclass correlation 0.88.
- Conclusion: "good internal consistency and strong test–retest reliability", supporting use "for high-quality, individualized interventions in clinical and research purposes".

## Bearing on voice training
- Supplies the measurement instrument for a population the standard questionnaires fit badly — the TWVQ is written for trans women, and non-binary voice goals are explicitly not "the other binary" [[nonbinary-voice]] [[patient-reported-outcomes]].
- Severity bands keyed to the SEM let a clinician tell a real change from retest noise, which the raw score alone cannot.

## Notes
- n = 55 (30 retested), recruited online from people wanting voice change but not currently in therapy — a help-seeking sample rather than a clinical one. Published as an open-access article in the Journal of Voice. VENI is also used as an outcome measure in other recent training studies [[hirsch2026]] [[chadwick2026]].
