# Fundamental voice frequency in female puberty measured with electroglottography during continuous speech as a secondary sex characteristic

## What it says
- Forty-seven girls; F0 in continuous reading taken from 2000 consecutive electroglottographic cycles and compared with serum androgens (DHEA, androstenedione, testosterone, SHBG), oestrogens (oestradiol, oestrone, oestrone sulphate) and somatic puberty (weight, height, breast and pubic-hair stages) (abstract).
- "Fundamental frequency in continuous speech was related only to oestrone r = -0.34, (P less than 0.05)" (abstract) — a weak relation, and the only hormonal one found for speaking F0.
- "But the tone range in continuous speech and the lowest tone in the phonetogram were found to be significantly correlated with many of the pubertal and hormone parameters. All these correlations could be explained by a common age-dependency" (abstract).
- "By multiple regression analysis different sets of variables for prediction of speaking fundamental frequency were found before and after menarche" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The counterpart to the male studies: girls' voices also change through puberty, but the change shows up more in the bottom of the range than in a dramatic drop of habitual speaking pitch, and it does not track a single hormone. Useful when a trans woman compares her voice against "what a girl's voice does at puberty" and finds the comparison less clean than expected.

## Notes
- 47 Danish girls, 1990 assays; cross-sectional in hormones though voice was measured during puberty. The title's framing of F0 as "a secondary sex characteristic" is the authors'.
