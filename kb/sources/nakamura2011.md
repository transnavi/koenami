# 変声障害に対する甲状軟骨形成術3型

Three men with mutational dysphonia who did not respond to therapy, with their F0 before and after — the case-level numbers behind the operation's sequence rule.

## What it says
- The clinical picture as stated: male speaking pitch is high in childhood and falls with the rise in androgens at puberty, but 「なかには思春期を過ぎても高いままの話声位が持続することがある。それを変声障害という」 — sometimes it stays high past puberty, which is what mutational dysphonia is. 「通常は音声治療が奏効し話声位は低下する。音声治療により低下しない症例では手術の適応となる。」
- Cases: three men aged 37, 35 and 38 with mutational dysphonia whose speaking pitch was 174.6 Hz, 170.2 Hz and 180.0 Hz respectively.
- All three had Kayser-Gutzmann voice therapy and 「話声位は低下しなかった」, so type 3 thyroplasty was chosen.
- Results: 「術後の話声位は，106.9 Hz, 115.4 Hz，87.5 Hzに低下した」 — a drop of 55 to 92 Hz per case.
- Conclusion: 「音声治療で改善が得られない変声障害に対し，甲状軟骨形成術3型は有用であった。」

## Bearing on voice training
- The Kayser-Gutzmann method is the named therapy these patients failed, and the drop it failed to achieve is what the operation delivered — therapy-first, surgery for non-responders, which is the sequence the vault's guidelines formalise ([[voice-therapy-techniques]], [[clinical-guidelines]]).
- The three pre-operative values (170–180 Hz) sit in the range where Japanese listener data shows gender judgements becoming ambiguous ([[sakuraba2009]]), but here they are read as a male voice that is too high, not as an ambiguous one — the same acoustic value means different things depending on who is speaking ([[gender-perception]], [[male-voice]]).
- The companion case from the same group is [[otsu2010]], with the same therapy-resistant pattern at 276 Hz.

## Notes
- n = 3, no control group, single centre; graded low. Only the Japanese abstract was read.
- No follow-up duration and no voice-quality measures are reported in the abstract.
