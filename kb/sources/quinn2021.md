## What it says
- Notes that gender-related perceptual rating scales are widely used both as outcome measures in gender-affirming voice training and in general research on vocal-cue/gender-perception relationships, but scale terminology has varied across studies with unclear impact, and self–listener rating relationships (and how they change with training) were underexplored.
- 34 trans participants rated their own voices before and after gender-affirming voice training on two visual analogue scales (feminine↔masculine; female↔male) plus a satisfaction VAS; 25 listeners rated the same pre/post voice samples.
- Differences in scale labeling (feminine/masculine vs. female/male) had minimal impact on ratings from either trans participants or listeners.
- Trans participants' self-ratings correlated with listener ratings, but only weakly; self-ratings correlated much more strongly and consistently with the participants' own satisfaction with their voice — and this self/listener divergence became more pronounced after training.
- Concludes results from prior studies using differently labeled rating scales remain comparable, and discusses implications of the self-vs-listener rating gap for research and clinical practice.

## Bearing on voice training
- Key methodological finding for training outcome measurement: self-rated gender/femininity and listener-rated gender/femininity are only weakly correlated, and this gap widens after training — meaning client satisfaction should not be assumed to track external listener perception, and vice versa.
- Validates comparing findings across studies that used differently labeled VAS scales (masculine/feminine vs. male/female), since label choice had minimal measured effect.

## Notes
- Modest sample (34 trans participants, 25 listeners); does not identify why self/listener ratings diverge (e.g., self-perception bias, listener unfamiliarity, acoustic cues beyond gender used by listeners).
- Explicitly ties into feature-scale-design questions relevant to any study using VAS-based gender/femininity outcome measures, including other sources in this cluster (e.g., leyns2023, leyns2024, hardy2020).
