# A proposal for needle projections in transcutaneous injection laryngoplasty using three-dimensionally reconstructed CT scans

## What it says
- Neck CT scans of 200 patients, three-dimensionally reconstructed and measured by two groups of raters, to describe laryngeal dimensions for the thyrohyoid, transthyroid and cricothyroid injection approaches, compared across sex, age group and ethnicity (abstract).
- Measurements included distance and angle from the superior border of the thyroid cartilage to the true vocal folds, distance from mid-thyroid cartilage to mid-true-cords, Hounsfield units at the mid-thyroid cartilage (a measure of cartilage ossification), and distance and angle from the inferior border of the thyroid cartilage (abstract).
- "There were statistically significant differences between males and females for all measurements except for CTa" — the cricothyroid needle angle — "(p < 0.0001)" (abstract).
- "No significant difference across age groups and ethnicities were found for all three approaches (p > 0.05)", though age correlated weakly with cartilage density at the mid-thyroid cartilage (abstract).
- "This study demonstrated a significant sex dimorphism that may influence the three TIL approaches" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- A recent, large in-vivo measurement confirming that the adult larynx differs by sex in nearly every linear dimension, not only in vocal-fold length. Cited here for the anatomy; the surgical proposal itself is outside this vault's scope.

## Notes
- 200 patients at one Malaysian centre, scanned for clinical reasons; the "ethnicity" comparison is within that population. The study's purpose is needle placement, so the dimensions reported are the ones a surgeon needs, not a complete morphometry of the larynx.
