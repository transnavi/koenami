## What it says
- Tests, experimentally, three hypotheses proposed to explain why Japanese women have been found to speak with higher average pitch than Dutch women: (1) high pitch is associated with physical/psychological powerlessness (short, weak, dependent, modest) in both Dutch and Japanese culture; (2) the ideal-woman/ideal-man contrast on the powerlessness-power dimension is stronger in Japan than the Netherlands; (3) Japanese respondents prefer high pitch in women while Dutch respondents prefer medium/low pitch.
- All three hypotheses were confirmed.
- Additional finding: results also point to a strong cultural emphasis on masculinity in Japanese men, which may drive men's pitch lower and thereby widen the sex-typical pitch gap in Japan relative to the Netherlands.

## Bearing on voice training
- Cross-language evidence that "how high is feminine-sounding" is not a fixed acoustic universal but is shaped by culture-specific gender ideology — directly relevant to setting language- and culture-appropriate pitch targets rather than importing one language's norms wholesale.
- Suggests that the F0 gap between men's and women's speech in a given language partly reflects how strongly that culture polarizes masculine/feminine ideals, not only anatomy.

## Notes
- Attitude/rating study (associations and preferences), not a direct acoustic measurement of natural speech; cited widely as foundational for cross-cultural pitch-and-gender research.
- Two-culture comparison (Japanese vs. Dutch); does not generalize claims beyond these two cultures without further study.
