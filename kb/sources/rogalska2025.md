# Long-term acoustic outcomes, perceptual voice assessment and voice-related quality of life in transgender women undergoing Wendler glottoplasty

## What it says
- Single-centre Polish study: records of 7 trans women who had Wendler glottoplasty, plus their self-assessed voice-related quality of life and a telephone test. Long-term results were compared with preoperative and short-term data and with 7 age-matched cisgender men and 7 cisgender women; 64 cisgender listeners judged the speech samples (abstract, "Methods").
- After an average of 18.63 months, "a persistent, significant increase in fundamental frequency, speaking fundamental frequency, and lower limit of the frequency range of spoken voice was observed, with the values being comparable to cisgender female controls" — the lower limit held here, unlike in training studies [[leyns2026]].
- "Despite a significant long-term improvement in the emotional domain of the Voice Handicap Index, the remaining voice-related QoL scores deteriorated compared to the short-term evaluation."
- "The gender of transfeminine individuals was significantly more often incorrectly attributed compared to cisgender controls."
- "The percentage of voice ratings as a female was not correlated with the acoustic measures, self-assessed vocal femininity, and satisfaction with WG results."
- Conclusion: acoustic measures "cannot guarantee voice attribution as female by external evaluators, indicating the beneficial role of perceptual assessment".

## Bearing on voice training
- Long-term acoustic success without perceptual success: the F0 gains persisted for a year and a half and still did not buy correct gender attribution on the telephone [[gender-perception]] [[fundamental-frequency]].
- The mismatch between acoustic measures, self-assessment and listener ratings is a concrete argument for judging voice work on more than one axis [[patient-reported-outcomes]] [[voice-surgery]].
- Also documents quality of life drifting *down* after the short-term gain had passed — the maintenance question applies to surgery as much as to training [[voice-health]].

## Notes
- n = 7, single centre, retrospective, no control arm (the cisgender comparisons are reference voices, not a matched clinical group). Telephone-only ratings and the abstract do not state the language of the samples.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
