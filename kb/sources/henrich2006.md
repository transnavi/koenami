# Mirroring the voice from Garcia to the present day: Some insights into singing voice registers

## What it says
- Traces the notion of vocal register from Manuel García's definition to the present; the abstract states that "the notion of registers is still confused in the singing voice community, and defined in many different ways."
- The confusion is partly definitional: "While some authors consider a vocal register as a totally laryngeal event, others define it in terms of overall voice quality similarities" (abstract).
- Introduces the concept of **laryngeal mechanism**, grounded in "laryngeal transition phenomena detected by means of electroglottography", as a way "to specify at least the laryngeal nature of a given singing voice register" (abstract).
- Surveys the physiological, acoustic and perceptual characteristics of the commonest singing registers on that basis (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The reason a teacher and a client can use the same word ("head voice", "falsetto") for different things: the register vocabulary names a percept, the mechanism names what the larynx is doing. Trans voice work that talks about "switching registers" is usually talking about M1↔M2 in the sense this paper sets out.
