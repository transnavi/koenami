# How to Practice (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- The self-check offered: "Ask the question 'is what I'm doing in the direction of the the thing I want to be doing more of in the future'. If the answer is yes, keep doing it."
- On exercise versus speech: "In general, focus on practice more than exercises. That is, don't spend all your time doing pitch slides or resonance exercises and not speaking. Fundamentally, speaking is what you want to be able to do, and you get good at the things you do."
- "The fundamental laws of voice training", as the page titles them: "The more you use a configuration the more consistent it will become"; "The further you train a part of your voice, the more you will be able to let it rest almost as far as you trained it"; "Almost everything is exploratory, and most of the rest is habit, so the more things you try to do, the more things you will be able to do"; "The more you do something, the better you can do it and the more you will tend to do it subconsciously in future"; "more effort means less results in most cases, so trying less hard is better".
- The strategy derived from those laws: "Do practice in the direction of the things you want"; "Develop things slightly past where you want them to be so they become comfortable where you want them to be"; "Explore things in opposite directions to where you want to be, to get more mastery".
- A general adjustment loop given when no exercise exists for a feature: "Try to do less of it; Try to do more of it; Try to isolate it so you don't change anything else; Find a balance you like."
- Time: "A good balance is to practice exercises for 5-10 minutes, and use your trained voice for a max of a few hours per day ... The most important thing to keep in mind is not to strain or add effort at any time for any reason. If we don't feel strain or fatigue, then we can practice for as long as we like, but it's best not to overdo training and get burned out mentally, just as it is important not to strain things physically."
- Frequency: "Doing short periods of exercises (2-5min) several times a day is a good method. It can be best to avoid spending all your time training, but also to not go more than a few days without at least using the voice you're working on."
- Also advised: listen back to recordings regularly and listen to voices in the direction you want to go, and treat "consistently not working" as a signal to take a break and change approach.

## Bearing on voice training
- The community's practice-plan doctrine, and its rules are about dose and direction rather than any particular exercise: short, frequent, in the direction of the target, no strain [[community-training]] [[voice-therapy-techniques]].
- "More effort means less results in most cases" is the same conclusion the clinical sources reach about pressed phonation and hyperadduction, stated as a practice principle [[sumi-hyperadduction]] [[vocal-fatigue]] [[voice-health]].
- The 5–10 minutes of exercise plus a few hours of use per day is a concrete time budget, and the caveat attached is the honest one: it depends on the individual and on strain.
