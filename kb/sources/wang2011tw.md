# 華文版「嗓音障礙指數量表」(VHI-10)發展與驗證（王南梅等2011）

## What it says
- The abstract is published in English inside a Traditional-Chinese article; it opens by calling the VHI-10 "one of the most psychometrically robust, well-studied, and efficient instruments among various instruments used to measure self-perceived voice related quality of life", and states the gap it fills: "the psychometric properties of the VHI-10 in the Mandarin Chinese version remain to be verified for Taiwanese culture" (abstract, BACKGROUND).
- Methods: a pilot psychometric analysis with ten voice-disorder subjects to "confirm the translation appropriateness of test items", then reliability testing against an experimental group with voice disorders and a control group with normal voice, plus test–retest within a dysphonic group (METHODS).
- Results: the experimental group was 176 patients, "involved six subgroups of voice disorders based on clinical laryngeal diagnosis and voice assessment"; the control group was 70 normal subjects. Between-group difference was significant (p < 0.0001), but "no significant difference was found between the six subgroups of voice disorders". Test–retest reliability over 34 dysphonic subjects was r = 0.720 (p < 0.001); internal consistency α = 0.861 (RESULTS).
- Conclusion: the Mandarin Chinese VHI-10 is "a powerful and applicable instrument and is appropriate for use in context of Taiwan culture", able to "quantify the patients' perception of their voice handicap" and "to compare treatment efficacy among voice disordered populations" (CONCLUSIONS).

## Bearing on voice training
- Gives the Traditional-Chinese (Taiwan) member of the instrument family the mainland TWVQ-SC validation leans on: [[wang2022]] correlated its trans-specific questionnaire against the simplified-Chinese VHI-10 (r = 0.85), so both sides of the strait now have a Mandarin voice-handicap short form with published psychometrics.
- The subgroup result — the six laryngeal diagnoses did not separate on total score — is the same ceiling the trans literature runs into with generic instruments, which is why the trans-specific questionnaires exist at all ([[patient-reported-outcomes]]).
- 王南梅 is the first author here and also the first author of the 1994 Chang Gung post-phonosurgery rehabilitation plan [[wang1994tw]], where PubMed romanises the byline as Wang NM.

## Notes
- Instrument-validation study in a national specialty journal; graded medium. Only the English abstract published with the article was read (via OpenAlex's reconstructed abstract text); the full Chinese text (Airiti) was not accessed.
- Population: Taiwanese Mandarin speakers, 176 dysphonic patients and 70 controls; not a trans population. Included here for the instrument, not for any trans finding.
