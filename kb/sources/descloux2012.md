# Transsexuality: speech therapy supporting the "voice" of transformation（Descloux et al. 2012）

## What it says
- From the Unité de Phoniatrie et de Logopédie, CHU Vaudois (CHUV), Lausanne: the unit had "been increasingly requested to provide care to transsexual patients", and the abstract states the asymmetry plainly — "If the sex change from female to male does not generally entail vocal problems as a result of hormonotherapy, such is not the case the other way around. Thus, our patients are mostly men converting into women."
- The clinical premise: "Since the male larynx cannot be modified on an organic level, speech therapy is of the utmost importance in ensuring vocal adaptation."
- The article "briefly describe[s] the complete gender transformation program, and detail[s] the therapeutic approach supported by our unit."
- Its scope statement is explicitly more than pitch: "In addition to raising the fundamental frequency, we also focus on various factors such as articulation, intonation, vocabulary, and phrase structure, keeping in mind that the female voice is not only high pitched."

## Bearing on voice training
- A 2012 Swiss university-hospital unit saying in French what the research literature took another decade to state as a finding: pitch is necessary and insufficient, and articulation, intonation and phrasing are therapy targets — the same list [[leung2018]] later weights statistically and [[loret2019]] finds in Francophone SLP practice.
- The "vocabulary and phrase structure" item is the part of the programme that the acoustic literature in this vault does not cover at all; it is the language-and-communication half of what is today called voice and communication training.
- Names the hormonal asymmetry that [[hormones-and-voice]] documents: testosterone does most of the masculinising work for trans men, while oestrogen leaves the trans feminine larynx unchanged.

## Notes
- Clinical account from a hospital phoniatrics/logopaedics unit, not a study; graded medium (peer-reviewed clinical review, no outcome data or sample sizes reported in the abstract).
- Only PubMed's English rendering of the French abstract was read; the original French title is not carried by PubMed, Europe PMC or OpenAlex, so the translated title is used. Full text not accessed.
