## What it says
- 81 American transgender women completed the Voice Handicap Index (VHI) and the Transgender Self-Evaluation Questionnaire (TSEQ); results were reviewed through the International Classification of Functioning, Disability, and Health (ICF) model.
- Reports a wide range of voice-related quality of life (vQOL): some participants severely restricted by voice/communication concerns, others not restricted.
- Most common concerns were emotional reactions to activity limitations and participation restrictions (ICF categories), rather than raw impairment.
- Personal factors related to demographics and gender transition characteristics generally were not significant contributors to vQOL scores (Spearman correlations).
- Concludes that clinicians should address both physiological voice function and the emotional/psychosocial impact of voice on participation.

## Bearing on voice training
- Supports designing therapy goals and outcome tracking around participation and emotional impact, not only acoustic targets — evidence that vQOL varies independent of demographic/transition-stage factors alone.
- Useful as a citation for the ICF framework applied specifically to trans women's voice care.

## Notes
- n=81, cross-sectional, US sample; self-report only, no acoustic measures included.
- Uses TSEQ (predecessor/parallel instrument to the TVQ(MtF)) alongside the general VHI.
