# Perceptual analysis of the male-to-female transgender voice after glottoplasty—the telephone test

## What it says
- Würzburg University Hospital study of everyday-listening conditions: speech of 18 German-speaking trans women after Wendler glottoplasty, plus 18 male and 18 female controls, band-limited to the telephone's 300–3,400 Hz transmission range and judged by 50 male and 50 female listeners (abstract, "METHODS").
- Only 7 of the 18 post-glottoplasty speakers were perceived as female by the majority of listeners; perception as female correlated with F0, and "the perception as female in the telephone test and the personal satisfaction shows the power of this instrument for evaluation of therapy success" (abstract, "RESULTS"/"CONCLUSIONS").
- Decision times were longer for the post-surgery voices; female listeners decided faster and judged the MtF voices as male more often.
- Formants: F2 was higher than in male speakers, but F1 and F2 were both significantly lower than in female speakers.

## Bearing on voice training
- A German-language demonstration that passing in degraded channels is harder than in the clinic: the telephone strips pitch-range extremes and exposes the formant deficit [[formants]] seen after glottoplasty in [[paltura2019]].
- Only about a third of satisfaction was explained by pitch; the rest lives where training acts.

## Notes
- German speech samples; the band-limitation mimics one channel, not all everyday listening. Listener sex differences are reported but not explained.
- Same Würzburg group as the "Würzburg approach" pitch-elevation papers; this is the perceptual evaluation side.
