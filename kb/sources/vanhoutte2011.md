# Pathophysiology and treatment of muscle tension dysphonia: a review of the current knowledge

## What it says
- "Muscle tension dysphonia (MTD) is a clinical and diagnostic term describing a spectrum of disturbed vocal fold behavior caused by increased tension of the (para)laryngeal musculature. Recent knowledge introduced MTD as a bridge between functional and organic disorders."
- Search of MEDLINE (1950–2009) and CENTRAL. The authors note that for the aetiology, pathophysiology and circumlaryngeal manual therapy "randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are nonexisting", so reviews and prospective cohorts had to be used; RCTs and systematic reviews were used for voice therapy and vocal hygiene.
- Aetiology falls into three subgroups: "(1) psychological and/or personality factors, (2) vocal misuse and abuse, and (3) compensation for underlying disease."
- Effective treatment options listed: "(1) indirect therapy: vocal hygiene and patient education; (2) direct therapy: voice therapy and CMT [circumlaryngeal manual therapy]; (3) medical treatment; and (4) surgery for secondary organic lesions."
- Conclusion: MTD "needs to be approached in a multidisciplinary setting where close cooperation between a laryngologist and a speech language pathologist is possible."

## Bearing on voice training
- Names the commonest injury pattern in self-taught pitch work: a voice held up by squeezing rather than stretching, hoarse and effortful with no lesion visible. Category (3), compensation for underlying disease, is a reminder that persistent strain can be the symptom of something else, which is why the review sends the patient to a laryngologist and not only to an exercise list.
- Vocal hygiene and education appear here as *indirect* therapy: useful, but not the treatment on their own.

## Notes
- A narrative review with an explicit statement that the evidence base for the core claims contains no RCTs.
