# Speech Pathology Considerations in the Management of Transsexualism—A Review

## What it says
- The review with which speech pathology entered transgender voice care: Oates and Dacakis, Sydney, survey what a speech pathologist can offer transsexual patients and how voice and communication modification fits within medical management (title and PubMed record; 1983, pre-dating any controlled outcome study).
- Published the same year as the pair's case-study report of speech pathology intervention for voice-sex reassessment, which the later literature treats as the field's first published intervention report (see Notes).

## Bearing on voice training
- The starting line of the discipline this vault's [[speech-language-pathology]] describes: everything after it — [[davies2015]], [[leyns2021]], the textbooks [[adler2019]] and [[mills2017]] — descends from this Australian pair's early-1980s work.

## Notes
- Graded `low` despite being a journal review: it predates any outcome evidence in the field and reports no data of its own.
- Abstract-only note: the 1983 full text sits behind a publisher paywall and no open copy was reachable on 2026-09-17 (Wayback offline), so the detailed content claims are not quoted here.
- The companion 1983 case study ("Speech pathology intervention for voice-sex reassessment: a case study", Medical Journal of Australia supplement) has no DOI and no PubMed record and could not be fetched; its existence and citation form are attested in the reference lists of later reviews. The peer-reviewed, indexed 1983 work of the pair is this review, which is what this note covers.
- Terminology ("transsexualism") is of its time; the note keeps the title verbatim as published.
