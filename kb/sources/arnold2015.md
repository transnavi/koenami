# Voix et transidentité : changer de voix pour changer de genre ?（Arnold Aron 2015）

## What it says
- Aim, in the author's words: "montrer comment des locuteurs transidentitaires utilisent la fréquence fondamentale et les fréquences de résonance pour rendre intelligibles leurs identités de genre" — how trans speakers use F0 and the resonance frequencies to make their gender identities intelligible (abstract, French).
- Method: "La mise en relation des résultats d'analyses acoustiques et d'expériences perceptives" — acoustic analysis set against perceptual experiments — "a permis d'investiguer les rôles respectifs de ces deux paramètres acoustiques dans la perception et dans la reconnaissance de locuteurs en tant que « femme » ou « homme »" (abstract). Pitch and resonance are treated as two parameters with separate roles, not as a single score.
- The ethnographic layer is the paper's distinctive move: "Des entretiens ethnographiques menés avec les locuteurs ont ensuite permis de mieux comprendre certains des résultats obtenus" — interviews with the speakers themselves were used to explain the acoustic and perceptual results.
- The finding those interviews were needed for: "le non-passing d'un locuteur peut être dû à un conflit entre l'identité de genre que celui-ci souhaite rendre intelligible par sa voix et les attitudes, postures ou autres identités que cette même voix indexe" — a speaker can fail to pass because of a conflict between the gender identity they mean their voice to convey and the attitudes, postures or other identities that the same voice indexes (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The French-language counterpart of the sociophonetic work on trans speakers that this vault otherwise holds in English: it puts F0 and resonance in one acoustic design and adds the speakers' own reading of why the result came out as it did ([[european-languages-voice]], [[gender-perception]]).
- The "conflict between what the voice is meant to convey and what it indexes" is the listening-side phenomenon the training literature treats as a target and the perceptual literature treats as a cue: a voice can carry several indexical signals at once, and passing depends on which of them a listener picks up ([[voice-impressions]], [[articulation]]).
- It is a language-specific data point for a question the vault keeps returning to: French, unlike the languages most of the acoustic studies use, marks grammatical gender on the speaker's own words, so "changing voice to change gender" sits inside a grammar the speaker also has to navigate.
