## What it says
- Reports Pitch Elevation Surgery (type IV thyroplasty / Isshiki type 4) performed on 32 MTF/GID patients treated at the authors' clinic 1999–2006, to raise speaking fundamental frequency (SFF).
- 24 of the 32 also received laryngeal-prominence reduction (喉頭隆起切除術, tracheal shave) at the same time.
- SFF rose in all 32 cases: mean preoperative SFF was 133.8 Hz, mean postoperative SFF was 237.8 Hz.
- Because the surgery is done under local anesthesia, the surgeons could tune the resulting pitch intraoperatively to a level the patient found satisfactory, and all patients reported satisfaction.
- Concludes type IV thyroplasty is a useful pitch-elevation surgery for MTF/GID.

## Bearing on voice training
- Gives a concrete before/after benchmark for surgical pitch elevation: ~134 Hz → ~238 Hz, roughly a 104 Hz (near-octave-range) rise, useful context when comparing to behavioral-training pitch gains, which are typically smaller.
- Intraoperative tuning under local anesthesia is a distinctive advantage of this technique for patient-specific target-pitch setting.

## Notes
- n=32, single Japanese institution (Kyoto, Isshiki/Nakamura group), 1999–2006 case series; no control group or long-term follow-up data in the abstract.
- Overlaps substantially with sanuki2007 (same technique, same institution/era, near-identical F0 figures) — likely reports on the same or heavily overlapping patient cohort from different angles (surgical technique paper vs. this one).
