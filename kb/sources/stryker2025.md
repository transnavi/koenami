# Vocal-Gender Incongruence, wellbeing, and safety: The medical necessity of gender-affirming vocal training

## What it says
- Cross-sectional electronic survey using the Quality-of-Life Scale (QOLS), a modified Transgender Self-Evaluation Questionnaire (TSEQ) and questions on violence, discrimination and misgendering (abstract, "Methods").
- "Of 187 TGD participants, 127 (68%) reported" vocal-gender incongruence (VGI).
- Participants with VGI scored significantly lower on quality of life (70 vs 76; p = .02) and were "significantly more likely to have been misgendered based on their voice in the past year (p < .001; 85% vs. 33%)".
- Higher TSEQ scores — more impact of VGI on daily life — were associated with lower QOLS scores (β = 0.06; 95% CI 0.04, 0.09; p < .001) and with greater likelihood of public discrimination, medical discrimination and verbal harassment (all p < .001), "but not physical harm (β = 0.03; 95% CI 0.002, 0.06; p = 0.05)".
- Conclusion: "healthcare that alleviates VGI, including gender-affirming vocal training, is medically necessary."

## Bearing on voice training
- Quantifies the harm being addressed: voice-based misgendering in the past year reported by 85% of those with vocal-gender incongruence, against 33% without [[transgender-care-context]] [[gender-perception]].
- Ties voice work to experienced discrimination and harassment in public and in medical settings, which is the argument used in coverage and access disputes [[clinical-guidelines]] [[voice-health]].

## Notes
- n = 187, US online survey, cross-sectional and self-report, so the direction of causation is not established; participants self-selected into the survey. The abstract reports no acoustic measurement, and physical harm did not reach conventional significance.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract (Int J Transgend Health 2026;27(2); published online 2025-03-06).
