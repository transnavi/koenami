# Spectrogram (English Wikipedia)

## What it says
- Summary endpoint, 2026-09-17: "A spectrogram is a visual representation of the spectrum of frequencies of a signal as it varies with time. When applied to an audio signal, spectrograms are sometimes called sonographs, voiceprints, or voicegrams. When the data are represented in a 3D plot they may be called waterfall displays."
- Short description: "Visual representation of the spectrum of frequencies of a signal as it varies with time."

## Bearing on voice training
- The display every voice tool shows: time left-to-right, frequency bottom-to-top, energy as darkness. Reading one is the single skill that lets a learner see the difference between harmonics (which move with pitch) and formants (which do not) — see [[harmonics-and-spectrum]].
- The word "voiceprint" appears here as a synonym; it carries a forensic-identification connotation that the neutral term does not, and is best avoided.

## Limits
- Community-edited, and general to signal processing rather than specific to speech; it says nothing about the narrowband/wideband window choice that decides whether a speech spectrogram shows harmonics or formants.
