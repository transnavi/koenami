# Further Validation of the GENDER-Q Voice Sound and Voice Distress Scales in 5424 Transgender and Gender Diverse Adults

## What it says
- International field test of the GENDER-Q with 5424 participants aged 18+, seeking or having received gender-affirming care within the past 5 years, who completed the Voice Sound and Voice Distress scales (abstract, "Methods").
- Predefined hypotheses about expected group differences and correlations with other GENDER-Q scales were tested (t tests, ANOVA, Pearson correlation); the bar was acceptance of at least 75% of hypotheses under COSMIN criteria.
- Sample: mean age 32.8 (SD 12.3, range 18–83); 33.6% men, 36.8% women, 23.8% non-binary, 5.7% another gender.
- Care uptake reported: "Voice therapy was undertaken by 21.4% of participants, and 30.5% wanted this therapy. Fewer participants had undergone voice-related surgery (1.7%), with 10% wanting this surgery."
- Construct validity supported: 16 of 18 (89%) hypotheses for Voice Sound, 18 of 19 (95%) for Voice Distress.
- Conclusion: the scales "can be used to assess voice-related outcomes for adults aged 18 years and older who are seeking or receiving gender-affirming voice interventions".

## Bearing on voice training
- The largest validation of a voice-specific patient-reported instrument in this population, and the first with a non-binary subgroup large enough to matter (23.8%) [[patient-reported-outcomes]] [[nonbinary-voice]].
- The demand figures are the useful context for service planning: about one and a half times as many people wanted voice therapy as had received it (30.5% vs 21.4%), and about six times as many wanted voice surgery as had it (10% vs 1.7%) [[transgender-care-context]].

## Notes
- Field test recruited through gender-affirming care services, so the sample over-represents people already engaged with care. Instrument developers are among the authors and hold royalty interests (declared in the record).
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
