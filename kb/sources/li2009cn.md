# Self-assessment characteristics of voice handicap index for voice disorders and its influencing factors（Li HY et al. 2009）

## What it says
- 1,766 dysphonic patients and 120 control subjects at Beijing Tongren Hospital; 227 of the patients were treated with phonosurgery or botulinum toxin injection. The instrument was the Voice Handicap Index (abstract).
- Dysphonic patients scored worse than controls (z from 8.039 to 17.043, P = 0.000), and the scores differed significantly between diseases. Ordering them by descending VHI: "spasmodic dysphonia, vocal fold paralysis, functional dysphonia, sulcus vocalis, benign and malignant tumor of vocal fold, vocal fold cyst, Reinke's edema, vocal fold polyp, vocal fold keratosis and chronic laryngitis, vocal nodule" (RESULTS).
- Subscale behaviour was not uniform: "the emotional scores were the highest in spasmodic dysphonia, and followed by functional dysphonia", while in another group "the physical scores were higher than functional scores and emotional scores" (RESULTS).
- Treatment improved VHI scores significantly (P < 0.05).
- The two demographic effects the abstract highlights: total scores differed by educational background and age (F from 8.701 to 27.371, P = 0.000) — "the higher the educational degree, the higher the VHI scores", while by age "the juvenile group's scores were the lowest, while the youth's group the highest, then the scores declined when ages increased" (RESULTS).
- Conclusion: the VHI is "a useful supplementary instrument to measure the voice disorder severity and the treatment's effect" covering physical, functional and emotional aspects, "but it is somehow subject to the educational degree and age" (CONCLUSION).

## Bearing on voice training
- The mainland Chinese baseline for the [[patient-reported-outcomes]] instruments: it shows what a self-report voice score means in this population before any trans-specific questionnaire is applied to it.
- The instrument it characterises is the one the TWVQ-SC validation used as its convergent-validity comparator on 279 trans women [[wang2022]] (r = 0.85), and the same family the Taiwanese VHI-10 was derived from [[wang2011tw]]. The education and age effects it reports are a caveat that carries into those uses.
- The disease ranking is a reminder that self-reported handicap tracks diagnosis and not only acoustic severity — relevant when a trans speaker's score is read as a measure of gender-related distress.

## Notes
- Cross-sectional study, peer-reviewed, from the Beijing Tongren Hospital ENT department; graded medium. Not a trans population.
- Only the English abstract as indexed was read; the original Chinese title is not carried by PubMed, Europe PMC or OpenAlex, so the translated title is used. Full text not accessed.
