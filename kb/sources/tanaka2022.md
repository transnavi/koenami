## What it says
- Notes prior work links higher F0 and wider formant dispersion (a proxy for shorter vocal tract length) in female voices to perceived smaller body size, higher femininity, and attractiveness — even though adult women's actual body characteristics are not reliably predictable from voice.
- This study used female voice stimuli spanning a wider F0 and formant-dispersion range than previous studies, and had male college-student listeners rate speaker attractiveness, femininity, body size, and age.
- Voice attractiveness peaked at approximately 280–290 Hz — slightly above average adult female F0 — and declined at both lower and higher pitches (i.e., an inverted-U, not monotonic).
- Formant dispersion showed the same pattern: attractiveness rose with dispersion up to a point, then fell above that value.
- Estimated body size and other characteristics from F0/formant dispersion broadly replicated prior studies' trends.

## Bearing on voice training
- Directly relevant to pitch-target guidance for voice feminization: this Japanese listener sample locates peak perceived attractiveness around 280–290 Hz, not at the highest achievable pitch — "higher is not always better" for these listeners, and extremely high pitch can reduce rated attractiveness.
- Same non-monotonic relationship holds for formant dispersion/vocal-tract-length cues, suggesting resonance shifts also have a "sweet spot" rather than a maximize-and-stop target.
- Caution: raters were male college students judging female voices for attractiveness — not necessarily representative of general gender-categorization judgments, and the endpoint measured is "attractiveness," not "perceived as female."
