## What it says
- Computational (finite-element, fiber-gel) simulation of anterior (Wendler) glottoplasty using a canonical male vocal-fold geometry with three-layer composition (superficial lamina propria, vocal ligament, thyroarytenoid muscle), simulating progressive anterior glottic fixation from 0 to 7/8 of membranous vocal fold length.
- Fundamental frequency (f0) rose from 110 Hz to 164 Hz when the anterior half of the vocal fold was fixed, and continued rising with further fixation.
- Sound pressure level (SPL) progressively decreased beyond about 1/8–1/4 fixation.
- Including the vocal ligament in the fixation did not further increase f0.
- Any degree of fixation increased acoustic-signal aperiodicity; the authors propose this as a mechanistic explanation for the vocal roughness sometimes reported after anterior glottoplasty.
- Concludes the optimal fixation length is a compromise between pitch elevation and loss of output acoustic power (loudness).

## Bearing on voice training
- Provides a biomechanical explanation for a clinical observation seen across surgical outcome papers in this cluster (mora2018, yilmaz2021): larger F0 gains from vocal-fold-shortening procedures come with a loudness/roughness tradeoff, not just an incidental side effect.
- Useful for framing "why" glottoplasty patients report roughness or vocal power loss, complementing purely clinical/outcome papers with a mechanistic model.

## Notes
- Not a clinical study — pure computational simulation using a single canonical male vocal-fold geometry; findings are theoretical/mechanistic, not directly generalizable to individual patient outcomes. Level of evidence: not applicable per the authors.
