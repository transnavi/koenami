# Adaptação transcultural do Voice-related Experiences of Nonbinary Individuals – VENI para o português brasileiro

## What it says
- The Brazilian adaptation of the VENI, the non-binary voice questionnaire whose English development is in the vault as [[shefcik2025]] (two of that paper's authors, Shefcik and Tsai, are co-authors here).
- Method: the WHO Guidelines on Translation combined with COSMIN, in five steps — two independent translations into Brazilian Portuguese (one by a translator who knows the construct, one who does not), a consensus synthesis, two back-translations by native English speakers, a committee of five voice-specialist speech-language pathologists producing the final version, and a virtual pre-test with 21 people from the target population (abstract, "Método").
- Disagreements to resolve: in translation, 「houve discordância no título, instruções, chave de resposta e em 15 itens」; in back-translation, form in 12 items and content in 4 items. The expert committee then changed the title, the response instructions, one answer-key option and eight items.
- In the pre-test, habitual responses were chosen significantly more often than the "não aplicável" option — taken as evidence the items fit. The final instrument is named 「Experiências relacionadas a Voz de Pessoas Não Binárias - VENI-Br」.

## Bearing on voice training
- Gives Brazilian practice a non-binary outcome measure at the same moment the VENI is being validated in English ([[shefcik2025]], [[nonbinary-voice]]). The vault's gap list notes how little non-binary outcome work exists; this is a translation, not an outcome study, but it is the instrument an outcome study would need.
- The 21-person pre-test population and the Brazilian naming choice (「Pessoas Não Binárias」 as a category, rather than folding the items into the trans-women questionnaire) are the interesting local decisions.

## Notes
- Adaptation study only: no therapy, no acoustic data, no comparison group, hence low.
- Claims taken from the Portuguese abstract; the English and Portuguese items were not compared item by item.
