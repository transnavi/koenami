## What it says
- Retrospective analysis, Department of Otorhinolaryngology Head and Neck Surgery, Beijing Friendship Hospital, Capital Medical University: 29 transgender women (age 19–47, mean 27.0±6.3) who underwent Wendler Glottoplasty (microlaryngoscopic vocal cord shortening) January 2017–October 2020, compared pre-op vs. 3 months post-op.
- Subjective outcome: Transsexual Voice Questionnaire for Male to Female (TVQMtF) score fell significantly, from 89.9±14.7 to 50.4±13.6 (t=11.49, P<0.001) — lower score indicates less voice-related handicap.
- F0 rose significantly from 152.7±23.3 Hz to 207.7±45.9 Hz (t=−6.03, P<0.001).
- F1, F2, F3 rose significantly; F4 showed no significant change.
- Highest pitch was not significantly changed; lowest pitch rose significantly, from 96.8±17.7 Hz to 120.0±28.9 Hz (t=−3.71, P=0.001).
- Habitual speech volume rose significantly, from 60.0±5.2 dB to 63.6±9.6 dB (t=−2.12, P=0.043).
- Jitter, shimmer, NHR and maximum phonation time (MPT) showed no significant change (P>0.05 for all).
- Authors' conclusion: Wendler Glottoplasty markedly raises vocal pitch, formant frequencies, and perceived femininity without impairing phonation ability or voice quality, and is an effective voice feminization treatment.

## Bearing on voice training
- Gives concrete before/after magnitudes for a single glottoplasty technique on both F0 and formants (F1–F3) in Mandarin-speaking patients, with the TVQMtF change as a validated-questionnaire proxy for perceived improvement — a useful surgical-outcome anchor to compare against voice-training-only F0/formant shifts.
- The unchanged jitter/shimmer/NHR/MPT after surgery suggests glottoplasty raised pitch without degrading baseline voice quality measures in this cohort, distinct from concerns about training-induced strain raised in [[huang2024]].

## Notes
- No control group; 3-month follow-up only, so longer-term stability is not addressed; single center (Beijing).
- TVQMtF is the (Cantonese-derived-lineage) Transsexual Voice Questionnaire for Male-to-Female, not the same instrument as TWVQ-SC in [[wang2022]].
