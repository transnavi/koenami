# Mapping sex and age effects on thyroarytenoid and cricothyroid muscle structure and function

## What it says
- Frames the two muscles this vault cares about: "the thyroarytenoid (TA) and cricothyroid (CT) muscles play a key role in controlling vocal fold tension, length, and closure, thereby influencing pitch and voice quality" (abstract).
- Morphometry on 44 retrospectively collected neck MRI scans: "consistent age-related decline of the TA muscle across all measured dimensions, whereas the CT muscle demonstrated a more selective pattern, with age independently associated with thickness and volume but not with length or height" (abstract).
- Electromyography in 29 healthy volunteers: "higher CT activation amplitudes in females, while increasing age was independently associated with lower activation amplitudes, higher resting amplitude, and longer return-to-rest time" (abstract).
- Conclusion: the intrinsic laryngeal muscles "may not respond uniformly to aging", and "age-related voice changes may reflect muscle-specific alterations in laryngeal structure and neuromuscular performance" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- Direct measurement of the two muscles a pitch exercise works on: the cricothyroid that lengthens and tenses the folds, and the thyroarytenoid that shortens and thickens them. The higher CT activation reported in women is the kind of finding that a pitch-raising protocol implicitly assumes; note it is an association in a small cohort, not a demonstration that training moves it.

## Notes
- Two independent cohorts (44 MRI scans, 29 EMG volunteers) from Greek university hospitals; the authors say so themselves. Online ahead of print at the time of verification, so pagination is not final.
