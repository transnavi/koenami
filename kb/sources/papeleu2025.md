# Effects of Intensive Intonation Training ... Aiming for a More Feminine-Sounding Voice: A Randomized Clinical Trial

## What it says
- Ghent randomised sham-controlled trial: 30 TGD people "presumed male at birth" aiming for a more feminine-sounding voice, allocated to either 4 weeks of intonation training first or 4 weeks of sham training followed by 4 weeks of intonation training (abstract, "METHOD"; a cross-over design, so every participant eventually trains).
- Recordings at baseline (group 2 only), pre, after training and 4 weeks later; acoustic and perceptual outcomes analysed with linear mixed models.
- "Statistically significant increases were found for all acoustic intonation parameters", plus significant increases in F0 median and in the 25th and 75th percentiles — the pitch *floor* rose, not just the average.
- Listening experiments showed "a significant increase in femininity ratings and increases in all perceptual intonation parameters".
- Participants reported their voice was more in line with their gender identity, "but they still needed voice and communication training (VCT)".

## Bearing on voice training
- The strongest design in this literature (randomised, sham-controlled) for a single training ingredient; it makes intonation a standalone, evidence-backed target rather than a by-product of pitch work [[intonation]] [[voice-therapy-techniques]].
- It is the feminine-direction twin of the same team's masculine-arm trial [[papeleu2026]]; together they test intonation in both directions.
- Raising the F0 25th percentile matches the argument that the *lower limit* of the speaking range drives masculine perception more than the mean [[fundamental-frequency]].

## Notes
- Sample 30, single centre, Dutch-speaking, 4-week training block. Follow-up in this report runs 4 weeks after training; the 3-month and 1-year acoustic follow-up is reported separately [[leyns2026]].
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract. Randomised Controlled Trial, indexed MEDLINE.
