# Vocal Weight (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Definition on the page fetched 2026-09-17: "Vocal weight is a perceived quality of voice. It describes a heaviness or rumble that is present in most masculine voices." "It can be thought of as an educated guess at the amount of Vocal Fold Vibratory Mass or 'how much of the vocal folds is moving'."
- "lighter vocal weight is considered more feminine, and heavier vocal weight is considered more masculine"; "heavy vocal weight is encouraged by low pitches and becomes more straining at high pitches"; "breathiness, unless we're making vocal weight heavier, is to be avoided"; "higher airflow and pressure encourages more vocal weight."
- "This is also known as: OQ/CQ open and closed quotient; vocal fold vibratory mass; thickness and thinness of the folds."
- "When we have high vocal weight at a high pitch, it becomes difficult and effortful or even straining because these muscles are fighting against eachother. This means that high pitches that are relaxed are also most likely light in vocal weight."
- Named lightening tools in the table of contents: PIPM / Pitch Naturalisation, the "awww" trigger, pitch slides, yawning. Named darkening tool: "Pitch Slide Vocal Weight 'Layering'." Warnings listed: hyperadduction; pressed phonation; "the heaviest vocal weight we can do is going to be the lowest note we can do before we go into vocal fry."
- The page is part of [[sumianvoice]]'s open wiki; the front page says the resource is "still mostly feminisation oriented."

## Bearing on voice training
- The most explicit English-community equation of vocal weight with closed quotient and fold mass. Acoustic literature measures related source features (open quotient, H1–H2, spectral tilt) without using this name ([[hanson1997]], [[hanson1999]], [[holmberg1988]]).
- The strain warning — high pitch plus heavy weight — is the community's version of not belting a feminized speaking voice.
