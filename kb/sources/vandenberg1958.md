# Myoelastic-Aerodynamic Theory of Voice Production

## What it says
- The record itself: an 18-page article by Janwillem van den Berg in the first volume of the Journal of Speech and Hearing Research, September 1958, which gave the theory its name — the one still used for how self-sustained vocal-fold vibration works.
- The theory as later stated: vibration is sustained by the interaction between the elastic and muscular properties of the folds ("myoelastic") and the pressure and flow of air passing between them ("aerodynamic"), not by a nerve impulse per cycle [[wikipedia-phonation]].

## Bearing on voice training
- Explains why pitch and loudness are set by two different controls: fold tension and length on one side, breath pressure on the other, with a measurable amount of cross-talk between them [[titze1989a]].
