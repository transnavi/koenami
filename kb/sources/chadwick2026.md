# Outcomes of Gender-Affirming Voice and Communication Modification Training for Non-binary Individuals: A Case Series

## What it says
- Retrospective consecutive case series of non-binary and genderqueer (NBGQ) individuals enrolled in a 12-week gender-affirming voice and communication modification training programme; the authors state there is "currently no research reporting solely on outcomes of voice and communication modification training (VCMT) in individuals who identify as non-binary and genderqueer in the English literature" (abstract, "Objectives", "Methods").
- Four participants enrolled between January 2019 and June 2021; mean age 27.0 years. Measures: Transgender Self-Evaluation Questionnaire (TSEQ), F0 and frequency range, before and after.
- "While all four participants represented in this case series showed improvement in at least one of their initial goals, only one improved both their F0 and TSEQ scores; the other three participants had mixed results."
- Conclusion: participants "experienced improvements in self-reported outcomes and changes in acoustic measures after completing VCMT", with "significant improvement in subjective outcomes despite small changes in acoustic measures, and vice versa".

## Bearing on voice training
- Shows why non-binary targets break the standard outcome model: across four people, acoustic change and self-reported improvement moved independently, so neither can stand in for the other [[nonbinary-voice]] [[patient-reported-outcomes]].
- Twelve weeks of training, four people, mixed results — the honest state of the evidence for behavioural work aimed at something other than one binary endpoint [[voice-therapy-techniques]] [[fundamental-frequency]].

## Notes
- n = 4, retrospective, single US centre (Weill Cornell / Stony Brook), no control group, level of evidence 4 as stated in the abstract. The abstract does not report the individual goals or the F0 values.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract (J Voice 2026 Jan;40(1); Epub 2023 Sep 4).
