# The Experiences of Transgender and Gender Diverse Individuals after Intonation Training for a More Feminine-Sounding Voice: A Qualitative Study

## What it says
- Semi-structured interviews with 10 participants from the Ghent intonation-training studies — "all identifying as (trans)feminine" — held more than a year after the 4-week training block, analysed with Reflexive Thematic Analysis (NVivo 11), with investigator triangulation, member checking and an audit trail (abstract, "Methods and Procedures").
- Participants "generally experienced gender-affirming intonation training positively, noting improvements in voice, communication, and well-being".
- Named as the factors that mattered: "a client-centred approach, a strong therapeutic relationship and a supportive environment".
- Named as persisting problems: "ongoing challenges with technique internalization and dissatisfaction with vocal outcomes".
- The authors conclude that "lasting success depends on individualized support, therapeutic alliance and social feedback" and call for counselling to be part of gender-affirming VCT.

## Bearing on voice training
- The internalisation complaint — carrying a trained pattern into unpractised speech — is the trained voice's version of the transfer problem; here it is a participant-reported theme, not an acoustic finding [[voice-therapy-techniques]] [[patient-reported-outcomes]].
- Says plainly that the therapy relationship and the surrounding social feedback are part of the intervention, not packaging around it [[speech-language-pathology]].

## Notes
- n = 10, self-selected from trials run by the same authors' group, in one Belgian centre; the abstract does not say who conducted the interviews. Qualitative, no acoustic measures — the acoustic results are in [[papeleu2025]].
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract (Int J Lang Commun Disord 2026 Sep-Oct).
