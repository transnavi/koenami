# Effects of lung volume on vertical larynx position during phonation

## What it says
- Larynx height measured with a multi-channel electroglottograph in 29 healthy, vocally untrained subjects phonating at different lung volumes, pitches and loudness levels (abstract).
- "High lung volume was clearly associated with a lower larynx position as compared to low lung volume" (abstract).
- "Vertical larynx position was strongly correlated with pitch" (abstract).
- "Both of these dependencies were shown to be stronger in males than in females" (abstract).
- Context given by the authors: a high larynx "is often seen in hyperfunctional and strained voices, [so] a lowering of a habitually elevated larynx is sometimes a specific goal in clinical voice therapy" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- Breathing and resonance are not independent exercises. Where you are in the breath moves the larynx up or down, which changes vocal-tract length and therefore the formants — so a learner practising a raised-larynx brightness at the end of a breath, and a lowered one at the start, is partly measuring lung volume rather than technique.
- Pitch drags larynx height with it, which is why pitch and resonance have to be trained apart before they can be combined.

## Notes
- 29 untrained Swedish speakers; larynx height inferred from multi-channel EGG rather than imaged directly.
