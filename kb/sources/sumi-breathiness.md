# Breathiness (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Mechanism: "When the vocal folds come apart (abduction) and air is able to rush through without helping the folds vibrate, we get some turbulence, and that is expressed as noise - breathiness. Adduction is the opposite, where the folds are pressed more together."
- "Closure" is defined as "the amount of air that's used in speech vs the amount of air that gets let through", and the page lays out a scale from "full closure, glottal stop, no air getting through" through "pressed phonation and creak, hyperadduction", "slight adduction used to add brightness", "flow phonation, balanced closure", "darkening abduction (think DJ announcer voice)", "breathy", "overblown, airplaning", to "no closure, just breathing / whisper". "If we add too much closure we get hyperadduction. If we add too little closure we get breathiness."
- On consequences: "breathiness can hide vocal weight (which is not a good thing) and makes it harder to train most characteristics. It also darkens the voice."
- Exercises given: *hard onsets* — "Normally, we use semi soft onsets. Aspirate onsets encourage breathy phonation, soft onsets encourage flow phonation, and hard onsets slightly encourage adducted / non-breathy phonation"; the page advises against sliding *to* a glottal stop, but says we "can start at a glottal stop and speak from that".
- *Humming* — "Humming tends to very slightly encourage less breathiness when left to become relaxed and passive ... make it as light as possible, imitating a sinewave. At this point, we should be in a low airflow, adducted state and therefore be less breathy."
- *Breathiness stepping* — start at default, go very breathy, then non-breathy, then speak; "Often we can end up less breathy than when we started", and repeating "can add layers of decreased breathiness". Explicit safety line: "It's very important, as with any exercise but especially when dealing with closure, to not add effort in this exercise."
- *Using creak* — "Since creak (not fry / M0) is a relaxed, adducted, low airflow configuration, we can use it to start non breathy and then add the slightest bit of air to come out of creak into flow phonation."

## Bearing on voice training
- States the community's position on breathiness as a training tool: avoid it, because it hides vocal weight and makes everything else harder to learn — matching the wiki's vocal-weight page and the meta-analytic uncertainty about breathiness as a femininity cue [[vocal-weight]] [[breathiness]] [[leung2018]].
- Gives an explicit adduction scale with six useful positions, which is the clearest written version of the "closure" continuum in the community material [[voice-quality]].
- The "don't add effort, especially when dealing with closure" warning is the safety boundary the wiki repeats around every closure exercise [[voice-health]].
