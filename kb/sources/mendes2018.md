# The effects of phonation into glass, plastic, and LaxVox tubes in singers: a systematic review

## What it says
- Systematic review of tube-phonation therapy in singers, searched February 2017 and updated October 2017 in LILACS, SciELO, PubMed and Cochrane, with no restriction of year, language or publication status. Eligibility agreement between examiners was kappa 0.88. Risk of bias was judged with Joanna Briggs Institute tools.
- Of 1965 records, six articles remained eligible, together accounting for 141 singers (65 men, 76 women) aged 18–72.
- "Electroglottography was considered as the most common method (83.33%) of assessment." The most prevalent exercises were "phonation into straws and phonation into glass tubes."
- Conclusion: "The phonation into glass tubes immersed in water, straws, and LaxVox tubes promoted positive effects on the voice quality in singers, such as more comfortable phonation, better voice projection, and economy in voice emission."

## Bearing on voice training
- The singer-specific counterpart of [[pozzali2021]] and [[asadollahpour2025]]: tube phonation (straw, glass, LaxVox) is the warm-up the singing studio already uses, and the reported benefits are comfort, projection and economy — the same reasons SOVT is handed to a trans speaker before a pitch session. Six small studies, EGG-heavy, no trans sample.

## Notes
- Print issue May 2019; epub 3 May 2018 (PMID 29731378). Claims from the PubMed abstract. "Positive effects" are the authors' synthesis; the abstract does not report a meta-analytic effect size.
