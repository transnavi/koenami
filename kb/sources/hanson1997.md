# Glottal characteristics of female speakers: Acoustic correlates

## What it says
- Abstract: aim is "to formulate a set of acoustic parameters of the voicing source that reflect individual differences in the voice qualities of female speakers."
- "a more open glottal configuration results in a glottal volume-velocity waveform with relatively greater low-frequency and weaker high-frequency components, compared to a waveform produced with a more adducted glottal configuration."
- The more open configuration "also leads to a greater source of aspiration noise and larger bandwidths of the natural frequencies of the vocal tract, particularly the first formant."
- Those attributes "can be measured directly from the speech spectrum or waveform"; the paper describes a parameter set, measures it in a group of female speakers, and hypothesizes each speaker's glottal configuration.
- Framed as describing "normal variations of voicing characteristics across speakers" and as an aid to "analysis and synthesis of female speech," with possible clinical use.

## Bearing on voice training
- This is the acoustic literature the community's "light / breathy / open" pole points at: more open glottis → stronger low harmonics relative to high ones (the H1–H2 family of measures) plus aspiration noise. Community "vocal weight" talks about the other pole — more adduction, more fold mass ([[sumi-vocal-weight]], [[erose2020a]]).
- The paper studies cis female speakers' individual differences, not gender-affirming training.
