# Trans Male Voice in the First Year of Testosterone Therapy: Make No Assumptions

## What it says
- Seven trans men assessed twice at baseline and once each at 3, 6, 9 and 12 months after starting testosterone, tracking pitch (fundamental frequency), vocal quality, phonation frequency range, habitual pitch level and self-perception (abstract).
- Vocal quality measures "varied between and within participants but were generally within normal limits throughout the year"; mean F0 during reading decreased "to variable extents and rates"; phonation frequency range shifted down the scale in the group but rose in some individuals (abstract).
- Combining mean F0 and frequency range into a habitual-pitch-level measure showed most participants spoke with an F0 low within their own range compared with cisgender norms (abstract).
- Self-reported voice masculinization was not correlated with mean F0, frequency range or habitual pitch level at any time point, nor with F0 change from baseline to 1 year; the authors caution that correlations are unreliable with seven heterogeneous participants (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The companion speech-science reading to [[irwig2017a]] (same cohort): acoustics and self-perception move independently during the first testosterone year, so a satisfied trans man is not evidence of a particular F0, and an unchanged F0 is not evidence of dissatisfaction — assessment must measure both ([[voice-masculinization]], [[patient-reported-outcomes]]).

## Notes
- n = 7, no control group; the title's "make no assumptions" is the paper's own framing of its scatter.
