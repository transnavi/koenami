# Auditory-Perceptual and Acoustic Methods in Measuring Dysphonia Severity of Korean Speech

## What it says
- 60 native Korean speakers with various voice disorders sustained [a:] and read the Korean text "Walk" ("걷기" — a standard Korean passage); 56 recordings passed signal-to-noise checks (abstract).
- GRBAS "G" and CAPE-V "Overall Severity" correlated at rS 0.955 (sustained vowels) and 0.965 (continuous speech) (abstract).
- The Acoustic Voice Quality Index (AVQI), computed on the concatenated vowel-plus-speech material, correlated strongly with G (rS 0.911) and with OS (rP 0.924) (abstract).
- Conclusion: supports "the cross-linguistic robustness of the AVQI as a valid and objective marker of overall dysphonia severity" in Korean (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- Establishes the Korean-language measurement stack (GRBAS/CAPE-V/AVQI on a Korean standard passage) that a Korean clinic or 언어재활사 would use to track voice quality — relevant when a feminization-training or post-surgery voice needs dysphonia monitoring [[voice-quality]] [[korean-voice]].
- Co-authored by the Yeson Voice Center director, linking the Korean clinical voice-assessment tradition to the feminization practice ([[yeson-voice-center]]).

## Notes
- Voice-disorder population; not a gendered-voice study — cited for the Korean-language validation of the measures, not for any gender claim.
