# Ephemerides: A Microtonal Feedback Instrument Based On Transgender Voice Training Technique

A NIME 2026 paper by June Kuhn and Andrew McPherson (Imperial College London, Augmented Instruments Lab), from the RUDIMENTS project, describing a musical instrument that plays the vocal tract like a filter without using the vocal folds. It is not a therapy tool, and it says so.

## What it says
- What the instrument is: 「This instrument does not require the use of vocal cords; the musician manipulates resonances in the vocal tract to change pitch with the help of audio feedback.」 The abstract adds that 「Concerning this unusual way of producing voice, the instrument questions what constitutes intention, agency, and gender in singing.」
- Signal path (section 3.1): 「Ephemerides consists of a Max patch, microphone, voice coil transducer, and the vocal tract. The signal from the Max patch plays through the voice coil transducer, which the musician then places firmly against their throat […]. The resonances of the throat and inside of mouth filter the signal from the transducer. The resonant frequencies are then picked up by the headset microphone, where the strongest resonance is reinforced by the feedback path.」 An oscillator bank plays several pitches at once; 「The musician 'selects' pitches from the oscillator bank by changing their vocal resonance, without the use of vocal cords. This is accomplished by manipulating the larynx, tongue, and soft palate, similar to techniques used in transgender voice training […].」
- What it deliberately does not do: 「Ephemerides only uses a microphone as a sensor, without detection of vowels, formant frequencies, or highest frequency peaks in the digital signal processing. In direct contrast to voice training apps that aim to 'detect' vocal resonance through formant analysis, the role of listening is passed on to the musician and the audience.」
- Why it avoided gender detection (section 3.2): 「Our first technical explorations included vowel synthesis and formant analysis to connect signal processing with gendered characteristics. The idea was to connect spectral qualities of gender in voice to digital mapping parameters. After further reflection, the idea of detecting gender, even indirectly for the purposes of modulation, leads to a slippery slope in determining gender through machine listening. Gender in voice cannot be wholly represented in or analysed from an audio file; it is fluid, dynamic, and culturally relative.」
- The technique it draws on, stated in its own terms: 「Gender-based voice training is an emerging practice, taking influence from both musical technique and speech therapy」; 「Apart from pitch and inflection, vocal resonance is one of the primary focuses of voice training as it is one of the biggest audible factors in a testosterone puberty. Vocal resonance is a quality of human voice determined by the shape and size of the vocal tract, distinct from the pitch of the voice.」 It adds that 「Many transfeminine people who go through voice training therapy face difficulty with manipulating vocal resonance, as it is a quality more abstract then the fundamental pitch.」
- The Noticing study (section 3.3) is the empirical part: four sessions, once a week in July 2025, using the Methods for Noticing Workbook, in which the first author sang at a steady pitch while sliding resonance high to low and low to high. The field notes quoted include 「At first there's that twinge of dysphoria, what if someone hears me or perceives me, or worse, that I perceive myself as male again? But I slipped into it and the maleness became a bit decontextualised as I drone from resonance to resonance」 and, on nasality, 「A lot of feminine voice training is about avoiding nasality, and found myself actively hitting a level of it just to see how it would sound and what it would feel like… It's one of those clocky features of transfeminine voice that comes from early on training and takes months or years to move away from.」
- The paper's own distinction between its exercises and training: 「These exercises bear strong resemblance to voice training exercises in how they are performed, but they are distinctly different in that they were not aimed at skill-building, but listening.」
- What it observed about the effect: 「By using sinusoids on a transducer instead of using vocal cords, the sense of imagined gender in the sound is modified. This kind of auditory imagery is not pitch-based, but timbre- and culture-based. While Ephemerides did not move us closer to directly manipulating or modulating gender presentation in voice as originally desired, it appears to relocate the imagined sense of vocal identity in-between them and the Max patch.」 And: 「The pitches of Ephemerides do not match typical vocal ranges, and so it sounds like the 'singing' could be of any gender.」
- On transnormativity (section 4.3): 「Ephemerides shares a similar sentiment in standing in opposition to transnormativity, which is the idea that there is a correct way to transition, or that transgender identity is in conflict with gender non-conforming identities. Even if gender based vocal training is used in clinical settings to acheive gender affirmation, it can still perpetuate transnormativity.」
- Limits it states about the instrument: a learning curve — 「As Ephemerides involves manipulating a group of muscles that are not commonly exercised in daily speech or vocal practice, there's a learning curve before the average musician or vocalist could produce a level of accuracy and versatility normally expected from a musical instrument」; and on scales, 「A current limitation is that it cannot produce scales with uneven spacing between pitches, such as just intonation, Arabic or Indian scales」.
- It reads the larynx as the source: quoting Sterne, 「A laryngeal vocality renders vocalization as a process made up of many elements and components. The laryngeal voice is more like an instrument, less like magic or a soul.」
- Disclaimers: 「we do not claim that this instrument in any way represents or speaks for the transgender community, nor does this instrument represent the experience of vocal dysphoria. This instrument is a product of creative practice, and not intended as a tool for use in therapy.」 The authors state they used no generative AI, no participants other than themselves, and plan to release the code open source; funding is the ERC Consolidator Grant RUDIMENTS (EP/X023478/1).

## Bearing on voice training
- It is a musical instrument, not an intervention, and the note records it because it is the only artefact found that takes the community's central manoeuvre — shape the tract, leave the folds alone — and builds a practice around it ([[sumi-resonance]], [[formants]]).
- The rationale it gives for refusing formant detection is the strongest statement in the vault against app-based voice scoring from a design-research side: machine listening that names gender is refused on principle, not for accuracy ([[machine-gender-scoring]], [[transvoice-app]]).
- Its account of the technique it borrows is a compact external description of voice feminisation as it is taught in the community — resonance as the abstract part, manipulated through larynx, tongue and soft palate, with nasality the habit hard to leave behind ([[sumi-nasality]], [[voice-feminization-therapy]]).
- The Noticing study is a first-person record of vocal dysphoria during singing, which the vault otherwise holds mainly as clinical categories ([[voice-impressions]], [[transgender-care-context]]).

## Notes
- A practice-based design paper: n = the first author, no outcome measures, no acoustic analysis of the instrument's output, and no clinical claim. It cites the community-facing literature only lightly (Davies & Goldberg 2006, Kawitzky & McAllister 2020, Camargo et al. 2012, Povinelli & Zhao 2024, Holmberg et al. 2023) and states no research with trans voice training participants of its own.
- The deposited record also carries an audio file (nime2026_29_file01.wav, 54 MB) and supplementary recordings of the Noticing sessions and ensemble performances; none of the audio was played.
