## What it says
- Randomized controlled crossover trial with 30 transgender women, comparing pitch elevation training (PET) versus articulation-resonance training (ART), each block 4–5 weeks, all participants receiving both trainings in one order or the other after an initial sham-training period.
- Participants self-rated via the Trans Woman Voice Questionnaire (TWVQ) and visual analogue scales (VAS); 75 listeners rated recordings on continuous masculinity–femininity VAS and categorical masculinity–femininity attribution at four timepoints (pre, post-sham, post-training-1, post-training-2).
- Participants rated their own voices as more feminine after training, with positive impact on vocal functioning and voice-related quality of life; many still felt they needed more training after 10 weeks.
- Listeners also rated participants' voices as more feminine after training on both continuous and categorical measures; combining ART and PET (in either order) produced higher femininity scores than either training alone, with no significant order effect.

## Bearing on voice training
- Direct clinical-trial evidence that pitch elevation and articulation-resonance training are complementary rather than substitutable — combined training outperformed either alone on both self- and listener-rated femininity, regardless of which was done first.
- Useful benchmark for how much training (10 weeks, ~9–10 sessions total across both modalities) shifts listener-rated femininity, and evidence that participant-perceived need for further training persists even after measurable listener-rated gains.

## Notes
- Crossover design with sham-training control block; 30 participants, 75 listeners — moderate sample size for a controlled trial in this literature.
- Short-term outcomes only (measured within the ~14-week training period); compare leyns2024 and Leyns et al.'s long-term follow-up work.
