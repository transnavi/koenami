## What it says
- Used a parameter-specific morphing method (nonuniform scale factors, allowing more direct comparison of parameter impact than spectral-envelope shifting) to independently vary F0, formant frequencies (FF), aperiodicity (noise), and spectrum level (SL, related to spectral tilt/source characteristics) between male and female speaker pairs.
- In two experiments, 16 listeners (8F/8M) classified voice gender for morphs varying single parameters or selected combinations, with all other parameters held androgynous.
- F0 was the strongest single cue to gender perception, followed by FF, then SL; aperiodicity did not systematically influence gender perception.
- Combined F0+FF morphing produced changes in perceived gender equivalent to morphing all parameters together; FF+SL combined (without F0) also produced effective gender-perception shifts.
- Conclusion: F0 and formant/vocal-tract resonance cues have comparable overall impact on voice-gender perception, and their relative contribution depends on which combination is manipulated.

## Bearing on voice training
- One of the cleanest controlled dissections of which acoustic dimensions actually drive gender perception: ranks F0 > formants > spectrum level, with periodicity/breathiness noise contributing little on its own — useful for prioritizing training targets (pitch and resonance first; voice-quality/breathiness training may matter more for naturalness than for the binary gender categorization itself).
- The finding that FF+SL together can shift perceived gender even without F0 change suggests resonance-plus-spectral-tilt work has value for speakers who cannot or do not want to raise/lower pitch substantially.

## Notes
- Stimuli were morphs (resynthesis) between only 2 male–female speaker pairs per experiment; small, controlled but low-ecological-validity design typical of parameter-isolation studies.
- German-speaking sample (Jena); syllable-token stimuli, not connected speech.
