## What it says
- 40 communicators (transgender and cisgender) were recorded on cartoon retell, sustained vowel, and carrier-phrase tasks; 20 raters then judged gender attribution (man/woman/androgynous — a three-way rather than binary choice), masculinity–femininity, and vocal naturalness from the cartoon-retell recordings.
- Multinomial logistic regression found mean fundamental frequency (f0) was the sole acoustic measure that changed the odds of being attributed as a woman or as androgynous rather than as a man.
- Multiple linear regression identified mean f0, average formant frequency of /i/, and mean sound pressure level as predictors of masculinity–femininity ratings, and mean f0, average formant frequency, and speech rate as predictors of vocal-naturalness ratings.
- Concludes results support continuing to target f0 and vocal-tract resonance in feminization/masculinization training, with additional evidence for attending to vocal intensity and speech rate to preserve naturalness.

## Bearing on voice training
- Directly supports the standard training targets (pitch, resonance/formants) as the acoustic measures with demonstrated predictive power over both binary gender attribution and continuous masculinity–femininity ratings.
- Novel contribution: identifies sound pressure level (loudness/intensity) and speech rate as predictors of naturalness ratings specifically — meaning a voice can hit target pitch/resonance and still be rated as "unnatural" if intensity and rate are not also addressed, which matters for avoiding a strained-sounding trained voice.
- Uses a three-way gender-attribution scale (man/woman/androgynous) rather than forced binary choice, which is a more ecologically valid design for populations including nonbinary-identifying speakers.

## Notes
- Sample of 40 speakers spans transgender and cisgender participants without further breakdown given in the abstract; regression results are correlational/predictive, not causal manipulations (contrast skuk2014, cartei2013 resynthesis designs).
- Raters (n=20) and stimuli limited to a single speech task (cartoon retell) for perceptual ratings.
