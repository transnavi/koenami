## What it says
- Systematic review and meta-analysis from the Department of Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery of Hospital das Clínicas da Universidade Federal de Pernambuco, Brazil; PRISMA method, searching Medline/PubMed, Cochrane, ScienceDirect, LILACS and Google Scholar with no date limit, and including studies "in English, Portuguese or Spanish".
- 14 studies, 566 patients; 13 entered the meta-analysis. Heterogeneity between studies in follow-up time, surgical technique and perioperative care was significant.
- Pooled pre- to post-glottoplasty mean differences: F0 +78.49 Hz (95% CI 75.69–81.30); maximum phonation time −1.11 s (95% CI −1.67 to −0.54); frequency range −3.55 semitones (95% CI −5.74 to −1.36); GRBAS grade of dysphonia +0.44 (95% CI 0.27–0.61).
- States in the introduction that "for 20% of patients seeking voice feminization, voice therapy is not completely satisfactory, and surgery should be considered".
- Concludes glottoplasty significantly raises F0 but slightly reduces maximum phonation time, frequency range and voice quality as measured by GRBAS.

## Bearing on voice training
- Quantifies the trade the surgical route makes: about +78 Hz bought with a measurable loss of range and a small but real rise in perceived dysphonia — the same trade [[titze2020]] models and [[yilmaz2021]] reports in one cohort.
- Because the search included Portuguese and Spanish, it is the meta-analysis most likely to have captured Ibero-American surgical series; a useful anchor for Spanish- and Portuguese-language claims about surgery ([[munoz2025]]).

## Notes
- Included studies are almost all single-arm pre/post series with no control group, so the pooled effect carries their bias.
- Follow-up length varies widely across the 14 studies; the pooled F0 gain is not a statement about durability.
