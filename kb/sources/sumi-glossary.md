# Glossary (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- The wiki's own definitions of its working vocabulary, grouped under headings: Sound Production Related, Pitch and Range Related, Resonance Related, Clarity Related, Exercise and Training Related, Cover Related, Speech Pattern Related, Anatomy Related.
- **Glottal behaviour** — "covers all behaviours to do with phonation / sound production ... a catch all term for how the voice is produced and how 'cleanly' it is produced ... has a lot to do with clarity and tone purity or HNR."
- **Vocal weight** — "how 'heavy' the voice sounds; doesn't take into account volume, pressed phonation, breathiness. Vocal Fold Vibratory Mass is likely the underlying cause of the sound."
- **Quotient / Open / Closed** — "measurement of how long it takes for the vocal folds to open vs how long it takes for them to close; used in research because it's objective and it's a good but not perfect correlation to vocal fold vibratory mass / vocal weight; usually measured using EGG / electroglottography". Open quotient is "related to low vocal weight"; closed quotient "related to high vocal weight".
- **Volume** — "the loudness of a voice; usually from airflow-closure more than vocal weight or pressed phonation." **Base pitch** — "the pitch a voice naturally returns to after intonation; defined by vocal weight / fold mass due to the connection between fold mass and pitch".
- **Formants and which manoeuvre moves them**: "F1 is the first formant and is often altered through larynx height and OPC; F2 is the second formant and often altered through mouth space; F3 is from the difference in size between the mouth and the space between the teeth ...; F4 is from the same but lips."
- **Macrovowel** — "the use of a vowel shape to color speech; keeping the mouth in the same position / posture as a certain vowel when speaking." **Microvowel** — "to modify individual vowels to be darker or brighter without changing how the vowel is interpreted." **OPC** — "raises resonance by decreasing the width of the back of the mouth; affects formants F1 and F2; associated with loli voice and some 'anime' voices, hyperfem, 'small' voices."
- **Twang / AES constriction** — "at the cost of buzziness (not vocal weight), adds the impression of high resonance ... seen in some country singers, some Australian accents; often induces hypernasality."
- **Subharmonics** — "rough quality similar to creak/fry; extra harmonics appear; one in between each harmonic, essentially making it as if the pitch was halved; this is due to every 2nd cycle of the vocal folds being interrupted ... can be a sign of slight FVF constriction interfering."
- **FVF (false vocal folds) constriction** — "'white noise' quality in background is louder and may be present even when not breathy; increased subharmonics; sometimes a 'rattle' like quality ... bad for vocal health in the long term."
- **Brightness** — "a perceptual quality caused by the amount of high frequency harmonic information ... a high resonance voice can sound dark due to muting effects like nasality and breathiness; clarity, vocal weight and twang are the main factors that allow resonance to highlight upper frequencies." The entry marks one older sense as "outdated": "the frequency of formants, particularly F1, essentially resonance (outdated)".
- **Breath support** — "low support voices are typically unstable and fluctuate in pitch uncontrollably, may flip between m1 and M2 unpredictably ... putting a fair bit of pressure into the /s/ sound can induce better support; M2 and quiet voices tend to be low in support without training."
- **SOVTEs, VFEs** — "Semi Occluded Vocal Tract Exercise and Vocal Function Exercises; helps give a baseline for airflow and glottal behaviour ... helps warmup and warmdown the voice to help with vocal health and fatigue; can show if there are any voice related problems."
- **Vocal size** carries a warning: "do not try to change the size of your voice intuitively or without proper exercises / guidance unless you know not to constrict or use extraneous muscles!"

## Bearing on voice training
- The community's definitions are written to be operational: which manoeuvre moves F1 versus F2, what OQ/CQ correlate with, what volume is not. In several places it lines up with the acoustic literature's own account [[formants]] [[vocal-weight]] [[vocal-tract-anatomy]].
- Its distinctive claims are the mappings: closed quotient to vocal weight, subharmonics to false-fold constriction, twang to an *impression* of resonance at the cost of buzziness [[voice-quality]] [[harmonics-and-spectrum]].
- The glossary marks its own obsolete usage ("outdated" beside the F1-as-brightness sense), which is the kind of internal maintenance the wiki's terminology page argues for [[sumi-questionable-terms]].
