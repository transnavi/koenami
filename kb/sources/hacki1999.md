# Development of the child's voice: premutation, mutation

## What it says
- Voice range profile (phonetogram) measurement in 180 children aged 4–12 with no voice pathology, ten boys and ten girls per age group; F0 and SPL displayed in real time for speaking, shouting and singing voice (abstract).
- "Vocal capabilities grow with advancing age, but not continuously" (abstract).
- "The lowering of the habitual pitch of the speaking voice as well as of the entire speaking pitch range occurs for girls between the ages of 7 and 8, for boys between 8 and 9" (abstract).
- A temporary loss of the ability to speak or sing softly appears in both sexes at 7–8; maximum speech intensity falls for girls at 7–8 and boys at 8–9; shouting pitch and intensity drop in both sexes from age 10 (abstract).
- "In contrast to earlier general opinion we note for girls a stage of premutation (between the age of 7 and 8) with essentially the same changes seen among boys, but 1 year earlier. The beginning of the mutation can be fixed at the age of 10-11 years" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- Shows the child's voice already stepping down before puberty proper, in both sexes. "Mutation" (声変わり, Stimmbruch) is the pubertal stage; "premutation" is the earlier, milder one the authors report at 7–8.

## Notes
- German clinic sample, 180 children, cross-sectional by age group rather than longitudinal.
