## What it says
- 30 Hebrew-speaking transgender women were recorded, self-rated their own voice femininity, and completed the Hebrew version of the TVQMtF; recordings were analyzed for mean F0, formant frequencies (F1, F2, F3), and vocal range (in Hz and in semitones), and rated by 20 naïve cisgender listeners on a 7-point voice-gender scale.
- Both F0 and F1 significantly correlated with listeners' and speakers' evaluations of voice femininity.
- TVQMtF scores significantly correlated with F0 and with the lower and upper boundaries of vocal range.
- Voice-femininity ratings correlated strongly with vocal range when measured in Hz, but not when measured in semitones.
- Listener and speaker self-evaluations of voice femininity were significantly correlated with each other, but TVQMtF scores correlated only with speakers' own femininity ratings, not with listeners' ratings.
- Concludes higher F0 and F1 jointly improve speaker satisfaction, and that self-evaluation does not mirror listener judgment because it is shaped by additional self-satisfaction and personal-experience factors; recommends combining listener, speaker, and acoustic measures for a holistic view.

## Bearing on voice training
- Adds F1 (not just F0/F2) as an acoustic correlate of both listener- and speaker-rated femininity, useful for the concepts page on formants and gender perception.
- The disconnect between TVQMtF scores (correlating with self-perception, not listener perception) is an important methodological point: patient-reported outcome scores are not simply a proxy for how well a listener perceives the voice as feminine.
- Hebrew-language validation data point for the TVQMtF instrument family.

## Notes
- n=30 speakers, 20 listeners; single-language (Hebrew), single-country (Israel) sample; cross-sectional, no pre/post treatment comparison.
