# Vocal register (English Wikipedia)

## What it says
- Summary endpoint, 2026-09-17: "A vocal register is a range of tones in the human voice produced by a particular vibratory pattern of the vocal folds. These registers include modal voice, vocal fry, falsetto, and the whistle register. Registers originate in laryngeal function. They occur because the vocal folds are capable of producing several different vibratory patterns. Each of these vibratory patterns appears within a particular range of pitches and produces certain characteristic sounds."
- The article therefore takes Hollien's laryngeal line ([[hollien1974]]): a register is a vibratory pattern, not a place in the body where the sound is felt.

## Bearing on voice training
- The four-register list (fry, modal, falsetto, whistle) is the speech-pathology triad plus whistle, matching M0–M3 in [[roubeau2009]]. Singing-studio words (chest, head, mix, belt) are not this list; they live in [[wikipedia-head-voice]], [[wikipedia-chest-voice]] and [[wikipedia-belting-music]].
