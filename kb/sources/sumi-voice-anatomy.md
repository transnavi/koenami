# Voice Anatomy (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Framing: "While usually unnecessary to train our voices, being aware of the physical changes happening in the vocal tract can provide useful context." The page is built around Sam Webster's anatomy video with timestamped bookmarks: "01:58 hyoid bone", "02:35 epiglottis", "03:18 thyroid cartilage, laryngeal prominence 'adams apple'", "03:27 cricoid cartilage", "04:05 aryrenoid cartilage", "05:20 cuneiform cartilages", "06:06 thyrohyoid muscle", "06:38 vocal folds", "07:20 false vocal folds", "09:04 pitch, adduction and abduction", "10:57 cricothyroid muscle, thyroid tilt for pitch", "12:58 thyroarytenoid muscles", "13:20 arytenoid muscles - adduction", "13:57 posterior cricoarytenoid muscles - abduction", "14:25 lateral cricoarytenoid muscles - adduction".
- The wiki's own summary notes: "The thyroid tilts in order to increase or decrease pitch, by stretching or relaxing the vocal folds." "CT: The cricothyroid muscle tilts the thyroid forward, increasing pitch." "TA: The thyroarytenoid muscles tilts the thyroid backward, decreasing pitch. (also responsible for heavier vocal weight(?))" — the question mark is on the page.
- Adduction and abduction: "The arytenoid muscles adduct (bring together) the vocal folds along with the lateral cricoarytenoid muscles. The posterior cricoarytenoid muscles abduct (pull apart) the vocal folds. Note: these are only used for breathing, not speech."
- Epiglottis: "helps encourage food and so on not to pass through the airway (but the vocal folds close as well when swallowing in order to be sure)."

## Bearing on voice training
- The community's anatomy primer, and notably it keeps the biomechanics to what a learner needs: cricothyroid tilt for pitch, arytenoid and lateral cricoarytenoid for closure, posterior cricoarytenoid for breath [[larynx-anatomy]] [[phonation]].
- The thyroarytenoid line is the physical backing for the community's central perceptual category, vocal weight, and the wiki marks the link with a question mark rather than asserting it [[vocal-weight]] [[vocal-folds]].
- "These are only used for breathing, not speech" about the posterior cricoarytenoids is the wiki's way of saying that opening the folds for speech is a release, not a muscular act.
