# Vocal Fry May Undermine the Success of Young Women in the Labor Market

## What it says
- "Vocal fry is speech that is low pitched and creaky sounding, and is increasingly common among young American females."
- In a large national sample of American adults, "relative to a normal speaking voice, young adult female voices exhibiting vocal fry are perceived as less competent, less educated, less trustworthy, less attractive, and less hirable."
- "The negative perceptions of vocal fry are stronger for female voices relative to male voices."
- The authors' own recommendation: "young American females should avoid using vocal fry speech in order to maximize labor market opportunities."

## Bearing on voice training
- The same feature that [[wolk2012]] finds in two thirds of young women is penalised by listeners, and penalised more when the speaker is heard as a woman. Any advice about fry is therefore advice about a social penalty, not about vocal pathology.

## Notes
- The listeners rated recorded voices, not real candidates; no hiring outcome was measured. The paper's closing advice is the authors' recommendation, and it has been criticised elsewhere as placing the burden on women's speech; that criticism was not read for this note.
