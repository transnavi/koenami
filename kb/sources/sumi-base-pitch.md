# Base Pitch (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- The definition: 「When normally speaking, the voice will raise for intonation and then eventually come back down to rest on the same pitch over and over. This pitch the voice naturally wants to return to is called the Base Pitch, and is determined mostly by vocal weight.」 Stated as a table: 「Heavier vocal weight –> lower base pitch / Lighter weight –> can maintain higher base pitch」.
- How to find one's own, without instruments: 「If you draw out the last word in the following phrases, that will tell you your current base pitch, provided you do it "naturally" without forcing the voice to stay high.」 The phrases given are 「Open the crate but don't break the glass .」 and 「Oak is strong and also gives shade .」
- On pitch control as a prerequisite: 「If you find it hard to keep a base pitch, it might be that you don't have much awareness or control over pitch. Pitch matching is a good way to fix this… Not everyone starts with an ability to match and control pitch, but almost everyone can learn it, usually quite rapidly too.」
- The main exercise, intuitive base pitch: 「it isn't tightly controlled or forced; we need the voice to naturally want to stay here, and forcing it to do so will not achieve that.」 Its steps include 「base pitch is the pitch that you always come back down to after inflection / intonation; it's almost always the lowest note in speech」; 「play the note or hum the note of where you want to speak」; 「use a phrase that starts at the base pitch like "the north wind and the sun had a quarrel"」; 「the more "hesitant" it is to fall down, the less likely it is to drop below the intended base pitch, so it's good to bias the voice higher in pitch for the intonation」; and 「gradually start to just speak naturally instead of control the pitch, but whenever your voice hits that base pitch, just notice that and draw attention to it」. The endpoint: 「eventually you'll memorise and keep the base pitch subconsciously without needing to drill for it」.
- The fallback, singing base pitch (contrived), 「more or less "forces" a base pitch if you have trouble with the more intuitive methods」: 「pick a pitch, and some other pitch that is a bit higher」, say 「one」 at the high note and 「two」 at the low one, then read a phrase that starts high and 「only at the very end comes down to the low note」, e.g. 「[high] open the crate but don't break the [low] glass」. The warning attached: 「if the pitch is too high it will be straining and achieve nothing, so only use this for getting used to controlling the base pitch at all, not for doing a higher and higher pitch.」

## Bearing on voice training
- The wiki's mechanism claim is a one-way link: base pitch follows vocal weight rather than being set independently ([[vocal-weight]], [[fundamental-frequency]]), which is the same ordering the curriculum uses when it puts weight and pitch in one first step ([[sumi-getting-started]], [[sumi-pipm]]).
- The drills are both self-measuring — draw out the last word and listen for where the voice lands — which is the wiki's alternative to a display, and it is what the pitch-floor alarm on its spectrogram is for ([[sumi-in-site-spectrogram]], [[sumi-spectrograms]]).
- The distinction between the intuitive and contrived methods, and the explicit warning that the contrived version is not for raising pitch, is the wiki policing the failure mode its own material invites ([[sumi-questionable-exercises]], [[voice-health]]).

## Limits
- Volunteer wiki, "under construction"; no source is cited for the weight-to-base-pitch relation, the "almost always the lowest note in speech" characterisation is the wiki's own, and the audio examples were not played.
