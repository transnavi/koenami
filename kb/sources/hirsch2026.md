# Acoustic Assumptions (AA): A Retrospective Study of an Approach to Optimizing Personal Resonance Goals in Gender-Affirming Voice Training

## What it says
- Named approach ("Acoustic Assumptions") for setting and reaching personal resonance goals; tested retrospectively on pre- and post-therapy samples from 15 transgender and gender-diverse clients. Material: the vowels /a/, /i/ and /u/, the first three sentences of the Rainbow Passage, and the TWVQ and VENI questionnaires.
- SLP students made blind, randomised ratings of the samples for gender perception (abstract, "Method").
- "Significant increases in post treatment F0 and F2 were observed in vowels /i/, /a/, and /u/, coinciding with significant posttreatment increases in vowel space area (VSA) and significant decreases in estimated vocal tract length (VTL)."
- Regression: the treatment difference in (a) sustained-vowel F0 and F2 and (b) sustained-vowel F0 and F1 "combined to significantly predict the treatment difference in listener-perceived femininity ratings" in vowel and sentence contexts respectively, while the F2 difference predicted the TWVQ change.
- The authors describe it as "the first analysis of treatment outcomes using the AA approach" and call the approach "a systematic approach to resonance modification".

## Bearing on voice training
- A named private-practice protocol that produces F2 and vowel-space change measured against client-chosen resonance targets, with predicted listener-femininity gains — i.e. an explicit method where most training descriptions stay generic [[formants]] [[voice-feminization-therapy]].
- The reporting chain matters methodologically: F2 appears in the predictor of listener ratings (together with F0) and on its own predicts the client's TWVQ change, so the acoustic, listener and self-report measures point the same way here [[patient-reported-outcomes]].

## Notes
- n = 15, retrospective, single private practice (Seattle), no control group; the protocol itself is described in prose, not published as a manual, so "Acoustic Assumptions" cannot be replicated from this abstract alone. First author is the practising clinician.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
