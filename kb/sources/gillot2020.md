# Étude de cas : Mesure de l'efficacité de la méthode Astudillo dans le cadre de la prise en charge logopédique pour féminisation vocale（Gillot 2020）

## What it says
- The demand context: « Depuis plusieurs années, un nombre croissant de demandes de prise en charge en féminisation vocale est observé », coming notably from trans women wanting their vocal characteristics to match their gender identity; among the possible treatments, speech therapy offers vocal training « se basant sur les différences acoustiques et prosodiques qui caractérisent les voix féminines et masculines » (abstract).
- What the method under test is: « La Méthode Astudillo intègre ces principes et propose un travail ciblé sur certains éléments prosodiques tels que le rythme, le débit, l'allongement des voyelles et l'intonation (Astudillo, 2019) » — rhythm, rate, vowel lengthening and intonation (abstract).
- The gap it addresses, stated bluntly: « Si de nombreux témoignages mettent en évidence la satisfaction des femmes transgenres ayant été prises en charge par Mariela Astudillo, l'efficacité de cette méthode n'a jusqu'à présent pas été éprouvée sur le plan scientifique » — testimonials existed, scientific testing did not.
- Design: a single case, a 34-year-old trans participant who received nine sessions from Mariela Astudillo; acoustic and prosodic measures taken at several points in the treatment (abstract).
- Results on the acoustic side: « une augmentation significative de la fo, tant sur voyelle tenue que sur parole continue » (F0 rose on both sustained vowel and connected speech), while « aucun changement n'a pu être mesuré au niveau des fR et de l'étendue vocale » (no measurable change in resonance frequencies or in vocal range) (abstract).
- Results on the prosodic side, measured with Prosogram (Mertens 2004): a significant increase in intonation and melodic variability, but not in pause proportion or vowel length.
- A second question — whether the participant converged towards her therapist's voice during sessions (citing Pardo 2013) — came out negative: « nous n'avons pas mesuré de lien significatif entre les caractéristiques prosodiques évaluées chez la participante et chez sa thérapeute », with the methodology and the variability of convergence effects offered as possible explanations.
- Overall: « sur le plan subjectif une satisfaction vocale accrue en fin de traitement » (increased self-reported vocal satisfaction), set against the mixed objective picture.

## Bearing on voice training
- The clearest Francophone case for what a prosody-led method moves and what it does not: intonation and melodic variability responded, resonance and range did not. That is the mirror image of the resonance-led protocols, and it is the same division the pitch/resonance trials find when one parameter is trained alone ([[voice-therapy-techniques]], [[intonation]], [[formants]]).
- It is also an instance of the community-to-clinic evidence sequence this vault documents elsewhere: a method built on practitioner experience and testimonials, then taken up by a university voice unit and measured. The result — better intonation, unchanged resonance — is the kind of finding that the self-taught guides' claims can be checked against ([[community-training]], [[voice-feminization-therapy]]).
- The failed convergence analysis is worth recording because therapist-modelling is a load-bearing assumption in much of the pedagogy (instructors demonstrating, students imitating): in this single case the participant's prosody did not measurably track her therapist's.
