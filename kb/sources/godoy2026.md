# Factors Associated With Vocal Fatigue In Transfeminine And Transmasculine Individuals Who Have Not Undergone Voice Harmonization

## What it says
- Online cross-sectional survey in Brazil with convenience sampling: 34 transmasculine and 24 transfeminine participants who had *not* had voice harmonisation training or surgery.
- Instruments: a characterisation questionnaire (age, gender, hormone therapy, whether they adjust their voice in communication, and in how many contexts), the Vocal Fatigue Index (VFI) and the global self-assessment item of the Trans Woman Voice Questionnaire (abstract, "Methods").
- "Mean VFI scores were outside the reference values for Brazilian Portuguese" — i.e. the group scored in the range associated with vocal fatigue.
- Transmasculine participants scored significantly higher on total VFI than transfeminine participants.
- Linear regression found one clear association: "for every 1-unit increase in the number of contexts [in which vocal adjustments are made], the total VFI score increased by an average of 4.11 points".

## Bearing on voice training
- The dose–response finding is the useful part: fatigue tracks how many *situations* a person adjusts their voice in, not how they adjust it. Voice use across many settings is the exposure [[vocal-fatigue]] [[voice-health]].
- Studies people before any clinical intervention, which is where most voice use actually happens; the higher transmasculine scores sit alongside the higher effort and misgendering reported for that group elsewhere [[fujiki2026]].

## Notes
- n = 58, Brazil, online convenience sample, self-report only (VFI and TWVQ global item), no acoustic measurement; cross-sectional so no direction of causation. VFI reference values are those for Brazilian Portuguese, not for a transgender population.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
