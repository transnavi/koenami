# Clarity and Harmonic to Noise Ratio (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- "Clarity is described as a multi-variable scale from clear and consistent to rough or noisy tone. For example, a voice with lots of breathiness or creak is considered low in clarity, whereas a voice that has a clear tone is considered high in clarity. This concept overlaps a lot with harmonic to noise ratio."
- "Harmonic to Noise Ratio (HNR) is the description of essentially how noisy a voice is." Harmonics are "the horizontal lines in the image" and noise lies between them, "either from constriction, breathiness or a combination".
- The wiki points at the limit of the measure: HNR "refers specifically to noise and not things like nasality or knodel (tongue root retraction) effects which might also make a voice sound 'out of place'".
- Primary sources of low HNR are listed as "abduction (breathiness)", "FVF constriction", and "vocal fry and creak".
- A second concept on the page, "Covers": "Covers are anything that 'cover up' or disguises a part of a voice or acts to modify the overall quality of the voice." Named covers: "nasality", "tongue root retraction (stitch voice)" and "M2 or too low weight".

## Bearing on voice training
- Maps the community's word "clarity" onto a measure the acoustic literature uses — HNR — and then says exactly where the mapping stops: nasality and tongue-root effects degrade perceived quality without showing up in HNR [[voice-quality]] [[harmonics-and-spectrum]].
- "Covers" is a useful frame the clinical literature does not have a term for: features a learner adds that conceal another problem rather than fixing it (a low-weight M2 tone hidden under nasality, for instance) [[breathiness]] [[community-training]].
