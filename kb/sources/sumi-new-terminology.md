# New Terminology (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- An invented, PGP-signed vocabulary: "The following is a set of definitions for new, completely invented terminology. The goal of this document is to provide a static, unchangeable base for terminology which does not have significant connotations with human language and cannot be misunderstood or have its definitions changed or muddied." The page states the terms may be prefixed "sts22-" for absolute reference and instructs readers not to distribute modified definitions.
- **Prekt** — "the frequency of the first (zero-th) harmonic in the voice, NOT including subharmonics. It is the cycles per second of the true vocal folds (vocal ligament) vibrating." ("If the vocal folds complete 148 cycles (open and close) in one second, the prekt is 148 or 148hz.")
- **Welit** — "the estimated amount of vocal fold vibratory mass (VFVM)". "Since VFVM cannot be easily measured, we estimate welit based on the sound qualities we exclusively associate with VFVM. If some quality of voice is known to be caused by factors other than VFVM or is not directly caused by VFVM then it does not affect welit." "Does not include any other factor such as adduction, twang, volume, creak / fry." "Lendis on its own does not affect welit, but people often add the two simultaneously due to conflation."
- **Rezion** — "the assumed level of F1 and F2 (the first and second formants above the fundamental) on average. If measured precisely, it is measured by the frequency of F1 on an /a/ vowel (UK 'bath')." "It is anchored to the F1, and is overridden by measurements of the F1 and F2."
- **Sezion** — the impression of the speaker's physical size: "If the voice sounds lun (more sezion) we expect the speaker to be large like a giant. If the voice sounds sul (less sezion), we expect the speaker to be like a small pixie or a goblin." "In use, this is essentially rezion, high rezion being typically sul and low rezion typically lun", with "a minor, secondary relationship with prekt".
- **Brint and Dern** — "Overly brint voices can sound buzzy, as a result of somewhat high rezion and sufficient tone clarity (less noise, breathiness or false fold activity). Dern voices can be a result of low lendis, too much air being directed through the nose (hypernasality) or too low rezion or any number of other factors." Rules given: "The voice sounds buzzy and bright > more brint"; "The voice sounds dull and dark > more dern"; "The voice has high rezion but something is making the voice 'dull' > more dern".
- **Lendis** — "The effect of amount of airflow and closure on overall amplitude ... It is an additive relationship between adduction and subglottal pressure that result in lendis." "More airflow and same closure (more adduction to balance increased airflow) > more lendis"; "Less airflow and less closure (breathy) > less lendis".
- The page keeps an older signed version too, which defined **vwit** (vocal fold vibratory mass, superseded by welit) and **prefol** ("A state of phonation where there is very high adduction and only the necessary subglottal pressure to produce voice, but low air flow ... can be creaky or squeezed and buzzy").

## Bearing on voice training
- The community's attempt to fix the vocabulary problem its own terminology page documents: strip the connotations of everyday words (weight, brightness, size) and define perceptual targets operationally as F0, F1/F2, vibratory mass, and airflow-by-closure [[vocal-weight]] [[voice-quality]] [[formants]].
- Welit is defined with an explicit exclusion rule — no adduction, twang, volume or creak may contribute — which is the discipline the "buzzy" confusion was missing [[sumi-questionable-terms]] [[sumi-hyperadduction]].
- Lendis separates loudness-as-amplitude from the airflow-versus-closure balance that produces it, which is the same separation the clinical literature makes between subglottal pressure and glottal closure [[breathing-and-support]] [[phonation]].
