# The physics of small-amplitude oscillation of the vocal folds

## What it says
- "A theory of vocal fold oscillation is developed on the basis of the body-cover hypothesis. The cover is represented by a distributed surface layer that can propagate a mucosal surface wave" (abstract).
- Predicts that the **oscillation threshold pressure** — the lung pressure needed to start and sustain vibration — "is reduced by reducing the mucosal wave velocity, by bringing the vocal folds closer together and by reducing the convergence angle in the glottis" (abstract).
- The vocal tract loads the source: "vocal tract inertance reduces the oscillation threshold pressure, whereas vocal tract resistance increases it" (abstract).
- The treatment covers falsetto and breathy voice as well as onset and release of phonation without fold collision, and is harmonised with earlier two-mass and collapsible-tube models (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- Gives the physical reason why a light, close-but-not-pressed adduction phonates at low effort, and why the resonance you set up above the larynx changes how hard the larynx has to work — the acoustic argument behind resonant-voice work.
