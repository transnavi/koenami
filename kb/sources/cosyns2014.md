# Voice in female-to-male transsexual persons after long-term androgen therapy

## What it says
- 38 female-to-male transsexual persons (22–54 years), including people under long-term androgen administration, and 38 controls frequency-matched by age and smoking behaviour; pitch, intonation and perturbation measured on sustained vowels, counting and reading (abstract, methods).
- "The FMT as a group did not differ significantly from controls for any of the acoustic voice variables studied. However, in about 10% pitch lowering was not totally unproblematic" (abstract, results).
- The lowest-pitched (more male-sounding) voices occurred in participants with higher hematocrit and longer CAG trinucleotide repeats in the androgen receptor gene — the authors read this as "diminished androgen sensitivity" behind the difficult cases (abstract).
- Level of evidence 3b by the authors' grading (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- Long-term testosterone usually delivers a voice acoustically indistinguishable from cis male controls, but roughly one person in ten still has pitch-lowering difficulty — the residual group that voice assessment and training serve ([[voice-masculinization]]).
- The androgen-receptor finding is a physiological reason why the same hormone dose does not produce the same voice in everyone.

## Notes
- Largest controlled voice study of trans men on long-term androgens to its date, and the first with a control group (abstract); terminology ("FMT", "transsexual") is of its time.
- Single centre (Ghent); cross-sectional, so the time course of change is not observed here (see [[nimura2015]], [[nygren2016]], [[irwig2017a]]).
