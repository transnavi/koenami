# Big Dog Little Dog Resonance Exercise

## What it says
- The post is by Renée Yoxon, who describes themself on the same page as "a queer, nonbinary, and disabled singer, songwriter and gender-affirming voice teacher… a voice teacher for over twelve years… degrees in physics, jazz, and songwriting" (they/them).
- Yoxon presents the exercise as their own current favourite, not as an inherited technique: "I've got a quick and easy voice exercise that's quickly becoming my new favourite for controlling resonance in trans voice training! It's called Big Dog Little Dog." No originator is credited anywhere on the page.
- The stated principle: "voice resonance is controlled by the size and shape of your vocal tract. A smaller vocal tract produces a brighter, lighter voice (more feminine). A larger vocal tract produces a darker, deeper voice (more masculine)."
- The exercise: to brighten, "try panting like a little dog: Use a tiny, light pant. Focus on how bright and small your vocal tract feels." To darken, "try panting like a big dog: Use a deep, heavier pant. Notice how it opens your vocal tract and creates a darker sound." Extended to barking — "yip yip → smaller vocal tract, brighter resonance", "woof woof → larger vocal tract, darker resonance".
- The transfer instruction: "The key is to choose your dog and maintain that shape when you speak."

## Bearing on voice training
- The most-circulated community name for a larynx-height-and-pharynx-width exercise. The physical claim it rests on — shorter tract, higher and wider-spaced formants — is the one in [[formants]]; the post itself offers no measurement.
- Note the equation of "brighter" with "lighter" and with "more feminine" in a single sentence. Brightness (resonance) and lightness (source weight) are separable ([[bozeman2022]], [[sundberg2010]]); the post's wording collapses them.
