# Dependence of phonatory effort on hydration level

## What it says
- Double-blind, placebo-controlled design; twelve adult untrained voice users with normal voices each received a 4-hour hydration treatment, a 4-hour dehydration treatment and a 4-hour placebo.
- Phonatory effort was measured physiologically as phonation threshold pressure (PTP) and psychologically as direct magnitude estimation of perceived phonatory effort.
- "The findings indicated an inverse relation between phonatory effort and hydration level, but primarily for high-pitched phonation tasks."
- The PTP findings replicated an earlier non-blinded study (Verdolini-Marston, Titze & Druker, 1990). The discussion attributes the effect to vocal-fold tissue viscosity, "although direct measures of tissue viscosity are lacking."

## Bearing on voice training
- The evidence behind "drink water": dehydration raises the pressure needed to start phonation, and it does so mainly at *high* pitch. That is exactly the condition a trans-feminine speaker practises in, which makes hydration more than generic advice for this population.

## Notes
- Twelve participants; the hydration manipulation was pharmacological/behavioural over four hours, not a long-term drinking habit. Nothing here establishes that extra fluid beyond normal hydration helps.
