# Acoustic Measures of Articulation and Vocal Quality in Transgender People Completing Vocal Feminization Therapy

## What it says
- Retrospective chart review of 16 clients (15 trans women, one nonbinary and genderqueer, mean age 31.0 ± 9.4) who completed vocal feminisation therapy; sustained vowels and sentences recorded before and after (abstract, "Methods").
- Measures chosen to go beyond the usual pitch and resonance: fundamental frequency; articulation via F1–F4, estimated vocal tract length, vowel articulation index and voice onset time; vocal quality via cepstral peak prominence, low-to-high spectral ratio and harmonics-to-noise ratio.
- "As expected, mean f(o) increased an average of 5.4 semitones following therapy. F2 also significantly increased, indicating a more forward tongue position during vowel productions."
- The low-to-high spectral ratio "significantly increased, but there was no change in HNR or CPP, indicating a shift in high-frequency harmonic content but no increase in vocal breathiness".
- "No other vocal quality or articulatory changes were found", and the authors conclude the study "did not support articulatory changes following successful GAVT" beyond F2.

## Bearing on voice training
- One of the few attempts to test whether feminisation therapy changes *articulation* beyond resonance — and the answer is mostly no, with F2 (forward tongue position) the only articulatory move it detects [[articulation]] [[formants]].
- Direct evidence against breathiness being a by-product of feminisation training: HNR and CPP, the standard acoustic proxies, were unchanged [[voice-quality]] [[vocal-weight]]. The spectral-ratio shift is a change in harmonic balance, not in breathiness.
- 5.4 semitones is a concrete dose to compare against other programmes' F0 gains [[fundamental-frequency]].

## Notes
- n = 16, single US centre (Cincinnati, with Central Florida co-authors), retrospective, no control group; the abstract names no statistical test. The abstract does not say how many sessions or which therapy protocol.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
