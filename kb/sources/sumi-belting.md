# Belting (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Definition: "Belting is the technique of adding weight and volume to high pitches to force the voice to stay in M1. It can be thought of as the opposite approach to adding range as blending M2. Instead of making M1 lighter so we can transition into M2, we're making it heavier so we don't transition into M2 at all."
- Safety warning, in the wiki's own heading: "Belting is loud, and essentially yelling so loud we can go higher. This has a tendency to encourage hyperadduction. It's best not to push the voice so hard it becomes hyperadducted. Slowly adding a note or two at a time in a scale can be a good way to reduce the chance of becoming hyperadducted."
- Components named: "adding weight", "adding airflow and closure (volume)", "raising pitch until just before mechanism break".
- On breaks: "Mechanism breaks or voice cracks are a sign that the voice is not heavy or loud enough for that pitch and so a threshold is crossed, flipping into M2. The answer is not to just force the voice to get louder, but instead question technique. Are you adding weight or just volume? The fact that a break happened is a sign that we're belting because we have enough vocal weight and volume to not just slide into M2 seamlessly."

## Bearing on voice training
- Defines belting as a *weight* strategy for range, the mirror image of the usual feminisation approach of lightening M1 until M2 blends in [[vocal-weight]] [[vocal-registers]] [[singing-and-voice]].
- Carries an explicit hyperadduction warning and a graded remedy (add a note at a time in a scale), which is the same failure mode the wiki's hyperadduction page describes for M2 screaming [[sumi-hyperadduction]] [[voice-health]].
- The diagnostic question — "are you adding weight or just volume?" — separates two things that sound alike and are trained differently [[voice-quality]].
