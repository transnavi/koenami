# Nasality (Voice Resource Project wiki)

## What it says
- Definition: "When a voice has too much air going through the nasal passage when speaking, we call it hypernasality or just nasality. Nasality darkens the voice by making formants less highlighted."
- Hypernasality "is when the voice lets air through the nose even when it's not doing nasal sounds like N, M, NG. This makes the voice 'dull' because some of the resonance features of it are now missing."
- Test given: "The main method of checking for nasality is to say a non nasal phrase (one without any N or M or NG sounds) without and then with the nose pinched. If there's a difference, there is nasality. The degree of nasality is how 'honky' the sound is when we pinch the nose."
- Sample non-nasal phrases: "Pick up the crate, be careful of the glass." / "Would you please take these three glasses to be washed below the tap." / "That black cat attacked the plastic bag." / "The elevator stopped for us at the third floor."
- Practice: make the voice more nasal and then less, using the pinched-nose state as feedback; "breathing out through the mouth and trying to hum at the same time is a good way to get nasality", and "a breathy sigh through the mouth can help getting non-nasal".
- Hyponasality is the opposite, "when the voice cannot go through the nose". Test: "say a non-nasal phrase" then "a nasal phrase" — "if only the nasal phrase sounds honky, we have hyponasality; if both sound honky, we have hyponasality and hypernasality simultaneously." Nasal phrase given: "my momma makes lemon jam".

## Bearing on voice training
- Supplies a self-administered test for a feature a learner cannot reliably hear in their own voice: the pinch test with non-nasal carrier phrases, which is a household version of what a clinician does [[voice-quality]] [[voice-therapy-techniques]].
- The stated mechanism — nasality darkens by reducing formant prominence — is the wiki's explanation of why nasal and bright are at odds, and it is consistent with the clinical framing of nasality as one perceptual axis [[formants]] [[voice-impressions]] [[vocal-tract-anatomy]].
