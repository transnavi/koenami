# Clinical Considerations in Speech and Language Therapy in Turkish Transgender Population

## What it says
- Survey of 31 transgender people in Istanbul (Üsküdar University speech and language department with a private rehabilitation centre), covering transition procedures, voice and nonverbal-communication priorities, and awareness of speech and language therapy (abstract, "METHOD").
- Pitch was the most important vocal quality for 41.9% of participants; facial expression (38.7%) the most important nonverbal skill (abstract, "RESULTS").
- None of the participants had received speech and language therapy during their transition; 17 of 31 (54.8%) "did not know that they could apply for the services".
- Those who did know had learned about the services "via online resources" — the abstract's closing sentence is that "Transgender participants are not aware of speech and language therapy services in Turkey."
- Participants had used many procedures during transition, "from hormone therapy to voice surgery".

## Bearing on voice training
- The access problem this vault's community notes describe for Japan and Chinese-speaking regions exists in Turkey too, with self-directed online learning as the only route for most — the setting [[oguz2021]] shares with [[community-training]] sources.
- Voice surgery being reached without therapy inverts the stepped pathway recommended where services exist ([[nhse1719]], [[heydrich2023]]).

## Notes
- A small convenience sample with no stated recruitment frame; graded low for that reason.
- "Transgendered" is the paper's own wording; the survey instrument is not published in the abstract.
