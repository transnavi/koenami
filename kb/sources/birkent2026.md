# Tensioning Glottoplasty: A Novel Surgical Technique for Voice Feminization

## What it says
- Retrospective analysis of 20 trans women (mean age 28.7 ± 5.1 years, range 22–41) who had "tensioning glottoplasty" for voice feminisation (abstract, "Methods").
- The technique "combines anterior glottic shortening with anteriorly directed tensioning": it uses a combined endolaryngeal and external approach, with excision of the anterior vocal fold mucosa, transcartilaginous sutures placed under endoscopic guidance, and tying externally over the thyroid cartilage.
- F0 was measured preoperatively and at 1 month postoperatively; statistics used a paired-samples t test after Shapiro-Wilk normality testing.
- "Mean fo increased from 126.2 ± 22.6 Hz preoperatively to 241.2 ± 34.1 Hz postoperatively, a mean increase of 115.0 Hz (95% CI: 102.19–127.87 Hz; p < 0.001; Cohen's dz = 4.19). All 20 patients demonstrated postoperative pitch elevation."
- The authors' claim: "combining vibratory length reduction with augmentation of longitudinal tension ... may offer a distinct biomechanical advantage within the spectrum of gender-affirming voice surgery".

## Bearing on voice training
- A large single-arm pitch gain (roughly +115 Hz, about a doubling) from a technique that adds longitudinal tension to the standard shortening, presented at 1 month only [[voice-surgery]] [[fundamental-frequency]].
- Contrast the biomechanics with the training route: cricothyroid activity raises pitch by tilting the thyroid cartilage forward, which is the muscle action this procedure mimics surgically [[larynx-anatomy]] [[voice-feminization-therapy]].

## Notes
- n = 20, Istanbul, retrospective, single-arm, no comparison group and only a 1-month follow-up — the abstract reports no long-term durability, no voice-quality measure, and no patient-reported outcome. The authors' own claim of a "biomechanical advantage" rests on comparison with published series, not with an in-study control.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
