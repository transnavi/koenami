# Preliminary study of two methods of treatment for laryngeal nodules

## What it says
- "To date, a limited number of formal studies have assessed the efficacy of voice therapy for laryngeal nodules." Thirteen women with nodules were paid participants; some received "confidential voice therapy", some "resonant voice therapy", some no therapy, over roughly two weeks.
- Pre- and post-therapy measures of phonatory effort, auditory-perceptual voice status and laryngeal appearance "provided evidence of a benefit from therapy."
- The key finding: "the likelihood of benefiting from therapy directly covaried with compliance scores (reflecting the reported extraclinical utilization of the therapy technique), but not with therapy type (confidential vs. resonant voice therapy)."
- The authors frame the result as pointing "to the importance of assessing not only therapy type but also compliance in future, larger studies."

## Bearing on voice training
- The foundational trial for resonant voice therapy, and its own conclusion is that *doing the homework* predicted benefit better than which technique was assigned. That is directly applicable to trans voice practice, where technique arguments are common and practice volume is rarely measured.

## Notes
- Thirteen participants, two weeks, no blinding described; the authors call it "a preliminary stage of inquiry". Graded `low`. The DOI printed in many citation lists (10.1016/S0892-1997(05)80189-6) does not resolve; PubMed gives 10.1016/s0892-1997(05)80225-5.
