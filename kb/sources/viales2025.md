## What it says
- Costa Rican narrative review built on 23 articles published 2017–2023, written to introduce gender-affirming voice work to a readership where it is little known: "estas técnicas son relativamente desconocidas en Costa Rica".
- Treats "terapia del lenguaje (fonoaudiología o logopedia)" as a viable route to feminise or masculinise the voice, alone or alongside medical treatment, and holds that voice therapists must understand hormonal and surgical treatment to adapt their strategies.
- Findings highlight the importance of fundamental frequency, the impact of voice on quality of life, and the influence of "los formantes o resonancia" (formants, i.e. resonance).
- Identifies the gap explicitly: "subrayan la falta de investigaciones sobre la prosodia, articulación y comunicación no verbal" — a shortage of research on prosody, articulation and non-verbal communication.
- Concludes that combined therapy plus hormonal or surgical treatment shows the most promising results.

## Bearing on voice training
- Names the same three-tier cue hierarchy used elsewhere in the vault — pitch, resonance/formants, then prosody and articulation — and states that the third tier is under-researched, matching what [[leung2018]] found in the meta-analytic literature.
- One of very few sources documenting the Central American situation, where neither training nor surgery is routinely provided.

## Notes
- Narrative review: no PRISMA flow, no appraisal of the 23 included articles, published in a regional Costa Rican university journal.
- Covers both directions (feminisation and masculinisation), unlike most Spanish-language reviews.
