## What it says
- Female voice actors performed speech intended to portray five different character types (young girl, teenage-ish girl, adult woman, elderly woman, and a boy), and the authors ran both listening experiments and acoustic-feature analysis on the recordings.
- Listener perceptions of the five intended characters were reliably differentiated from one another.
- For the four female-coded characters (girl through elderly woman), the acoustic features tracked with real aging-related vocal changes across the lifespan.
- For the boy-voiced character, the acoustic strategy used appeared to be a distinct, character-specific convention rather than a mapping onto real male-child vocal acoustics.
- Authors discuss limitations from using controlled/directed performance speech and note implications for media-arts character-voice research and engineering applications.

## Bearing on voice training
- Useful cross-check: when trained voice actors portray age/gender categories, their choices for female-coded ages largely mirror real biological aging patterns in acoustic features — suggesting listener-validated feminine voice targets do track genuine physiological correlates, not just stylization.
- The finding that the "boy voice" does not track real boys' acoustics is a caution: not every performed-gender vocal convention is acoustically "real," so training material based on performance/anime conventions (e.g. yasuda2023's descriptor set) should be checked against genuine physiological voice data before being treated as a training target.

## Notes
- Speech was directed/performed by professional voice actors, not spontaneous speech — a limitation the authors themselves flag.
- Population: Japanese female voice actors and (implicitly) Japanese listeners; exact n not stated in the abstract excerpt available.
