# Long-term Acoustic Effects of Gender-Affirming Voice Training in Transgender Women

## What it says
- Randomised sham-controlled cross-over trial: 26 trans women followed up at 3 months and 18 at 1 year after the last session; every participant received 14 weeks — 4 weeks sham plus 10 weeks of feminisation training (5 weeks pitch elevation, 5 weeks articulation–resonance), in different orders (abstract, "Methods").
- F0 was measured on a sustained /a:/, reading and spontaneous speech; F1–F3 were measured on /a/, /i/, /u/ and a vowel space computed. Analysis used linear mixed models.
- Most F0 and formant increases present immediately after training "were stable at both follow-up measurements".
- But the median F0 slipped between post-training and 1 year: 16 Hz / 1.7 semitones in reading (169 Hz → 153 Hz) and 12 Hz / 1.5 semitones in spontaneous speech (144 Hz → 132 Hz).
- "The lower limit of f(o) did not change during reading and spontaneous speech, both directly after the intervention and during both follow-ups" — the pitch floor never rose, even though the median did.
- The lower-limit result prompts the recommendation: "More time should be spent on increasing the fifth percentile of f(o), as the lower limit of f(o) also contributes to the perception of more feminine voice."
- F1–F2 of /a/ and vowel space increased after training and stayed up at both follow-ups.

## Bearing on voice training
- The retention evidence: resonance changes held for a year, pitch gains partly decayed — pitch training needs maintenance, resonance apparently less so [[voice-feminization-therapy]] [[formants]] [[fundamental-frequency]].
- The team's own conclusion names the target that their protocol missed: the 5th percentile of F0. A mean that rises while the floor stays put leaves the voice dipping into its old range [[pitch-and-scales]].
- Also reports that individual results depend on uncontrolled factors (home practice, extra sessions) — the practical variables a clinic cannot see.
