# Overtone (English Wikipedia)

## What it says
- Summary endpoint, 2026-09-17: "An overtone is any resonant frequency above the fundamental frequency of a sound. An overtone may or may not be a harmonic. In other words, overtones are all pitches higher than the lowest pitch within an individual sound; the fundamental is the lowest pitch."
- "The relative volume or amplitude of various overtone partials is one of the key identifying features of timbre, or the individual characteristic of a sound."
- On the voice: "The human vocal tract is able to produce highly variable amplitudes of the overtones, called formants, which define different vowels."

## Interlanguage titles

## Bearing on voice training
- Keeps 倍音 (overtone) and 高調波 (harmonic, [[wikipedia-harmonic]]) from being collapsed: every harmonic is an overtone, but an overtone need not be a harmonic. In a modal vowel they coincide; in a noisy or inharmonic source they do not.

## Limits
- Community-edited. The last sentence equates formants with "highly variable amplitudes of the overtones"; the speech-science definition used in this vault is the tract *resonance* that produces those amplitude peaks ([[wikipedia-formant]], [[fant1960]]). Treat that Wikipedia wording as loose.
