# The Cisgender Listening Subject in Sociolinguistic Perception: Transgender Identity Affects Sibilant Categorization in American English

A perception experiment on /s/: listeners adjust what they expect a speaker's sibilant to sound like once they are told the speaker is trans.

## What it says
- Design: a replication of the Strand experiment in which 90 listeners heard a male and a female voice, "but half of the listeners were told that the speakers were transgender" (abstract).
- The result: "Listeners' categorizations suggest that they expected transwomen to produce higher frequency /s/ than ciswomen."
- Where listeners had no personal acquaintance with a trans person, "this difference was magnified, and transmen were also expected to produce lower-frequency /s/ than cismen".
- The authors' reading: "the cisgender listening subject expects trans voices to diverge from cis voices, and this ideological expectation can be so pervasive that a perceiver's representations of sounds for trans speakers may differ from the sounds those trans speakers produce."

## Bearing on voice training
- Names a listener-side effect that sits underneath sibilant work: the same /s/ is categorised against a different internal reference once the listener knows the speaker is trans, in both directions and more strongly for listeners who know no trans people [[articulation]] [[gender-perception]] [[voice-impressions]].
- The vault's community material treats a bright /s/ as a feminisation target; this study shows the expectation runs the other way as well, with trans men expected to have *lower*-frequency /s/ than cis men [[articulation]].

## Notes
- One experiment, 90 listeners, US English, following a published paradigm; the abstract does not report effect sizes, the stimuli's own acoustic values, or whether the manipulation was of production or of belief about the speaker.
