# Vocal tract configurations in tenors' passaggio in different vowel conditions

## What it says
- "The role of the vocal tract in registers is still unclear." Four world-leading tenors sang ascending scales C4 (261 Hz) to A4 (440 Hz) on /a, e, i, o, u, ae/ under real-time MRI at 8 frames per second, once letting the modal→falsetto transition happen and once carrying stage voice across the passaggio.
- "In contrast to falsetto, stage voice was associated with an increase of lip and jaw opening and a lower larynx position for most conditions." Pharynx width behaved differently depending on the vowel.
- "In general, the dramatic voices have been found to show stronger articulatory changes than lyrical voices"; the conclusion is that "the vowel condition might, in part, affect vocal tract modifications due to register functions."

## Bearing on voice training
- Shows the passaggio being negotiated by articulation, not only by the larynx: crossing it without flipping meant opening the jaw and lowering the larynx. For a trans-feminine trainee the relevant point is the inverse — larynx height and jaw opening change register *feel* and timbre independently of what the vocal folds are doing.

## Notes
- Four singers, all professional classical tenors, one per Fach; graded `low` for sample size.
