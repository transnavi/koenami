## What it says
- 32 native British-English adult speakers read words, a sentence, and a passage in their normal voice, then again while deliberately sounding as "masculine" and as "feminine" as possible.
- Both men and women raised F0 and formant frequencies (Fi) when feminizing their voice, and lowered F0 and Fi when masculinizing it.
- Authors interpret this as evidence for spontaneous, deliberate control over both glottal (F0) and vocal-tract-length-related (formant) parameters to express gender.
- Frames the pattern as a conventionalized "gender code": speakers exaggerate the naturally occurring sex dimorphism in F0/formants to signal masculinity or femininity, without needing training.

## Bearing on voice training
- Direct evidence that both F0 and formant frequency (resonance/perceived vocal tract length) are under voluntary control in typical speakers, and that raising both together is the naive/spontaneous strategy people use to sound more feminine — validates the standard dual-target (pitch + resonance) approach in feminization voice training.
- Because these are untrained, spontaneous adjustments, they represent a baseline "default" strategy; explicit training may need to refine articulation and naturalness beyond this spontaneous pattern to avoid sounding strained.

## Notes
- Speakers were not selected for gender-diverse identity; all were cisgender adults asked to role-play masculine/feminine voice, so results describe deliberate imitation ability, not habitual voice.
- British English sample only.
