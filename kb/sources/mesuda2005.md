# 性同一性障害に対する手術後音声障害の1例

A 2005 Japanese case report about a patient who went abroad for pitch-elevation surgery and came back with a serious voice problem — the complication report that the Japanese guideline discussion is built on.

## What it says
- The situation as the authors describe it: GID had been diagnosed and treated in Japan for the previous eight years, 「が、音声の変化を望む症例に対する治療、特にMTF (male to female) に対するピッチ上昇手術はこれまでガイドラインに明確な定義がされていなかったこともあり、本邦における報告は極めて少なく」 — pitch-elevation surgery for MtF people had no clear definition in the guidelines and had been reported almost nowhere in Japan, so some patients were travelling abroad for it.
- The case: a patient who had pitch-elevation surgery in a Southeast Asian country and, after starting to phonate again at home, presented with 「高度の気息性発声」 — severe breathy phonation.
- Management and outcome: 「肉芽切除と各種保存的治療が施行されたが手術後約5カ月の時点で患者の満足は得られていない」 — granuloma excision and conservative treatment, with the patient still not satisfied about five months after the operation.
- The authors' conclusion is a call for referral discipline: 「今後本邦でも各地で性同一性障害で音声変更を望む例が出現すると予想されるため、ピッチ上昇手術の適応の検討、またこの問題における関係各科との連携の重要性について改めて認識することが必要であると考えられた」.

## Bearing on voice training
- The Japanese counterpart of the medical-tourism complication reports the vault carries from East Asian surgical centres: an operation performed outside the country's own guidelines, followed by an ENT service managing the consequences ([[voice-health]], [[voice-surgery]]). It is also why the later Japanese clinical pages emphasise follow-up and reversibility ([[kyoto-ent-clinic]]).
- Breathiness as the failure mode is consistent with what the operation does — [[villasboas2021]] measures softer, less aperiodic voices in trans women, and [[aires2023a]] counts breathiness among the GRBAS parameters it checks for post-operatively.
- The guideline gap it names is the gap [[jspn-guideline2018]] and the Japanese ENT society's later training material ([[nakamura2024]]) eventually filled.
