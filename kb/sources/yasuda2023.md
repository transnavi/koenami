## What it says
- Motivated by reducing the sense of mismatch between an animation/game character and the voice actor cast for it; the authors collected Japanese descriptive terms used for female voice-actor voice quality.
- They gathered a range of candidate descriptive terms, tested their intelligibility, synonymy, and similarity among listeners, then ran cluster analysis.
- The analysis yielded five bipolar descriptor-term pairs: 大人っぽい声–幼い声 (adult-sounding – childlike), 上品な声–荒々しい声 (elegant/refined – rough), 優しい声–冷たい声 (gentle/kind – cold), 元気な声–おしとやかな声 (energetic – demure), 高い声–低い声 (high – low).

## Bearing on voice training
- Offers a small, empirically clustered vocabulary of Japanese voice-quality descriptors specifically tied to perceived femininity/character archetypes — useful for a KB glossary of impression-rating terms (声質評価語) distinct from acoustic parameters like F0/formants.
- The five axes (age-coded, refinement, warmth, energy, pitch) suggest voice feminization training could be framed along more dimensions than pitch alone, at least for the "character voice" aesthetic this paper studies.

## Notes
- This is a conference/technical-committee proceedings paper (not peer-reviewed journal), no abstract detail on sample size or exact clustering method beyond what's summarized.
- Population/domain: Japanese voice-actor (声優) character voices, not clinical/general trans voice-training population — the descriptor set reflects anime/game character conventions and should be treated as an adjacent, not a validated clinical, source.
