# Changes in the male voice at puberty: vocal fold length and its relationship to the fundamental frequency of the voice

## What it says
- Ultrasound measurement of vocal-fold length in boys passing through puberty, grouped by Tanner stage: "there was a gradual increase in the length of the vocal folds as puberty progressed" (abstract).
- Fundamental frequency was recorded by laryngography, and "a good correlation between the length of the vocal folds and the frequency of the voice was seen" (abstract).
- But the timing does not line up: "The sudden drop in frequency seen between Tanner stages 3 and 4 did not correlate with similar changes in the length of the vocal folds at this time but stroboscopic findings suggest a change in the structure and mass of the vocal folds at this time of maximum frequency change" (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- Length is not the whole story. The steepest part of the male pitch drop coincides with a change in the folds' structure and mass, which is the part a trans woman's voice work cannot undo by stretching alone — and the reason "vocal weight" is treated as a separate dimension from pitch.

## Notes
- Small longitudinal sample from the same Cambridge/Brighton group as [[harries1997]]; ultrasound fold-length measurement in children is indirect.
