# An Evaluation of the Prevalence and Efficacy of Gender Affirmative Vocal Therapy in Poland

## What it says
- Anonymous questionnaire distributed "through secure social media forums" to transgender people in Poland who had completed or were completing transition; it covered demographics, self-perception, societal perception and voice quality perception (abstract).
- "Out of 200 responses, 21 confirmed participating in gender affirmative vocal therapy."
- "Ten elements of voice quality perception were significantly improved in the gender affirmative vocal therapy group compared to controls (p < 0.033)."
- "The study showed a significant impact of societal and self-perception on voice quality perception."
- The authors' conclusion: "It is crucial for health and well-being that people who undergo gender reassignment have access to gender affirmative vocal therapy."

## Bearing on voice training
- The Polish access picture, in one ratio: 21 of 200 respondents had had any vocal therapy [[transgender-care-context]] [[european-languages-voice]].
- The finding that societal and self-perception shape how a voice is rated is the recurring theme — a voice is judged inside a social setting, not in isolation [[voice-impressions]] [[patient-reported-outcomes]].

## Notes
- Self-selected social-media sample, self-report only, no acoustic measures, and the therapy group is 21 people compared against the rest; the abstract does not say what the ten improved elements were, nor whether the comparison group wanted therapy. Polish-language population, published in English.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract.
