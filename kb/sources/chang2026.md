# Impaired Voice-Related Work Productivity, a Functional Effect of Gender Dysphoria

## What it says
- Argument in the abstract's own words: treatment for vocal gender dysphoria "has historically been considered cosmetic due, in part, to the lack of anatomic or neurologic vocal pathology", whereas other anatomically typical conditions count as reconstructive "when there is an associated functional impairment".
- Multi-institutional cross-sectional study: 50 cisgender and 50 transgender participants without pre-existing vocal pathology, measured with VHI-10 and WorkHoarse (voice-related work-productivity impairment scored 0 = none to 32 = maximal).
- The transgender group was 64% trans women, 10% trans men and 26% non-binary or other gender non-conforming.
- Average VHI-10 16.2 vs 2.7 (p < 0.001) and WorkHoarse 14.2 vs 2.1 (p < 0.001).
- Controlling for age and volume of voice use at work, "transgender individuals had a WorkHoarse score 12.4 units higher than cisgender individuals (95% confidence interval 9.7–15.0)".
- Conclusion: this "identifies a functional impairment associated with vocal gender dysphoria, which fulfills one of multiple criteria used to define medically necessary and reconstructive interventions".

## Bearing on voice training
- Supplies the coverage argument for voice care: the impairment is measured in work output, not in preference, and the numbers are large on a 32-point scale [[transgender-care-context]] [[clinical-guidelines]].
- WorkHoarse is a work-productivity instrument borrowed for voice — an outcome type the voice literature rarely uses [[patient-reported-outcomes]].

## Notes
- n = 100, cross-sectional and self-report; the 2:1 preponderance of trans women means "transgender" here is mostly a transfeminine sample. Sub-group results for trans men (n = 5) and non-binary participants are not reported in the abstract.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract (Laryngoscope 2026 Jan;136(1); Epub 2025 Aug 19).
