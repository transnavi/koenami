# Acoustic Voice Properties of Turkish-Speaking Trans Women Who Have Not Received Gender-Affirming Voice Intervention

## What it says
- 82 Turkish speakers — 41 trans women and 41 age-matched cis women — none of whom had had gender-affirming voice training or surgery. Sustained /a/ and connected speech recorded under standardised conditions; 13 acoustic parameters covering pitch, intonation and voice quality (abstract, "Methods").
- Pitch measures used CSL Multi-Dimensional Voice Profile and Real-Time Pitch; cepstral-spectral measures used CSL Analysis of Dysphonia in Speech and Voice.
- Cis women showed significantly higher mean F0 on both tasks, and higher minimum F0, maximum F0 and F0 standard deviation in connected speech (P < 0.05) — the untrained trans women's ranges were *narrower*, not merely lower.
- Cepstral peak prominence and its standard deviation were significantly *higher* in trans women on most samples (P < 0.05); the low-to-high spectral ratio did not differ.
- Conclusion: "Beyond mean F0, between-group differences in pitch variability and cepstral-spectral voice quality measures highlight the importance of comprehensive acoustic evaluation."

## Bearing on voice training
- Untrained baseline data for a language not previously covered in the vault: a Turkish-speaking trans woman's voice differs from a cis woman's in F0 *variability* as much as in mean F0, which is an intonation target rather than a pitch target [[voice-across-languages]] [[intonation]] [[european-languages-voice]].
- Higher CPP in the trans group is a periodicity difference, not a "better voice" finding — CPP is a measurement of how cleanly the folds cycle, and the authors do not interpret it as an advantage.

## Notes
- n = 82, Hacettepe University and collaborators; cross-sectional, no intervention, so it cannot say whether the differences are trainable. Groups are age-matched but the abstract does not report hormone or smoking history.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract. Title and author names are given in Latin script with Turkish diacritics; the paper is published in English.
