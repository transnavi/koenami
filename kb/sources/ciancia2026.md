# Long-Term Effects of Gender-Affirming Hormone Therapy from Adolescence to Adulthood on Voice

## What it says
- Cross-sectional Ghent study of adults who started gonadal hormone suppression (GHS) and gender-affirming hormone therapy (GAHT) in adolescence and had 5–10 years of GAHT: 88 transgender men and 23 transgender women, compared with 12 cisgender men and 17 cisgender women; sustained vowel, phonetically balanced reading and spontaneous speech analysed in Praat for f0, voice intensity and Acoustic Voice Quality Index (abstract).
- Transgender men's f0 matched cisgender men across all speech tasks. Transgender women had significantly higher f0 than cisgender men but, in reading and spontaneous speech, often significantly lower than cisgender women; their voice intensity was lower than cisgender men's and similar to cisgender women's (abstract).
- No significant AVQI differences between groups, though a high proportion of participants in every group showed very mild dysphonia (abstract).
- Among transgender women, those whose GHS began with GnRH agonists at earlier pubertal stages tended to have higher f0 than those who started later on cyproterone acetate; phonosurgery appeared to increase f0 while "the impact of voice training was inconsistent" (abstract).
- Conclusion as stated: masculinization from adolescent-onset GAHT typically yields vocal characteristics comparable to cisgender men; for transgender women "earlier GHS may help preserve a more feminine voice, but complete alignment with cisgender women is not guaranteed (if this is the goal)", underscoring early pubertal intervention and access to individualized voice care including speech-language pathology (abstract).

## Bearing on voice training
- The puberty-suppression answer for [[puberty-voice-change]] and [[hormones-and-voice]]: blocking testosterone puberty keeps the larynx and voice nearer the pre-pubertal shape, and starting suppression earlier (GnRHa at early Tanner stages) tends to leave a higher adult f0 than later anti-androgen suppression.
- For trans men, adolescent-onset testosterone reaches cis-male-typical voice; for trans women, hormones started in adolescence do not by themselves deliver a cis-female-typical f0 in connected speech — therapy and, for some, surgery remain part of the pathway ([[voice-feminization-therapy]], [[voice-surgery]]).

## Notes
- Cross-sectional, single centre (Ghent University Hospital); group sizes unequal (88 vs 23); voice-training history not controlled, which the authors acknowledge makes the training effect "inconsistent" rather than absent.
- Authors include the Ghent speech-language pathology group behind [[leyns2021]] and [[leyns2023]].
