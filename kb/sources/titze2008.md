# The Human Instrument

## What it says
- The framing question, from the standfirst: "When judged by its size, our vocal system fails to impress as a musical instrument. How then can singers produce all those remarkable sounds?"
- On the source: "Vocal folds generate a fundamental frequency by rapidly oscillating as they contact each other, separate and come in contact again."
- On the tissue that makes the oscillation possible: "The mucosa, which consists of a very thin skin with a fluidlike substance underneath, is easily deformed and can support a so-called surface wave."
- On the tract as an instrument body: "The laryngeal vestibule, an airway passage just above the larynx, acts like the mouthpiece of the trumpet to couple the sound to the remaining part of the resonator."
- On feedback from tract to source: "The vocal tract gives a 'kick' to each cycle of oscillation of the vocal folds so as to increase the amplitude of vibratory motions."
- On sex: "Singers can find inertive reactance as high as 800 or 900 Hz for males and 20 percent higher for females."

## Bearing on voice training
- The clearest plain-language statement of why the tract is not a passive filter: shaping the tube above the larynx changes how easily the larynx vibrates, which is the physical content of "resonance makes the voice easier, not just brighter".
