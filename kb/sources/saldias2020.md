# Water Resistance Therapy as Vocal Warm-Up Method in CCM Singers

## What it says
- "Although water resistance therapy (WRT) has been widely used in voice training, no data are supporting the effectiveness of WRT as vocal warm-up for singers."
- 22 contemporary commercial music singers were randomised to a 15-minute open-vocal-tract warm-up or a 15-minute WRT warm-up; aerodynamic, electroglottographic, acoustic and self-perception measures were taken before, immediately after, and after 40 minutes of vocal loading.
- Immediately after warm-up, subglottic pressure and inspiratory airflow duration fell in both groups; SPL fell for the open-vocal-tract group but not the WRT group.
- After vocal loading, subglottic pressure and inspiratory airflow duration fell in both groups; expiratory airflow duration and EGG contact quotient fell for the open-vocal-tract group.
- Conclusion: "Some objective data suggest that the WRT method is more effective as vocal warm-up than OVT exercises. Since outcomes in self-perceived resonant voice quality for both methods were similar but physiological effects were different, vocal warm-up strategies might produce a placebo effect."

## Bearing on voice training
- Warming up before a long voice-practice session has measurable aerodynamic effects, and the felt effect is the same whichever method is used — the authors themselves raise the placebo possibility.

## Notes
- 22 singers, a single session; the placebo remark is the authors' interpretation of a self-report/physiology mismatch, not a controlled test of placebo.
