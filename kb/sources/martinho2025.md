# Diferentes medidas de frequência fundamental e satisfação vocal de homens e mulheres transgênero

## What it says
- 11 trans women (mean age 26.91) and 7 trans men (mean age 26.57) recorded a sustained vowel and automatic speech; seven acoustic measures of f0 position and variability were extracted, and the Trans Woman Voice Questionnaire (TWVQ) was applied (abstract, "Método"). Cross-sectional, quantitative.
- Group means: the women's f0 was 165.2 Hz on vowels and 144.5 Hz in speech; the men's 143.3 Hz on vowels and 138.9 Hz in speech. On vowels the two groups are 22 Hz apart; in connected speech they nearly coincide.
- Self-rated targets differed: the trans women 「desejavam uma voz um pouco feminina」 (TWVQ 72.8) and the trans men 「uma voz um pouco masculina」 (68.4) — most participants wanted a slightly-gendered voice, not an extreme.
- Of the seven f0 measures, "only maximum f0 during number counting in the women" correlated with the TWVQ, and negatively (p = 0.043).
- Conclusion: 「embora a fo possa desempenhar um papel na percepção do gênero na voz, ela não é o único determinante da satisfação vocal nesta população」.

## Bearing on voice training
- The clearest recent Brazilian statement of the vault's central practical problem: measured f0 explains almost nothing about how satisfied a speaker is ([[patient-reported-outcomes]], [[fundamental-frequency]]). One weak correlation out of seven measures is a null result worth keeping.
- The vowel-versus-speech gap — 22 Hz apart on vowels, 6 Hz apart in speech — is the same task dependence [[sakuraba2009]] and [[formants]] describe, and it argues against vowel-only targets ([[intonation]], [[voice-therapy-techniques]]).

## Notes
- n = 18, single centre (Unicamp with USP), no therapy and no listener ratings; graded low for size and design.
- Both groups' mean speech f0 sits in the same 139–145 Hz band, which the paper does not discuss; trans men after testosterone and trans women without it are being compared as groups by gender rather than by treatment status.
- Claims taken from the Portuguese abstract.
