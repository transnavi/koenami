# A nonrandomized trial of the Global Voice Prevention and Therapy Model with Estill Voice Training

## What it says
- 82 vocally healthy student teachers completed in-person treatment, telepractice treatment, or control. "Estill Voice Training was used as the stimulability component of the GVPTM to train multiple new voices meeting all the vocal needs of the student teachers."
- Outcomes were acoustic, perceptual and aerodynamic measures captured with the VoiceEvalU8 app, before and after in autumn and again during student teaching in spring.
- "Significant improvements were achieved for several acoustic and perceptual measures in the treatment conditions, but not in the control condition. The in-person and telepractice conditions produced similar results."
- "The all-voiced phrase and connected speech were more successful in demonstrating voice change for some of the perturbation measures as compared to sustained /a/."
- "The treatment conditions were successful in decreasing the negative impact of voice-related quality of life and vocal fatigue during student teaching."
- Future work is called for on "the relevance of defining auditory-perceptual terms by the anatomy and physiology of the voice production system (i.e., Estill Voice Training)."

## Bearing on voice training
- The clearest peer-reviewed outcome study using Estill Voice Training, whose "figures" (true vocal fold thick/thin, twang, larynx height, cry) are the vocabulary many trans voice teachers borrow. It also shows connected speech detects change that a sustained vowel misses — an argument for measuring trans voice on sentences.
- Telepractice matched in-person, which matters for trans voice work that is largely delivered online.

## Notes
- Nonrandomized, healthy speakers, single investigator who also authors the assessment app; no trans participants.
