# Trans and Gender-Diverse Voice & Communication Therapy: Competency Framework (RCSLT 2019)

## What it says
- The UK profession's training and competency framework "for speech and language therapists (SLTs) working with trans and gender-diverse people across the UK", written for the Royal College by the National Clinical Excellence Network in Transgender Voice and Communication and gender specialists — 17 named contributors, mostly specialist SLTs at gender identity clinics (Tavistock and Portman, Sheffield, Kent, Belfast, NHS Lothian) together with gender-clinic physicians (title page, acknowledgements; contributors include Matthew Mills and Gillie Stoneham, the authors of [[mills2017]]).
- The role statement: "As part of a multidisciplinary team (MDT), SLTs have a key role to play"; Voice and Communication Therapy "enables trans and gender-diverse people to align their vocal and communicative expression with their identity", can reduce distress related to gender dysphoria, and "can also help reduce the occurrence of transphobia and being misgendered" (section 2.3).
- Access and workforce: "Trans clients frequently report difficulty accessing local voice services, with SLTs citing lack of training, clinical experience or expertise with this client group" (section 3.6, guidance to higher education institutions).
- Prerequisite core voice competencies before working autonomously with trans clients: voice case-history taking, auditory-perceptual assessment (GRBAS or VSPP), voice-care advice rationale, core therapy skills (posture, breath support, reducing hyperfunction, resonance and flow), objective instrumentation such as electrolaryngography and Praat, appreciation of voice pathologies, and basic counselling skills (Core voice competencies).
- A three-level hierarchy: Level A — developing skills, caseload "in the region of 15 different clients in a 12-month period", likely mostly trans women; Level B — "highly experienced in clinical voice disorders", caseload 6–19, may supervise others; Level C — "highly specialised practitioners … predominantly working with a trans and gender-diverse caseload at a GIC/SGIS/SCSGD", at least 20 clients a year, able to assess and refer for pitch surgery within the MDT (Levels A–C). GIC/SGIS/SCSGD are given as the UK's gender-identity-service acronyms (Terminology).
- Supervisors signing off competencies must themselves be signed off at Level B or C, and employers are responsible for arranging supervision (section 3.4, 3.5).
- Development method (Appendix 2): an RCSLT workshop in June 2014 identified the need; a Clinical Excellence Network founded October 2015 revised the draft; a profession-wide public consultation ran 9 January – 3 February 2017; of the SLTs who responded, 92.8% reported working with trans and gender-diverse clients and 32.1% saw more than 20 clients per year; a review group met four times in 2017 before publication.
- Education guidance: universities should "introduce SLT students to the topic of the trans client group" in pre-registration courses, and a specialist SLT and trans service users "should be involved in the development and delivery" of any modules (section 3.6).

## Bearing on voice training
- The most explicit document on what makes a provider qualified: the UK profession defines competency tiers by caseload and supervision, and ties pitch-surgery referral to the top tier — the workforce structure behind [[speech-language-pathology]].
- The "difficulty accessing local voice services / lack of training" line is the UK workforce-side counterpart to the demand-side access gap in [[ziltzer2023]] and the Taiwan readiness survey [[litosseliti2019]] ([[transgender-care-context]]).
- The framework's competency lists (pitch, range, resonance, intonation, voice quality interventions; flexible vocal modelling; telephone and work-context carryover) mirror what the trial literature measures ([[voice-feminization-therapy]]).

## Notes
- Graded `high` as a professional-body framework validated by a documented, profession-wide public consultation; it standardizes workforce competency, not clinical effectiveness — no outcome data.
- Terminology ("trans and gender-diverse", GIC/SGIS/SCSGD) is UK and current to 2019; the references list still cites SOC7 (WPATH 2012).
