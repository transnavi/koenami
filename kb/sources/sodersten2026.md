## What it says
- Prospective repeated-measures study: 74 transgender women completed an 8–12 session voice training program, with recordings taken twice before and twice after training; 40 cisgender speakers provided reference data.
- Extracted f0 (average/min/max), formant frequencies F1–F4, sound pressure level (Leq), and the level difference between the first and second harmonic (L1–L2, a spectral-tilt/voice-quality-related measure) from reading-passage and spontaneous speech; 79 naive listeners rated gender-related impressions of the recordings.
- Group data showed substantial training effects on f0 and formant frequencies; individually, many participants also increased Leq (loudness), and some increased L1–L2 (shifted spectral tilt).
- The acoustic measures most strongly predicting listener ratings of a "female-sounding" voice were f0, average formant frequency, and Leq.
- Describes this as the largest prospective study to date of acoustic outcomes of gender-affirming voice training in trans women, confirming smaller prior studies and demonstrating that listener perceptions track f0/formant/loudness changes.

## Bearing on voice training
- Largest-sample confirmation that f0, average formant frequency, and vocal intensity (loudness) together are the acoustic measures most predictive of listener "female-sounding" ratings — a concrete, ranked target list for training programs.
- Notes L1–L2 (spectral tilt/voice quality) changed for only some participants, suggesting voice-quality/breathiness adjustment is a secondary, individually variable lever rather than a universal necessity for successful outcomes.
- Explicitly argues acoustic measures alone are not sufficient for evaluating training success — client-reported outcomes should be combined with acoustic and listener-rating data.

## Notes
- N = 74 trainees + 40 cisgender reference speakers is large for this literature; linear mixed-effects modeling used for group-level training effects, with individual-level analysis linking acoustic change to listener ratings.
- Population and language not stated in the abstract excerpt available; venue and prior Södersten/Oates work suggest a Swedish/Australian clinical collaboration.
