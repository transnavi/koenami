# Vocal tract setting in hyperfunctional dysphonia and in belting: a CT study

## What it says
- Background: "Vocal tract setting in hyperfunctional patients is characterized by a high larynx and narrowing of the epilaryngeal and pharyngeal region. Similar observations have been made for various singing styles, eg, belting." Belting is described as "loud, speech like, and high pitched" and "often described as sounding 'pressed' or 'tense'", which "has led to the hypothesis that belting may be strenuous to the vocal folds. However, singers and teachers of belting do not regard belting as particularly strenuous."
- Four male patients with hyperfunctional dysphonia and one male contemporary commercial music singer were scanned with CT while phonating [a:] at habitual speaking pitch; the singer additionally belted on G4.
- "Various similarities were found between belting and hyperfunction: high vertical larynx position, small hypopharyngeal width, and epilaryngeal outlet. On the other hand, belting differed from dysphonia (in addition to higher pitch) by a wider lip and jaw opening, and larger volumes of the oral cavity."
- Conclusion: "Belting takes advantage of 'megaphone shape' of the vocal tract." Future work should address sound-energy transfer, aerodynamics and fold vibration "to evaluate the 'price of decibels' in these phonation types."

## Bearing on voice training
- Shows why belting can look like strain on a scan and still be a trained, sustainable setting: the narrowed epilarynx is shared with MTD, the open mouth is not. A trans speaker raising pitch with a high larynx and a closed mouth is closer to the dysphonia side of this comparison than to the belt.
