## What it says
- Compared perceptual femaleness ratings of 9 MtF transsexuals after cricothyroid approximation (CA) against 9 non-transsexual males and 9 non-transsexual females, using video recordings presented auditory-only and then audiovisually to 42 student judges (21 female, 21 male) rating on a 100 mm visual analog scale from "very male" to "very female".
- The MtF group's scores fell between the male and female comparison groups in both auditory-only and audiovisual presentation.
- Perception of femaleness significantly correlated with average fundamental frequency in both presentation modes.
- Concludes that CA is a viable option for raising pitch but that surgery alone may not be sufficient to produce a voice perceived as fully female.

## Bearing on voice training
- Evidence-based caveat for surgery-only approaches: even with a successful pitch increase, CTA alone did not move perceptual ratings fully into the "female" range, supporting combined surgery + therapy approaches (see park2024, chowdhury2025).
- Demonstrates that F0 remains the acoustic parameter most tightly linked to listener-perceived femaleness even post-surgery.

## Notes
- Very small samples (9 per group, 42 judges); Belgian sample; audiovisual condition adds a facial/visual confound to the auditory femaleness judgment.
