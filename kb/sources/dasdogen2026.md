# Long-Term Average Spectrum Analysis of Voice After Endoscopic Vocal Fold Shortening and Therapy

## What it says
- Rationale given in the abstract: endoscopic vocal fold shortening (EVFS) "is a common gender-affirming surgical procedure for transgender women, yet postoperative acoustic outcomes have largely been described using only fundamental frequency (f o) and traditional clinical measures, leaving broader acoustic characterization limited".
- This study analysed Long-Term Average Spectrum (LTAS) at baseline, 3 months and 12 months after surgery, using spectral slope and Formant Cluster Prominence (FCP in dB and Hz); at 12 months the trans participants were compared against cisgender male and female control voices (abstract, "Methods").
- At 3 months: "spectral energy increased in the higher frequency range, spectral slope flattened, and FCP [dB] was significantly reduced".
- At 12 months: spectral slope "remained flatter than baseline" and FCP [dB] "demonstrated a modest rebound, but remained below preoperative levels".
- Group comparison: "clear approximation of transgender women toward cisgender female spectral profiles at 12 months, with divergence from cisgender male patterns".
- Sound pressure level "remained stable, suggesting spectral changes primarily reflected structural and behavioral adaptation rather than intensity".
- Conclusion: the time course "suggest[s] behavioral adaptation to surgical modification may extend for up to a year", making LTAS a useful monitoring tool.

## Bearing on voice training
- Shows the surgery's spectral effect, not just its pitch effect — the long-term average spectrum is a different measurement of "brightness" from formants, and it is the one that converges on cis female reference profiles after glottoplasty [[harmonics-and-spectrum]] [[voice-surgery]].
- The year-long drift after the operation is evidence that the outcome is surgical *plus* behavioural: a shortened fold length still has to be re-learned into the speaking pattern [[voice-quality]].

## Notes
- Single centre (all four authors listed at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai in the record); the abstract does not report participant numbers or whether all had voice therapy as well as surgery.
- Claims taken from the PubMed abstract (Laryngoscope 2026 May;136(5); Epub 2025 Dec 25).
