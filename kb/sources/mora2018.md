## What it says
- Retrospective study of 53 MtF transsexuals (28 cricothyroid approximation [CTA], 23 glottoplasty [GL]) treated 2007–2016, mean age 35, median follow-up 40 months.
- Outcomes: fundamental frequency (F0), perceptual GBRAS/Hirano rating, Voice Handicap Index-10 (Spanish validated version), self-perceived femininity of voice, and overall satisfaction (visual analogue scale).
- Mean F0 improved after surgery in both groups, but the increase was 27 Hz higher after GL than after CTA (P < 0.05); F0 decreased over time after CTA but remained stable after GL.
- GBRAS score did not change after CTA; roughness and total GBRAS score increased from 0 to 1 after GL (P < 0.05).
- VHI-10 scores decreased similarly in both groups; self-perceived femininity increase was higher after GL; satisfaction was mild and similar between groups; one case (4%) of severe dysphonia from fibrous synechia occurred after GL.
- Conclusion: both procedures raise F0 and improve quality of life, but GL gives a larger and more durable F0 increase at a higher risk of postoperative roughness. Level of evidence: 4.

## Bearing on voice training
- Direct head-to-head surgical comparison — useful for a "surgery vs surgery" (not surgery vs. therapy) evidence page and for quantifying the CTA-vs-glottoplasty F0 tradeoff (27 Hz difference) plus the roughness risk tradeoff.
- VHI-10 improvement in both arms supports the general claim that pitch-raising surgery improves voice-related quality of life regardless of technique chosen.

## Notes
- Level of evidence 4 (retrospective, no randomization); single center in Madrid, Spain.
- Population: MtF transsexual adults; n is modest (53) and follow-up length varied.
