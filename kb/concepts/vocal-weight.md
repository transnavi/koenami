# Vocal weight — the community's name for the source dimension (声の重さ)

In English-community training vocabulary, vocal weight is the perceived heaviness of the voiced source: how much vocal-fold mass is vibrating and how long the folds stay closed each cycle. It is drilled as one axis of three, separate from pitch (F0) and from size/resonance (vocal-tract length). The acoustic literature measures the same source behaviour as closed/open quotient, H1–H2 and spectral tilt, without using the community's word.

## What is known

**The community's definition.**
- The Voice Resource Project wiki defines it as "a perceived quality of voice. It describes a heaviness or rumble that is present in most masculine voices" and "an educated guess at the amount of Vocal Fold Vibratory Mass or 'how much of the vocal folds is moving'", with lighter heard as more feminine and heavier as more masculine [[sumi-vocal-weight]].
- The same page gives the aliases the community treats as one thing: "This is also known as: OQ/CQ open and closed quotient; vocal fold vibratory mass; thickness and thinness of the folds" [[sumi-vocal-weight]].
- Sumi's pitch-slide lesson describes the exercises as designed to change all of those at once and "result in the sound quality we hear that is sometimes called 'harsh' 'buzzy' or 'heavy' in tone"; the author calls weight "one of the more powerful gender determining factors of voice" — his claim, not a listener study [[sumi2021]].
- TransVoiceLessons taught the same contrast earlier as "thick" and "thin" fold mass, "rooted in physiology from hormones and genetics" but behaviorally controllable "across a great spectrum of options" [[erose2020a]], and later as "vocal weight" drilled at constant pitch, i.e. as a source change separable from F0 [[erose2022a]].
- Weight interacts with tract size in TVL's "fullness" model: "Overfull is a harsh, saturated, twangy sound formed by a heavy—small relationship. Underfull, on the other hand, is a hollow, dull, empty sound formed by a light—large relationship" [[erose2022]].
- The vocabulary has spread beyond its originators: an independent coach's ear-training video credits "these concepts … to Clover and Z of TransVoiceLessons" [[dasilva2022]], and weight exercises for lightening or heavying are taught for trans and nonbinary voices in both directions [[yoxon2025]].
- Classical singing uses the same words for a different contrast — lyric vs dramatic voice classification — so the singing article must not be read as the community definition [[wikipedia-vocal-weight]].
- The wiki's pitch naturalisation method is a weight-reduction drill in which the learner speaks at their relaxed, default pitch, lets the last word of each phrase float up a little, and attends to the sound rather than to any single feature: "the goal is not to 'raise pitch', but to improve the voice at every pitch" [[sumi-pipm]].
- For a low-pitched feminine voice the wiki prescribes descending in 20 Hz steps from 200 Hz while speaking at each step, and redrilling resonance voiced at the low pitch [[sumi-low-fem-voices]].
- The wiki gives base pitch as 「the pitch the voice naturally wants to return to」 after intonation, states that it 「is determined mostly by vocal weight」, and sets the pairing out as 「Heavier vocal weight –> lower base pitch / Lighter weight –> can maintain higher base pitch」 [[sumi-base-pitch]].
- The wiki's separately signed invented vocabulary defines welit as the estimated vocal fold vibratory mass, with adduction, twang, volume and creak explicitly excluded from it [[sumi-new-terminology]].

**The acoustics that measure the same thing.**
- A more open glottal configuration gives a source with "relatively greater low-frequency and weaker high-frequency components" plus aspiration noise and wider formant bandwidths — the H1–H2 family of measures [[hanson1997]].
- Male speakers close more completely and show less spectral tilt: the first harmonic relative to the third formant was "9.6 dB lower for the male speakers than for the female speakers", while "there is considerable overlap across gender" [[hanson1999]].
- The male–female source difference at normal and loud voice lies in AC flow and maximum airflow declination rate — how sharply the glottis closes — not in air pressure; in soft voice "the male and female glottal waveforms were more alike" [[holmberg1988]].
- Register differences survive pitch control: open quotient and H1–H2 differed among chest, mix and falsetto "even at the same fundamental frequency", ranking chest < mix < falsetto [[lee2023]].
- The mechanism is fold bulk and closure time: thyroarytenoid contraction thickens and shortens the folds, and the longer closed phase of chest register "typically results in a 'brighter' voice sound with stronger overtones", together with a higher maximum flow declination rate [[herbst2020]].
- On Bozeman's account the voice source contributes only two things, F0 and spectral slope, with mode 1 near −3 dB/octave ("brassier") and mode 2 near −12 dB/octave ("warmer, flutier") [[bozeman2022]].
- The pressed pole of weight — glottal constriction, "a small peak glottal opening, long closed phase, and low glottal airflow" — shows up acoustically as low H1–H2 [[keating2015]].
- H1–H2 and H2–H4 are perceptually real dimensions for listeners: changes in them are heard as breathiness changes, at least where phonation is contrastive in the listener's language [[garellek2013]]; English listeners sit at the least-sensitive end of a cross-language continuum, so community weight ear-training is teaching a cue English does not contrast phonemically [[kreiman2010]].
- Caution against any single number: 78 existing measures of source spectral slope showed "high levels of duplication and overlap" and "are unlikely to be good predictors of voice quality" [[kreiman2007]].
- In the DSFD teacher dataset, electroglottograph contact quotient tracked the teacher's weight settings alongside F0 and ΔF — but in perception from that dataset ΔF, not CQ, was the strongest predictor of perceived gender [[netzorg2025]].
- In the largest prospective training study, the spectral-tilt measure L1–L2 shifted for only some participants, while F0, average formant frequency and loudness were the measures that most strongly predicted listener "female-sounding" ratings [[sodersten2026]].

**What Koenami's 質感 (HNR) does and does not measure.**
- 質感 is the median harmonics-to-noise ratio by Praat's cross-correlation method; the method page states it is affected by breath, hoarseness and recording noise and 「声の重さを直接表す指標ではありません」 ("is not a direct measure of vocal weight") [[koenami-method]].
- HNR indexes how periodic the source is — how much noise mixes with the harmonics — not how long the folds stay closed or how steeply the spectrum falls, the quantities the weight literature measures as OQ/CQ, H1–H2 and tilt [[hanson1997]] [[lee2023]] [[sodersten2026]].
- The community draws the same boundary from the other side: the wiki keeps Breathiness as its own page, separate from Vocal Weight, and its weight page warns that breathiness "is to be avoided" as a route to lightness [[sumianvoice]] [[sumi-vocal-weight]].

## Contested or unclear
- How much weight per se moves perceived gender: the community ranks it "one of the more powerful" factors [[sumi2021]] and its gendered-factors page puts vocal weight first, ahead of microbehaviours, resonance and speech patterns [[sumi-vocal-gender]], but in DSFD perception ΔF dominated over CQ [[netzorg2025]], in training outcomes tilt changed for only some participants [[sodersten2026]], and breathiness as a gender cue had mixed results in the meta-analysis [[leung2018]].
- Quietness is not lightness: Sumi warns that getting "really quiet it will sound similar, but you won't be changing vocal weight" [[sumi2021]], and Holmberg's data agree from the other direction — the male–female source difference shrinks in soft voice, so quiet speech is a less gendered source, not a lighter one [[holmberg1988]].
- No validated single acoustic proxy exists: existing slope measures overlap and predict perceived quality poorly [[kreiman2007]], and HNR and tilt are confounded by microphone and noise [[voice-quality]].

## Open questions
- Whether the community's lightening drills (pitch slides, SOVT, onset work) change CQ or H1–H2 in connected speech, and how durable the change is — no study has measured any of them [[sumi2021]] [[erose2020a]].
- How weight and loudness decouple in practice: many trainees raised loudness alongside F0 and formants [[sodersten2026]], and the community teaches loudness and weight as coupled but separable [[sumi2021]].
- Safety of heavy practice: the community's own warnings name hyperadduction and pressed phonation [[sumi-vocal-weight]]; no data exist on injury rates from community drills.
