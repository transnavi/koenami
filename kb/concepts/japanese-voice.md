# Japanese voice and gender

What is known about gendered voice specifically in Japanese: pitch norms and their cultural basis, clinical practice, vocabulary for voice impressions, and the 両声類 tradition of one speaker producing both voices.

## What is known
- Japanese women's higher pitch relative to Dutch women is explained by cultural associations, with Japanese masculinity pulling men's pitch lower [[vanbezooijen1995]]; young Japanese women use creaky voice far less than Californian women [[yuasa2010]].
- Attractiveness of female voices peaks near 280–290 Hz and falls above; formant dispersion shows the same inverted U [[tanaka2022]]; 両声類 naturalness reported highest at 200–240 Hz from one contest [[hasegawa2017]].
- Everyday voice-quality vocabulary: three factors (clarity, power, pitch/youth) [[kido2001]]; singing-voice impression scales (power, politeness, brightness) [[kanato2016]]; voice-actor descriptor pairs [[yasuda2023]]; free-text descriptions corpus [[watanabe2023]]; voice-actor character voices follow conventions [[hayashi2026]].
- Clinical: type IV thyroplasty as the dominant feminization surgery, tuned under local anaesthesia [[nakamura2007]] [[sanuki2007]], paired with therapy [[kobayashi2022]], anterior glottoplasty now compared [[hiroshiba2024]]; CTA with intraoperative monitoring [[maeda2025]]; testosterone time course in trans men [[nimura2015]] [[nimura2022]] [[yanagi2015]].
- Reference corpora: JVS (100 speakers, studio) [[takamichi2019]]; Koenami's Common Voice selection (method page).
- Within-speaker impression shifts can be predicted from self-supervised representations on one professional speaker [[fujita2026]].

## Contested or unclear
- Naturalness ceiling for feminine F0 in Japanese rests on weak data [[hasegawa2017]] [[tanaka2022]].

## Open questions
- No Japanese listener study of trans voices; no validated Japanese TVQ; no data on pitch accent versus training targets.
