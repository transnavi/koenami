# Japanese voice and gender

What is known about gendered voice specifically in Japanese: pitch norms and their cultural basis, clinical practice, vocabulary for voice impressions, and the 両声類 tradition of one speaker producing both voices.

## What is known
- Japanese women's higher pitch relative to Dutch women is explained by cultural associations, with Japanese masculinity pulling men's pitch lower [[vanbezooijen1995]]; young Japanese women use creaky voice far less than Californian women [[yuasa2010]].
- Attractiveness of female voices peaks near 280–290 Hz and falls above; formant dispersion shows the same inverted U [[tanaka2022]]; 両声類 naturalness reported highest at 200–240 Hz from one contest [[hasegawa2017]].
- Everyday voice-quality vocabulary: three factors (clarity, power, pitch/youth) [[kido2001]]; singing-voice impression scales (power, politeness, brightness) [[kanato2016]]; voice-actor descriptor pairs [[yasuda2023]]; free-text descriptions corpus [[watanabe2023]]; voice-actor character voices track real aging acoustics except the boy voice, which follows a character convention [[hayashi2026]].
- Clinical: type IV thyroplasty as the dominant feminization surgery, tuned under local anaesthesia [[nakamura2007]] [[sanuki2007]], paired with therapy [[kobayashi2022]], anterior glottoplasty now compared [[hiroshiba2024]]; CTA with intraoperative monitoring [[maeda2025]]; testosterone time course in trans men [[nimura2015]] [[nimura2022]] [[yanagi2015]], with a 2013 report on speaking F0 during testosterone administration recorded in CiNii but unread [[masaoka2013]]. The psychological side of the same operations is thinner still: a 2003 follow-up of voice feminisation by cricothyroid approximation is recorded but its text was not reachable [[umemiya2003]].
- Reference corpora: JVS (100 speakers, studio) [[takamichi2019]]; Koenami's Common Voice selection (method page).
- Within-speaker impression shifts can be predicted from self-supervised representations on one professional speaker [[fujita2026]].
- A Japanese listening experiment with 119 MtF speakers and 32 cis women found the F0 at which 70% or more of listeners judged a voice female was on average 270 Hz for sustained vowels and 217 Hz for connected reading, against cis women's own means of 243 Hz and 217 Hz; within the cis F0 range, close to 70% of the MtF voices were still not judged female [[sakuraba2009]]. The same group's earlier work is a 2003 conference abstract on MtF voices heard as female [[imaizumi2003]] and a 2007 master's thesis whose automatic estimator of 知覚的女声度 reached r = 0.86 against listener ratings, where listeners agreed with each other at r = 0.88 [[maruyama2007]]; it also produced a single-case MtF voice-training report in 2005 [[sakuraba2005]] and a 2007 試案 for classifying MtF connected speech [[sakuraba2007]].
- The national guideline has no voice chapter: the 身体的治療 chapter of the 第4版改 (2018) and the 第5版 (2024) covers hormone therapy, mastectomy and gender-affirming surgery, and voice is not among the 第5版's listed revision points [[jspn-guideline2018]] [[jspn-guideline2024]].
- The laryngeal operations are ENT practice published outside gender medicine: one Kyoto clinic reports 87 type-IV cases whose mean F0 moved from 162 Hz to 240 Hz with a slow decline over six months, and 404 声帯短縮術 from 2023 to October 2024, with post-operative voice training billed separately [[kyoto-ent-clinic]]; a Tokyo clinic offers both directions and lists its prices as 保険適用外, stating an intra-operative voice check except for 声帯短縮術 [[met-ent-tokyo]].
- Money and law: surgery at GI学会 certified facilities in line with the guideline has been covered by national health insurance since FY2018 while hormone therapy has not [[jspn-guideline2024]] [[gidjp-opinion2023]]; community accounts say the ban on 混合診療 and a requirement that the 戸籍 already read 女性 keep the insured surgery route out of reach in practice [[note-maru2blog]].
- A 2005 case report describes severe breathy phonation after pitch-elevation surgery performed abroad, noting that such surgery for MtF people 「これまでガイドラインに明確な定義がされていなかった」 and had been reported almost nowhere in Japan, and that granuloma excision and conservative treatment left the patient unsatisfied about five months after the operation [[mesuda2005]].
- A voice-therapy series treated 29 MtF patients between January 2001 and December 2005; some already had speaking F0 above 180 Hz and still complained that the voice was low or that the high voice was unstable, so the targets became raising speaking F0, producing high notes without constriction or register break, and sustaining a high pitch in connected speech, mainly through Vocal Function Exercises and Pitch Matching [[sato2007]].
- The gender treatment team established at Okayama University Hospital in 1999 listed 声の変化 and 喉頭手術 as two entries among its ancillary MtF procedures, beside reduction thyroid chondroplasty, hair transplant and laser epilation [[takahashi2001]]; a review from the same hospital's gender centre is recorded under the keywords 性同一性障害, 包括的治療 and 性別適合手術 but deposits no abstract [[namba2014]].
- A YouTube lesson series titled 「ナチュラル女声の出し方」 gives its first instalment (第1章) to 音色, timbre; the later chapters were not examined [[ryoseiruiuniv2021]].
- A 専攻医トレーニング講座 article in the Japanese ENT society's journal for 2024 covers the diagnosis and treatment of voice disorders in general, under a title with no gender-specific scope [[nakamura2024]].
- The 両声類 tradition has a published pedagogy outside trans circles: a 声優 vocational school teaches メラニー法, ささやき法 and 濁り抽出法 for male speakers and defines チェストボイス・ミドルボイス・ヘッドボイス・ウィスパーボイス by where the vibration is felt [[anime-academy-ryoseirui2021]]; see [[japanese-community]].

## Contested or unclear
- The Japanese basis for a feminine F0 target is thin: naturalness from a single undergraduate contest [[hasegawa2017]], and attractiveness ratings from male college students, an endpoint separate from whether a voice is judged female [[tanaka2022]].

## Open questions
- No Japanese listener study of 両声類 voices beyond one naturalness contest [[hasegawa2017]], and none measuring laryngeal or pharyngeal posture during 女声 production; no validated Japanese TVQ; no data on pitch accent versus training targets.
