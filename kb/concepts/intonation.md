# Intonation, pitch range and prosody (抑揚)

The movement of pitch, loudness and timing across an utterance. Koenami's 抑揚 is the 10–90th percentile F0 span in semitones. In Japanese, lexical pitch accent and sentence-final contours carry much of this movement, so range is not a free stylistic variable.

## What is known
- Intonation is associated with listener gender perception; tempo and stress are not [[leung2018]].
- Pitch variation and intensity correlated with femininity ratings of trans women in one thesis [[dahl2018]]; vocal range in Hz (not semitones) correlated with femininity ratings and TVQ scores in Hebrew-speaking trans women [[diamant2021]].
- Speech rate and loudness predicted naturalness ratings, alongside F0 and formants [[hardy2020]].
- Cross-cultural pitch differences between Japanese and Dutch women were explained by cultural ideals rather than anatomy [[vanbezooijen1995]].
- A randomised sham-controlled trial (n = 30) found four weeks of intonation training raised every acoustic intonation parameter, the F0 median and its 25th/75th percentiles, and listener femininity ratings [[papeleu2025]].
- Ascending intonation predicted gender congruence in trans women's connected speech, and for trans men tense vocal quality and descending intonation were among the markers named for masculine perception [[irineu2025]].
- The English-language wiki's feminine speech-pattern list runs a higher and more frequent intonation range, tempo variation, and emphasis by high pitch, with the masculine list inverting each using weight and volume [[sumi-speech-patterns]].

## Contested or unclear
- A wider range is not uniformly "more feminine"; lexical tone and pitch accent set part of the contour (Koenami method page).

## Open questions
- How Tokyo- and Kansai-type accent systems affect measured range for the same sentence.
- Japanese-specific evidence on intonation and gender perception is missing from this vault.
