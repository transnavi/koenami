# Voice impressions beyond gender

Warmth, dominance, age, attractiveness and the everyday words listeners use.

## What is known
- Sub-second "hello" yields consistent impressions on valence and dominance; female attractiveness is driven by valence [[mcaleer2014]].
- A Russian-language review of the voice-and-personality literature reports that negative emotions are recognised from the voice better than positive ones, anger and sadness most accurately of all, and that among the Big Five extraversion showed the largest number of significant correlations with acoustic-prosodic and auditory features [[rafikova2022]].
- Japanese vocabularies: [[kido2001]], [[kanato2016]], [[yasuda2023]], [[watanabe2023]]; English tags [[diwan2025]].
- Attractiveness of female voices in Japanese listeners follows an inverted U in F0 and formant dispersion [[tanaka2022]]; voice actors' character voices are perceived reliably [[hayashi2026]].
- Perceived sexual orientation and masculinity/femininity are separable dimensions [[munson2007]].
- "Microbehaviours" is the English-language wiki's word for the residual that survives pitch, weight and resonance work, which it says takes years to change and is reachable mainly by mimicking a target voice [[sumi-microbehaviours]]; its list of them holds two entries — low pitch brightness, which 「might not be a microbehaviour and instead just be something as simple as adding twang」, and high resonance deepness, deepness without brightness — and concedes that 「there are no terms available that can describe most of these」 [[sumi-microbehaviour-list]]. The same wiki keeps a separate hall-of-fame list of voices held for interest, the five entries read each an embedded player with no description text and no tags [[sumi-hall-of-fame]].
- Transgender and gender non-conforming participants reported lower vocal congruence than cisgender participants in every condition, most starkly where auditory feedback was present [[delivio2026]].
- A Korean STS thesis frames vocal transition as 「becoming a voice」, adapted from Latour's 「becoming a nose」, argues the female voice it aims at is 「not a fixed endpoint but a direction that takes shape through dynamic relational processes」, and treats telephones, voice chat, recording devices, apps, spectrograms and listeners as actors in the process, the journey beginning with 「becoming an ear」 [[yun2025]].
- A narrator's essay separates "voice gender" from self-identification and ends by treating the voice as a work tool [[note-zunba]].
