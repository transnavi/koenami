# Voice health (声の健康)

The risk side of using the voice hard, and of changing it: hydration, warm-up, hygiene, muscle-tension dysphonia, nodules, puberphonia / mutational falsetto, and the strain that can come with pushing pitch. These are health-adjacent notes for adults; they record what the sources state, including when to see a laryngologist. They are not dosing advice and not a treatment plan.

## What is known
- **When to see a laryngologist.** Pathologic stroboscopy findings were found in 5% of 61 trans women presenting for gender-affirming voice care at a tertiary centre; the authors "encourage[d]" stroboscopy "before initiating voice intervention in transgender patients" [[young2021]]. Muscle tension dysphonia "can be confirmed by history, physical exam, laryngoscopy and videostroboscopy" [[wikipedia-muscle-tension-dysphonia]]. Vocal cord nodules are "bilaterally symmetrical benign white masses … at the midpoint of the vocal folds", and "visualization … via laryngeal endoscopy remains the primary diagnostic method" [[wikipedia-vocal-cord-nodule]]. Persistent strain can be compensation for an underlying disease, which is why a review of MTD sends the patient to a laryngologist and a speech-language pathologist together, not to an exercise list alone [[vanhoutte2011]]. Guidelines recommend assessment and training with qualified clinicians [[coleman2022]] [[davies2015]].
- **Muscle tension dysphonia.** Coined in 1983 by Morrison: "a dysphonia caused by increased muscle tension of the muscles surrounding the voice box: the laryngeal and paralaryngeal muscles" [[wikipedia-muscle-tension-dysphonia]]. A review covering 1950–2009 treats MTD as "a bridge between functional and organic disorders", with aetiology in three subgroups — "psychological and/or personality factors, vocal misuse and abuse, and compensation for underlying disease" — and with no RCTs for aetiology, pathophysiology or circumlaryngeal manual therapy. Effective options listed: vocal hygiene and education (indirect); voice therapy and circumlaryngeal manual therapy (direct); medical treatment; surgery for secondary organic lesions [[vanhoutte2011]]. A high larynx "is often seen in hyperfunctional and strained voices, [so] a lowering of a habitually elevated larynx is sometimes a specific goal in clinical voice therapy" [[iwarsson1998]].
- **Nodules and related lesions.** Nodules are a visual diagnosis, bilateral and midfold [[wikipedia-vocal-cord-nodule]]. In 13 women with nodules, two weeks of confidential or resonant voice therapy showed benefit, and compliance predicted outcome better than which technique was assigned [[verdolinimarston1995]]. Resonant voice in people with nodules still used a barely-adducted configuration, but electroglottographic closed quotient differed between healthy speakers and speakers with nodules [[verdolini1998]].
- **Puberphonia / mutational falsetto.** "A functional voice disorder … characterized by the habitual use of a high-pitched voice after puberty", often referred to as a "falsetto" voice, "breathy, rough, and lacking in power", with onset usually at 11–15 years, "in the absence of other communication disorders" [[wikipedia-puberphonia]]. A narrative review of eleven studies of voice therapy for mutational falsetto (cisgender men) found "positive effects … on multiple aspects of vocal function" from eclectic techniques (yawn-sigh, glottal fry, laryngeal manipulation, inspiratory phonation, humming); voice therapy is "the first line of intervention", and "further research is needed to determine which approach or technique is most effective" [[adabi2026]]. One 22-year-old man who had not responded to therapy dropped speaking F0 from 152 Hz to 102 Hz after bilateral hyaluronic-acid injection laryngoplasty, maintained at 108 Hz at 24 months [[vandenbroek2016]]. Type III thyroplasty lowers F0 in high-pitched voice disorders including primary mutational dysphonia ([[vendramini2025]]).
- **Hydration.** In a double-blind crossover of twelve untrained adults, phonatory effort (phonation threshold pressure and perceived effort) showed "an inverse relation between phonatory effort and hydration level, but primarily for high-pitched phonation tasks" [[verdolini1994]]. That is the condition a pitch-raising speaker practises in. Nothing in that study establishes that extra fluid beyond normal hydration helps.
- **Warm-up.** "A series of exercises meant to prepare the voice for singing, acting, or other use" [[wikipedia-vocal-warm-up]]. In CCM singers, a 15-minute water-resistance warm-up and an open-tract warm-up both changed aerodynamics; self-perceived resonant quality was similar, and the authors raise a placebo possibility [[saldias2020]]. Tube phonation in singers is associated with more comfortable phonation and economy of emission [[mendes2018]].
- **Hygiene as indirect therapy.** MTD treatment lists vocal hygiene and patient education as *indirect* therapy: useful, not the treatment on their own [[vanhoutte2011]]. Among 186 Brazilian singers, knowledge of vocal health and hygiene did not correlate with vocal symptoms, even though both popular and classical groups scored above the hygiene-questionnaire cut-off [[coelho2020]]. Voice-use patterns associated with problems in 502 US adolescents included "frequent loud voice use, coughing, and/or throat clearing"; risk factors included singing, musical theatre, karaoke, vaping and secondhand smoke [[fujiki2024]].
- **Phonotrauma from pitch-raising and from loading.** Untrained Mandarin-speaking trans women already shifting formants showed acoustic patterns the authors read as a risk of fatigue and voice disorders [[huang2024]]. Trans women report speaking and singing handicap (VHI-10, Singing VHI-10) that correlates only weakly with gender-related voice concern (TVQ), so a better-gendered voice is not automatically a healthier one [[young2021]]. Adolescents identifying as transgender were more than four times as likely to report voice problems, but the confidence interval crossed 1 (7 trans participants of 502) [[fujiki2024]]. On testosterone, 24% reported instability or fatigue and had therapy [[nygren2016]]; 46% voice problems in a masculinization meta-analysis [[ziegler2018]]. Semi-occluded work is recommended around pitch-raising because it is "more economic (in terms of tissue collision)" [[titze2006]]. Belting shares a high larynx and a narrowed epilarynx with hyperfunctional dysphonia on CT, but differs by a wider jaw and a larger oral cavity — the "megaphone shape" — and the authors ask about the "price of decibels" [[saldias2019]].
- **Surgery as a health event.** Roughness after glottoplasty [[mora2018]], increased jitter/shimmer/NHR and revision requests after laser reduction [[yilmaz2021]], granuloma and fatigue with combinations [[chowdhury2025]]; postoperative rest and therapy are stressed [[catani2024]]; a Japanese clinic's patient-facing page states about two weeks without phonation with written communication instead, at least six months before the voice is stable, and that the operation cannot be undone [[kyoto-ent-wendler]].
- **Effort and load.** Trans women reported more mental demand on the NASA Task Load Index after a speaking task than cisgender participants and than patients with adductor laryngeal dystonia; physical demand was raised in trans women, dystonia and vocal-fold-paralysis groups alike [[tang2026]].
- **Avoidance and what it costs.** A Brazilian letter sets trans people and laryngectomised speakers against each other on the same apparatus: both avoid the telephone and, especially now, voice messages, the laryngectomised from fear of not being intelligible and trans people 「evitam enviar áudios que possam revelar, pela voz, que se trata de uma pessoa trans」, while trans women 「controlam sua intensidade vocal para não chamar atenção」 [[nemr2026]]. The same letter records, as the authors' own clinical observation in Brazil, people injecting industrial silicone into the neck without medical supervision to disguise the laryngeal prominence and smoking deliberately to alter the voice [[nemr2026]].
- **The meanings speakers attach to voice.** In focus groups with 13 trans women, the senses given to voice ran through fear, shame and acceptance, their own and others', and the authors conclude that voice is an essential resource for their inclusion and acceptance and needs attention from the health system [[aguiar2026]].
- **Community safety warnings.** The English-language wiki rates swallow-and-hold for raising the larynx "dangerous" and glottal-stop slides "risky" because they conflate false-fold closure with true fold closure [[sumi-questionable-exercises]]; it says hyperadduction is often mistaken for vocal weight, and advises stopping training, resting, and doing only very light SOVTEs until the habit clears [[sumi-hyperadduction]].

## Contested or unclear
- Incidence of strain injuries from self-directed feminization training is unmeasured; [[huang2024]] is a risk signal, not an incidence figure.
- The transgender odds ratio in [[fujiki2024]] is a reason to ask, not a finding to quote as established (wide CI, n = 7).
- Hygiene education is widely recommended and did not predict fewer symptoms in singers who already knew the rules [[coelho2020]].
- Puberphonia is a disorder in the cis-male literature; the same M2 production, wanted, is not. The encyclopedia does not make that distinction [[wikipedia-puberphonia]] [[adabi2026]].

## Open questions
- Rate of phonotrauma, MTD and nodules in adults doing self-directed pitch-raising, with laryngeal examination rather than self-report.
- Whether hydration advice beyond "don't practise dehydrated" has a measurable effect on high-pitch practice sessions.
