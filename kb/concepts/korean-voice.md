# Korean voice and gender

What is known about gendered voice in Korean: conversational F0 norms by sex, the Seoul surgical ecosystem built around VFSRAC, domestic care access, and the Korean vocabulary learners and clinics use.

## What is known
- Korean conversational F0 by sex, from a 2,740-speaker dialogue corpus: median 114 Hz for men and 199 Hz for women, an ≈85 Hz gap, with a divergent age slope between the sexes [[yang2023]].
- The Yeson Voice Center in Seoul built the largest single-surgeon voice-feminization series: VFSRAC plus phonatory-pattern retraining, 362 patients 2003–2014, mean F0 144.1→207.4 Hz at 6 months and +73.6 Hz in the MTF group after retraining [[kim2017]]; the Type II update reports 506 of 1,025 patients, 134.5→196.7/212.3/207.5 Hz at 3 months/6 months/beyond 1 year [[kim2024]].
- The same group frames feminization as four elements — fundamental frequency, resonance frequency, formant tuning, phonatory pattern — and classifies surgery by how many vocal-fold factors (length, tension, mass) it modifies [[kim2020]].
- The Korean otolaryngology society's own journal now reviews gender-affirming voice surgery, repeating the F0-increment ladder (CTA 40–60 Hz; Wendler-type glottoplasty 60–100 Hz with 75–85% success) and naming preoperative voice therapy and intensive rehabilitation among what good outcomes need [[son2026]].
- Domestic care access and satisfaction: in a 2024 multicenter survey of 844 Korean transgender and gender-diverse adults, voice feminization had the lowest satisfaction of the gender-affirming procedures (56.6%), and the authors call for improved accessibility [[lee2025]].
- Korean-language clinical measurement is validated: AVQI, GRBAS and CAPE-V agree on a Korean standard passage in a voice-disorder cohort [[maryn2016]].
- The clinic ecosystem presents itself to foreign patients in Chinese community media — a Japanese patient's Korean vocal surgery at roughly 60,000 RMB [[bili-luna2023]], and a Chinese-language case video of a glottoplasty done in Korea [[bili-luna2023]].
- Community layer: a personal trans channel's surgery account uses the community shortening 트젠 and the term 음성여성화 [[ssal2020]]; commercial coaching spans an SLP-credentialled speech channel that has addressed transgender voice among general coaching [[eruseora]] and before/after-marketing "voice rehabilitation schools" selling non-surgical 여성음성화 [[voiceschool-kr]].
- Terminology anchors in Korean Wikipedia: 음성/목소리 (voice) [[wikipedia-ko-eumseong]], 성대 (folds) [[wikipedia-ko-seongdae]], 후두 (larynx) [[wikipedia-ko-hudu]], 변성기 (voice change) [[wikipedia-ko-byeonseonggi]], 포먼트 (formant) [[wikipedia-ko-pomeonteu]], 트랜스젠더 [[wikipedia-ko-teurenseujendeo]], 성전환 수술 [[wikipedia-ko-seongjeonhwan-susul]], and the register set 성구/가성/흉성/두성 [[wikipedia-ko-seonggu]] [[wikipedia-ko-gaseong]] [[wikipedia-ko-hyeongseong]] [[wikipedia-ko-duseong]].

## Contested or unclear
- The Yeson outcome numbers are surgeon-reported retrospective series, and the clinic's public comparison table rates rival techniques as falsetto- or rough-voiced — marketing, not independent comparison [[kim2017]] [[kim2024]] [[yeson-voice-center]].
- How much of the +73.6 Hz in the MTF group comes from retraining rather than surgery cannot be separated in a retrospective uncontrolled design [[kim2017]].

## Open questions
- No Korean-institution outcome study of voice therapy (as against surgery) for trans women was found; domestic Korean-language journals could not be searched from this machine (KoreaScience, KCI search and DBpia unreachable), so Korean-language trans-voice work may exist unfound.
- No Korean validation of the Trans Woman Voice Questionnaire was found, and no Korean listener study of trans or gender-diverse voices.
