# Speech-language pathology — who provides gender-affirming voice work

Speech-language pathology (speech and language therapy in the UK, 言語聴覚療法 in Japan) is the licensed profession whose scope covers voice and communication; gender-affirming voice work is a specialty inside it, provided by specially trained clinicians alongside laryngologists, with vocal coaches, singing teachers and other non-clinical resources named alongside them.

## What is known
- The profession evaluates, treats and prevents communication and voice disorders — the scope within which gender-affirming voice sits [[wikipedia-speech-language-pathology]]; the US professional body states SLPs "play a central role in clinical services for gender affirmation", listing assessment, treatment, education, advocacy and interdisciplinary-team membership, while diagnosing gender dysphoria is explicitly outside SLP scope [[asha-gender-affirming-voice]]; a Brazilian speech-language-pathology journal marked pride month 2024 with a bilingual letter to the editor on the profession and LGBTQIAPN+ populations [[depolli2024]].
- WPATH SOC8's voice and communication chapter covers assessment, training by qualified clinicians and voice surgery [[coleman2022]]; the UCSF guideline assigns behavioural change and vocal efficiency to "specialty trained" SLPs and phonosurgery, as an adjunct to therapy, to laryngologists, and requires evaluation by both before treatment starts [[ucsf2016]].
- The specialty starts with Oates and Dacakis's 1983 review of what speech pathology could offer transsexual patients [[oates1983]]; the standard clinical textbook is now in its third edition [[adler2019]], with a client-facing companion written by two UK specialist SLTs [[mills2017]].
- What a delivered course looks like: the largest prospective cohort had 74 trans women complete 8–12 sessions [[sodersten2026]]; the telepractice pilot compressed 12 sessions into 4 weeks with 9 of 10 participants completing [[lin2026]]; the crossover RCT ran two 4–5-week blocks of pitch and articulation–resonance training [[leyns2023]]; five sessions of oral resonance therapy raised formants and F0 in a ten-participant study [[carew2007]]; and in routine clinical care "greater change in f0 was directly correlated with increased number of voice training sessions" [[adessa2023]]; one Japanese ENT clinic's page gives the dose and price of its own 言語聴覚士 rehabilitation — insurance-covered at 100 points per 20-minute unit, 520 yen per visit at the 30% copay and 1,120 yen for 60 minutes — running every one to two weeks with home practice between sessions and reviewed after three months against whether surgery is needed, and suspended with no restart date when the page was fetched [[kyoto-ent-rehab]].
- Sessions are individualized in practice: a social-media survey of 52 Brazilian SLPs found multidimensional assessment plus individual sessions as the norm, with a defined strategy repertoire [[viana2025]]; group intervention is used for carryover [[asha-gender-affirming-voice]].
- Telepractice evidence: voice therapy by telepractice produced outcomes comparable to in-person in a nonrandomized trial with student teachers [[grillo2021]]; a clinical focus article reports comparable outcomes for in-person and telepractice voice therapy, including a transgender client who raised habitual pitch and quality of life remotely [[myers2022]]; an intensive feminization program delivered entirely by telepractice raised f0 and TWVQ scores [[lin2026]]; the professional body treats telepractice as "a viable way to provide services" [[asha-gender-affirming-voice]].
- Qualification is tiered and supervised in the UK: the RCSLT competency framework defines core voice competencies plus Levels A–C set by caseload and supervision, with pitch-surgery referral a Level C skill, developed through a profession-wide consultation in which 92.8% of SLT respondents already worked with trans clients [[rcslt2019]].
- Readiness lags scope: most of 140 surveyed Taiwanese SLPs accepted trans voice work as within scope but "did not feel sufficiently skilled" [[litosseliti2019]]; UK SLTs cite "lack of training, clinical experience or expertise" behind clients' difficulty accessing local services [[rcslt2019]].
- In England the pathway is commissioned: specification 1719 requires "expertise in gender-specific voice and communication development" in the specialist MDT and gives voice and communication its own lane — SLT assessment, then GIC-specialised 1:1 or group intervention or referral to local speech and language therapy [[nhse1719]].
- Access is the binding constraint on the demand side: only 23% of transgender and nonbinary survey respondents had ever sought care with an SLP, against want-versus-had gaps of 52%/23% (feminization-seeking) and 35%/8% (masculinization-seeking) for voice therapy [[ziltzer2023]].
- Goal-setting is client-determined: clinicians are told to refrain from imposing their own gender constructs, and some clients' goals are not binary [[asha-gender-affirming-voice]]; self-perception and clinician measurement disagree often enough that asking the client is part of assessment [[azul2018]].

## Contested or unclear
- Whether services belong inside specialist gender clinics or general speech-language pathology services — both models run in parallel in one system [[nhse1719]]; the Brazilian survey describes what sessions contain without recording the setting [[viana2025]].
- Whether competency tiers and supervision actually change client outcomes: the framework standardizes workforce, not effectiveness, and no study links framework level to outcome [[rcslt2019]].
- Drop-out: the only attrition numbers in the vault are one pilot's 9-of-10 completion [[lin2026]] and a retrospective series with missing pre/post data in its surgery-only arm [[adessa2023]].

## Open questions
- What a course of therapy costs a service, and what session dose buys what acoustic change beyond the single-centre dose–response correlation [[adessa2023]].
- How telepractice outcomes hold outside pilot conditions, and for masculinization as well as feminization [[lin2026]].
- Whether community-provided training ([[community-training]]) reaches the people the licensed system misses, and with what results [[ziltzer2023]].
