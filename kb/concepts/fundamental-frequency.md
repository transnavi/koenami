# Fundamental frequency (F0, pitch, 高さ)

The rate of vocal-fold vibration, heard as pitch. Koenami reports the median F0 of voiced frames (高さ). It is the single strongest cue to perceived gender, but not sufficient on its own.

## What is known
- Meta-analysis of 38 studies: speaking F0 accounts for 41.6% of the variance in listener gender perception [[leung2018]].
- Resynthesis: shifting F0 alone rarely flips perceived sex; F0 and formants together do so in about 82% of sentences, F0 somewhat more important than formants [[hillenbrand2009]]; morphing studies rank F0 as the strongest single cue, followed by formants and spectral level [[skuk2014]]; with F0 removed by filtering, sex identification of vowels still reaches 91%, with F0 present 96% [[lass1976]].
- In connected speech, female-typical formants keep a voice heard as female even with F0 lowered into the male range; male voices become ambiguous at SFF ≥165 Hz, and formants matter most in the 145–165 Hz zone [[gelfer2013]].
- Among trans women, the speakers heard as female had higher mean SFF and a higher SFF upper limit, and the upper limit correlated with femininity ratings [[gelfer2000]]; F0, average formant frequency and loudness were the best predictors of "female-sounding" ratings after training in the largest prospective cohort (n = 74) [[sodersten2026]]; F0 alone changed the odds of being attributed as a woman or androgynous rather than a man in a three-way task [[hardy2020]].
- Testosterone lowers F0: in trans men, mean F0 fell to 125 Hz by 12 months with large individual variation [[nygren2016]]; the drop is fastest in months 1–3 and plateaus by month 6 [[nimura2015]]; about 21% do not reach cisgender male norms (≤131 Hz) after a year [[ziegler2018]].
- Feminising hormones do not raise F0 [[leyns2021]] [[kobayashi2022]]; training raises it (+39.9 Hz weighted mean across studies) and surgery more (+30 to +73 Hz by technique) [[chowdhury2025]].
- Speakers deliberately raise F0 and formants to sound feminine and lower both to sound masculine, without training [[cartei2012]].
- Japanese female-voice attractiveness ratings peak around 280–290 Hz and fall above that (inverted U) [[tanaka2022]]; naturalness ratings of Japanese 両声類 voices were reported highest around 200–240 Hz and lower above ~300 Hz, from one contest sample [[hasegawa2017]].
- Japanese women speak with higher average pitch than Dutch women; experiments tied this to cultural associations of high pitch with modesty and dependence, and of Japanese masculinity with low pitch [[vanbezooijen1995]].

## Contested or unclear
- Whether F0 or formants matter more depends on the material: isolated vowels favour F0 [[lass1976]] [[hillenbrand2009]], connected speech gives formants a larger role [[gelfer2013]]; a synthesis study found no fixed F0 or VTL threshold, with cue weights shifting across the range [[neuhaus2026]].
- One thesis on trans women found pitch, pitch variation and intensity correlated with femininity ratings but formants and breathiness did not [[dahl2018]] — a small-sample outlier against the resynthesis literature.

## Open questions
- What F0 range Japanese listeners hear as natural for a feminine voice — only weak data [[hasegawa2017]] [[tanaka2022]].
- How pitch accent and sentence-final intonation interact with target F0 in Japanese.
