# Pitch and scales (音高)

Pitch is what a listener hears as "higher" or "lower". Fundamental frequency is the physical rate of fold vibration, in hertz. The two are related, not identical, and the mapping is not a straight line: equal perceived steps, equal musical intervals and equal hertz are three different rulers.

## What is known
- "Pitch is the quality that makes it possible to judge sounds as 'higher' and 'lower' in the sense associated with musical melodies. Pitch is a perceptual property that allows sounds to be ordered on a frequency-related scale" [[wikipedia-pitch-music]].
- An octave is "an interval between two tones, one having twice the frequency of vibration of the other" — C4 to C5, 100 Hz to 200 Hz, 200 Hz to 400 Hz are all one octave [[wikipedia-octave]].
- Twelve-tone equal temperament "divides the octave into 12 identical parts. It is a logarithmic scale with a ratio equal to the 12th root of 2. The scale unit is known as a semitone or half step" [[wikipedia-equal-temperament]].
- Each of those twelve semitones has "a frequency ratio of the twelfth root of two (100 cents)" [[wikipedia-semitone]].
- The cent "is a logarithmic unit of measure used for musical intervals"; "a single cent is too small to be perceived between successive notes" [[wikipedia-cent]].
- The mel scale is "a perceptual scale of pitches judged by listeners to be equal in distance from one another", anchored by "assigning a perceptual pitch of 1000 mels to a 1000 Hz tone, 40 dB above the listener's threshold"; "above about 500 Hz, increasingly large intervals are judged by listeners to produce equal pitch increments" [[wikipedia-mel-scale]].
- That scale was constructed in 1937 by fractionating tones: "This scale differs from both the musical scale and the frequency scale, neither of which is subjective"; a 1000 Hz tone was assigned 1000 mels [[stevens1937]].
- Measuring octaves in mels in that experiment showed "that the intervals become larger as the frequency of the mid-point of the interval increases (except in the two highest audible octaves)" [[stevens1937]] — an octave is a fixed frequency *ratio*, not a fixed amount of perceived pitch.
- A later textbook of hearing states its aim as specifying "the relationships between the characteristics of the sounds which enter the ear and the sensations which they produce" [[moore2003]].
- In chest-register phonation, pushing more air raises F0 only 2–6 Hz per cm H₂O [[titze1989a]] — a small linear-Hz effect next to the logarithmic steps a listener counts.

## Contested or unclear
- Mel-scale formulae after 1937 differ in their constants; the 1000 Hz = 1000 mels anchor is historical [[stevens1937]] [[wikipedia-mel-scale]]. Speech-technology "mel filterbanks" inherit the curve's shape, not those exact numbers.
- Pitch of a speaking voice is an average over a moving F0 contour; the music-theory articles do not address that [[wikipedia-pitch-music]].

## Open questions
- Just-noticeable F0 difference in connected speech, as opposed to steady headphone tones, is not recorded in this vault.
- Which scale (Hz, semitones, mels) Japanese listeners implicitly use when they rate a voice as high or low is not tested here; attractiveness ratings in Japanese peak around 280–290 Hz on an inverted U ([[tanaka2022]], on [[fundamental-frequency]]).
