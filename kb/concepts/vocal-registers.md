# Vocal registers (声区)

The vocal folds can vibrate in more than one pattern. Each pattern covers a stretch of pitches with a characteristic quality, and the jump from one pattern to another is heard as a break. Speech pathology names four: pulse/fry, modal, falsetto, whistle. A parallel physiological labelling, grounded in electroglottography, calls them laryngeal mechanisms M0–M3. Singing-studio words — chest, head, mix, belt — name sensations, ranges and timbres, and do not map one-to-one onto those mechanisms.

## What is known
- A register, on the encyclopedia's definition, is "a range of tones in the human voice produced by a particular vibratory pattern of the vocal folds", originating in laryngeal function because "the vocal folds are capable of producing several different vibratory patterns" [[wikipedia-vocal-register]].
- Hollien's definition, as quoted by later writers from a text that could not be fetched here, is "a series or range of consecutive voice frequencies which can be produced with nearly identical voice quality", to be defined on perceptual, acoustic, physiologic and aerodynamic levels; his inventory is pulse, modal, loft [[hollien1974]] [[herbst2020]] [[bozeman2022]].
- "The notion of registers is still confused in the singing voice community, and defined in many different ways": some authors treat a register as "a totally laryngeal event", others as "overall voice quality similarities" [[henrich2006]]. A review of scholarly writing found "more than 100 different terms for registers" [[herbst2020]].
- Electroglottographic study of transitions supports four laryngeal mechanisms, labelled M0–M3. A jump to a higher-rank mechanism shows "a jump in frequency, a reduction of EGG amplitude, and a change in the shape of the derivative of the EGG (which may correspond to a reduction of the vibratory mass)"; M1 and M2 overlap in frequency rather than tiling the range [[roubeau2009]].
- Herbst's tabulation of that paper maps M0 to fry/pulse, M1 to chest/modal, M2 to falsetto/head, M3 to whistle [[herbst2020]].
- Registers can be discussed on separable levels — proprioception, psychoacoustic perception, laryngeal mechanism, vocal-tract effects, and didactic systems — and disagreements often come from mixing the levels [[herbst2020]]. Chest and head vibrations occur but "do not significantly contribute to the radiated sound and may (only) serve as a feedback"; fundamentals above about 300 Hz are not felt in the chest [[herbst2020]].
- **M1 (modal / chest).** "Modal voice is the vocal register used most frequently in speech and singing in most languages", characterised on the encyclopedia's wording by "balanced airflow and glottal tension to produce maximum vibration" [[wikipedia-modal-voice]]. In chest, the thyroarytenoid is more contracted, "leading to a thickening, shortening, and medial bulging" of the membranous fold, and the cover vibrates with a mucosal wave of typically 60–90° [[herbst2020]].
- **M2 (falsetto).** "Falsetto is the vocal register occupying the frequency range just above the modal voice register and overlapping with it by approximately one octave. It is produced by the vibration of the ligamentous edges of the vocal cords, in whole or in part" and is "a characteristic of phonation by both male and female sexes" [[wikipedia-falsetto]]. TA relaxation with cricothyroid contraction gives "a greatly reduced or even absent mucosal wave" [[herbst2020]]. In 18 untrained men singing a glissando, falsetto was "associated with a higher open quotient compared with modal register" [[echternach2010]].
- **Why falsetto is not a feminine voice.** The same mechanism is available to any larynx: "there is no reason to assume that the register above the chest is functionally any different in females and males … Both females and males have comparable laryngeal anatomy and morphology, and they have access to the same laryngeal configurations through activity in the same intrinsic laryngeal muscles" [[herbst2020]]. A given speaking pitch does not tell you which mechanism produced it, because M1 and M2 overlap [[roubeau2009]] [[wikipedia-falsetto]]. Everyday speech in most languages is modal, not falsetto [[wikipedia-modal-voice]]. Listener gender perception is driven first by F0 and then by formants and spectral level, not by a register label: speaking F0 accounted for 41.6% of variance in a meta-analysis, with resonance, loudness, articulation and intonation also associated [[leung2018]]. At the same F0, mix sits between chest and falsetto on open quotient and on H1–H2, so a lighter speaking voice can be a mix setting rather than a flip into M2 [[lee2023]]. In the mutational-falsetto literature, habitual post-pubertal falsetto in men is framed as a disorder (puberphonia) whose first-line treatment is voice therapy to *leave* M2 [[adabi2026]] [[wikipedia-puberphonia]]; the production is the same one a trans-feminine speaker is sometimes told to "just use".
- **Mix.** Twelve singers producing chest, mix and falsetto showed mix as the low-pressure, low-flow combination: "The PSub of the mix was as low as the falsetto, and the MFR of the mix was as low as the chest." Open quotient at the same F0 ranked chest < mix < falsetto, and H1–H2 of the source spectrum ranked the same way. The authors conclude mix "proved to have a distinct voice-production mechanism" [[lee2023]]. TA/CT activity is gradual, "particularly in trained singers … likely leading to intermediate scenarios", so mixed voice can exist at the laryngeal level [[herbst2020]].
- **M0 (fry / pulse).** "The vocal fry register is the lowest vocal register and is produced through a loose glottal closure that permits air to bubble through slowly with a popping or rattling sound of a very low frequency" [[wikipedia-vocal-fry-register]]. Relative to modal, fry shows no correlation between F0 and fold length, lower airflow, lower cricothyroid and interarytenoid activity, increased thyroarytenoid activity, and in one type subharmonic vibration; the perceptual upper threshold sits at about 70 Hz [[herbst2020]]. There is not one kind of creak: prototypical creak, vocal fry (periodic, high damping, "picket fence"), multiply pulsed voice, aperiodic voice, nonconstricted creak and tense/pressed voice have different acoustic signatures, and no single measure detects all of them [[keating2015]]. About two-thirds of 34 young American women used fry, most often at sentence ends [[wolk2012]]; American women in California used creaky voice more than comparable men or Japanese women, and listeners read it as informal yet educated [[yuasa2010]]. The same feature is penalised in a hiring-perception study, more strongly for female voices [[anderson2014]].
- **M3 (whistle).** "The whistle register is the highest register of the human voice, lying above the modal register and falsetto register", so called because the timbre is "similar to that of a whistle" [[wikipedia-whistle-register]]. High-speed endoscopy of a classical singer on C6–G6 showed full glottal closure along the whole glottis, suggesting whistle "is [not] fundamentally different from the falsetto register as far as fundamental laryngeal mechanics are concerned. Rather, the main difference probably may be found in resonatory adjustments" [[herbst2020]].
- **Passaggio.** Classical pedagogy names the transition area between registers the passaggio, with a primo and secondo passaggio and, in the male voice, a zona di passaggio; "a major goal of classical voice training … is to maintain an even timbre throughout the passaggio" [[wikipedia-passaggio]]. In 18 untrained men, 10 showed irregular fold vibration at the modal–falsetto transition [[echternach2010]]. Four professional tenors crossing C4–A4 without flipping used more lip and jaw opening and a lower larynx than in falsetto [[echternach2014]]. On a mezzo-soprano scale, EGG showed a laryngeal transition and a later resonatory transition; singing teachers and therapists split roughly evenly when asked to name "one register transition" [[herbst2020]].
- **Acoustic versus laryngeal registers.** Once a voice is trained out of the binary TA/CT flip, remaining perceptible register changes can be acoustic: rising harmonics passing through stable tract resonances at locations "completely predictable per voice type and vowel" [[bozeman2022]]. In elite sopranos above C6, "there is no exclusive register-defining resonance-strategy" [[koberlein2021]].

## Contested or unclear
- "Head voice" and "chest voice" have no consistent definition among vocal-music professionals: each may mean a range, a resonance area, or a timbre [[wikipedia-head-voice]] [[wikipedia-chest-voice]]. German-language authors sometimes reserve "falsetto" for male voices and "head voice" (Kopfstimme) for the female register above the primo passaggio; Herbst rejects the functional distinction [[herbst2020]].
- Whether whistle is a distinct laryngeal mechanism or a resonatory extreme of falsetto is disputed: the encyclopedia asserts a "specific physiological production that is different from the other registers" [[wikipedia-whistle-register]]; the endoscopy cited by Herbst does not [[herbst2020]].
- Hollien's claim that registers are "totally laryngeal events" with "little or no overlap in fundamental frequency" [[hollien1974]] is contested by the M1/M2 overlap [[roubeau2009]] and by acoustic-register arguments [[bozeman2022]].
- Closed quotient from electroglottography is not a reliable stand-in for glottal configuration without per-speaker calibration [[verdolini1998]], and EGG open quotient agreed only weakly with high-speed imaging [[echternach2010]].

## Open questions
- How wide the M1/M2 overlap is in untrained *speaking* voice, as opposed to sung glissandi — the practical content of "weight at a given pitch".
- Whether a habitual trans-feminine speaking voice that listeners accept as female is typically still M1, a mix setting, or M2; no study in this vault classifies trans speakers by laryngeal mechanism.
- A validated acoustic proxy for the "weight" dimension teachers control (closed quotient, H1–H2, spectral tilt) that survives microphone and loudness confounds.
