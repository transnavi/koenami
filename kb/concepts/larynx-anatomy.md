# Larynx anatomy (喉頭)

The larynx — the voice box — sits at the top of the neck, just below where the throat splits into the windpipe and the gullet. Three cartilages do the mechanical work of voicing: the thyroid (the shield whose front angle is the Adam's apple), the cricoid (the only complete ring), and a pair of arytenoids that open and close the vocal folds. Two muscles set pitch and "weight": the cricothyroid tilts the thyroid forward and stretches the folds; the thyroarytenoid *is* the body of each fold and shortens them.

## What is known
- The larynx is "an organ in the top of the neck involved in respiration, producing sound and protecting the trachea against food aspiration"; it "houses the vocal cords, and manipulates pitch and volume", and sits "just below where the tract of the pharynx splits into the trachea and the esophagus" [[wikipedia-larynx]].
- The thyroid cartilage is "the largest of the nine cartilages that make up the laryngeal skeleton" and "does not completely encircle the larynx" [[wikipedia-thyroid-cartilage]].
- The cricoid is "the only complete ring of cartilage around the trachea" and "functions as an attachment site for muscles, cartilages, and ligaments involved in opening and closing the airway and in producing speech" [[wikipedia-cricoid-cartilage]].
- The arytenoid cartilages are "a pair of small three-sided pyramids", "the site of attachment of the vocal cords", and "allow for movement of the vocal cords by articulating with the cricoid cartilage" [[wikipedia-arytenoid-cartilage]].
- The cricothyroid "is the only tensor muscle of the larynx aiding with phonation", is innervated by the superior laryngeal nerve, and "tilts the thyroid cartilage forward to help tense the vocal cords, thus increasing the pitch of the voice" [[wikipedia-cricothyroid-muscle]].
- The thyroarytenoid "forms the body of the vocal fold" and "functions to shorten the vocal folds" [[wikipedia-thyroarytenoid-muscle]].
- The folds themselves are innervated by "the recurrent laryngeal branch of the vagus nerve" [[wikipedia-vocal-cords]]; that nerve supplies all the intrinsic muscles except the cricothyroid [[wikipedia-cricothyroid-muscle]].
- The Adam's apple (laryngeal prominence, 喉頭隆起) is "the protrusion in the neck formed by the angle of the thyroid cartilage", "typically visible in men and less so in women", and "increases in most men as a secondary sex characteristic during puberty" [[wikipedia-adams-apple]].
- In ten male and ten female cadaveric larynges aged 9–18, pubertal measurements were significantly larger than prepubertal ones in both sexes; prepubertal larynges showed "no clear sexual dimorphism", while "linear and weight measurements of the pubertal male larynx were significantly larger than in the female" [[kahane1978]].
- In that same sample "the angle of the thyroid laminae was not significantly different in pubertal male and female cartilages" while "the thyroid eminence was clearly more prominent in the male" [[kahane1978]].
- Comparing those circumpubertal larynges with 40 adult specimens (ages 37–70) locates the adult male–female difference in the larynx as a circumpubertal event rather than a slow lifelong divergence [[kahane1982]].
- Modelled scale factors put the F0 difference primarily on membranous vocal-fold length (factor ≈1.6) and airflow, power and vibration amplitude on overall larynx size (factor ≈1.2) [[titze1989]].
- Neck MRI (n = 44) and electromyography (n = 29) of the two pitch muscles found age-related decline of the thyroarytenoid across all measured dimensions, a more selective age pattern in the cricothyroid, and "higher CT activation amplitudes in females" [[malkidou2026]].
- Vertical larynx position, measured by multi-channel electroglottography in 29 untrained speakers, was "clearly associated" with lung volume (high lung volume, lower larynx) and "strongly correlated with pitch"; both dependencies were stronger in males than in females [[iwarsson1998]].

## Contested or unclear
- The encyclopedia explanation of the Adam's apple as simply "the angle of the thyroid cartilage" is the usual one [[wikipedia-adams-apple]]; Kahane's cadaveric series found the lamina *angle* not significantly different by sex while the *eminence* was [[kahane1978]]. Prominence and angle are not the same measurement.
- How much of adult larynx-height behaviour is anatomy versus learned posture is not settled by the lung-volume study, which used untrained speakers and inferred height from electroglottography rather than imaging [[iwarsson1998]].

## Open questions
- In-vivo, population-level MRI or CT norms for thyroid-lamina angle, prominence and fold length by sex and by language community are not in this vault; Kahane's samples are cadaveric and described as Caucasian.
- Whether cricothyroid activation differences by sex ([[malkidou2026]]) are a cause of habitual pitch, a consequence of it, or both.
