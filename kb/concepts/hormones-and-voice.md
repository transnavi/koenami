# Hormones and the voice

Sex hormones act on the larynx in one direction: androgens enlarge it and lower pitch, permanently; estrogens and anti-androgens do not raise an adult pitch back. Everything a transfeminine voice change achieves therefore comes from training or surgery, while a transmasculine pitch drop usually comes from testosterone alone.

## What is known
- Testosterone lowers speaking F0 into the cis-male range for most trans men: group means matched male reference data by 12 months (mean 125 Hz) in a 50-person longitudinal cohort [[nygren2016]]; all seven participants of a prospective 12-month study reached cis-male mean F0 within 6 months, with a mean 12-month drop of 6.4 semitones (49 Hz) [[irwig2017a]]; a Japanese cohort saw the steepest fall between months 1 and 3, with a further significant decline to month 6 [[nimura2015]].
- The change is not evenly timed or evenly complete: some people show no drop in the first 3 months while others show their greatest drop then [[irwig2017a]], about 10% of trans men on long-term androgens found pitch lowering not entirely unproblematic, and in the same cohort the lowest-pitched voices went with higher haematocrit and longer androgen-receptor CAG repeats [[cosyns2014]], and a meta-analysis estimated 21% do not reach ≤131 Hz after a year [[ziegler2018]].
- Estrogen and anti-androgens do not raise adult pitch: a systematic review of speech therapy in transgender women states gender-affirming hormone treatment does not affect trans women's voices [[leyns2021]], and the English Wikipedia's feminizing-hormone-therapy article states "A person's voice is unaffected by feminizing hormone therapy" [[wikipedia-feminizing-hormone-therapy]].
- Testosterone puberty leaves a lasting mark that later estrogen does not erase: trans women who took GAHT from adolescence still often had f0 significantly lower than cisgender women in reading and spontaneous speech after 5–10 years [[ciancia2026]], and trans women whose puberty suppression started at earlier Tanner stages tended to have higher adult f0 and reported more satisfaction with their voice [[ciancia2026]], [[boogers2025]].
- Testosterone's voice effects go beyond F0: over a year, shimmer decreased and fricative kurtosis increased while jitter and vowel duration did not change [[eagen2025]]; voice-related handicap after testosterone fell to the level of controls without voice disorders [[nimura2022]].
- Acoustics and self-perception can move apart during the first year: trans men's self-reported masculinization did not correlate with F0, range or habitual pitch level at any time point [[hancock2017a]].
- The endocrine guideline frames all of this inside multidisciplinary assessment and recommends no hormone treatment before puberty [[hembree2017]].

## Contested or unclear
- Whether trans men on testosterone need voice services at all: one Japanese cohort study argues intervention is unnecessary because VHI normalises [[nimura2022]], while a participant-centred mixed-methods study found 79% of its transmasculine sample showed indications of gender-related voice problems [[azul2018]], and a critical review rejects the assumption that testosterone satisfies every transmasculine client [[azul2015]].

## Open questions
- Whether the haematocrit and CAG-repeat associations with pitch replicate outside the Ghent cohort, and whether they explain the difficult 10% [[cosyns2014]].
- How much f0 difference each Tanner stage of puberty-suppression onset buys: present evidence is cross-sectional and from one centre [[ciancia2026]].
- Whether voice training in the first testosterone year changes the features hormones leave untouched (jitter, vowel duration, fricative skew) [[eagen2025]].
