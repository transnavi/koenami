# Male voice (男声)

A hub for what makes a voice hear as a man's. The pubertal growth of the larynx under testosterone lengthens and thickens the vocal folds and lengthens the vocal tract, which lowers F0 and the formants; after that, masculinity is carried by pitch, resonance, and learned style, and it can be reached by training or, for trans men, mainly by testosterone.

## What is known
- Men's membranous vocal folds are about 1.6 times longer, and the larynx larger, which sets the F0 ratio between the sexes and gives men greater power and airflow at the same effort [[titze1989]]; at puberty the male vocal tract grows longer with a disproportionately longer pharynx [[fitch1999]].
- Taller men with higher testosterone have lower F0 and closer formant spacing and are rated more masculine, the height effect running partly through formant spacing [[cartei2014]]; within a sex, formants carry only weak body-size information [[pisanski2014]].
- A man's voice is heard as male until F0 rises past about 165 Hz; between 145 and 165 Hz formants decide [[gelfer2013]]. Lowering F0 and lengthening the implied tract both raise the odds of a male judgement, with no single threshold [[neuhaus2026]].
- Testosterone lowers trans men's speaking F0 over the first year, but a meta-analysis found about one in five had not reached cis-male norms at 12 months and voice problems were common [[ziegler2018]]; in a Swedish cohort mean F0 reached 125 Hz at 12 months with wide individual variation, and satisfaction tracked lower F0 [[nygren2016]]; Japanese cohorts give the same time course [[nimura2015]] [[nimura2022]]. Over the first year shimmer also falls and fricative spectra change, while jitter and vowel duration do not [[eagen2025]].
- For those who want a lower voice without testosterone, or whose pitch dropped but who still hear as female, training and surgery exist: see [[voice-masculinization]] and the scoping review of masculinizing procedures [[kalra2026]].
- Perceived masculinity is separable from binary sex judgements and from perceived sexual orientation [[munson2007]]; gay men were rated less masculine than heterosexual men in that study. Voice-actor "boy" voices follow a convention rather than real boys' acoustics [[hayashi2026]].

## Contested or unclear
- How much of the male–female difference in Japanese pitch is cultural pulling of men's pitch downward rather than anatomy [[vanbezooijen1995]].

## Open questions
- Japanese norms for adult men's F0 and formants by age are not yet collected here.
- Whether resonance training alone (without pitch lowering) moves listener judgements for speakers assigned female at birth.
