# Source–filter model of voice

Speech is a glottal source (vocal-fold vibration, whose rate is F0 and whose closure pattern shapes voice quality) filtered by the vocal tract, whose resonances are the formants. Respiration supplies the airflow. The three stages — breathing, phonation, articulation/resonance — are the frame under which every training target and every Koenami indicator is placed.

## What is known
- The model was formalised by Fant; the tract transfer function is set by tract shape and length [[fant1960]].
- Respiration, phonation (myoelastic–aerodynamic vibration) and tract resonance are treated as separable stages in voice-science textbooks [[titze1994]].
- Sex differences at the source come mainly from membranous vocal-fold length (scale factor ≈1.6 for F0) and larynx size (≈1.2 for airflow and power); the male glottis converges less linearly because the folds bulge medially [[titze1989]].
- Sex differences at the filter arise at puberty: overall tract length and the oral/pharyngeal proportions diverge in males, with no such difference in children [[fitch1999]].
- Humans exert voluntary control over both F0 and formants beyond what speech articulation requires [[pisanski2016]].

## Contested or unclear
- How much of adult sex difference is anatomy versus learned behaviour is discussed rather than settled [[simpson2009]] [[whiteside2001]] [[vanbezooijen1995]].

## Open questions
- Which source/filter parameters can be trained independently, and which move together (e.g. F0 rising during resonance therapy [[carew2007]]).
