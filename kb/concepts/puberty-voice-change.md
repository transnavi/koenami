# Pubertal voice change (変声)

Before puberty, boys' and girls' larynges and speaking pitches are similar. In male puberty the larynx grows, the folds lengthen and thicken, the tract lengthens, and speaking F0 falls; the change is late, uneven, and longer than the folk "one year, one octave" picture. Girls' voices also change, more in the bottom of the range than in a dramatic drop of habitual pitch.

## What is known
- The encyclopedia definition: "A voice change or voice mutation, sometimes referred to as a voice break or voice crack, commonly refers to the deepening of the voice of men as they reach puberty. Before puberty both sexes have roughly similar vocal pitches, but during puberty the male voice typically deepens an octave, while the female voice gradually becomes richer" [[wikipedia-voice-change]].
- The same article describes a "voice crack" as the voice "suddenly and unintentionally enter[ing] a higher register (usually falsetto) for a brief period" [[wikipedia-voice-change]].
- The Adam's apple "increases in most men as a secondary sex characteristic during puberty" [[wikipedia-adams-apple]].
- Prepubertal cadaveric larynges (ages 9–18) showed "no clear sexual dimorphism"; after puberty, "linear and weight measurements of the pubertal male larynx were significantly larger than in the female", and male vocal-fold length "had increased by over two times that of the female", with adult fold length reached by puberty in both sexes [[kahane1978]].
- Extending that series to adult larynges (ages 37–70) places the adult male–female laryngeal difference as a circumpubertal event, not a slow accumulation through adulthood [[kahane1982]].
- MRI of the vocal tract found the sex differences in overall length and in oral/pharyngeal proportions "absent in children and arise at puberty, as part of the male vocal remodelling at puberty" [[fitch1999]].
- In 26 boys followed longitudinally, "there were abrupt changes in voice characteristics between Tanner stages G3 and G4"; "voice fundamental frequencies were seen to change abruptly in late puberty, in contrast with previous studies"; salivary testosterone concentrations "were not predictive of the changes" while testis volume correlated [[harries1997]].
- In the same group's ultrasound series, fold length increased gradually through puberty and correlated with F0, but "the sudden drop in frequency seen between Tanner stages 3 and 4 did not correlate with similar changes in the length of the vocal folds"; stroboscopy suggested a change in fold structure and mass at that point [[harries1998]].
- Forty-eight males tracked over five years: "both the onset and duration of voice change extended over a longer period of time than had been previously thought", and "maturation patterns appeared predictable on the basis of" adolescent-voice-change measurements [[hollien1994]].
- In 436 children aged 5–17 plus 56 adults, "differentiation of male and female fundamental frequency and formant frequency patterns begins at around age 11, becoming fully established around age 15"; male vowel formants changed approximately linearly with age in that window [[lee1999]].
- In 47 girls, speaking F0 from 2000 electroglottographic cycles "was related only to oestrone r = -0.34, (P less than 0.05)"; tone range and the lowest tone in the phonetogram correlated with several pubertal and hormone parameters, "all these correlations could be explained by a common age-dependency" [[pedersen1990]].
- Voice-range profiles in 180 children aged 4–12 found the habitual speaking pitch lowering "for girls between the ages of 7 and 8, for boys between 8 and 9", a "stage of premutation" in girls "with essentially the same changes seen among boys, but 1 year earlier", and "the beginning of the mutation" placed at 10–11 years [[hacki1999]].
- Adult F0 then scales primarily with the membranous-fold length laid down in that growth (factor ≈1.6) [[titze1989]].

## Contested or unclear
- "Typically deepens an octave" is encyclopedia wording [[wikipedia-voice-change]], not a measurement from the longitudinal series. Hollien's five-year study is explicit that onset and duration were *longer* than previously thought [[hollien1994]]; Harries locates the *abrupt* part of the drop late (G3–G4) [[harries1997]]. Both can be true: a long window with a steep stretch inside it.
- "The female voice gradually becomes richer" [[wikipedia-voice-change]] is unquantified. Pedersen's girls show a weak hormone correlation for speaking F0 and a clearer age-linked change at the bottom of the range [[pedersen1990]]; Hacki reports a premutational step-down in both sexes [[hacki1999]].
- Salivary testosterone did not predict the boys' F0 change [[harries1997]]; that is a caution against reading a single hormone level as a forecast, and it sits next to the adult testosterone-therapy literature on [[hormones-and-voice]].

## Open questions
- Longitudinal F0 and formant tracks for girls through Tanner stages, comparable to Harries' and Hollien's male series, are thin in this vault.
- Japanese-speaking samples of pubertal voice change are not in this vault; the measured series are UK, US, German and Danish.
