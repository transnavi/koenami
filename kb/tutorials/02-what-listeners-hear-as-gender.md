# 2 · What listeners hear as gender

Which properties of a voice make a listener say "a woman" or "a man", how much each counts, and where the research disagrees with itself.

## Pitch first, then resonance, then the rest

The meta-analysis of 38 studies puts speaking F0 at about 42% of the variance in gender perception, with resonance, loudness, articulation and intonation also contributing and tempo and stress not [[leung2018]]. Resynthesis experiments agree on the order: shifting F0 alone or formants alone rarely flips the perceived sex of a sentence; both together do so in about 82% of cases, with F0 somewhat more important [[hillenbrand2009]]. Parameter-specific morphing ranks F0 first, formants second, spectral level third, and finds aperiodicity (noise) irrelevant; F0 plus formants together do as much as morphing everything [[skuk2014]]. In 40 trans and cis speakers only mean F0 changed the odds of being heard as a woman, while formants and intensity predicted how feminine the voice was rated [[hardy2020]].

## A threshold that is really a band

Men's voices in English stay heard as male until F0 passes about 165 Hz, women's stay heard as female even when lowered into the male range, and in the ambiguous 145–165 Hz band the formants decide [[gelfer2013]]. Synthesis with F0, tract length and spectral tilt varied together finds no single threshold at all: each cue's weight shifts across the range of the others, and a breathier tilt helps a female judgement mainly when the implied tract is already short [[neuhaus2026]]. Two cautions: these numbers are from English, and Japanese pitch norms sit higher for cultural reasons [[vanbezooijen1995]]; and a very high pitch is not more feminine without limit, since Japanese listeners' attractiveness ratings of female voices peak near 280–290 Hz and fall above it [[tanaka2022]].

## Resonance works even without pitch

Listeners tell 8-year-old boys from girls although children's F0 does not differ by sex; shifting only formant spacing (ΔF) moves both the sex judgement and the masculinity rating smoothly [[cartei2013]]. In a voice teacher's recordings of pitch, resonance and weight settings, ΔF was the strongest predictor of perceived gender, and naturalness was unpredictable from any of the acoustic measures [[netzorg2025]]. Short biofeedback training moved F2 up or down on demand, and higher F2 raised perceived femininity [[kawitzky2020]]. See [[formants]].

## Voice quality: contested

Breathiness had mixed results across studies [[leung2018]]; noise did nothing in morphing [[skuk2014]]; spectral tilt interacts with the other cues rather than acting on its own [[neuhaus2026]]. One small study of trans women found the pattern differently from the resonance literature and is recorded as an outlier [[dahl2018]]. Creaky voice is a cultural cue: in young Californian women it reads as feminine [[yuasa2010]], in a US labour-market study it read as less competent and less hirable, more so for women [[anderson2014]], and young Japanese women use it far less [[yuasa2010]]. Several kinds of creak exist and no single measure catches them all [[keating2015]]. See [[voice-quality]] and [[vocal-weight]].

## Consonants and words

The boundary between "s" and "sh" moves when listeners believe the talker is a woman or a man, so sibilants are heard through an assumed gender [[munson2017]]; sibilant spectra shifted gender ratings of children's voices in morphed stimuli but not in natural speech [[funk2025]], and gender-expansive listeners perceive them differently [[hope2023]]. Articulation contributes to gender perception in the meta-analysis [[leung2018]]. See [[articulation]].

## Perceived femininity is not the same as "is a woman"

Masculinity and femininity ratings are correlated with, yet separable from, perceived sexual orientation, and different acoustic sets predict each [[munson2007]]. Listeners rate trans and non-binary speakers toward the middle of the scale and are confident in proportion to how typical, not how correct, their judgement is [[taylor2026]]. Across 45 recent studies, gender-diverse voices are often perceived as between the categories, and the field's binary methods may bias that picture [[rosi2025]]. Listener age and being gender-diverse barely changed group ratings of one trans man's voice over a year of testosterone: consistently female through week 14, consistently male after week 28 [[brown2021]]. See [[gender-perception]] and [[nonbinary-voice]].

## The speaker's own ear

Trans people's self-ratings correlate only weakly with listeners' ratings and strongly with their own satisfaction, and the gap widens after training [[quinn2021]]; quality of life tracks self-rating more than listener rating [[hancock2011]]. Pitch is the aspect trans women most often name as problematic [[dacakis2013]]. Both kinds of measure are worth keeping. See [[patient-reported-outcomes]].

## Machines

Random-forest scoring of perceived masculinity and femininity reaches correlations of about 0.6–0.8 with human ratings at seven seconds of speech [[chen2021]]; calibrated deep classifiers predict a continuous "voice femininity percentage" better than F0- or tract-length models, with accuracy depending on speaker age [[doukhan2024]]. Koenami's verdict is an uncalibrated projection of five measures on the axis between its sample speakers, and its page says so [[koenami-method]]. See [[machine-gender-scoring]].
