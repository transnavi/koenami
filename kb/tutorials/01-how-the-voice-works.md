# 1 · How the voice works

A reading path through the physics of the voice for someone who wants to change theirs. Every statement links to the note that holds the evidence; the concept notes hold the detail.

## Two parts: a source and a filter

A voice is a buzz shaped by a tube. The vocal folds in the larynx make the buzz (the source); the throat, mouth and nose above them (the vocal tract) resonate at certain frequencies and shape it (the filter). Fant's source–filter theory says the two can be treated separately: the source spectrum is multiplied by the tract's transfer function, whose peaks are the formants and depend on the tract's shape and length [[fant1960]]. This is why pitch and resonance are separate things to train: pitch lives in the larynx, resonance in the tract [[source-filter-model]].

## The source: how the folds vibrate

The folds do not flap because a nerve fires once per cycle. Their vibration sustains itself from the interplay of their elastic tissue and the air rushing between them, the myoelastic-aerodynamic theory named by van den Berg [[vandenberg1958]]. Hirano described the fold as layers, a pliable cover over a muscular body, and that body–cover picture is the base of later models [[hirano1974]]. In Titze's theory the cover carries a surface wave, and the lung pressure needed to start and keep vibration falls when the folds are brought closer together and when the tract above them is inertive; the tract gives each cycle a "kick" [[titze1988]] [[titze2008]]. The rate of this vibration is the fundamental frequency, F0, heard as pitch [[fundamental-frequency]]. Men's membranous folds are about 1.6 times longer, which is the main reason adult F0 differs by sex; the larger larynx adds power and airflow [[titze1989]]. How hard the breath pushes sets loudness in both sexes alike; at the same loudness there is no sex difference in air pressure, while men show a higher pulsing airflow [[holmberg1988]] [[breathing-and-support]]. See [[larynx-anatomy]], [[vocal-folds]] and [[phonation]].

## Pitch is a log scale

Hertz counts vibrations per second; the ear hears ratios. A semitone is the twelfth root of two [[wikipedia-semitone]], and twelve of them make an octave, a doubling of frequency, so a rise from 100 to 200 Hz sounds like the same interval as 200 to 400 Hz [[wikipedia-octave]]. That is why Koenami shows intonation spans in semitones rather than hertz [[koenami-method]], and why Hebrew listeners' femininity ratings tracked vocal range measured in hertz but not in semitones, a reminder that the two scales answer different questions [[diamant2021]]. See [[pitch-and-scales]].

## The filter: formants and the length of the tube

The tract's resonances are the formants. Their positions come from the tube's cross-sectional area along its length, which MRI can measure directly [[story1996]]. The first two formants carry the vowel: the classic tables of men's, women's and children's vowels [[peterson1952]] were remeasured forty years later with clear shifts, so no table is a timeless norm [[hillenbrand1995]]. The overall spacing of the formants tracks the tube's length, and the male tract grows longer at puberty, with a disproportionately longer pharynx [[fitch1999]]. Lowering the larynx lengthens the tube; that is how classical male singers produce the singer's formant near 2.8 kHz [[sundberg1974]]. Raising it, spreading the lips and bringing the tongue forward shorten it [[carew2007]]. See [[formants]] and [[vocal-tract-anatomy]].

## Harmonics are not formants

The source supplies a fundamental and its harmonics at whole-number multiples, plus a spectral slope: a shallow slope has many strong high harmonics and sounds brassy, a steep one has few and sounds fluty [[bozeman2022]]. The formants sit at fixed frequencies set by the tract and let through whichever harmonics fall near them. On a spectrogram the harmonics are the lines that move with pitch and the formants are the dark bands that stay put when you hold a vowel and slide the pitch [[harmonics-and-spectrum]]. This independence is the reason a low-pitched voice can still sound small and a high-pitched one large [[cartei2013]].

## Registers: the four ways the folds can vibrate

Electroglottography shows four laryngeal mechanisms, M0 to M3; moving up a mechanism jumps the pitch and cuts the vibrating mass, and the two main ones, M1 (chest, modal) and M2 (falsetto), overlap in pitch rather than tiling it [[roubeau2009]]. Falsetto has a higher open quotient than modal voice: the folds spend less of each cycle closed [[echternach2010]]. The words "chest" and "head" name sensations; the vibrations felt there are feedback and add nothing to the radiated sound, and chest vibration is felt only up to about 300 Hz [[herbst2020]]. Definitions of register have been argued about since Garcia, with over a hundred terms in use [[hollien1974]] [[herbst2020]]. See [[vocal-registers]].

## Puberty: where the difference comes from

Boys' voices change abruptly in late puberty, between Tanner stages G3 and G4, later than earlier studies had claimed [[harries1997]], and both the onset and the duration of the change extend over a longer period than had been thought [[hollien1994]]. Across 436 children the male and female F0 and formant patterns begin to separate around age 11 and are fully established around 15 [[lee1999]]. In girls the speaking pitch relates only weakly to one estrogen and the changes are explained by age [[pedersen1990]]. The cartilages and folds grow on sex-specific trajectories through this period [[kahane1982]]. What this means for adults: an estrogen-based transition does not undo the growth, and testosterone starts it [[hormones-and-voice]] [[puberty-voice-change]].
