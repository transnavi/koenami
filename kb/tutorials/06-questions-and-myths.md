# 6 · Questions people ask first

Short answers, each pointing at the note with the evidence.

**Is falsetto a female voice?** No. Falsetto is a laryngeal mechanism, M2, with thinner folds and a higher open quotient than modal voice [[roubeau2009]] [[echternach2010]]; it works the same way in women and men [[herbst2020]]. A feminine-sounding voice needs the shorter tract too: formant spacing alone shifts sex judgements [[cartei2013]], and tract length and pitch trade off with no single threshold [[neuhaus2026]]. The Chinese community guide sets its method apart from "假声" (falsetto) teaching for this reason [[digital-transchinese-mtf-voice]]. See [[vocal-registers]].

**Will estrogen raise my voice?** No; the systematic review of speech therapy for trans women states that hormone treatment does not affect their voices [[leyns2021]]. Training and surgery are the routes [[03-changing-a-voice]].

**Will testosterone always give me a male voice?** Usually, with exceptions. After a year about one in five trans men have not reached cis-male pitch norms and nearly half report voice problems [[ziegler2018]]; group means reach about 125 Hz with wide variation [[nygren2016]]; up to a fifth report dissatisfaction with pitch, which is what masculinizing surgery reviews start from [[kalra2026]]. See [[voice-masculinization]].

**Is higher always more feminine?** No. Women's voices stay heard as female when their pitch is lowered into the male range because other cues carry it [[gelfer2013]]; attractiveness ratings by Japanese listeners fall above roughly 280–290 Hz [[tanaka2022]]; and naturalness depends on loudness and speech rate, not pitch alone [[hardy2020]].

**Can an app tell me whether I pass?** No app is a listener. Machine scores correlate with human ratings at about 0.6–0.8 [[chen2021]] [[doukhan2024]]; Koenami's verdict is uncalibrated against listeners and its bands are a sample, not a norm [[koenami-method]]; and how you hear yourself correlates only weakly with how listeners do [[quinn2021]].

**Does surgery fix everything?** Surgery raises pitch more than training does, and the best patient-reported outcomes come from surgery combined with therapy [[chowdhury2025]]. After cricothyroid approximation alone, voices still sat between the male and female groups [[vanborsel2008]]; glottoplasty gives a larger and more durable rise with added roughness [[mora2018]], and any anterior fixation trades loudness and periodicity for pitch [[titze2020]]. See [[voice-surgery]].

**Is vocal fry feminine?** It depends on who is listening. In young Californian women it became a feminine marker [[yuasa2010]]; in a US employment study it read as less competent, more so for women [[anderson2014]]; young Japanese women use it much less [[yuasa2010]]. See [[voice-quality]].

**Will the change last?** Self-perception held at one year after a ten-week programme; listeners' ratings slipped by three months and then stayed [[leyns2024]]. Maintenance practice is part of the plan [[05-practice-plan]].

**Is this research about my language?** Mostly not. The cue weights come from English, Dutch, Swedish and German; pitch norms differ by culture [[vanbezooijen1995]]; questionnaires exist in Chinese [[wang2022]] and several European languages but not Japanese, and there is no Japanese listener study of trans voices [[japanese-voice]]. See [[voice-across-languages]].

**Can training hurt?** Forcing pitch up can produce muscle-tension dysphonia [[vanhoutte2011]]; untrained modification already shows fatigue-related patterns [[huang2024]]. Semi-occluded exercises are the gentle way in [[titze2006]]. See [[voice-health]].
