# 3 · Changing a voice: training, hormones, surgery

What each route does, what the best studies measured, and what they warn about.

## Training

A systematic review of 14 studies found positive effects of speech therapy for trans women on pitch, oral resonance, self-perception and listener perception, with small samples, no blinding and short follow-up as the recurring weaknesses; it also states that hormone treatment does not affect trans women's voices, which is why training is the primary route [[leyns2021]]. The randomised crossover trial that followed (30 trans women, two blocks of 4–5 weeks) found that pitch elevation and articulation–resonance training each helped, that doing both did more than either, and that the order did not matter [[leyns2023]]. At one year the participants' self-perception and satisfaction were unchanged, while listeners' femininity ratings had slipped after training and again by three months, staying in the ambiguous zone [[leyns2024]]. The largest cohort so far, 74 trans women after 8–12 sessions, shows group gains in F0 and formants, individual gains in loudness and in the level of the first harmonic over the second, and finds F0, average formant frequency and loudness the strongest predictors of a female-sounding rating [[sodersten2026]].

At the exercise level: five sessions of oral resonance work (lip spreading, forward tongue carriage) raised F1–F3 and, without being asked, F0, and made most of ten participants sound more feminine [[carew2007]]; visual F2 biofeedback shifted F2 on demand in one session, and higher F2 read as more feminine [[kawitzky2020]]; trans women heard as female had a higher speaking F0 and a higher upper limit of it [[gelfer2000]]. Community exercises such as "big dog / little dog" work on the same tract-size principle without trial evidence [[yoxon2023]]. See [[voice-feminization-therapy]] and [[voice-therapy-techniques]].

Masculinization by training has thinner evidence; the clinical review of the field under SOC7 notes it had received little attention [[davies2015]]. See [[voice-masculinization]].

## Hormones

Estrogen-based therapy does not change the adult voice [[leyns2021]]. Testosterone does: in 50 trans men, mean F0 fell by 3, 6 and 12 months to a group mean of 125 Hz with wide individual variation, satisfaction tracked lower F0, sound level did not change, and a quarter reported instability or fatigue [[nygren2016]]. A meta-analysis of 19 studies estimates that after a year about 21% have not reached cis-male norms and 46% report voice problems [[ziegler2018]]. Listeners heard one trans man as consistently female through week 14 and consistently male after week 28 [[brown2021]]. Over the first year, shimmer falls and fricative spectra change while jitter and vowel duration do not [[eagen2025]]. Japanese cohorts record the same time course [[nimura2015]] [[nimura2022]]. See [[hormones-and-voice]].

## Surgery

Pitch-raising surgery works by tensing the folds (cricothyroid approximation), shortening them (glottoplasty variants) or reducing their mass; shortening produces the largest F0 gains [[song2017]]. The 2025 review of 22 studies (977 patients) gives weighted mean F0 gains of about +73 Hz for vocal-fold shortening with anterior-commissure retrodisplacement, +54 Hz for Wendler glottoplasty, +40 Hz for voice therapy alone, +30 Hz for cricothyroid approximation, and finds that surgery plus therapy gives the best patient-reported scores, with granuloma and vocal fatigue more common after surgical combinations [[chowdhury2025]]. Glottoplasty gave a larger and more durable rise than cricothyroid approximation in a Spanish series, at the cost of added roughness and one severe dysphonia in 23 [[mora2018]]. A simulation explains the trade: fixing the anterior half of the folds raises F0 from 110 to 164 Hz, any fixation increases aperiodicity, and loudness falls once more than about an eighth to a quarter of the fold is fixed [[titze2020]]. Surgery alone leaves voices between the male and female groups in listeners' ratings [[vanborsel2008]]; reviews call postoperative therapy essential [[catani2024]]. Japanese practice is recorded separately: type IV thyroplasty tuned under local anaesthesia [[nakamura2007]], now compared with vocal-fold shortening [[hiroshiba2024]]. Masculinizing surgery (type III thyroplasty, fold injection) is rare and reviewed by analogy with puberphonia [[kalra2026]]. See [[voice-surgery]].

## Health

Untrained Mandarin-speaking trans women already differed from both cis groups and the authors flag a risk of fatigue and voice disorders as they modify their voices [[huang2024]]. Muscle-tension dysphonia, strain from excess laryngeal tension, is the pattern to watch for when pitch is forced up; it is treated with hygiene, voice therapy and manual therapy in a laryngologist–SLP team [[vanhoutte2011]]. In a US adolescent sample transgender teens were more than four times as likely to report voice problems, with a wide confidence interval [[fujiki2024]]. Semi-occluded exercises (straws, lip trills, humming) have a mechanism: they raise pressure above the folds and make phonation more efficient with less tissue collision [[titze2006]]; a four-week programme of vocal function exercises changed airflow and range in a randomised trial of healthy voices [[stemple1994]]; and in nodule therapy the benefit tracked compliance rather than technique [[verdolinimarston1995]]. See [[voice-health]] and [[vocal-fatigue]].

## Standards

WPATH's Standards of Care, version 8, has a voice and communication chapter covering assessment, training by qualified clinicians and surgery [[coleman2022]]; the clinical companion review under the previous version sets out protocols and good-care recommendations [[davies2015]]. See [[clinical-guidelines]] and [[transgender-care-context]].
