## What it offers
- Free, open-source phonetics software authored by Paul Boersma, David Weenink, and Anastasia Shchupak (Phonetic Sciences, University of Amsterdam), distributed for Macintosh, Windows, Linux, Raspberry Pi, Chromebook, FreeBSD, SGI, Solaris and HPUX, under source-available license.
- Site's own "Functionality" list: speech analysis (spectrograms, pitch analysis, formant analysis, intensity analysis, jitter/shimmer/voice-breaks, cochleagram, excitation pattern); speech synthesis (from pitch/formant/intensity, articulatory synthesis, Klatt acoustic synthesis); listening experiments (identification and discrimination tests); labelling and segmentation (multi-tier interval/point labels, phonetic alphabet, sound files up to 2GB/3 hours); speech manipulation (pitch and duration contour changes, filtering); learning algorithms (feedforward neural networks, discrete/stochastic Optimality Theory); statistics (multidimensional scaling, PCA, discriminant analysis); graphics (publication-quality PDF/PNG/EPS output); a built-in scripting language for programmability and automation; portability across machine-independent binary files and many sound/data file formats.

## What it measures/teaches
- Core acoustic measures relevant to gendered-voice work: fundamental frequency (pitch), formant frequencies, intensity, jitter/shimmer (voice quality perturbation measures), and spectrographic display.
- Includes an introductory tutorial and extensive manuals accessible from the program's own Help menu, plus a maintained FAQ and a public Praat Users List mailing group.

## Limits
- Desktop application (not web-based); the site lists this as the reference implementation other tools (e.g. Phonia, VoiceLab) describe themselves as "validated against."
- Scripting-capable but has a technical learning curve; the site itself directs troubleshooting first to upgrading versions, then the built-in Intro/manual search, then the FAQ, then the mailing list, before direct email to the author.
- No transgender-voice-specific guidance built in — it is general-purpose phonetics software, not a voice-feminization training program.
